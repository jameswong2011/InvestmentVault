# Nvidia’s Risky Business

*Stratechery by Ben Thompson — Tuesday, August 11, 2026. Brief score `4`. Holdings: NVDA (High); financing-risk macro for AI-infra book.*

## Thesis of the piece

Thompson analogizes Nvidia’s AI-infrastructure financing push to Jay Cooke’s financing of the Northern Pacific Railway, which collapsed into the Panic of 1873. Liaquat Ahamed’s book *1873* translates early-1870s US railway bond issuance of ~$500m/yr to ~$600bn in 2026 terms — “roughly what is projected to be invested by major tech companies in 2026.” Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella cited *1873* as “the book to be read” on the recent earnings call; Microsoft alone among hyperscalers still shows substantial FCF ($19.6B last quarter) and CapEx not funded by debt.

## Hyperscaler funding shift

- A year ago most Big Tech CapEx was equity/FCF-funded. Between September and November, Oracle, Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon issued a combined **$80B** in debt for infrastructure.
- After raising a combined **$108B in all of 2025**, these four had already raised **$194B YTD as of July 7**. Spreads are rising; **86%** of bonds issued this year already trade at higher yields than issuance; interest-coverage cover fallen to **<2x** from **5x in February**.
- In June Google announced an **$85B equity raise**, including a special **$10B issuance to Berkshire Hathaway** — signaling willingness to fund supply with all means including equity.

## Google infrastructure vs frontier

- SemiAnalysis declared DeepMind “no longer a frontier lab” after Hassabis/Dean departures; biggest beneficiary framed as Google Cloud / TPU monetization.
- Anthropic is a major TPU customer: SemiAnalysis says **>20% of total TPU shipments from 3Q26 to 4Q27** are being sold directly to Anthropic (ex-rental). Anthropic buying TPUs for its own DCs converts compute cost from marginal to capital.
- Google’s platform posture (Kurian) monetizes stack layers even when labs compete with Gemini — CapEx/equity raise underwrites infrastructure dominance, not necessarily frontier-model leadership.

## Nvidia’s $500B “investable asset class”

- Huang’s X Article announced partnerships with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilize **over $500B of third-party capital** for AI factory financing platforms.
- Pitch: Nvidia AI factories are fungible, CUDA extends economic life, compute is revenue — characteristics of investable infrastructure.
- Not a free ride: Nvidia backstops with **up to 25% residual-value-based financing**, i.e. puts its own profits on the line to lower customers’ cost of capital — a de facto price cut responding to Google/Amazon custom-silicon aggressiveness.
- Strategic risk: Anthropic has not been CUDA-dependent for years; OpenAI moving that way for inference. If those labs win, Nvidia’s profits get squeezed; novel funding that taps insurance floats and pension liabilities spreads unmarked risk beyond investment-grade debt.

## Closing frame

“It’s one thing to spend all of your free cash flow; it’s another thing to tap the debt markets. And, beyond that, it’s a completely new nerve-racking thing to bring safety-seeking assets to bear. AI better deliver before it’s too late.”
