**Subtitle:** The market bought four different bets as one thing: a currency trade, a hedged growth story, an acid arbitrage, and one name priced like $14,100 lasts forever.

Somewhere in Monday’s Bloomberg Intelligence sits a number that made me stop mid-scroll: one mid-cap copper miner is now priced as if the current record copper price lasts FOREVER.

So, which miner and whether the price is earned takes a few mins of setup, but that’s where the money is.

In June this letter said copper miners were too cheap, and we were specific about how.

Marimaca’s own study was worth $709 million at $4.30 copper and $347 million at $3.50.

Run the study's own numbers against the share price and the market was buying Marimaca at about $4/pound while spot was above $6.

“You are not buying a mispriced asset. You are buying the deck.”

And there were two separate ways to get paid, either the price rises, which is slow, or someone buys the company from under the market.

Marimaca closed at C$7.50 when we posted and is now at C$7.69 on 18 August. Up 2.5% through the highest copper price ever recorded.

Hot Chili went from A$2.00 to A$1.68, down 16%.

Meanwhile the second route June gave you kept going: Hudbay completed its takeover of Arizona Sonoran the day after that post went out, at the 30% premium it had struck in March.

The producers repriced hard but the devs I named haven’t.

So why?

Front-month COMEX settled at a record $6.7030 a pound on August 5th, the highest settlement ever. September contract traded as high as $6.7415 a pound on the 17th.

London went properly strange: cash traded $34 over 3-month metal at the end of July, $207.50 over on August 11, and $535 over by the 17th on official prices.

LME warehouses bled for 42 straight sessions through August 14, the LONGEST draw since 2014, down to 204,975 tonnes. Then stocks jumped to 223,550 by the 18th and the cash premium more than halved.

Every Q2 filing quoted below came between July 29 and August 5.

The question now is which copper you own, no longer “copper miners are too cheap.”

## June thesis number retirement

BI’s Alon Olsha and Salma Belmaachi published some nice work on August 17 that sparks my interest: they reverse-engineered the long-term copper price baked into each mid-cap copper equity.

It basically answered what price you would need to believe, forever, to justify the current share price.

Lundin: around $12,050 a tonne.

KGHM: $13,080.

Ivanhoe: $10,880, marked down until consensus decides whether Kamoa-Kakula comes fully back.

It’s all in LME terms btw, where spot sat at $14,100. The COMEX works out nearer to $14,800 a tonne. The gap is the tariff premium.

And Hudbay? $14,100. Which is SPOT.

One company in the group is priced, on this table, as if current LME spot is the permanent condition of the universe.

BI attributes the premium to the value the market puts on the Cactus project, lets hope its wonderful!

But you should know what you’re holding: an implied copper price is a solved-for-residual. It soaks up every other assumption in the model, same as an implied discount rate, so its not a seance on what shareholders actually believe.

Even so, a valuation that only works if the record price never ends has used up all the arguments in its favour before you’ve bought a single share.

If everything goes right, you get what you paid for.

That’s the bull case. That’s it.

Before anyone runs off and shorts something, there’s two things I’d want to know if I were you.

BI read this same table and concluded the OPPOSITE of what I’m about to, they think Ivanhoe’s $10,880 makes it cheap (can’t say if implied prices are real or nominal).

Ivanhoe to Hudbay is a 30% gap in embedded copper price inside one sub-sector (on one consistent method). The market re-rated these companies as one thing.

On Ivanhoe back in November I called it a backfill-methodology failure rather than an act of God, flagged the reserve-sterilization risk, and said the document that was important was the revised mine plan.

On this plan they cut reserves 25%, a published path back above 500,000 tonnes a year from 2028.

The market has had 5 months to read it and still embeds $10,880. So the Ivanhoe discount is no longer waiting on information, its more the belief in a plan from a management team whose last plan broke. (Also on the acid chapter its guidance reads in anodes these days because the Kamoa smelter is running, which makes it an acid SELLER in a region full of leachers paying up. They said the smelter is “delivering additional margin via significant by-product sulphuric acid sales,” and Friedland calls the acid “ever more valuable.”)

The market is discounting a mid-cap whose chairman is openly gloating about the acid squeeze.

They aren’t one thing. Watch.
