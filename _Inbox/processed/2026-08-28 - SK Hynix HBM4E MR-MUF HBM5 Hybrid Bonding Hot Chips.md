# SK Hynix: HBM4E Stays MR-MUF; Hybrid Bonding Slips to HBM5

**Source:** https://igorslab.de/en/sk-hynix-delays-hybrid-bonding-hbm4e-mr-muf-hbm5-turning-point
**Date:** 2026-08-27 · **Brief score:** 10 · **Tags:** daily-intel-triage

## Why it matters (portfolio)
Direct packaging roadmap for held **000660**: Hot Chips VP confirms hybrid bonding not ready for HBM4E; 775 µm JEDEC height; 16-Hi ~50 µm dies; thermal 2.2× across gens; HBM5 ~2029–30 for larger hybrid bonding.

## Article body

SK Hynix will apparently continue using its existing packaging technology for HBM longer than expected. At Hot Chips 2026, Jaesik Lee, Vice President of Package Engineering at SK Hynix America, explained that Hybrid Bonding is unlikely to be ready in time for HBM4E. This could mean that the new interconnect technology will enter mass production at the earliest with HBM5. The background is one of the greatest technical conflicts in modern High-Bandwidth Memory systems: the number of vertically stacked memory chips is increasing, while the complete HBM stack may only become higher to a limited extent. Symbol image 775 Mikrometer impose a hard limit on HBM4 JEDEC has increased the maximum specified package height for HBM4 from approximately 720 to 775 Mikrometer. This limit was not chosen randomly. According to SK Hynix, it corresponds roughly to the thickness of a typical 300-mm logic wafer. The HBM memory and the logic chip located next to it must later be at approximately the same height so that the shared cooler, or Coldplate, can reliably contact both components. A significantly higher memory stack would make packaging and cooling considerably more difficult. At the same time, the number of memory layers is increasing. With 16-Hi HBM4, 16 DRAM dies are already stacked on top of one another. SK Hynix therefore has to manufacture the individual dies increasingly thinner and also reduce the gaps between them. According to the company, the actual memory dies in the 16-Hi configuration are only approximately 50 Mikrometer thick. The distances between the layers have also been significantly reduced compared with 12-Hi. MR-MUF will be further developed through HBM4E SK Hynix currently relies primarily on MR-MUF. With Mass Reflow Molded Underfill, the memory dies are first stacked on top of one another and then bonded and stabilized together. The process is now well established, but increasingly reaches physical limits as the chips become thinner. In particular, reliably filling the ever-smaller gaps and controlling possible deformations are becoming more difficult. Nevertheless, SK Hynix apparently plans to continue using MR-MUF for HBM4E. Hybrid Bonding will initially remain a technology for later generations. With Hybrid Bonding, the memory dies are connected to one another much more directly. Copper contacts can be coupled without conventional solder connections. This makes it possible to reduce the distances between the chips while also shortening electrical signal paths. This promises advantages in terms of package height, energy efficiency, and signal quality. This technology could become particularly important for future HBM stacks with 20 or more memory layers. However, Hybrid Bonding places extremely high demands on manufacturing. The surfaces of the individual dies must be processed and aligned with very high precision. In a stack consisting of numerous memory chips, even a single incorrectly bonded die can render the entire stack unusable. Package height is not the only issue. Waste heat is also increasing significantly. According to the data presented by SK Hynix at Hot Chips, the thermal load has increased by a factor of 2.2 across several HBM generations. At the same time, data rates have risen from around 1 Gbit/s in early HBM generations to up to 8 Gbit/s per pin with HBM4. SK Hynix is therefore also working on new cooling concepts such as iHBM, which are intended to dissipate heat more specifically from the memory stack. HBM5 could bring the final transition Based on the current status, HBM5 is the earliest generation in which SK Hynix could use Hybrid Bonding on a larger scale. Industry forecasts anticipate broader mass production of corresponding processes approximately between 2029 and 2030. Until then, SK Hynix is attempting to further exploit the capabilities of the existing MR-MUF technology. The transition to Hybrid Bonding will therefore not be determined solely by higher memory bandwidth. Package height, cooling, manufacturing costs, and yield are increasingly determining how quickly future HBM generations can continue to scale at all.
