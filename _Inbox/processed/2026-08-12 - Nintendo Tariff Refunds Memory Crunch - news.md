# Nintendo recoups $300M in tariff refunds as memory crunch intensifies

*Source: Supply Chain Dive / Phil Neuffer — Aug 2026. Brief score `3`. Portfolio: demand-side confirmation of memory pricing power — 000660 (Full), SNDK (High), 285A (Low).*

Nintendo secured **$300 million** in refunds for invalidated U.S. IEEPA tariffs paid last year amid Switch 2 launch, per fiscal Q1 earnings. It says it absorbed most duties last year even while raising prices on certain hardware due to unspecified 'market conditions.'

## Key facts

- Refund helped fuel a **22-percentage-point YoY increase in gross profit margin**; operating margin surged **150.5%**.
- Nintendo joined mass of companies suing for IEEPA refunds after Supreme Court struck down the levies; consumers filed class action seeking pass-through of refunds — Nintendo moved to dismiss.
- Despite tariff relief, Nintendo faces another supply-chain hurdle: **rising memory prices**.
- Company took a **¥100 billion (~$630 million)** hit in fiscal Q1 2027 due to rising component prices, namely memory inputs, and further raised console prices.
- Demand-side OEM confirmation (not supplier claim) that AI-datacenter allocation is crowding out consumer bit supply and lifting memory costs — supports ASP power for DRAM/NAND suppliers into next quarters.
