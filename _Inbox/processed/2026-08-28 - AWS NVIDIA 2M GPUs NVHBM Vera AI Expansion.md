# AWS, NVIDIA Expand AI Infrastructure — 2M Additional GPUs (2027–28)

**Source:** https://convergedigest.com/aws-nvidia-2-million-gpus-ai-infrastructure
**Date:** 2026-08-26/27 · **Brief score:** 10 · **Tags:** daily-intel-triage

## Article body

AWS and NVIDIA are expanding their AI infrastructure partnership with plans for 2 million additional NVIDIA GPUs in 2027–2028, alongside Vera CPUs, NVLink Fusion, NVHBM, Spectrum networking and deeper integration with AWS custom silicon. Amazon Web Services and NVIDIA are expanding their AI infrastructure partnership with plans to deploy 2 million additional NVIDIA GPUs across AWS infrastructure in 2027 and 2028, while extending the relationship into CPUs, high-bandwidth memory, scale-up networking, government AI factories, data processing and robotics. The planned deployment will span NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra, Rubin and Rubin Ultra GPUs and follows AWS’s earlier commitment to add more than 1 million NVIDIA GPUs beginning in 2026. AWS said demand has moved beyond those earlier expectations as frontier AI labs, enterprises and governments scale training, inference, agentic AI and physical AI workloads. The expansion goes considerably deeper than GPU capacity. AWS plans to offer infrastructure based on NVIDIA’s Vera CPU while Amazon’s Annapurna Labs will extend its use of NVIDIA NVLink Fusion to NVIDIA’s new custom high-bandwidth memory technology, NVHBM. AWS previously committed to incorporating NVLink Fusion into its next-generation Trainium architecture. The new work would allow Trainium to tap NVIDIA memory technology and scale-up connectivity while AWS continues using its Nitro System and Elastic Fabric Adapter for infrastructure virtualization, security and scale-out networking. AWS and NVIDIA are also collaborating on NVIDIA Spectrum networking for large GPU clusters. For U.S. government deployments, the companies plan AI factories incorporating 100,000 GPUs on secure AWS infrastructure for federal and national-security workloads at Impact Level 6 and above. The collaboration also extends above the hardware layer. NVIDIA Nemotron open models will continue to be offered through Amazon Bedrock and SageMaker, while NVIDIA cuDF will accelerate data processing on Amazon EMR and cuVS will support GPU-accelerated vector indexing on Amazon OpenSearch. AWS said EMR workloads using G7 instances and cuDF can deliver up to 3.7x faster processing with 30% better price-performance than CPU configurations, while GPU-assisted OpenSearch indexing can reach up to 9x faster indexing at one-quarter of the cost. Amazon Robotics is separately adopting NVIDIA Jetson, Omniverse and Isaac technologies for simulation, synthetic-data generation, robot training, route optimization and real-to-sim validation. Software + Robotics GPU acceleration expands across Amazon EMR , OpenSearch and Amazon Robotics, including NVIDIA Jetson, Omniverse and Isaac technologies. “Customers want the freedom to choose the best tools for their AI workloads, and they want confidence that everything works seamlessly together,” said Matt Garman, CEO of AWS. “That’s why we’ve invested deeply with NVIDIA to make AWS the best place to run NVIDIA AI technologies, optimizing performance across our infrastructure from networking and security to deployment.” 🌐 Analysis The announcement reinforces an important feature of AWS’s semiconductor strategy: Amazon is simultaneously making enormous commitments to merchant NVIDIA silicon and accelerating its internally developed chips. AWS’s custom silicon portfolio now spans Graviton CPUs, Trainium AI accelerators and Nitro infrastructure. Recent Graviton5 announcements have emphasized higher core counts and CPU performance for workloads including the increasing CPU component of agentic AI, where accelerators operate alongside host compute for orchestration, code execution and other processing. Trainium is developing in parallel. Trainium3 is AWS’s 3nm AI accelerator generation , while AWS has continued expanding the Neuron software stack and large-scale Trainium infrastructure. The result is a custom-silicon strategy that gives AWS another path for AI acceleration alongside its very large NVIDIA GPU deployments. The more consequential architectural development is that AWS is increasingly connecting its custom-silicon and NVIDIA roadmaps rather than treating them as isolated alternatives. AWS previously disclosed plans to integrate NVIDIA NVLink Fusion with next-generation Trainium technology, and the new NVHBM collaboration extends that relationship into memory. The resulting AWS infrastructure model is heterogeneous: NVIDIA GPUs for customers requiring the NVIDIA platform; Trainium for AWS-optimized AI acceleration; Graviton for CPU-intensive portions of AI applications; Nitro for infrastructure offload and isolation; NVLink Fusion for scale-up connectivity; and EFA for scale-out networking. The commitment to another 2 million NVIDIA GPUs therefore does not represent a retreat from AWS custom silicon. It points toward an AI factory architecture in which merchant GPUs and hyperscaler-designed silicon increasingly coexist within the same infrastructure stack. We should also note that AWS’s semiconductor push has accelerated markedly in 2026. Amazon said this month that its custom-chip business has surpassed a $25 billion annual revenue run rate, with triple-digit year-over-year growth, while Trainium3 is delivering up to 40% better price-performance than Trainium2 and Graviton now serves 98% of AWS’s top 1,000 EC2 customers. Earlier this year, AWS said Trainium2 was largely sold out, Trainium3 was nearly fully subscribed, and customers had committed more than $225 billion in future Trainium revenue; Anthropic, OpenAI and Uber are among the customers making large Trainium commitments. AWS has also moved Graviton5 into general availability, offering 192 cores and up to 25% higher compute performance than Graviton4, while Meta has agreed to deploy tens of millions of Graviton cores for AI workloads. Taken together with the new NVIDIA agreement, these developments show AWS pursuing both sides of the semiconductor equation at enormous scale: expanding access to NVIDIA’s leading GPU roadmap while simultaneously building Trainium, Graviton and Nitro into a substantial in-house silicon platform of its own.
