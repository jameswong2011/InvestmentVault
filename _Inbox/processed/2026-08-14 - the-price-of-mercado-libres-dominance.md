# The Price of Mercado Libre's Dominance

## **Another Earnings Slump for Mercado Libre**

Unsurprisingly, Mercado Libre’s second quarter earnings did not impress Wall Street. Wall Street was more concerned about the 550 basis point compression in operating margins than surpassing $10 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time with 43 percent FX-neutral growth. There was also a fair amount of concern over whether the credit portfolio is beginning to crack as it expanded by 75 percent year-over-year to $16.4 billion. [MELI 0.00%↑](https://substack.com/search/%24MELI)

I largely disagree with Wall Street’s consensus. Mercado Libre is not suffering from competitive erosion. Management has demonstrably continued to execute every quarter with strategic capital allocation, and this quarter is no different. They continue to utilize highly profitable segments to subsidize behavioral change in user habits across Latin America. In this post, I will focus on connecting the dots and examining some major insights that I found throughout this past quarter.

## **Re-investing Margins Back into the Ecosystem**

Last year it was celebrated that the credit card portfolio was beginning to reach breakeven for cohorts that were 12-18 months old. This quarter, management revealed that the credit cards NIMAL (Net Interest Margin After Losses, which is essentially the profitability of the credit portfolio) compressed from breakeven back down to negative 2.5 percent.

In the first quarter of this year, MELI actually accelerated loan originations by 25% quarter-over-quarter and extended maximum terms for top-tier users from 5 months to 8 months. Martin de los Santos clarified this in the Q1 CFO Perspectives:

> “For certain users, particularly those that had paid us well and have behaved very well with our credit, we’re extending the term of the loans... on average we’re going from five to eight months... However, because of the longer terms of the loans, we are required to also provision more in this particular quarter.” - Martin de los Santos, CFO

SVP of Corporate Development, Leandro Cuccioli, also clarified that a fair amount of the credit cards issued are being used solely as a payment tool:

> “If you issue a $100 of cards you need to provision for as if you have $100 of loan exposure but in reality a lot of those people will use the card as a payment card and they pay at the end of the month the full balance... in the short term there is a compression in margins as you’re issuing cards.” - Leandro Cuccioli, SVP of Corporate Development

Make no mistake: it is critical for the long term that NIMAL remains positive to offer a margin of safety in the event of a severe credit crisis. But the entire credit portfolio is sitting above 20 percent NIMAL, which means regardless of what the default rates are, they are continuing to appropriately price risk. As for the credit card portfolio, the economics of it naturally have lower margins, so NIMAL will still trend lower over time as it makes up a greater share of the portfolio.

The reason for a year-over-year drop in NIMAL was a calculated acquisition cost. This past quarter, Mercado Libre issued 2.6 million new credit cards, which is a 62 percent year-over-year increase in credit cards issued. This strategy involves intentionally taking an upfront hit on these early cohorts because there is already a clear path to profitability, but more importantly, they know what credit cards do to user habits in the ecosystem. Over time, engagement with both platforms increases for seasoned cohorts.

It is also worth noting the short duration of these loans. Currently, 98 percent of the credit portfolio consists of loans with an average duration of under four months. This is very low compared to its competitors in Latin America and gives Mercado Libre a structural advantage in pivoting when the credit environment begins to sour.

To better understand why management views this compression as a re-investment back into the business, here is a direct quote from Martin de los Santos from the Q1 CFO Perspectives:

> “The investments that we’re making are bringing more people to our platform, are converting more people, and actually expanding the market… and those users are more sticky, they tend to come and buy in more categories and make use of our ecosystem in a much broader way.” - Martin de los Santos, CFO

Ecosystem users still represent a small portion of Mercado Libre’s customers and an even smaller portion of Latin America. This quarter, ecosystem users grew by 37% year-over-year, which outpaced the steady ~30% growth in monthly active users for Mercado Pago. Management also revealed this quarter that ecosystem users generate 70% more GMV and 90% more TPV than single platform users.

## **The Ad Ecosystem**

Mercado Libre’s high-margin ad segment is scaling very nicely. They continue to make key strategic partnerships, such as the integration with HBO Max. This partnership alone added 50% in volume to their video ad inventory, which is a huge step-change for adding a single partner.

The high-converting display ad landscape was historically dominated by brands with sufficient capital and ad agencies. This was not something that small and medium-sized businesses had resources for. Last year, Mercado Libre partnered with AWS and Stability AI to build GenAds to help integrate the necessary generative AI tooling into the seller platform for these smaller brands. This helped empower SMBs on Mercado Libre’s platform to participate in Mercado Libre’s integrated ad network. At the time of the launch, the VP of Mercado Libre’s marketplace said that Amazon Bedrock was pivotal in improving data processing and creative capabilities. The initial rollout resulted in over 90,000 product banners generated across Latin American countries, which drove a 45 percent increase in display impressions and a 25 percent higher click-through rate compared to non-GenAds campaigns.

CMO Sean Summers pointed out in a recent interview that AI allows Mercado Libre to move beyond local advertisers to the long tail. By lowering the barrier to entry for ad spend, MELI unlocked a new total addressable market of high-margin revenue. This has been complemented by the marketplace’s recent deployment of AI-powered search across its 5 largest regional sites, which generated uplifts in both conversion and ad click-through rates that are offsetting the cost of 3P LLM queries.

## **Social Commerce**

This past May, Mercado Libre launched the MeliConnect affiliate program. This integration allows them to seamlessly inject their catalog into major social platforms and bypass paying for impressions on auctions. They just pay for guaranteed performance conversions. Ariel, the CEO, revealed during the call:

> “The affiliates channel is actually scaling very, very nicely and becoming more efficient simultaneously. So, we think it’s actually working. The affiliate GMV share grew across every market in Q2, including Mexico where we dialed down some of the couponing that we’re deploying in the past.” - Ariel Szarfsztejn, CEO

Ariel also made it clear that the affiliate program is not just driving up one-time engagement metrics but actually bringing in ‘high-quality’ users:

> “The channel is not only driving one-time purchases, it’s also acquiring users who come back later to our platform, and that’s really exciting as well.” - Ariel Szarfsztejn, CEO

This creates another tailwind for the ecosystem where Ariel further explained how the economics are improving:

> “Economics are improving. Seller co-founding for affiliate is growing. So, extremely excited and positive on the outlook for the different markets.” - Ariel Szarfsztejn, CEO

This strategy has also been strengthened by a deepening alliance with TikTok. MELI’s strategy is to turn the social media giant into a discovery engine for Mercado Libre products. A TikTok rep at the Cannes Lions 2026 festival shared their view on the value of this integration:

> “In addition to Discovery Commerce, we also talk about entertainment commerce or shoppertainment. In shoppertainment, we see a very clear hierarchy: first entertainment, then conversion. I believe that with the Mercado Libre alliance, given the robustness of Mercado Libre’s product, that conversion comes very fluidly.” - Dani Craim, Business Director at TikTok

## **The Physical Moat**

At a recent logistics summit, Mercado Libre shared details on some of the R&D they have been doing with automotive manufacturers to customize vehicles specifically for urban Latin American last-mile delivery. They partnered with Ford Pro Latin America and their representative shared some of the latest features they developed such as equipping the vehicles with integrated camera systems and ensuring the rear doors only open with specialized proximity wristbands.

Their newly launched $140 million XEM3 cross-docking facility in Mexico adds the capacity to process 1 million more packages per day and deliver more same and next-day deliveries across 28 cities. Given that Mexico is where management sees their biggest opportunity, this is critical for scaling and capturing more of the market.

Something that got less attention during earnings was the quarter-over-quarter acceleration in same and next-day shipments while operating margins remained relatively stable. The new scale and density that Mercado Libre achieved over the last year is beginning to make previously unprofitable segments profitable:

> “The unit economics of this decision are also evolving as we expected. Free and slow shipments are now variable contribution-positive across half of the ASP ranges between R$19 and R$79, as technology, scale, and the use of previously unused capacity continue to bring costs down.” - Mercado Libre Q2 2026 Letter

## **Cross-Border Trade**

Mercado Libre is actively building a cross-border pipeline that connects the most efficient manufacturing hubs in the world directly to Latin American buyers. The VP of Cross-Border Trade shared on the management podcast:

> “It goes beyond just connecting supply and demand. We want to provide sellers in China and in the US with a full suite of technological and logistical solutions they need to unlock these markets… Increasingly we think about China and the US first when we build our tools to make sure they are scalable.” - Karen Bruck, VP of CBT

This represents a big strategic pivot beyond local merchants that will allow Mercado Libre to strengthen its positioning around product assortment and pricing. Management is focused on easing friction with international sellers and building relationships with Chinese factories. This will allow the business to lower its cross-border delivery speeds by injecting supply directly into its proprietary logistics network. Volume at MELI’s dedicated China fulfillment center surged 170 percent quarter-over-quarter. This comes back to strengthening the ecosystem offerings for the user and increasing buyer frequency:

> “CBT is another lever for expanding supply… in this case by giving buyers access to a far broader range of products and price points than domestic sellers alone could offer.” - Karen Bruck, VP of CBT

## **What about AI?**

Mercado Libre is decoupling its revenue growth from headcount. They spent an incremental $80 million on AI computing this quarter and this is already resulting in saved costs. Product Development expenses fell from 8.4 percent to 7.2 percent while productivity increased. This operating leverage was a result of unprecedented developer productivity where overall code submissions increased 110 percent year-over-year, and deployments increased around 75 percent year-over-year.

COO Daniel Rabinovich shared some compelling data on this front last year. Back then, he mentioned how AI was already writing 20 percent of their new code, and management has alluded to this ratio being a lot higher this year. Rabinovich noted that based on their growth trajectory, they should require half as many customer service representatives while continuing to achieve record-high Net Promoter Scores. They are seeing AI is contributing to an elevated deflection rate:

> “Using LLM models, for example, to resolve customer service... today we have almost a 90% deflection rate and record NPS. We grow double digits every year, and we are lowering the absolute number of reps because our robots understand problems better and resolve them faster.” - Daniel Rabinovich, COO

Andre Chavez recently confirmed that AI is rewiring their core financial engine. He said:

> “We are investing in better models for credit underwriting, better models for fraud prevention… We are investing in a lot of automation of back offices like customer experience, customer service, fraud etc... So we are all in on AI in general and I’d say it’s going to be transformative on all three fronts.” - Andre Chavez, SVP for Mercado Pago Brazil

The AI Seller Assistant resolves over 50 percent of Help Portal requests entirely autonomously without any human intervention.

## **The Stablecoin Pivot**

Earlier this year, Mercado Libre sunsetted Mercado Coin to focus on its stablecoin, Meli Dolar. This is more than a consumer crypto play because Meli Dolar will serve a critical role in B2B financial infrastructure. Meli Dolar allows merchants to hedge working capital against local fiat volatility, such as the Argentine Peso, and facilitates frictionless cross-border settlements. Mercado Libre is deploying blockchain rails to lower operational costs and retain merchant liquidity entirely within its ecosystem.

## **Beyond E-Commerce**

One concept the broader market struggles to grasp is Mercado Libre’s endgame. Management views it as the entirety of Latin American commerce, banking, and media. In a recent interview, CMO Sean Summers detailed the strategy.

When evaluating their runway, experts focus on the reference regional e-commerce penetration rates. This metric is actually flawed because Mercado Libre is also targeting offline incumbents. When asked about their trajectory, Summers said:

> “27 years on, I still think that the next 10 years are going to be fantastic. I think that this is a company that could be the biggest retailer in every market where we operate. Retailer, not e-tailer.” - Sean Summers, CMO

Summers projected that Mercado Pago will be:

> “a top five bank in every market where we operate over the next five years, and we have a fair chance of being a top one over the following three to five years.” - Sean Summers, CMO

Summers also provided a glimpse into their efficient marketing scaling strategy. Mercado Libre actively takes their proprietary performance marketing capabilities built for internal operations and packages them as high-conversion external products for retail media clients.

Summers noted that this operational dominance is the direct byproduct of Amazon entering Mexico in 2015. Rather than ceding market share, this competitive threat forced Mercado Libre’s leadership to completely pivot strategies. They had to aggressively learn how to:

> “…out-innovate them, how to move faster, how to be more aggressive on investment, how to be smart on capital allocation.” - Sean Summers, CMO

For the last year, Mercado Libre’s growth has consistently outpaced analysts’ expectations. This raises the question of when their growth will actually begin to slow down.

The biggest differentiator for Mexico is fintech. Unlike Brazil, where Mercado Libre fights several fintechs and entrenched banks, Mexico remains largely unbanked. ## **What’s the Catch?**

While MELI’s tailwinds are huge, any objective analysis must address the risks. As a long-term investor, I would say the biggest risk for the business would be government intervention because ultimately what makes this business so special is its flywheel business model. Without the marketplace and fintech segments adding value to each other, the ecosystem thesis pretty much dies. At the end of the day, Mercado Libre is just another story of Nick Sleep’s ‘Scaled Economies Shared’. The marketplace-fintech flywheel is their competitive advantage and is what allows them to provide more value to their customers at a lower internal cost.

Regarding credit, I don’t believe it poses an existential threat to the business, but I do think there is potential for it to become a multi-quarter headwind. A credit shock would likely tank profitability, but it would also hurt mid-term growth because the credit business has been a big source of outsized profits that continue to get re-invested back into user acquisition and ecosystem growth. My bigger worry is that any slowdown in credit would largely hurt MELI’s ability to compete for growth. There was nothing unusual about credit this quarter. NIMAL continues to trend down year-over-year because credit cards are less profitable than the remaining mix. NPLs remained relatively stable, and the average duration for loans is still under four months.

Management does not get into details about how they mitigate credit risk on the call but Andre Chavez shared further insights on the latest management podcast. They monitor collections on a daily, granular basis, which allows them to throttle credit card issuance or personal loans instantly if a specific segment deteriorates. Since Mercado Pago is the preferred financial institution in the biggest regions that it operates in it actually helps shield them in times of credit friction:

> “The level of NPS actually moves us up in the order of collections. So if a crisis hits, everything else equal, we are able to collect more than we did in the past just because we are the preferred financial institution of a lot of our customers.” - Andre Chavez, SVP for Mercado Pago Brazil

The competitive landscape varies a lot for each market. In Mexico, Amazon and Nubank are MELI’s biggest competition for their respective markets. Despite this, Mercado Libre is uniquely positioned to lead both markets because of its flywheel. In Brazil, Shopee is continuing its upmarket push and racing MELI to build out its fulfillment network. Mercado Libre successfully widened its gap this past year by lowering its free-shipping threshold but Shopee just recently made another push in Q2 by launching VIP in Brazil.

Back in April, MELI rolled out PIX discounts for buyers and cut take rates for sellers who kept their prices competitive on certain items. ## **The Price of Dominance**

To end the Q2 call, Ariel asserted that investment decisions are made in a disciplined manner with the goal of strengthening the flywheel of the business:

> “These are not two businesses running in parallel. This is one flywheel, each side making the other one more valuable. As we said in the letter, we invest in it carefully, with discipline, and only when we see that the economics are working. If we get that flywheel right, the result is structurally higher engagement, loyalty, and scale and that is what will maximize our long-term profitability.” - Ariel Szarfsztejn, CEO

This is exactly what I like to hear from management. Leadership is fully focused on influencing consumer habits across the region, and unlocking long-term value in their ecosystem.
