## ⚠ Thesis divergence

**$NVDA** — The highest-view non-earnings claim is still that Rubin rack shipments are gated by merchant CW-laser/CPO-grade allocation (LITE/COHR sold out, Landmark 4-year offtake, LTAs to 2030), not by CUDA lock-in, ASIC share, or hyperscaler demand—the thesis flags TSMC concentration and ASICs and does not model an optics/laser chokepoint as the ship-rate governor.

**$MRVL** — NVHBM co-designed into the AWS custom-XPU package with Annapurna as first partner is a more direct attack on the memory-interface/near-memory content Marvell bought Celestial, XConn and Polariton to own—and on the Amazon warrant the thesis ties to Photonic Fabric/Trainium 4—than the thesis’s stated Celestial falsifier (CMX/BlueField-4 + HBF taking the KV-cache socket).

**$000660** — NVHBM is a specific claim that Nvidia is standardizing the HBM implementation and pulling the controller into the base die so Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron compete as interchangeable commodity suppliers—undercutting the thesis’s mechanism that dual-source share shift (Samsung >35% of Rubin SKUs) is what compresses SK Hynix’s 60%+ HBM gross margin, because Nvidia is trying to erase vendor process differentiation itself.

**$SNDK** — The crowd reads $31B of Japan NAND expansion as confirmation that AI storage tightness justifies more supply; that is the classic late-cycle capex response the thesis flags when it asks whether ~80%+ gross margins are a peak that reverts once someone breaks discipline and 2027 capacity arrives.

**$TSM** — The tariff thread’s specific claim is that the US may tax finished electronics (laptops/servers) to force ~40% of Taiwan’s supply chain onshore—an industrial-policy demand/cost shock and accelerated geographic split that the thesis models only as a voluntary $265B Arizona build with 2–4pt margin dilution, not as a finished-goods tariff regime.

**$INTC** — The crowd treats US tariff policy and Commerce-on-the-fab-floor as a volume-allocation machine that can load Intel Foundry and justify six fabs now—whereas the thesis reads the USG 10% stake as a structural constraint that blocks a foundry spin and only puts a floor under survival, with IFS still a loss-leader on internal wafers.

**$NBIS** — The crowd locates the investable binary in moving up the stack into Google-style agentic software (Tavily, Agents Blueprint, Echo) with data centers as a foundation, challenging the thesis that NBIS is decided by whether it can physically energize 800MW–1GW by YE2026 off a ~170MW base.

**$SIVE** — The ER’s six new pluggable engagements, dated Jabil H1 2027 production orders, and a second foundry with capacity available now are being treated as commercial conversion—challenging the thesis that every named partnership remains pre-revenue/binary and that the 4-inch Glasgow line is structurally locked out until LITE/COHR 6-inch ramps. The Nasdaq dual-listing and Swedish Economic Crime Authority probe remain unmentioned as legal risk; the crowd’s listing complaint is that management will not commit to filing.

**$285A** — The crowd treats the $31B Japan/Iwate build as tightness-justified expansion; that collides with Kioxia’s own archived stance against share-chasing capex and with the thesis’s cycle-end question—whether 2027 is the inflection if someone breaks discipline—rather than as confirmation of a durable supercycle.

**$IREN** — The crowd is treating IREN’s $25–30B FY27 spend (after $19B raised) as evidence it is now a larger AI-infrastructure buildout than Nebius; the thesis treats a ~$21B funding gap inside the fragile tranche as the thesis-killer and locates value in owned power, not in winning a capex-size contest.
