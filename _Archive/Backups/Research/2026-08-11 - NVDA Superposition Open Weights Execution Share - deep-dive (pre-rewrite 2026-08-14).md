## Thesis Delta
Consensus treats open weights as either “bearish CUDA” or “irrelevant” → Superposition_V’s Part 1 decomposes open weights into **demand-side fungibility vs silicon portability**, with NVIDIA capture ≈ **N×A×S×V**; openness can expand tasks/intensity while contesting execution share. ## Summary
Open weights expand who can deploy and which execution paths can be attempted—not automatically net accelerator-hours. Distinguishes demand-side fungibility (remarket capacity across customers/workloads) from silicon portability (move workload across NVDA/AMD/TPU/Trainium/XPU). Portability has formal / technical / economic layers; HIP≠CUDA drop-in; Trainium’s PyTorch/XLA→TorchNeuron shift shows entry barriers ≠ economic substitutability. NVDA both signs open-weights leadership letters and discloses open-source foundation models on competitors as a 10-K risk—interpreted as expand-the-market while defending default execution. CUDA moat framed as accumulated production cost across kernels, validation, and ops—not syntax. Testable prediction: if major open models reach qualified production on alt silicon faster/cheaper, switching costs weaken; if demos proliferate but production gaps remain, open weights may *concentrate* demand on NVDA.

## Framework / Mental Model
**N×A×S×V heuristic:** N=successful production tasks; A=normalized accelerator work/task; S=NVIDIA execution share; V=stack value per NVDA workload unit. Separate market expansion (N,A) from allocation (S,V).
