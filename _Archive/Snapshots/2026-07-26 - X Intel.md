**$NBIS** — The open-weights/local-inference argument (@ripster47) contends an industry shift toward efficient local inference erodes the mega-training-cluster demand that underpins NBIS's contracted backlog. The thesis carries hyperscaler in-housing risk but treats demand as de-risked (sold-out, contracted through 2031) and does not engage a demand-erosion vector that attacks the backlog itself.

**$HIMS** — @JonahLupton (an actual peptide user) flags that if legal peptide prices run 50-100% above grey-market prices, customers simply keep buying grey-market — a demand ceiling the thesis's $300-500M incremental peptide-revenue catalyst treats as clean upside without engaging grey-market price competition as a monetization cap.

**$MRVL** — The crowd frames Marvell's >$10B custom-silicon target as a compounding, near-monopoly/AVGO-class franchise. The thesis argues the opposite — the custom-ASIC 'second seat' is a structural procurement feature won by elimination, carrying a ~5pp lower margin ceiling than Broadcom and churning seat-by-seat (Trainium 3 lost to Alchip, Maia contested, Google fourth-in-line) — so the category compounds even as individual sockets are lower-quality than the crowd assumes.

**$SIVE** — The crowd treats SIVE's ecosystem integration (GF SCALE reference laser, MI500 read-through, CW-WDM MSA membership) as concrete, imminent design-win de-risking, while the thesis holds every named partnership is still pre-revenue and binary — and the thesis's load-bearing catalyst/risk, the Nasdaq NY dual-listing under the Swedish Economic Crime Authority preliminary investigation, is entirely absent from the crowd's bull case.

**$SOI** — The crowd frames Soitec's optical strength as a clean pure-play monopoly beat, foregrounding only Photonics-SOI/Edge & Cloud AI growth — it does not engage the thesis's central caveat that RF-SOI/Mobile (52% of revenue, -41% YoY) still dominates the P&L and drove the FY26 revenue collapse, nor the rich ~11.5x EV/revenue multiple and CEO-transition overhang that gate the equity.

**$AAOI** — The crowd claims AAOI's internal InP fab 'insulates it from 200G laser-diode shortages while safeguarding gross margins' — directly contradicting the thesis's core insight that AAOI's fab makes only 1310nm DFB lasers (CATV/lower-speed), NOT the 200G EMLs that gate 1.6T, so its 200G supply is merchant-sourced like the Chinese assemblers' and provides no margin protection at the bleeding edge (evidenced by flat ~29-30% gross margin despite 51% revenue growth).

**$CRWV** — The crowd's central claim is that CRWV's backlog is 'safe' and its financials 'de-risk as the backlog converts' — the direct inverse of the thesis, which argues the load-bearing risk is credit/counterparty (67% Microsoft, OpenAI's $22.4B commitment with no operating cash flow, and the DDTL amortization-cliff on second-cycle GPU re-rent rates already down 50-70%). The crowd treats contracted backlog as forward protection; the thesis treats the re-rent economics behind it as the unpriced credit risk.
