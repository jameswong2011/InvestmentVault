# Batteries & Energy Storage

## Active Theses
- [[Theses/CATL - Contemporary Amperex Technology]]

## Key industry questions
- **Margin durability through lithium reflation**: how much of cell-leader 2025 margin expansion (CATL op margin 10–15% → 18%) was cost leadership vs lithium pass-through timing that reverses as carbonate re-inflates (CNY >200k/t Jan 2026 rally)?
- **Does ESS escape the EV-battery commoditization arc** — or repeat it faster as every idle EV line pivots to storage? Global BESS shipments +75.5% to 421 GWh in 2025, 450–600 GWh forecast 2026; ESS gross margins currently *above* power-battery margins.
- **Geopolitical bifurcation endgame**: does the US wall (FEOC, 1260H, tariffs stacking to ~54%) produce a protected-but-uncompetitive Western industry (Northvolt base rate) and who arbitrages the gap — LRS-style licensing, Korean AMPC-subsidized capacity, or nobody?
- **Anti-involution**: does Beijing's forced capacity exit (utilization 50–55% → >60% target) mark the capital-cycle turn that restores leader pricing power, or does political price-flooring cap the upside for a 47%-share national champion?
- **Chemistry succession risk**: do sodium-ion (mass deployment 2026) and solid-state (small-scale 2027, mass ≥2030) extend incumbent scale advantages — incumbents lead both — or open a leapfrog window (Toyota, QuantumScape) that voids LFP scale?
- **What replaces China EV demand growth** (NEV sales −13% H1 2026, purchase tax returning) as the sector's volume engine — exports, ESS, or swap/replacement infrastructure?

## Industry history
- **1991–2008 — Japanese consumer era**: Sony commercializes lithium-ion (1991); Sanyo/Sony/Panasonic dominate consumer cells; Korea enters late-1990s (LG Chem, Samsung SDI) and takes share on cost. Pricing power sits with cell buyers (consumer electronics OEMs); cells are a component business.
- **2008–2014 — EV irruption**: Tesla-Panasonic 18650 partnership, Nissan Leaf (AESC JV), BYD builds LFP for its own buses/cars. ATL's EV unit spins out as CATL (2011, Ningde) — the pivotal founding in today's structure.
- **2015–2019 — China's whitelist incubation**: MIIT subsidy "whitelist" effectively excludes Korean/Japanese cells from China NEV subsidies, handing the world's largest EV market to domestic cell makers. CATL passes BYD (which restricted cells to its own vehicles) to become #1 (2017). NCM chemistry wins on energy density; LFP relegated to buses.
- **2020–2022 — LFP resurgence + scale race**: cell-to-pack engineering (CATL CTP, BYD Blade, 2020) closes the pack-level density gap and LFP's cost/safety/cycle-life advantages retake the majority of China installations. Lithium spikes (~CNY 600k/t, Nov 2022); leaders demonstrate pass-through pricing power for the first time. LGES IPO (Jan 2022, ~$10.8bn, Korea's largest); US IRA (Aug 2022) launches a subsidized Western localization boom (AMPC: $35/kWh cells + $10/kWh modules).
- **2023–2025 — glut and Western failure**: Chinese capacity build overshoots (~4,800 GWh planned vs ~1,000 GWh demand); pack prices collapse $139 (2023) → $94 (2024) → $84/kWh (2025, BNEF); cell prices reach $53–56/kWh wholesale. Northvolt — Europe's champion, ~$15bn raised — files Chapter 11 (Nov 2024), Swedish bankruptcy (Mar 2025): the definitive proof that cell manufacturing at frontier cost is process-craft that capital alone cannot buy. CATL H-share IPO (May 2025, $4.6bn, world's largest of 2025) funds European localization.
- **2026 — bifurcation + consolidation**: US halts EV purchase subsidies (end-2025) and walls off Chinese cells (FEOC, 1260H, tariffs); Korean makers post first-ever simultaneous quarterly losses (Q1 2026) then recover on AMPC + ESS pivot; Beijing's anti-involution campaign begins forcing low-end capacity exit; ESS demand (grid + AI datacenters) becomes the sector's marginal growth engine. Pricing power arc: buyers → leaders (2021–22) → buyers (glut) → tentatively re-floored by policy (2026).

## Competitive dynamics
**Global EV battery share (H1 2026, SNE — market 608.5 GWh, +20% YoY):**

| Rank | Player | Share | GWh | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CATL | 39.9% | 242.7 | 9th straight year #1; China share 47.0% (Apr 2026) |
| 2 | BYD (FinDreams) | 14.4% | 87.7 | 58% captive to BYD vehicles; share falling (17.0% → 14.4%) |
| 3 | LG Energy Solution | ~8.6% | 52.6 | +8.4% YoY; 440 GWh order backlog; AMPC-dependent profitability |
| — | Chinese top-10 members (7) | 72.4% combined | — | CALB, EVE, Gotion, Sunwoda scale as tier-2 |

- **Cost curve is the moat**: CATL/BYD LFP packs at $50–60/kWh vs ~$108 global blended and $120–130 US/EU — Western/Korean production is structurally underwater without subsidy (AMPC) or tariff protection. Korean utilization fell below 50% (LGES 47.6% 2025; Samsung SDI 65% Q1 2026); SK On is in restructuring/voluntary retirements; Samsung SDI–Stellantis are unwinding their US JV (Feb 2026).
- **ESS is the growth battleground**: CATL leads (30.4% 2025; 27.1% H1 2026 as non-China demand overtakes China); Tesla pre-committed 20 GWh of CATL cells for storage 2026–28; LGES repurposes NMC EV lines to LFP ESS cells (50 GWh+ US target) — the one segment where Western-localized capacity has a genuine tariff-protected opening.
- **Durability read**: CATL+BYD dual-oligopoly is not contestable medium-term on cost or scale; the contest is at the edges — regional share (localization mandates), chemistry transitions, and the protected US market where Chinese leaders can participate only via licensing (CATL LRS: Ford live 2026, GM in talks) or not at all.
- **Pricing power today**: trough passed in China ESS (cell quotes recovering toward 0.4 RMB/Wh; systems 0.55 → 0.61 RMB/Wh Dec 2025 → Feb 2026) with anti-involution as the enforcement mechanism; EV cells remain buyer-favored except at the fast-charge frontier (10C+), where only 1–2 suppliers qualify.

## Acquisitions and new entrants
- **Failed challengers define the entry barrier**: Northvolt (~$15bn raised → bankruptcy Mar 2025 → assets to US lithium-sulfur startup Lyten for ~$5bn, restart targeted H2 2026); ACC (Stellantis/Mercedes JV) paused German/Italian plants (2024); SPAC-era solid-state names (QuantumScape, Solid Power) burned a decade of cash without volume — QS's Eagle Line (Feb 2026, VW PowerCo) is the survivor's next test. The pattern: yield/process craft is tacit knowledge that capital cannot transplant.
- **Strategic stakes over outright M&A**: VW–Gotion 26% (2020); Envision acquires AESC from Nissan (2018–19); Tesla–Maxwell dry-electrode (2019, fed 4680 — which still misses cost targets); CATL ≤RMB 2.5bn into NIO's swap network (2026); LG–SK trade-secret war settled $1.8bn (2021) — IP litigation as competitive weapon.
- **Vertical integration as margin defense**: CATL's Jianxiawo lepidolite mine (~100k t/yr LCE refinery; its Aug 2025 suspension alone moved global lithium prices — single-mine systemic sensitivity) + Brunp recycling; BYD mines-to-vehicle integration. Upstream ownership is now table stakes for the cost curve's bottom quartile.
- **New-entrant vectors that could matter**: Lyten's lithium-sulfur (if it converts Northvolt's shell into a chemistry leapfrog rather than inheriting its cost problem); Chinese tier-2 (CALB, EVE, Sunwoda, SVOLT) consolidating or exiting under anti-involution; Indian entrants (Reliance giga-ambitions) licensing rather than inventing. Net effect on incumbent pricing power so far: **strengthening** — every failed entrant validates the moat.

## Macro shifts
- **AI datacenter electricity demand** → BESS as grid-balancing + on-site power infrastructure: global storage capacity projected ~6x 2025→2030 (42% CAGR); datacenter consumption toward ~945 TWh by 2030. Cross-refs: [[Sectors/Data Center Power & Cooling]], [[Macro & Technology/800VDC Adoption]].
- **China EV maturity**: NEV sales −13% H1 2026, purchase tax reinstating, trade-in subsidies scaled back — the sector's historical volume engine decelerating structurally; UBS sees China EV growth halving.
- **US bifurcation**: FEOC credit exclusions, Pentagon 1260H (direct DoD ban 6/30/26, indirect 6/30/27), storage tariffs 7.5% → 25%+ (stacking ~54%), EV purchase subsidies halted end-2025, ~$32bn/21 GWh US storage projects cancelled, 50+ municipal BESS moratoria. The US is simultaneously the highest-margin storage market and the least accessible.
- **EU pragmatism**: cells import at 1.3% tariff (vs EV tariffs up to ~45%); battery passport live Feb 2026 (90 data points); local-content rules under active draft (Jul 2026) — policy pushes Chinese *plants* into Europe (CATL Hungary 40→100 GWh NMC live 2026; Spain 50 GWh LFP JV with Stellantis late 2026) rather than keeping Chinese cells out.
- **Lithium cycle re-inflation**: carbonate CNY ~169k/t (Jun 2026) off a >200k Jan spike; 2026 supply +10% to 1.63m t LCE vs Fastmarkets' structural-deficit call — input costs are re-entering cell pricing after three deflationary years, testing who can pass through.
- **Anti-involution**: state-directed capacity exit is the deliberate engineering of a capital-cycle turn — the single largest swing variable for sector-wide margins 2026–27.

## Investor heuristics
- **Consensus**: cell manufacturing is a commodity cyclical — own upstream lithium in upcycles or OEMs with captive supply, avoid the middle; China names deserve a structural discount (CATL ~20x vs Western clean-energy peers ~45x); Korean names are valued as IRA/AMPC-protected Western share plays; ESS is a lower-quality volume filler.
- **What is priced in**: China EV deceleration, the glut, US exclusion of Chinese cells, Korean margin dependence on subsidy.
- **Where consensus could be wrong**:
  1. **ESS demand elasticity** — falling $/kWh is unlocking storage workloads (grid, datacenter) orders of magnitude larger than the EV base; consensus models ESS as a side-line, not a second S-curve earlier in its adoption arc than EV batteries.
  2. **Anti-involution as a genuine capital-cycle turn** — if enforced, leader margins are trough-bound and the "commodity cyclical" label breaks precisely at the leader tier.
  3. **Licensing is invisible to the commodity frame** — CATL's LRS model monetizes process IP inside the protected US market at royalty economics no cell multiple captures; if it scales, the "excluded from America" discount is partly wrong.
  4. **The protected Western industry may be the value trap, not the Chinese discount names** — Northvolt is the base rate; Korean profitability is a subsidy pass-through with sub-50% utilization underneath. Paying "national-champion premium" for tariff-walled capacity inverts the actual quality ranking.
  5. **Chemistry-transition asymmetry** — sodium-ion and semi-solid are incumbent-extending (CATL/BYD lead both); consensus treats solid-state as a disruption risk when the nearer reality is it consolidates the leaders further through 2030.

## Mental Models
- **Models applied**: [[Generalist - Overview]] · [[Lens - Value Layer Monopoly]] · [[Lens - Automation & AI Readiness]] (sector-level pass 2026-08-08, from the CATL thesis build; [[Industry - Semiconductors]] not applied — different physics, but its capex-cycle/process-craft analogues inform the yield-moat read).
- **Triggers that fired** (hypotheses to test, not verdicts):
  - VLM · *non-rivalry test* — **fails sector-wide**: cells carry real marginal cost, so no battery maker earns software-monopoly economics; leadership must be continuously re-earned on cost/yield. Explains why the sector's best business trades at 20x, not 45x.
  - VLM · *geopolitical ceiling* — the sector's defining constraint: the more essential the layer became, the faster it was walled (FEOC/1260H/tariffs). Binding limit is political, not economic.
  - Generalist [G-1] · *capital cycle / antimatching flows* — anti-involution is a state-directed capacity-cycle turn; test whether it restores leader pricing power (2026–27 margin prints) or price-floors the upside.
  - Generalist [G-3] · *mean-reversion vs trend* — 2025 sector margin prints are cyclically flattered by lithium deflation; the expensive error is extrapolating them as secular.
  - Generalist [G-4] · *Perez installation overbuild* — the glut is frenzy-phase over-investment laying cheap substrate; the golden-age winners may be the *appliers* of cheap cells (grid operators, datacenters, EV OEMs) more than the makers — test where the surplus lands.
  - Generalist [G-14] · *Jevons* — $/kWh decline ($139→$84 in two years) is unlocking storage workloads that more than offset price declines: the mechanism behind ESS +75.5% in 2025.
  - Automation Lens · *§6 physical/craft overlay* — frontier cell yield is tacit process knowledge; capital cannot transplant it (Northvolt), automation of it is bounded, and it is the true entry barrier protecting incumbents.
- **Disconfirming check**: every lens agrees "leaders consolidate a growing market" — so hunt the bear: (1) anti-involution could *cap* leader pricing rather than floor it (political ceiling inside China, mirroring the one outside); (2) ESS could commoditize faster than EV cells did — lower spec barrier, every idle line pivoting in; (3) a solid-state leapfrog by a non-incumbent (Toyota 2027 Lexus line, QS/PowerCo) would strand LFP scale advantages. Base rate to beat [G-10]: commodity-manufacturing capital cycles mean-revert margins to cost of capital; the sector thesis requires the leader tier to be a justified outlier, and the single falsifying datapoint is leader gross margin converging toward the tier-2 pack through 2026–27.
