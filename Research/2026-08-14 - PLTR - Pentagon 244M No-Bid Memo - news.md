# Pentagon Memo Directs up to $243.9M Palantir Spend Through March 2027

## Thesis Delta
Consensus still debates whether [[Theses/PLTR - Palantir]] federal is a $10B Army floor or a CR/DOGE cut — this 13 August 2026 Defense One piece (memo viewed by Washington Technology) has Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg directing up to $243.9 million of Palantir software use through 31 March 2027 and options for more spend from 1 April 2027 through 28 December 2028, citing “valuable support to improve efficiencies with the defense industrial base,” with no FAR sole-source justification named in the memo. That is incremental no-bid services on top of the July 10-year $10 billion Army consolidation, not a ceiling cut. Trigger touch (flag only): → CLOSE if Army EA or Maven ceilings are materially cut — this memo is the opposite direction. → HOLD HIGH is about U.S. commercial ≥100% YoY + RDV, which this piece does not print.

## Summary
Defense One reports a Feinberg memo ordering DOD departments to find uses for Palantir software and to identify further spending options after March 2027. The near-term pledge is up to $243.9 million through 31 March 2027. Feinberg cites industrial-base efficiency — flagging delays in munitions and delivery-vehicle production and maintenance — and names no specific contract vehicles. The action follows a July award of a 10-year, $10 billion Army agreement that consolidated many existing Palantir contracts.

The piece frames a broader no-compete trend: 14.8% of all federal awards in H1 2026 vs 12.6% in H1 2025. FAR allows sole-source for urgency, only-one-source, critical follow-on, or national security; the memo “mentions none of those reasons.” DOD told Federal News Radio it has issued similar memos for other companies. Palantir’s no-compete book includes USDA One Farmer, One File and a return-to-office tool. GovTribe: $3.2 billion in obligations since 2024, “roughly half” no-compete. Last week Palantir reported Q2 revenue $1.9 billion (+93% YoY); federal $809 million (+90%). Ranked No. 40 on the 2026 Washington Technology Top 100, up 22 spots.
