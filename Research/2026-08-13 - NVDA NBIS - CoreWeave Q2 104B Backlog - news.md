# CoreWeave Q2: $2.58B Rev, $104B Backlog, FY Raise; Losses and Debt Climb

## Thesis Delta
Consensus (after-hours +14%, CRWV +26% YTD vs S&P +13%) prices the 11 August 2026 SiliconAngle recap as proof that neocloud demand and GPU-rental pricing still clear at scale → the print itself pairs that demand with a $35B-debt, loss-widening, capex-up-more-than-revenue build: $104B booked-not-fulfilled backlog *excluding* ~$25B of post-period commitments, Blackwell/Vera Rubin SKU pricing and margins at new highs, FY revenue/adj. operating-income raised, while GAAP net loss jumped to $626M and FY capex was lifted $4–5B against a $0.2–0.4B revenue-guide lift. That combination is a sell-through/pricing print for [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]], a peer demand print that leaves [[Theses/NBIS - Nebius Group]]'s energization binary untouched, and a confirmation of [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] §Summary's levered-derivative read rather than the independent-compounder read the tape just bought ([G-4] Perez frenzy, [G-7] ROIIC × runway, [G-10] levered-builder base rate, [G-13] price-implied operating variable = execution/pass-through not backlog, Industry #1 bottleneck = power/permitting, VLM layer-renter).

## Summary
SiliconAngle's 11 August 2026 recap of CoreWeave's Q2 print is a demand-and-pricing article that treats the after-hours surge as earned and then, in the same breath, inventories the cash-flow and competitive reasons the equity is still an all-or-nothing infrastructure vehicle. Shares jumped more than 14% in extended trading after an adjusted loss of $1.03 per share beat the $1.20-per-share consensus loss and revenue of $2.58B (+112% YoY) cleared a $2.56B Street target. GAAP net loss widened from $290M a year earlier to $626M. Management guided Q3 revenue to $3.4–$3.6B (~158% YoY at the midpoint) against Street $3.43B, and raised full-year revenue to $12.4–$13.2B (from $12–$13B) and adjusted operating income to $960M–$1.18B (from $900M–$1.1B). Street full-year sales sit at $12.63B — inside the new range, not below it. The stock is +26% year-to-date versus +13% for the S&P 500.

The mechanism SiliconAngle and management offer is contracted GPU-hours plus a power build that is still being levered into existence. Booked-not-fulfilled backlog is $104B and *excludes* about $25B of new commitments secured in the current (post-Q2) quarter — a definitional split that makes the $104B a quarter-end stock and the $25B a subsequent-period flow. Active power ended the quarter at 1.5 GW with a year-end target above 1.85 GW. Expanding that base is why capex was raised from $31–$34B to $35–$39B. The eight-year-old company is framed as a racer against AWS, Microsoft, and Google Cloud to fill sites with AI chips, and unlike those incumbents it is "not remotely profitable," finishing the quarter with $35B of debt borrowed to buy the chips and build the halls. Holger Mueller of Constellation Research calls the strategy unique in its tilt toward AI inference and agentic workloads, then immediately prices the equity as binary: match competitor build-out pace *and* deliver a software stack that attracts and sticks enterprise AI workloads, or the good times do not roll. He writes that debt is "approaching $40 million"; that unit is irreconcilable with the $35B balance-sheet figure in the same article and should be treated as a likely million/billion error, not a second observation.

Pricing, not just volume, is the non-consensus color versus a spot-compression tape. CEO Michael Intrator: "Pricing and margins for our Blackwell and Vera Rubin SKUs are setting new highs, while pricing for prior generation SKUs is at or above where it was years ago." CFO Nitin Agrawal says rising memory-component costs are being passed through and that customers have not been deterred. Named intra-quarter demand: [[Theses/META - Meta]] announced plans to spend an additional $21B renting AI compute; Anthropic struck a multiyear agreement (no dollar figure in this piece); Jane Street committed $6B. The article does not disclose blended gross margin, interest expense, Microsoft mix, Hopper re-rent rates versus original contracts, or any 25% list-price hike — those remain vault questions, not this source's claims.

The same recap inventories the constraints that keep the print from being a clean compounder confirmation. New York Governor Kathy Hochul established a July moratorium on new large-scale data-center projects; Intrator told analysts guided numbers are not impacted "as of today," then conceded that when parts of the U.S. become unwilling even to engage, siting "becomes more challenging." Competitive pressure is no longer only the public clouds pouring tens of billions into AI infrastructure: [[Theses/SPCX - SpaceX]] has started selling excess compute to third parties, and Meta is considering launching its own cloud infrastructure business. Agrawal's rebuttal is that demand, pricing, and margin are still expanding "in an already massive total addressable market." Mueller's closer is execution plus software stickiness against a >$100B backlog; he does not resolve whether contracted GPU rental is a durable layer or a Perez-frenzy overbuild whose capacity becomes the next cycle's cheap substrate.
