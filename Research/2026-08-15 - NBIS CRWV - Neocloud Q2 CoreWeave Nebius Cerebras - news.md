## Summary
Dan Swinhoe's 14 August 2026 Data Center Dynamics recap opens with the sector frame the tape already knows: CoreWeave, Nebius, and Cerebras all printed Q2 2026 this week; AI-cloud revenues accelerated with demand, and losses widened as the three kept building data-center capacity. The piece then inventories each print. It is a journalist's compression of company letters/calls, not a substitute for the 12 August Nebius shareholder letter or CoreWeave's own release; every figure below is what DCD attributed, including the Cerebras "core" vs GAAP split that does not reconcile on the face of the article.

**CoreWeave.** Revenue $2.575 billion in Q2 2026, up from $1.212bn in Q2 2025. Operating loss $49 million, a swing from $19m of operating income a year earlier. Adjusted operating income $128 million sat next to a GAAP net loss of $626 million (from $290m). Adjusted EBITDA $1.51 billion, up from $752 million. CEO/co-founder Michael Intrator called the quarter "an important inflection point" as "scale began to translate into expanding operating leverage," with "customer demand… accelerating, as enterprise adoption broadens" and the platform deepening; "AI is foundational to every industry" and "realizing its full potential requires a purpose-built platform." Q2 capex $9.4 billion; full-year capex expected at $35–39bn. Active power rose by nearly 500 MW in the quarter — 300 MW of that in June — to 1.5 GW across 51 active data centers. Eight facilities added in 2026; year-end live-capacity target 1.85 GW. Contracted power 4.2 GW, with an 8 GW target by 2030. First self-build due online later this year. Revenue backlog approximately $104bn as of 30 June, *not including* more than $25bn of net new customer commitments added in early Q3. Managed inference platform grown from $1 million to more than $100m ARR, with an exit-2026 target of at least $250m managed-inference ARR. On the earnings call, executives said demand for older-generation Nvidia GPUs "remains solid" because it is installed and energized production-grade compute available quickly; the company "recently signed an A100 contract that extends into 2029 at an 'attractive price' despite the A100s coming out in 2020." On US data-center moratoriums, Intrator: they "are not going to impact the demand for this infrastructure. They are going to impact *where* this infrastructure gets built"; the required posture is "extremely collaborative with the communities that ultimately host the infrastructure" and to be "good neighbors."

**Nebius.** Group revenue $582.3 million, +454% vs Q2 2025. Adjusted EBITDA swung from a $21m loss in Q2 2025 to $236.2m. Adjusted net losses narrowed from $91.5m to $33.2m. GAAP net income swung from $584.4m to a $190.4m *loss*; DCD flags that much of the 2025 figure was gains from a revaluation of investments in equity securities (the ClickHouse-mark pattern the thesis already treats as non-cash). Almost all group revenue is AI cloud: Nebius AI cloud $575m, +514% YoY. Total operating costs and expenses $758.2m vs $216.3m. This week: deployments with Vantage in Wales, Greenergy in Estonia, and plans for a second self-built facility at the Mäntsälä, Finland campus. Founder/CEO Arkady Volozh's investor letter: four deals valued at more than $1bn each, at a yield of $20–25 million per megawatt; another 1 GW contracted in the quarter; YE2026 contracted power anticipated at 5 GW; deploy more than 1 GW of compute per year starting in 2027; production inference workloads more than tripled in Q2.

**Cerebras.** Wafer-scale chips sold on-premise or as cloud. Feldman: "outstanding quarter"; "Core revenue more than doubled to $210 million, and our cloud business nearly quadrupled year-over-year." "Speed changes what AI can do. It makes AI more useful, more productive, and opens entirely new markets." "Demand for fast inference is enormous"; Cerebras is "securing more data center capacity, expanding manufacturing, and growing with customers and partners including OpenAI, AWS, AMD, and CrowdStrike." GAAP total revenues $180.1m, +74% YoY; GAAP cloud and other services $126.0m, +281% YoY. Gross profit $25.56m, down from $32.1m. Net loss $450.4m, versus $309.5m of *net income* in Q2 2025. Adjusted EBITDA a $53.1m loss, versus a $38.3m loss a year earlier. Data-center capacity live and under contract for delivery by the end of 2027 increased to more than 600 MW; "total pipeline is in the gigawatts." Contracts in the quarter with AI coding companies including Cognition and Lovable.
