# SK Hynix at Hot Chips: HBM4E Stays on MR-MUF; Hybrid Bonding Earliest at HBM5

## Thesis Delta
Consensus had been sliding hybrid bonding into the HBM4E narrative as the next packaging unlock — Hot Chips VP Jaesik Lee (SK Hynix America Package Engineering) says **hybrid bonding is unlikely ready for HBM4E**, so larger-scale use is **earliest with HBM5 (~2029–30 mass-production forecasts)**. **MR-MUF iterates through HBM4E** under the **775 µm JEDEC HBM4 max package height** (≈300 mm logic wafer thickness for cold-plate coplanarity). Consensus priced a packaging leap; this source prices **incremental MR-MUF + thermal (iHBM) work** instead. **Supports** [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] near-term process continuity / yield-over-novelty; flags HBM5 as the real hybrid-bonding gate. No HIGH/LOW/CLOSE fire.

## Summary
On 27 August 2026 at Hot Chips, SK Hynix explained why hybrid bonding slips: package height is capped at 775 µm while layer count rises (16-Hi already; dies ~50 µm thick with tighter gaps). MR-MUF (Mass Reflow Molded Underfill) remains the workhorse through HBM4E despite filling/deformation difficulty on thinner dies. Hybrid bonding (direct Cu–Cu) promises height, efficiency, and signal-quality gains for 20+ layer stacks but demands extreme alignment precision — one bad die kills the stack. Thermal load up ~2.2× across HBM generations while pin rates rose from ~1 to 8 Gbit/s (HBM4); SKH working iHBM cooling concepts. Bottom line: transition timing set by height, cooling, cost, and yield — not bandwidth alone.
