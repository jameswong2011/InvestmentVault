# SK hynix at Hot Chips: HBM4 12-Hi in Mass Production; 16-Hi in Customer Qualification; Hybrid Bonding for 20-Hi+

## Summary
Park Ji-young (Herald Business, English via AI translation) reports SK hynix's Hot Chips announcement (Sunday local time, Silicon Valley): sixth-generation HBM4 12-layer is in mass production; 16-layer has entered the qualification stage. The piece frames this as a rebuttal to late-2025 redesign-defect rumors and to speculation that NVIDIA raising Rubin HBM4 from 8–10 Gbps to 11 Gbps delayed certification. Those rumors were already largely retired when Jensen Huang told Korean press on 5 June that Samsung, SK hynix and Micron had all passed HBM4 qualification; SK hynix's own Q2 call said HBM4 MP supply to major customers began in Q2 with yields approaching HBM3E maturity. What is new is the **16-Hi qualification** stamp — CES 2026 had only a prototype plus "proceeding smoothly."

Samsung's contrast is restated from its Q4 2025 call: customer demand for 16-layer HBM4 is "very limited"; Samsung would rather sample 12-layer HBM4E of equivalent capacity and sees no need to mass-produce 16-Hi, while claiming the technology is ready if customers change their mind.

Lee identifies hybrid bonding as the key technology for stacking beyond 16 layers to 20+. Current HBM uses micro-bumps plus underfill; taller stacks force thinner dice and worsen warpage/thermals. Hybrid bonding (no conventional bumps) lets dice stay thicker at the same stack height and cuts bump pitch to ≤18 µm. Two performance axes named: more I/O pins and speed per I/O for bandwidth; logic die + more TSVs for power efficiency. The article does not give Rubin SKU mix, Gbps of the shipping 12-Hi part, or a 16-Hi customer name.
