# SK hynix Approves KRW 54T Yongin Y2 + Cheongju M17 Fabs for AI Memory

## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] as a 2026–27 HBM-share-erosion tape (Samsung/Micron catching Rubin; the thesis’s own older “NAND capex pullback” line) and prices WFE as a China/ICAPS-drag cohort — this 7 August 2026 board PR authorizes KRW 54T of *greenfield* DRAM/HBM (Yongin Y2, first cleanroom June 2029) plus NAND (Cheongju M17, December 2028) on top of already-building Y1, executing the June master plan of KRW 600T Yongin + KRW 100T Cheongju. Near-term this is demand-visibility cash generation for 000660 and a 2027–31 WFE pull for [[Theses/AMAT - Applied Materials]], [[Theses/LRCX - Lam Research]], [[Theses/KLA - KLA Corporation]], [[Theses/ASMI - ASM International]], [[Theses/BESI - BE Semiconductor Industries]]; the same decision seeds 2028–29 bit supply that the HBM-scarcity / L1-less-cyclical narrative has not fully discounted — hypotheses, not verdicts: Industry #1 (bottleneck still HBM through 2027) + #17 (supply lag 18–30 months; cleanrooms ~28–34 months from the board date) + #3/#18 (company calls it structural; [G-10] asks whether a 19% CAGR cite is frenzy overbuild); Generalist [G-4] Perez installation, [G-7] ROIIC of new-mine capex, [G-8] shells-now / tools-later allocation.

## Summary
SK hynix announced on 7 August 2026 that the board approved approximately KRW 54T of new-fab spend: KRW 35.2T for Yongin “Y2” and KRW 19.1T for Cheongju “M17.” The release frames the vote as execution of the mid-to-long-term investment strategy disclosed the prior June — a master plan of KRW 600T into the Yongin Semiconductor Cluster and KRW 100T to expand the Cheongju production base — not as a 2026–27 HBM print. Y1, the first Yongin fab, is already under construction toward a February 2027 first-cleanroom target; Y2 and M17 are the subsequent shells. This PR is not the EE Times Asia “2027 mass-prod / 300k wpm” teaser: those dates and wafer capacities are absent here. Use the company cleanroom dates.

The company’s mechanism is two-speed capital. Fabs are built to the master schedule; cleanroom expansions and equipment installation run later, “sequentially in line with demand trends,” to “maximize capital efficiency.” That is the live [G-7]/[G-8] claim: 54T buys optioned floor space and utilities now, while WFE dollars (the AMAT/LRCX/KLA/ASMI/BESI print) remain demand-gated. An official said technological competitiveness alone is no longer enough and that “the ability to supply the required volume at the exact moment customers need it is the ultimate competitive advantage.” Demand is cited via Omdia: DRAM and NAND both at 19% CAGR from last year through 2030, read as structural — memory “transcends its role as a mere component to become core infrastructure determining AI performance itself” — not a temporary supercycle. NAND demand is specified as enterprise SSDs plus key-value (KV) cache storage for AI inference, with the field expected to broaden as Agentic and Physical AI arrive. KV cache is defined in-release as storing and reusing previously computed Key and Value vectors to cut redundant inference compute. [G-14] Jevons / workload-Y is the company’s own frame; [G-10] is the outside-view counter: 19% through 2030 is an inside-view extrapolation until bit-ship and ASP prints confirm it.

Yongin Y2 is the second of four fabs planned sequentially on the cluster (Wonsam-myeon, Cheoin-gu, Yongin-si, Gyeonggi-do; ~1.26 million pyeong / ~4,160,000 m²). Y2 itself spans 341,000 pyeong (~1,130,000 m²) as a DRAM production base for HBM and other next-generation DRAM. Groundbreaking is July 2027; first cleanroom June 2029; the investment period runs sequentially to October 2031. The company had already pulled the cluster’s completion target forward 12 years, 2045 → 2033, so all four fabs finish on the accelerated clock; Y2 is phase two of that pull-in. The KRW 35.2T envelope is not a WFE PO: it includes a Business Support Building (admin and welfare), an Integrated R&D Center consolidating product-development testing, analysis, and experiments, plus water-treatment plants, utility conduits, substations, and warehouses. Phase-1 power and water required through Y2 operation is “nearing completion” at a 99% progress rate, with fab construction and infrastructure running concurrently so Y2 can hold schedule.

Cheongju was picked for NAND because it is the fastest construction path. The campus already holds M11, M12, and M15, with power and water in the ground and production that can be linked to existing operations. M17 covers 206,000 pyeong (~680,000 m²), breaks ground February 2027, opens its first cleanroom December 2028, and invests through April 2031. The company states it will “proactively secure production infrastructure based on mid-to-long-term demand discussed with customers, while expanding actual production capacity in alignment with the timing of customer needs.” The political overlay in the release is domestic: simultaneous Yongin and Cheongju spend is expected to lift supply-chain partners, employment, and local commerce, and to “contribut[e] to the stability of the global AI semiconductor supply chain.” Industry #15 (subsidized multi-region footprint) is the lens on that language; it is not a customer LTA disclosure.
