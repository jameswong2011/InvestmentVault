# Beyond Chips: Washington Expands Semiconductor Security to Polysilicon

## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices the 6 Aug 2026 Section 232 polysilicon proclamation as a solar-trade overlay (MIP + 15% on named derivatives) with a rounding-error GPU BOM and no change to HBM/CoWoS allocation → SemiVision/TSPA argues Washington is moving the semiconductor-security perimeter one layer *upstream* of chips, tools, and packaging into feedstock, and is writing friend-shoring into the tariff schedule so provenance / approved-source clauses can migrate into multi-year OEM / OSAT / hyperscaler LTAs the way CoWoS and HBM allocation already did. Hypotheses: [G-13] the mispriced lever is sourcing *rules*, not poly kilograms on a Rubin board; [Industry #16] parallel US-aligned vs China-aligned materials ecosystems; [Industry #2] country-of-origin becomes a second qualification gate; [G-10] US poly capacity already fell ~50% (2005) → <2% (2024), so the reference-class hit rate on onshoring a commodity feedstock is poor until construction starts and quals print.

## Summary
On 6 August 2026 the White House issued a Section 232 proclamation covering polysilicon and derivative products, framed as a national-security input for *both* semiconductors and solar. The instrument is not a single tariff. It stacks (1) minimum import prices of $21/kg poly, $100/kg ingots/wafers, $0.22/W cells, and $0.38/W modules — a below-MIP entry pays a duty equal to the gap versus the floor; (2) an *additional* 15% Section 232 from 4 December 2026 on specified ingots and downstream derivatives, country-conditioned, and **not** a blanket extra 15% on every raw-poly shipment; (3) a Commerce onshoring window in which approved builders of US poly / ingot / wafer / cell capacity can take tariff relief on necessary imports while the plant is built, with construction required to begin by 20 January 2029. Most provisions take effect 4 December 2026 — about 120 days to rewrite contracts, inventories, sourcing, and capex plans. A conventional tariff raises landed cost. An MIP plus a tariff-offset tied to a US groundbreaking attempts to change where capital is allocated. That is industrial policy, not a solar AD/CVD sequel.

The proclamation’s own numbers are why TSPA treats this as a semiconductor event. US global poly *capacity* share collapsed from ~50% (2005) to <2% (2024); US wafer-fab share from 37% (1990) to ~10% (2024). Semiconductor-grade poly is only ~2.4% of global poly output; solar-grade sets the scale economics. Electronic-grade security therefore rides a solar-scale industrial base — energy security and chip security share the same upstream plant. The 6 August action extends a 14 January 2026 Section 232 already aimed at semiconductors, semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and their derivatives. The live policy question is no longer only “can the US fab enough advanced logic / CoWoS / HBM / EUV tools?” It is “what if the materials under those processes sit in supply chains Washington already calls strategically vulnerable?” Once that question is on the table, the candidate perimeter includes silicon wafers, high-purity chemicals, specialty gases, photoresists, advanced substrates, copper foil, glass fabrics, packaging materials, rare metals, and power-electronics inputs. Polysilicon is the precedent, not the claimed AI-cycle bottleneck.

Asia’s hit is not uniform. China keeps scale in many upstream categories and is expected to deepen domestic integration and redirect incremental tons to the internal market plus regions less exposed to US rules — a second, parallel industrial ecosystem, not a clean reshoring. Japan and Korea sit on the other side of the hierarchy: high-purity materials, wafers, chemicals, equipment, specialty parts. For Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, and EU member states the combined ordinary + 232 rate is generally structured to **15%**, rather than stacking a fresh 15% on existing duties; the UK is carved out at 10%. That is friend-shoring written into the tariff arithmetic — a ranked list of acceptable foreign supply, not a wall. Taiwan is not asked to become a commodity poly producer. TSPA’s Taiwan question is downstream: can a vendor demonstrate that materials, plant location, upstream sources, and ownership meet a US-aligned sourcing screen? Provenance starts to price next to spec. The piece cites two-way Taiwan–US investment since 2020 (Taiwanese semi / server / electronics plants into the US; US firms into Taiwan’s semi, cloud, and AI stack) and a forward figure that future Taiwanese US commitments could exceed $250B — the same order of magnitude as [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]]’s already-disclosed $265B US build.

The restriction lands into a boom, not a trough. SIA, on the same 6 August date, printed Q2 2026 worldwide sales of $403.3B (+35.1% QoQ); June $134.5B (+123.6% vs June 2025, +9.7% vs May); 2026 global semi revenue expected to exceed $1.5T. Upstream price, lead-time, or approved-source changes propagate harder when CoWoS/HBM/logic are already tight: inventory builds, LTA renegotiation, customer re-qualification, then ASP. TSPA is explicit that poly will not become a material share of an advanced GPU bill of materials. The claim is that *rules* governing semiconductor-material sourcing are becoming structurally more restrictive. That claim is timed against a system-level, not chip-level, AI race: at FMS 2026 Samsung showed zHBM / zNAND-O concepts and a 400-plus-layer V10 BV-NAND architecture (memory moving from side-by-side HBM toward vertical integration over the accelerator); the same week [[Theses/SNDK - SanDisk]] and [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] released the first High Bandwidth Flash technical specification through the Open Compute Project — a NAND tier between HBM and SSD. Logic, memory hierarchy, packaging, interconnect, thermal, power, *and* the geography of the materials used to build the system are being scored as one industrial-security equation. SemiVision’s stack: GPU → HBM → advanced packaging → wafers → equipment → chemicals → raw materials → power infrastructure.

Four 12-month watches: (1) who files US onshoring plans and whether named poly / ingot / wafer suppliers announce American capacity — facilities, construction starts, quals, volumes, not press releases; (2) whether materials policy is wired into semiconductor tariffs, CHIPS incentives, procurement preference, and export controls as one architecture; (3) whether US-facing chains qualify non-Chinese sources while China-facing chains deepen domestic ones; (4) whether hyperscalers, IDMs/foundries, OSATs, and substrate makers write country-of-origin / material-provenance / approved-source clauses into multi-year paper. (4) is the commercial tell that policy has left the Federal Register and entered procurement.

## Framework / Mental Model
TSPA does not score vendors. It names a **three-mechanism industrial-security architecture**, a **friend-shoring tariff hierarchy**, an **expanding-stack typology**, and a **four-indicator watchlist**.

| Mechanism | What it does | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum import price | $21/kg poly; $100/kg ingots/wafers; $0.22/W cells; $0.38/W modules. Below-floor entry → duty ≈ MIP − declared value | A quantity quota |
| Additional Section 232 | +15% from 4 Dec 2026 on *specified* ingots and downstream derivatives, country-conditioned | A blanket +15% on every raw-poly kilo |
| Commerce onshoring offset | Tariff relief on needed imports while an approved US poly/ingot/wafer/cell plant is built; construction start by 20 Jan 2029 | A production tax credit with no location clock |

**Friend-shoring hierarchy (combined ordinary + 232, as written):** Japan / Korea / Taiwan / Switzerland / Liechtenstein / EU members generally **15%**; United Kingdom **10%**; residual (China and others not named in the carve-out) face the MIP + specified-derivative 232 without that 15% combined cap. Method: rank *acceptable foreign supply* rather than autarky.

**Expanding security stack (SemiVision):** GPU → HBM → advanced packaging → wafers → equipment → chemicals → raw materials → power infrastructure. Each layer is treated as part of one AI industrial-security equation. Method: once a layer is inside Section 232, the next-upstream layer becomes eligible.

**12-month watch (pass/fail on observed behavior, not on the proclamation text):**

1. US capacity announcements → construction starts → customer quals → tons.
2. Materials 232 fused with chip tariffs + CHIPS + procurement + export controls.
3. Bifurcation: US-customer quals of non-Chinese sources vs China-internal deepening.
4. Provenance / approved-source language inside multi-year OEM, OSAT, substrate, hyperscaler LTAs.

## Contradiction Check
Does **not** move near-term HBM or optics scarcity. Poly is not the GPU BOM line and is not the CoWoS or 200G-EML gate. Leaves [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] §Conviction Triggers (Samsung Rubin >35% + HBM price −10% → LOW; CXMT qualified HBM + Samsung HBM5 HB yield → CLOSE) and the ~70/30/0 first-wave board meter in [[Theses/MU - Micron Technology]] §Key Non-consensus Insights / → HIGH **unmoved**. Leaves [[Theses/LITE - Lumentum]] InP/EML arms-dealer math untouched. Leaves [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] CUDA / Physical-AI stack as a software-and-allocation thesis, not a feedstock thesis.

**Does** challenge the assumption that US industrial policy *stops* at fabs and tools — the assumption still sitting under [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] §Key Non-consensus Insights #2 (silicon shield = $265B US co-investment + Jan 15 US–Taiwan framework / Section 232 *relief* on chips) and §Risks #8 (export-control expansion modeled as a China-shipment hit, not an inbound-materials screen). TSPA’s $250B+ Taiwanese-into-US figure is the same order of magnitude as TSMC’s disclosed US commitment; the *new* claim is that Arizona / US packaging plants will eventually have to show approved poly / wafer / chemical provenance, not just that the fab sits on US soil. Does **not** fire TSM → LOW (Arizona N2 slip past Q3 2028; Intel 18A landing 2+ external customers; named CoWoS alternative) or → CLOSE (blockade; NVDA+AAPL >50%; GM <52%). Watch item (1) — US poly/wafer construction starts — is adjacent to the Arizona durability question, not a substitute for it. [Industry #15] subsidized multi-region oligopoly: a US poly rebuild is another duplicated, subsidized node whose trough economics [G-10] have already been demonstrated (50%→<2%).

For [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]], the piece is a **partial confirm** of two already-written materials legs and a **non-event** for the kill switch. §Risks #9 (Japan photo-materials / Hormuz → PGME/PGMEA → PR/BARC/HBM adhesives) is the same perimeter TSPA now says Section 232 can swallow (photoresists, chemicals, gases). §Outstanding Questions Q7 (Taiwan-tail HBM: HBM cubes still go to TSMC CoWoS) is unchanged as a kinetic tail; the new overlay is that Korean supply into a US-aligned chain is *favored* at a 15% combined rate, not shut out. Insight #3 (HBF as unpriced option with SNDK) gets a dated spec print — first HBF technical specification via OCP in FMS week — without changing HBF’s pre-revenue status or the 2027-deployment clock in Q4. Samsung zHBM / 400+ layer V10 is a system-architecture datapoint, not a Rubin-allocation datapoint; it does not substitute for the Q3–Q4 2026 board meter.

For [[Theses/MU - Micron Technology]], the US-HBM-winner tape is about *who is on the Rubin board*, not who melts sand. TSPA’s parallel-ecosystem / friend-shoring map is closer to MU Insight “CXMT is complementary this cycle” than to a feedstock alpha: CXMT bits stay in the China-optimized stack; Western LTAs can add origin screens without putting MU cubes on first-wave Rubin. Does **not** fire MU → HIGH (meter ≥10% **and** LTA-cap-off **and** two TrendForce prints) or → CLOSE (two −20% DRAM contracts + rising OEM days **or** CXMT Western-HBM qual **or** GB300 LPDDR5X dual-source). A US poly/wafer plant announcement would be a geography datapoint for MU’s US-IDM identity, not a board-share datapoint.

Materials-adjacent vault names TSPA does not score but that sit on the named next-perimeter list: [[Theses/3110 - Nitto Boseki]] (glass fabrics), [[Theses/2802 - Ajinomoto]] (ABF / packaging film), [[Theses/2383 - Elite Material]] (CCL / copper-foil stack). Those are perimeter-expansion *candidates*, not claims this source makes about share or pricing.

Falsifiers of TSPA’s own claim (policy as precedent, not poly as bottleneck): no material US poly / ingot / wafer groundbreaking before the 20 Jan 2029 clock; MIPs waived or unenforced in practice; 232 stays solar-only in subsequent proclamations (wafers / chemicals / photoresists / glass fabric never named); hyperscaler / foundry / OSAT / substrate LTAs through 2027 add no origin or approved-source clause. [G-4] reading held as hypothesis: this is Perez-style institutional recomposition around an AI installation boom, *or* it is another tariff that dies at the first solar-price spike — the four-item watch is how that hypothesis is tested.
