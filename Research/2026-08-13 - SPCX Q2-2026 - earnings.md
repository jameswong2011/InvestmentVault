## Summary

Management's core argument is a three-engine compounding story told as if the AI campus were already a cash engine. Elon opens on Starship tonnage-to-orbit (Falcon ~2,500 t/yr → Starship 1–10 million t/yr), Starlink V3 as a two-order-of-magnitude bandwidth step (10× capability × 10× satellites; even 10× worse monetization per bit still 10× revenue), then AI: Grok 4.5/4.6/4.7 cadence, Grok 5 trained on "the entire corpus of SpaceX data," NVIDIA exclusivity on Vera Rubin, YE26 ">2 GW," YE27 "closer to 10 than 5" then 20 GW of power/cooling as the stretch, Starmind NVL72 sats launching next year, Cursor "quite soon." Gwynne operationalizes: 78 H1 launches / 1,041 tons, Falcon cadence at a high, aviation still <10% penetrated, EchoStar 65 MHz FCC-approved, Grok 4.5 token consumption "tripled out of the gate." Bret is the only one who prices it: Connectivity is the profit pool ($1.7B op. income, $2.6B adj. EBITDA); AI is $2.6B revenue, $(1.3)B operating loss, $1.1B adj. EBITDA, $15.8B of the $18.4B capex; IPO $85.7B + $25B IG notes; $100B cash; $47.5B backlog; "<1 year payback" on new compute; $100B December ARR including Cursor.

We believe this puts us on a trajectory, including contribution from Cursor, to reach $100 billion of ARR … by the end of this year based on our expected revenue in the month of December of this year."
