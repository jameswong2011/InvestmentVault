## Thesis Delta

Consensus files On as one more premium challenger brand surfing the Nike-disruption wave — a high-multiple name carrying cyclical fashion risk. The sharper, un-asked question the story raises: does patented CloudTec cushioning (and the Speedboard plate / Helion superfoam / robotic LightSpray successors it seeded) plus a ~60% gross margin, full-price DTC-shifting mix, and engineered scarcity give On durable, software-like pricing power and a repeatable innovation flywheel — or does athletic footwear's low switching cost and taste-cyclicality make a 45% revenue CAGR off a CHF 2.9B base structurally un-extrapolable (base-rate check)?

## Summary

On traces to Olivier Bernhard, a Swiss endurance athlete — three duathlon world titles, multiple Ironman finishes — who, winding down his career in the mid-2000s with chronic Achilles pain and a conviction that there was "room for another running sensation," chased a different kind of sole. Working with a Swiss engineer named George, an early prototype (cut-up garden hose glued vertically onto a pair of Nike soles) delivered a soft landing followed by a springy toe-off that convinced him the idea was real. Nike, Adidas, and Puma all rejected the still-crude design. Bernhard built the company himself instead: he recruited former agent Caspar Coppetti and business partner David Allemann, and in January 2010 the trio each invested $150,000 — forfeitable if any took another job within three years — and founded On. They acquired the underlying technology from George and industrialized it into CloudTec: circular rubber "cloud" pods on the outsole that compress on landing then lock into a firmer platform for push-off. A designer hired specifically for having no footwear background executed a deliberately minimal, technical aesthetic — divisive by design, so the shoe would read as something different. The first model, the Cloudsurfer, launched into a market whose incumbents had already passed on the concept.

The go-to-market inverted the mass-marketing playbook. Rather than buy broad awareness, On seeded the specialist communities it knew — endurance athletes, coaches, specialty running retailers — betting that broader demand would follow more durably if it came organically. Validation was immediate: days after founding, samples shown at the ISPO trade show won the BrandNew award and generated over CHF 500k in orders, giving instant retailer credibility and working capital. Athlete proof points then did the marketing money could not buy: Frederick van Lierde won the 2013 Kona Ironman World Championship in an unreleased On prototype; Tim Don set an Ironman world-best in the Cloudflow in 2017; the On Athletics Club (launched 2020 in Boulder, later Europe and Oceania) industrialized the "let victories do the marketing" model; and Hellen Obiri won the 2025 New York City Marathon in a 2:19:51 course record wearing the carbon-plated Cloudboom Strike Lightspray — built on the Helion HF superfoam platform under a robot-sprayed LightSpray upper. Roger Federer, who discovered On on his own and then joined in 2019 as investor and ROGER-line creative partner for a ~3% stake reportedly worth more than his entire career prize money, anchored the tennis vertical and handed the brand global reach beyond running. "Swissness" — precision, minimalism, technical credibility — became an operating principle rather than a tagline: engineering, design, development, and testing stay centralized in Zurich, while manufacturing is outsourced (footwear primarily Vietnam; apparel and accessories across Vietnam, Slovenia, Portugal, Turkey, and China).

The financial signature is premium discipline enforced across the whole operation. On has run minimal discounting since inception, using full-price sales to compound brand equity and signal scarcity, and it curates wholesale partners hard — closing doors that dilute presentation, pricing, or environment. That discipline shows up as a ~60% gross margin against Nike's typical 43-46% and Adidas's 48-52%. Product is organized as durable "franchises" — nine footwear franchises each contributing more than 5% of revenue — updated iteratively rather than replaced, which stretches product life beyond the seasonal launch cadence that governs most of the industry. The portfolio spans five Performance verticals: running (still the core and the fastest-growing), outdoor, tennis, training, and all-day, each extended into matching apparel. Apparel — introduced in 2016, CHF 191M and +87% YoY in the twelve months to Q3 2025, ~7% of revenue — is being repositioned from afterthought to customer-acquisition engine and future margin lever, precisely because it skews far more DTC (roughly 20% of DTC revenue versus 7% of the total). The 2025 LOEWE (LVMH) collaboration — a $590 Cloudtilt capsule that "sold out almost entirely within days" — marks how far up the premium ladder the brand can credibly stretch.

On IPO'd in September 2021 at roughly a $7B market cap (up nearly 50% on day one), with founders retaining more than half of voting power through 10-vote Class B shares on only ~15% economic ownership — a control structure that lets management prioritize long-run brand-building over quarterly optics. The pandemic ran revenue +60% (2020) and +70% (2021); the more telling result came after the boom, when On kept compounding — +69% (2022), +47% (2023), +29% (2024) — while larger peers drowned in a bullwhip inventory glut that On sidestepped because its own earlier supply constraints had mechanically capped over-ordering. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to Q3 2025 reached CHF 2.9B (~$3.5B), a 45% CAGR since Q3 2021 and still +35% YoY; adjusted EBITDA scaled from ~CHF 96M to CHF 535M (54% CAGR; margin 13.3% → 18.6%). Management's stated ambition — "the most premium global sportswear brand" — is being pursued by widening the addressable market (it claims ~75% of people in its own markets still do not know On), pushing DTC (25% of revenue in 2019 → 42% in Q3 2025), deepening the US (59% of revenue, up from 51%), and accelerating Asia-Pacific (6% → 16%), led by triple-digit-growth China. The question the story frames but deliberately leaves open: whether this is a genuine compounder escaping the footwear base rate, or a very well-run brand near the top of a taste cycle whose past doubling cannot be linearly extended from a CHF 2.9B base.

**Competitive divergence through the 2022-2025 bullwhip**

The durability evidence is relative, not just absolute. Across 2020-2021 the entire sportswear industry over-ordered into pandemic demand; Nike, Adidas, Lululemon, Deckers, and Under Armour all reported demand outpacing supply and responded by ordering more. When delayed shipments landed into a normalizing market — consumers rotating spend toward experiences, gyms reopening, inflation making discretionary purchases more considered — the result was a classic bullwhip: excess inventory, mistimed product, and an industry-wide promotional clean-up. On avoided the glut almost entirely because its own earlier supply constraints (Vietnam/China manufacturing lockdowns through H1 2022) had mechanically capped over-ordering; it secured new capacity in Indonesia, dual-sourced key materials, and redesigned the supply chain while prioritizing top-line momentum and retail partnerships over short-term profitability. The only close structural comparable that stayed resilient was Hoka (Deckers) — also premium-priced, also lightly discounted, also entering the period with momentum — which sharpens rather than dilutes the read: the two brands that held share are the two that refused to compete on price. When the market turned promotional, On did not follow, and management framed itself as "less exposed to the normal competitive set" than peers.

**The brand-building flywheel (mechanism detail)**

On's marketing model is earned-awareness compounding, not paid reach. The sequence: seed specialist credibility → let athlete wins broadcast proof → convert proof into consumer demand. Concrete links in that chain from the source: the ISPO BrandNew award days after founding (>CHF 500k in first orders); van Lierde's 2013 Kona win in an unreleased prototype; Tim Don's 2017 Ironman world-best in the Cloudflow; the On Athletics Club (2020, Boulder, later Europe and Oceania) built to supply a steady flow of podium moments in On shoes; and Obiri's 2025 NYC Marathon record, which Coppetti called "the ultimate proof point" and tied directly to consumer demand. The tennis vertical — originated by Federer's unsolicited outreach, then built out with Iga Swiatek, João Fonseca, and Ben Shelton — extends the same mechanism into a second sport, with Shelton's 2023 US Open run driving ~4x YoY US DTC tennis apparel growth in that quarter. Product architecture reinforces the flywheel: franchises are updated iteratively rather than replaced, so a Cloudsurfer or Cloudmonster accretes multi-year brand equity instead of resetting each season, and the ambassador mix (Obiri for runner credibility, Fonseca for Brazil hype, Zendaya and Burna Boy for Gen Z) is deliberately spread so On is "not fishing in the same pond as everyone else." The through-line positioning — the most premium option for "communities defined by how they move" — is what lets the brand occupy running routes, tennis courts, gym floors, and city streets without diluting the premium signal.

## Contradiction Check

No existing ONON thesis or research note exists in the vault, and there is no "Athletic Footwear & Apparel" sector note (nearest sector notes — Consumer Edtech, Consumer Telehealth — are unrelated). There is therefore nothing to contradict; this note stands alone as a **new-thesis candidate primer** and frames the lenses a future thesis must resolve. Each item below is a hypothesis to test against evidence, not a verdict (READING PROTOCOL).

- **Pricing power / brand moat [G-6]** — the bull read is software-like pricing power: ~60% gross margin, structural full-price discipline, patented CloudTec, a $590 LOEWE sell-out. The adversarial read: footwear switching costs are low, and CloudTec/Speedboard IP is design-and-marketing differentiation more than a legal chokepoint competitors must license — so the margin premium may be brand-cycle rent, not a structural toll. **Test:** does full-price share and ~60%+ gross margin survive a genuine demand air-pocket (not merely the post-COVID up-cycle that lifted the entire sector)?
- **Base rates / outside view [G-10]** — the reference class is punishing: few firms sustain >20% revenue CAGR for a decade, and the rate collapses as the base scales. On is being asked to compound 20-25% off CHF 2.9B. The compounder narrative must *beat* that base rate on named, company-specific edge, not assume the historical doubling extrapolates. **Single falsifying datapoint to watch:** the first sub-20% constant-currency growth print absent a macro shock.
- **Customer cohort economics [G-12]** — the true value driver is DTC cohort retention vs. rising athlete/collaboration acquisition spend. The bull mechanism is "repeat pairs" in sticky running franchises plus apparel lifting basket size and purchase frequency; the article asserts loyalty ("switching is rare") but supplies **no** cohort retention curves, LTV, CAC, or payback. That data gap is the central thing a thesis must source before underwriting durability.
- **Intangibles expensed [G-11]** — On's real economic capital (brand, athlete contracts, OAC, Federer/Zendaya/LOEWE, retail build-out) runs through the P&L, so reported earnings, invested capital, and ROIC understate the asset being compounded — a reason screens may misprice it. The symmetric risk: that intangible is taste-dependent and can impair rapidly if the brand cools, unlike a capitalized physical asset.
- **Value Layer Monopoly (Lens) — hypothesis: WEAK / NO FIT.** On is a contestable consumer footwear/apparel brand: low switching costs, no controlled standard or interface others must traverse and pay to cross, and an **application-layer** position (a brand riding on outsourced manufacturing and platform-agnostic distribution). Premium is real but *rentable*, not a layer toll. Historically, athletic-footwear brand moats behave as melting assets absent perpetual reinvention (cf. Under Armour, Skechers, and Nike's own cycles). State as a hypothesis to disconfirm, not a conclusion — the disconfirming evidence would be sustained pricing power through a full down-cycle.

**Honest signal read (base-rate discipline):** On is a genuinely high-quality operator with unusually clean premium discipline, founder control aligned to the long term, and a defensible innovation cadence. But the investable question is entirely about **durability and price**, neither of which this article resolves. A new thesis is warranted, and its first two jobs are (1) reverse-engineer the revenue growth and margin the current multiple embeds [G-13] and ask which operating variable the market is mispricing, and (2) source the cohort retention / LTV-CAC data the narrative omits. Until then the honest classification is "high-quality, likely fully-appreciated growth brand — edge unproven," not "obvious mispricing."
