# The Clocks on One Building

## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices [[Theses/META - Meta]] AI "capex" as one GAAP growth number and treats A+ project notes plus residual-value guarantees as proof a gigawatt campus is a liquid, portable asset → Shanaka's public-record reconstruction of Hyperion (Blue Owl 80/20, ~$27B, Beignet notes to May 2049) and the 28 Jul 2026 El Paso repeat (BlackRock, ~$14B/1GW) splits four clocks that cannot substitute: credit recovery, accounting control, physical succession, workload continuity. [G-1][G-4][G-10][G-13] The binding question is which clock hits first, not which is true; the essay files no forecast, probability, or position.

## Summary
On 28 July 2026 Meta repeated the Louisiana architecture in El Paso: an external partner owns 80 percent, Meta keeps 20 percent, manages construction, and occupies the whole campus. Leases open at four years with four extension options that can carry occupancy to twenty. A declining residual-value guarantee runs sixteen years — Meta pays the shortfall versus a contractual floor only if written conditions are met; payment is not automatic on departure. BlackRock-managed funds supply equity. About $12.5B of debt funds ~$14B of development for a 1GW campus expected to begin coming online in 2028. Nine months earlier, Blue Owl funds took the same 80 percent of Hyperion in Richland Parish, Louisiana, against ~$27B of development for power, cooling and connectivity. Louisiana leases commence in 2029, four-year initial term, options to twenty years, residual-value threshold ~$28B for the first sixteen operating years. Behind that venture sit ~$27.3B of amortising Beignet Investor LLC notes due May 2049, rated A+ by S&P — one notch below Meta's then-corporate rating. Four years of initial lease, sixteen years of residual support, debt to 2049 are three obligations held by different parties and activated by different events.

The fourth disclosure is the one that changes the read. Meta does not consolidate the Louisiana venture. The 10-Q names remarketing the campus, negotiating with future tenants, and selling individual properties as the activities that most affect the VIE's economics; Meta says it lacks power to direct those activities and therefore fails the first half of the primary-beneficiary test (control, not merely significant exposure). That is a control conclusion. It is not a conclusion about whether another tenant can use the campus, whether the debt will be repaid, whether a workload can migrate, or what the property is worth after Meta leaves. S&P's public credit analysis is built around non-renewal, not despite it: the residual-value mechanism is judged sufficient in most plausible Meta non-renewal cases to repay remaining debt, with a marketed sale that must start promptly and guarantee proceeds designed to arrive on a short timetable even if the sale has not completed. That is strong creditor protection and the boundary of the rating question. A guarantee can repay noteholders before a market-clearing sale reveals unsupported campus value. A sale can clear below the floor while Meta absorbs the gap. A workload can continue elsewhere while the original property loses its tenant. An accounting determination can stay correct throughout. Credit recovery, accounting control, physical succession and workload continuity are four different outcomes. The public sees one campus; the documents disclose quarterly cash flow, multi-year commitments, four-year leases, twenty-year options, declining guarantees, debt amortisation, power service, regulatory oversight, insolvency transfer, and several forms of finality. Each clock is valid. None can be substituted for another.

Meta's Q2 2026 accounts place the campuses inside a larger perimeter that must not be collapsed into one liquidity ratio. Three months ended 30 June 2026: revenue $60.80B, operating cash flow $31.86B, capital expenditure $31.08B including finance-lease principal, free cash flow $784M versus $8.549B a year earlier. Same date: $349.31B of non-cancellable contractual commitments (~$53.52B due in 2026, ~$81.65B in 2027), mainly third-party cloud, servers and network, data centres and Reality Labs hardware; plus $278.99B of leases not yet commenced — signed, dated, generally off the balance-sheet totals until commencement, expected to start between the rest of 2026 and 2036, terms from more than one year to thirty. After quarter-end, July added further data-centre leases of ~$68B, expected to commence 2027–2028 and run eighteen to twenty years. Restricted cash: $10.80B of money-market funds reclassified because multi-year infrastructure purchase-agreement escrow made them unavailable for general use, expected release 2028–2030. Louisiana maximum exposure to loss $46.03B (investment carrying value, lease commitments, estimated future funding, maximum guarantee threshold) against a recognised investment of $2.92B. Maximum exposure is a boundary, not expected loss. Meta also held $90.26B of cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities and an AA-minus rating with a stable outlook. Calling $784M "headroom" against $349.31B would treat one quarter's residual cash as if it had to discharge the entire commitment stock immediately. The burden is dated, not instantaneous. [G-1][G-13]

The broad claim that large data-centre assets cannot transfer after distress is false. Cyxtera's Chapter 11 (June 2023) produced a Brookfield asset purchase; Hut 8 took 50 percent of the 280MW King Mountain venture through the Compute North bankruptcy auction; Core Scientific emerged and, by early 2025, its [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] relationship covered ~590MW across six sites. Those cases do not reveal the recovery value of a vacated, purpose-built, single-tenant hyperscale campus above 100MW. Shanaka's search of public English-language sources covering the US, UK, EU and global data-centre markets from 2015 through 8 August 2026 did not locate a transaction meeting every condition of that narrower object: already constructed, built for one hyperscale tenant, actually vacated rather than sold occupied or transferred as an operating platform, then sold, re-tenanted or repurposed at arm's length with enough disclosure to separate property value from guarantees, public support, assumed liabilities and continuing contracts. Cyxtera was a multi-tenant colocation platform (APA consideration $775M; Brookfield described ~$1.3B for selected data centres and associated real estate — different perimeters). King Mountain is a behind-the-meter bitcoin-mining/hosting site at a wind farm. Core Scientific is conversion of powered sites for CoreWeave workloads, not an empty-hyperscale sale. Fully leased data-centre trades show a market for assets still supported by rent. The missing observation is the support-adjusted clearing value of the physical campus after contractual support, continuing rent, incentives, assumed liabilities and successor commitments have been stripped. The first qualifying print will replace an assumption with a price; it will not settle everything.

The rest of the essay maps unsynchronized institutional calendars from adjacent regimes, then refuses to treat any local success as a complete outcome. Silicon Valley Bank: operational transfer in sixteen calendar days (10 Mar 2023 close → 26 Mar First Citizens P&A) while the FDIC states exact cost is known only when receiverships terminate; the uninsured-depositor special-assessment estimate on the same defined object moved $15.8B (May 2023) → $20.4B (31 Dec 2023) → $16.7B (30 Sep 2025). Bulb Energy: special administration 24 Nov 2021 kept ~1.5 million households supplied; Energy Transfer Scheme 20 Dec 2022 moved customers into an Octopus ring-fence; by Dec 2025 HM Treasury reported full repayment of the Administration Funding Agreement (~£3.8B recovered including ~£306M interest) while June 2026 still contemplated creditors' voluntary liquidation for unsecured distributions. V.C. Summer: the Base Load Review Act wrote a recovery path before the plant generated a watt; after 2017 abandonment, Act 258, a joint resolution, the Public Service Commission, litigation and settlement rewrote the path — a statutory route is a starting architecture, not terminal incidence. Wholesale power sits in two federal systems at once (Bankruptcy Code assumption/rejection vs FERC filed-rate authority); *Mirant*, *Calpine* and *FirstEnergy* drew the line differently, and the sharpest clash (PG&E 2019–20) ended in Ninth Circuit vacatur with no governing rule. FERC's 18 June 2026 six large-load §206 show-cause orders start work on cost allocation, reliability, transparency, co-location and demand growth; they are proceedings, not completed national tariff reform. 2026 regulation attached to *services*, not buildings: UK critical-third-party oversight from 13 July (AWS EMEA, Google Cloud EMEA, Microsoft Ireland, Oracle UK) can test resilience of designated services to UK finance without authority to sell a campus or assign landlord debt. EU DORA and the Data Act, and Australia CPS 230, protect customer switching and regulated-institution continuity. Resilience can be achieved by migrating the workload off the financed property. That can be the intended public-interest mechanism — and the event that leaves owner recovery open. El Paso expects initial capacity in 2028; Louisiana leases commence 2029; first four-year renewals cluster around 2033 if that timetable holds; Beignet amortises to May 2049. Five dated, falsifiable map updates: a qualifying vacated-campus print; a financing that breaks the 4y/20y/16y template; a final large-load tariff on successor power rights; a sponsor that supports beyond contract or withholds support the market assumed; a supervisory regime attached to physical compute or campus transfer rather than to a provider, customer, service or grid function.

## Framework / Mental Model
**Clocks on one building** (Shanaka; public-record reconstruction, cut-off 8 Aug 2026). One physical campus is a stack of rights, not one duration. The building is not the lease; the lease is not the guarantee; the guarantee is not the debt; the debt is not the power right; the power right is not the workload; the workload is not the regulated service. Analytical method: keep those boundaries intact, then ask which clock becomes binding first and which authority can reset it.

| Object | Question it answers | What it does not answer |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting control | Who directs the activities judged most significant to VIE economics (remarketing, successor negotiation, property sale) | Portability of the campus, debt repayment, workload location, unsupported residual |
| Creditor recovery | Whether enough cash arrives before the binding payment deadline (guarantee proceeds can precede a completed sale) | Standalone property liquidity; equity residual after the floor is paid |
| Physical succession | Whether another operator/tenant can use the property on acceptable terms (power studies, retrofit, tariff, technical fit) | Whether the original tenant's service continues |
| Workload continuity | Whether the digital service can continue, including from another site | Whether the financed building still has a rent-paying occupant |

**Five forms of finality** (mapped from SVB / Bulb, then applied descriptively to hyperscale): operational (can customers use the service), control (who operates or owns the transferred assets), legal (have proceedings and claims ended), financial (have recoveries, expenses and distributions settled), political (has loss allocation ceased to be contested or revised). A closed account does not close the case; an open case does not reopen a closed account.

**Qualifying-transaction filter.** A reference-class print that would update residual-value priors must be: already built; single hyperscale tenant; actually vacated (not sold occupied, not transferred as a going operating platform); then sold, re-tenanted or repurposed at arm's length; with enough disclosure to separate property recovery from sponsor support, incentives, assumed liabilities and continuing contracts. Public search 2015–8 Aug 2026, US/UK/EU/global English-language sources: no hit meeting every condition. [G-10]

**Diligence sequence** (allocator / lender / board / regulator): what mission must continue; which rights are indispensable; who controls each after default, exit or non-renewal; which payments become usable before the next deadline; which authority protects the workload, grid, creditor or ratepayer; which number is an estimate, which is a completed transfer, which is a terminal outturn. Map physical asset and operating rights → lease commencement and renewal dates → each support obligation (trigger, amount, decline schedule, payment latency) → debt service and maturity → the authority over power succession, service continuity, contract assignment and loss allocation. Only then ask whether one instrument bridges another instrument's interval.

## Contradiction Check
Challenges [[Theses/META - Meta]] §Summary and §Bear Case / Risk #1 (Capex ROI), which still treat the $115–145B AI-infrastructure step-up as one on-balance growth-and-depreciation object. Off-balance 80/20 campuses, uncommenced-lease stock ($278.99B) and a $46.03B Louisiana maximum-exposure perimeter mean GAAP capex and PP&E understate both the physical build and the residual-value put Meta wrote to creditors. Directly answers META §Outstanding Questions ("Do El Paso / Louisiana-style DC financing structures transfer residual-value risk back onto Meta when non-renewal clocks hit, or are guarantees creditor-complete and equity-silent?"): S&P's A+ is engineered so the guarantee is *creditor-complete in most plausible non-renewal cases*; the same mechanism is *equity-silent on unsupported property value* and silent on whether Meta consolidates, whether a successor can take the power position, and whether Llama/Muse/ad-ranking workloads have already moved. META has no Conviction Triggers section (thesis Mental Models flag, 2026-07-10); this source does not mint one — it supplies the clocks any future trigger set would have to name (first qualifying vacated-campus print; a break in the 4y/20y/16y template; a sponsor over-support or under-support event). [G-7][G-8][G-11 inverted: physical investment is being *de*-recognised, the opposite of expensed intangibles] [G-10]: the vacated single-tenant >100MW hyperscale recovery distribution has no public reference class; do not borrow Cyxtera/colo or miner-conversion base rates.

Challenges [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] Insight #2 (DDTL IG ratings as backward-looking artefacts of contracted DSCRs, not standalone residual). Beignet A+ is the hyperscaler-sponsor twin of that mechanic: S&P rates the *guarantee-plus-sale-process*, not campus liquidity after Meta leaves. The source's Core Scientific → CRWV ~590MW/6-site print is explicitly *not* a residual-value comp — it is conversion of powered sites, the exact object the qualifying filter excludes. CRWV → CLOSE (DDTL covenant breach / MSFT cut >30%) is a different clock from Meta walking a 2GW campus at year four while remaining AA-minus; both can be true. [Semi #10]: Hyperion/El Paso are single-anchor physical plants; loss of the tenant is existential for *that building's* cash flows even when it is not existential for Meta the corporate.

Challenges the funding-quality contrast in [[Theses/NBIS - Nebius Group]] Insight #5 / → CLOSE ("funds the capex gap primarily via GPU-collateralized debt"). Meta is simultaneously the 80/20 off-balance tenant at Hyperion/El Paso *and* a ~$27B NBIS offtaker. Customer prepayments and residual-value-backed SPV notes are different instruments; both move duration and residual off the equity that looks "clean." If Meta can non-renew a GW campus at year four while S&P still calls the notes whole, that is a demand-duration input to NBIS take-or-pay books, not a proof that prepayment funding is duration-matched. [G-4] Perez-frenzy hypothesis to test: financial capital is installing GW campuses on 20–25 year amortisation against 4-year initial occupancy optionality — the over-build is the installation cost; the unobserved object is who holds the empty-box residual after the turning point.

Challenges [[Theses/VRT - Vertiv Holdings]] Insight on backlog duration / Outstanding Q on cancellation ("hyperscaler POs signed by Meta… with balance-sheet backing that makes cancellation economically harder"). Vertiv ships power and thermal kit into campuses whose *owner, tenant, guarantor and grid* clocks already diverge. A Meta residual-value payment that makes Beignet whole does not keep the CDU plant utilised; a migrated workload that satisfies a UK CTP or DORA continuity test can leave the original hall dark. FERC's June 2026 large-load dockets are the power-succession clock VRT's "grid interconnect is the binding constraint" claim sits on — and they are not finished rules. Equipment already installed is a sunk physical clock; uninstalled backlog is a different object. [Semi #1 / #8]: the bottleneck can move from kit to interconnection-and-succession rights without Vertiv's order book diagnosing which clock bound.

Does not fire a META/CRWV/NBIS/VRT conviction change on its own. Shanaka files no mispricing claim and no security-level view. Falsifiers the source itself lists: a qualifying vacated-campus print; a new financing that breaks 4y/20y/16y; a completed large-load successor-rights tariff; observed sponsor over- or under-support versus the written contract; a supervisor attached to physical compute or campus transfer. Additional vault falsifiers: Meta consolidates later; residual guarantees never trigger and campuses recontract at or above the declining floor; the power clock binds first and dominates every other calendar.
