# Samsung and SK hynix Went Optical. What About Micron? — PhotonCap

## Thesis Delta

Consensus ranks the three memory makers' optical readiness by what they have said out loud. Samsung Foundry's March 2026 PDK plus [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]]'s 20 August Nature Electronics review and newsroom pooling claim read as "they went optical," while [[Theses/MU - Micron Technology]]'s ten-year silence (no roadmap, no OFC/ECTC talk, no optics press release) reads as lag, and the MU file already prices the name as coverage-grade / don't own on first-wave Rubin mix, LTA caps, and destock, with VLM none 2027+ and no optical line in the spine. This 23 August 2026 PhotonCap follow-up to [[Research/2026-08-20 - 000660 NVDA MRVL - PhotonCap SKHY CPO Track - deep-dive]] implies the ranking is an announcement-volume artefact. Narrowed to the silicon-photonics package family, Micron has priority dates in 2012, 2018, and 2023; the 2023 filing (US20240268131A1, optical vias through stacked memory, still under examination as of 23 August) is the most specific of the three; requisition JR95848 is an open hire whose qualifications name silicon photonics and whose job is long-term optical interconnect strategy facing hyperscalers. Public silence can be character (Micron would put an optical chip inside its own memory package and sell to hyperscalers, so it has less reason to broadcast than a foundry selling a PDK) or emptiness; the posting plus a live examination does not settle which. Near-term revenue is still electrical: CXL 2.0 modules (CZ120/CZ122 on RHEL 9.3) hold that slot, and optics is a paper plus a pending application, not a 2026 EPS line. Trigger touch: **no-touch** on [[Theses/MU - Micron Technology]] → HIGH (Q3 2026 Rubin board meter ≥10% first-wave HBM4 bits AND LTA-cap removal on the SKH 2 Jul pattern AND two TrendForce DRAM prints ≥+13% QoQ with OEM days flat-to-down), → LOW (meter ~70/30/0 AND next earnings GM compression ≥400 bps with destock language), or → CLOSE (two consecutive DRAM contracts −20%+ AND rising OEM days OR CXMT qualified HBM to a Western hyperscaler OR GB300 LPDDR5X dual-source before YE2026). **no-touch** on [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] → HIGH (Rubin ≥60% AND HBM4E sole-source AND Kinex 16-Hi by Q4 2026 AND Namics ≥2028), → LOW (Samsung >35% Rubin in Q3–Q4 2026 AND HBM pricing −10% YoY H1 2026), or → CLOSE (CXMT qualified HBM at a hyperscaler by end-2028 AND Samsung HBM5 hybrid-bonding yield >70% at 16-Hi by mid-2027). Indiana groundbreaking 27 August is already in [[Research/2026-08-14 - 000660 - SK hynix Indiana 3.9B Packaging Groundbreaking - news]]; this piece adds the ~₩5.9T envelope, R&D and reliability-evaluation centres, and an explicit refusal to read the 20/27 August date pair as manoeuvring. [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] HIGH-reaffirm / LOW / CLOSE are GM, capex, HPC, Arizona, and CoWoS-alternative handles: Samsung's 2027 thermocompression optical-engine / 2029 turnkey CPO schedule is competitor colour for Insight #4 (COUPE as photonics foundry), not those observables. [[Theses/FORM - FormFactor]] HIGH/LOW/CLOSE are HBM-segment growth, Technoprobe, buyback, and socket-loss handles: the Micron 2023 spec's optical-probe at-speed test is evidence that optical test is a design-stage constraint once memory talks in light, not a FORM print. [[Theses/6857 - Advantest]] HIGH is HBM4 final-test time >14 hours: optical memory test is a later, larger-volume bottleneck, not that handle. Conviction/status unchanged (MU low / draft; 000660 medium / active). MU is not a live-book holding.

## Summary

On 20 August 2026 SK hynix announced that a co-packaged-optics roadmap paper had run in Nature Electronics. CPO puts the optical chip that moves signals as light inside the same package as the compute chip. PhotonCap had already walked the twelve authors and the fifteen-month Avicena cap-table track in the 20 August piece. What stayed with the author is a split: the paper is a Review (tables of energy and bandwidth per integration method, open problems still listed), while the newsroom put the company's name behind a sharper claim, that memory would be bound into a single pool to get past the memory wall. The question this follow-up asks is whether that direction is SK hynix's alone. Samsung is already out in public. Its foundry declared entry into silicon photonics at OFC 2026 on 17 March, said the PDK was complete (more than forty device models, verification flow, 300 mm process, free-carrier absorption and self-heating in the models), and published a schedule: thermocompression-bonded optical engines in 2027, turnkey CPO service in 2029. A PDK is the toolkit customers design with; it means customers have a base to start design and tapeout from, not that production yield and process qualification are finished. Optical devices diverge from simulation more than electrical ones do, which is why a foundry bothers to name the physics that went into the models, and why a foundry has to say where it is, repeatedly: a foundry only has a business when customers knock. That leaves Micron. PhotonCap searched silicon photonics, optical I/O, CPO, and conference presentations and found nothing recent: no OFC or ECTC optical talk, no optical-interconnect startup investment in this search, no press release on the subject in ten years. When there are no announcements you open the patent file and the job postings.

Binding memory with light is more than twenty years old and predates the silicon-photonics wave. Micron filed in 2002 (multiple wavelengths carrying more than one bit per pulse, linking a memory-module hub to the controller optically); Samsung filed in 2008 on optical channels between a memory controller and modules. The technology of the day could not deliver it, and electrical wiring still had room. The order below is not when each company first thought about this. It is when each one touched the problem once you narrow to the silicon-photonics package in use today, using the SK hynix Nature review as the yardstick because several structures it presented this year already appear in these patents. SK hynix's 2014 pair is an ITO-on-Schottky optical modulator filed with Seoul National University (the core active device, so the company was designing the device rather than reading up on it) and a package with transceivers on the top and bottom faces of a substrate, light permeating the interposer between them: the same bones as the 2026 photonic-interposer drawing, twelve years apart, priority November 2014, running to 2035. Samsung's 2018 cluster covers three layers in a year: an optical via inside HBM (data path optical, command and address still partly electrical), a photonic integrated-circuit package, and PIC devices. Where the 2008 filing linked modules, this set comes inside the package; the foundation of the foundry PDK sits here. Micron touched the narrowed family three times. 2012/2013 is the DARPA POEM era: Roy Meade and Gurtej Sandhu (a Micron senior fellow with more than 1,300 US patents) on a paper titled *Integration of Silicon Photonics in a Bulk CMOS Memory Flow*, objective "a cost-efficient optical interconnect on a memory process flow," which the author reads as the same problem statement the SK hynix review wrote thirteen years later. Monolithic integration sits in the SK hynix paper's last, hardest box; what Micron touched was a small-scale version of that box. Academic co-authors Rajeev Ram and Vladimir Stojanovic of MIT and Milos Popovic of Colorado co-founded Ayar Labs in 2015 (Series E $500M in March 2026 at a valuation in the $3.75B range). Micron wrote papers alongside the people who built Ayar Labs inside the same government program; there is no basis for saying Micron IP moved to that company. Results should not be oversold: the bulk CMOS flow was modified, extra process steps went in, waveguide loss ran around 18 dB/cm, active devices were left as future work. Looking only at public activity, this is where it stops. The patents keep coming. October 2018 priority: memories on an electro-optical substrate, electrically coupled to a circuit board and optically coupled to an optical routing layer; a sibling filing puts memory dies and an optical chip on one substrate, converts electrical to light, and connects individual memories directly to optical fibre, with only power coming through the substrate electrically. The SK hynix paper's XPU-pool / memory-pool drawing across a photonic interposer overlaps structurally with the memory-pool side of that patent (paper is a whole system, patent is one module). A continuation went in during 2021 and registered in May 2025; seven years from 2018 priority to grant, holding on through a continuation, means at minimum there was a reason to keep the family in the portfolio. Keeping a patent and intending to build a product are separate things. The third generation, priority 2 February 2023, is still under examination: *optical signaling for stacked memory device architectures*, inventors Timothy M. Hollis and Eric J. Stave. Memory dies stack vertically; an interface component carrying an array of optical receivers and emitters attaches to that stack; electrical signalling from each die's channels converts to optical there; optical vias run vertically through the memory dies. Same problem as Samsung's 2018 optical via inside HBM, filed five years later, still pending. The specification also describes aligning an optical test probe above the optical interface and running full functional at-speed testing through that interface directly: how you inspect a memory that talks in light is handled inside the same document.

Patents are a record of the past. What a company is doing now shows up in hiring. JR95848 is up on Micron's own careers system as Sr. Principal Data Center Solutions Architect (some job sites surface it as optical interconnect solutions architect; both carry the same requisition). Duties: establish long-term network and optical interconnect strategy aligned to AI-scale and hyperscale datacenter requirements; define system and interconnect architectures across electrical and optical fabrics, endpoints, and scale-up/scale-out topologies; lead technical engagements with hyperscale customers and ecosystem partners. Qualifications: 12+ years in optical interconnect, high-speed networking, silicon photonics, or datacenter system architecture. Silicon photonics sits in the qualifications as written, and the position faces hyperscale customers directly, so this is not an optical-metrology or lithography misread. The listing establishes that the role got approved for hire. Whether the strategy already exists and they need someone to run it, or it does not and they need someone to build it, the posting alone does not settle. Either way the company has come far enough to put a full-time person on the subject. Set beside a 2023 filing still in examination, the direction gets sharper. On 20 August Micron announced a Boise research lab: $10B over ten years, groundbreaking 2027, capacity for hundreds of researchers, four named domains (critical memory technologies, advanced memory and compute architectures, packaging, future semiconductor manufacturing). The word optical is absent. The last two leave a door open, because a memory company nailed compute architecture to its research list and because doing CPO takes packaging capability underneath it. CPO itself is PIC and EIC, light source, thermal management, and test tangled together, so it does not reduce to packaging alone. SK hynix's West Lafayette advanced-packaging fab (~₩5.9T, HBM packaging plus R&D and reliability-evaluation centres) has groundbreaking scheduled for 27 August. The dates sit a week apart. Large announcements get scheduled around quarterly calendars and permitting; the author will not use the proximity as evidence. What is worth looking at is character: SK hynix is building a US packaging fab, Micron a lab with packaging on the research list. Neither is a story about making memory cells better. Both put weight on packaging and system architecture rather than cell scaling. Across AIM Photonics membership (no Micron, no Samsung, no SK hynix, no Intel; two EDA companies present), the CHIPS NAPMP connector-technology line including photonics (October 2024 solicitation closed 24 September 2025 with no awards from that competition), the January 2025 $300M substrate/materials split ($100M each glass-core Absolics, silicon-core Applied Materials, FOWLP Arizona State), and July 2026 Commerce letters of intent totaling $874M including up to $300M proposed to GlobalFoundries for co-packaged optics, Micron does not appear. NSTC public materials show only the chief people officer on a workforce advisory board. The company was not quiet from the start (papers in 2013 and 2015). What stopped after that stretch was the activity that faces outward. PhotonCap's interpretation, labelled as interpretation: if Micron is drawing a picture where an optical chip goes inside its own memory package and sells straight to hyperscalers, it has relatively little reason to broadcast. A company staying quiet because it has nothing worth showing yet cannot be ruled out.

When memory moves outside the package and the channel becomes light, the obvious earners (optical engines, light sources, silicon-photonics foundries, packaging) are already watched and priced to some degree. Four layers underneath recur across the three companies' patents and the government lists, and a good share of them is needed on any path, whichever memory maker wins. (1) Attaching fibre to a chip: Micron's 2012 patent uses liquid crystal to steer the beam so light out of a fibre lands on the on-chip waveguide, with a sensor reading coupling power to adjust voltage, because mechanical alignment of a fibre core onto a waveguide within micrometres, held through thermal cycling for years, is where CPO yield and cost split. The company's most senior inventor took this rather than a device specialist. (2) Testing with light: electrical chips get probed with needles; optical chips need light in and out at wafer level on different equipment. Memory sells on volume, so per-unit test time lands directly on cost; a few extra seconds of optical test is margin. The 2023 specification carrying the optical-probe at-speed method is a signal that this process works as a constraint at the design stage rather than an attachment bolted on later. PhotonCap's earlier 100-second CPO test-stack piece was written looking at switch-side CPO; moving the target to memory puts the same bottleneck in front of far larger volumes. (3) The substrate light travels through: the 2018 claim contemplates a circuit board plus an optical routing layer, including polymer waveguides. Organic substrates get less favourable as reach and dimensional stability climb, which is why glass comes up as a strong candidate, not a confirmed path. (4) Design tools: a PDK only runs inside the customer's EDA environment; AIM Photonics has two EDA companies and not one large memory company. Optical EDA rarely gets covered because the market looks small, and it is the layer that attaches first once PDKs start landing. Configurations using connector-style coupling instead of fibre arrays, or routing inside the PIC instead of through the substrate, mean the four layers do not all carry equal weight. Convert them into observables rather than a theme map: CPO share of alignment/fibre-array orders and equipment lead times; new orders for optical probing equipment and the memory-customer share; glass-substrate pilot lines running and whether they clear customer qualification; whether tools supporting optical PDKs actually get used in customer tapeouts. Until those numbers move, this stays at the story stage. Volume these companies take today comes from switches and accelerators. Memory volume arrives only after CPO moves toward memory, and that is still drawn in a paper and a pending application.

The Micron date-line the author will defend: a 2002 optical memory-bus patent; a 2012 priority filing from the DARPA POEM period; papers in 2013 and 2015; a 2018 patent putting memory and an optical chip on one substrate; a continuation in that family in 2021; a 2023 application on optical signalling for stacked memory; grant in 2025; and as of August 2026 an open hire for optical interconnect strategy. The filing record has no gap. What stopped after 2015 was conferences and press releases, not applications. That does not let the author claim research ran without pause for twenty years: patents recurring at several points and an organisation running continuously in between are different claims. A picture where Micron walked away from optical memory does not match the public record. Priority dates say nothing about when a company started researching or how mature that research is. A patent existing does not make a product. Samsung and SK hynix starting points of 2008 and 2014 are not very different. Getting past the bottleneck through memory alone is hard; making cells finer or stacking more layers keeps getting more expensive; as the weight shifts toward packaging and system architecture, optics comes in as an increasingly strong option. Electrical SerDes improvements, near-memory compute, compression, and tiering all advance alongside, so calling optics the only destination would go too far. Time axis: of what the three companies have put furthest forward, SK hynix's is a paper that left coherence protocols as an open problem, and Micron's is an application still under examination. Neither has arrived, so neither is an event that lands in near-term results. What comes in as revenue now is still electrical. Next steps the author will watch: whether the optical-interconnect-strategy role actually gets filled and gains headcount behind it; whether the 2023 filing moves to grant and new priority dates follow; whether optics gets named explicitly under the Boise lab's packaging line. On Samsung: the 2027 optical-engine schedule and whether a customer tapeout using the PDK appears. On SK hynix: whether the Indiana site's scope widens beyond HBM packaging. The other direction is open too. If the hiring ends at one or two roles and does not repeat, this reads as opening a position rather than staffing an organisation. If the 2023 filing fails to register or no new priority dates follow, the patents become assets held and nothing more. If optical memory cannot solve coherence and keeps slipping, all three companies sit in the electrical tier for a long time, which pushes this article's time axis back wholesale. The hypothesis the author started with was that Micron had fallen behind on optics because there were no announcements. Digging changed it. The author confirmed carrying the habit of ranking by volume of announcements too.

## Framework / Mental Model

**Name:** Public disclosure versus patent priority, then four layers you pass through whichever architecture wins. Ranking memory makers by press releases, PDKs, and journal papers inverts the order you get from priority dates plus hiring.

**Components.**

| Axis | Definition | How the source applies it |
|---|---|---|
| Public disclosure | Roadmap, PDK, conference talk, newsroom, journal corresponding-author seat | Samsung Foundry OFC 2026 PDK + 2027/2029 schedule. SK hynix Nature review + 20 Aug newsroom pooling claim. Micron: none in ten years |
| Patent priority (narrowed family) | Silicon-photonics package structures, not the 2002/2008 optical-bus idea | SK hynix 2014 modulator + permeating-interposer package. Samsung 2018 optical via / PIC package / PIC device. Micron 2012, 2018, 2023 (pending) |
| Package ownership | When the far end of the channel is memory, the optical chip has to come inside the memory package | The company that designs, assembles, and tests that package is the memory company. It stops being a networking/foundry problem |
| Foundry-must-talk vs IDM-can-stay-quiet | A foundry only has a business when customers knock | Samsung tells you because Samsung has to sell a PDK. If Micron puts optics inside its own package and sells to hyperscalers, it has less reason to broadcast |
| Job posting as present-tense | Approved FTE on a named subject, not a product | JR95848 silicon-photonics qualification + hyperscale-customer engagement. Does not settle strategy-exists vs strategy-to-be-built |
| Continuation as portfolio, not product | Maintenance and continuations cost money; defence and optionality are real uses | 2018 family: continuation 2021, grant May 2025, expires 2040-07-31. Necessary not sufficient for a build intent |
| Four pick-and-shovel layers | Fibre attach / alignment; optical test; substrate the light travels through; optical EDA | Needed on any path. Observables: CPO share of alignment orders and lead times; optical-probe orders and memory-customer share; glass-pilot qualification; PDK tools used in tapeouts |
| Coherence tax (carried from the 20 Aug piece) | Optical bandwidth versus added conversion and link latency | Target <10 ns chip-to-chip; controller, protocol, queuing, and coherence stack on top; optical memory interface that keeps coherence is still on the open-problems list |
| Electrical holds the P&L | CXL is the channel that ships | Micron CZ120/CZ122 CXL 2.0 on RHEL 9.3. Optics comes after |

**Methodology.** Line the three companies up on two axes (public disclosure above, patent priority below) after narrowing to the silicon-photonics package, using the SK hynix Nature review as the yardstick for structures. Do not treat 2002/2008 optical-bus patents as the same object. Do not read announcement volume as research intensity. Do not read a week of calendar proximity (Boise 20 Aug, Indiana 27 Aug) as manoeuvring. Tag interpretation as interpretation (quiet-because-hyperscaler-channel). A pending application plus a job posting is a direction, not a product. Convert the four layers into observables before treating them as an investment map. Falsifiers are staffing (one or two roles, no repeat), prosecution (2023 fails to grant, no new priority dates), and physics (coherence unsolved, time axis slips).
