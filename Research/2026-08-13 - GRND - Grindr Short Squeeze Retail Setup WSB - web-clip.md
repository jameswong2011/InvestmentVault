## Summary

The author's product argument: Grindr is not "Gay Tinder," and the niche is the edge, not a discount. Mainstream apps fail structurally — men are 75–80% of Tinder's base and male/female intent is misaligned (men skew casual, women skew serious), so a tiny fraction of men capture most attention and the median user gets nothing. A gay-male app removes that asymmetry: intent is symmetric, "hookup culture" clears the market, and the author claims biology drives dramatically higher partner counts (invoking the Bell & Weinberg 1978 study). The asserted result is engagement of ~67 min/day — second only to TikTok's 96, versus Hinge 10 and Tinder 12 — plus "over 50% of US gay relationships start on Grindr" and retention of users even after they couple up. He layers on an "extremely desirable user base" (educated, high-disposable-income, urban, highly engaged) that supposedly makes monetization and vertical integration (Right Now paid queueing, Edge/"gAI" premium tier, Woodworking telehealth at ~0 CAC) frictionless.

The valuation and moat framing: Q1 2026 delivered +38% revenue and 45% adj. EBITDA margin; management raised FY26 guidance to ≥$535M revenue / $227M adj. EBITDA; at a ~$3.4B market cap the stock trades ~15x EBITDA and ~6x sales with >25% FCF margin, >$100M TTM net income, and ~$2.7M revenue per employee. A self-described "quick ai analysis" DCF (30% revenue growth fading to 10% over ten years, 10% discount rate, stable margins) outputs a $13.5B / $69-per-share "fair value." The moat is framed as un-recreatable ("hand someone $3.4B and they couldn't rebuild the user base — the moat is not priced in at all"), CEO George Arison (since 2022; ~30% revenue CAGR; a Grindr user himself; serial founder) is praised, and Morgan Stanley's $29 bull target (Feather/Nowak) is cited as third-party validation.

The actual pitch is the technical setup, and the source quality is the caveat. Six insiders hold ~146.8M shares (the author says "82.58% of float"); the public float is ~29M shares; short interest is 10.58M shares (~35% of float, per Robinhood) against 1.7M average daily volume (>6 days to cover); and the incremental $450M buyback at ~$17 could retire ~26.5M shares — over 90% of the public float — which the author claims can "send a massive short squeeze without any additional buying pressure" into the Aug 6 earnings. Every one of these figures is single-sourced to a promotional post whose author holds shares and calls, whose valuation is an admitted AI one-liner, whose anchor biological "stat" is a 1978 study, and whose ownership math conflates float with shares outstanding (six insiders cannot own 82.58% "of float" while the public float is 29M shares — the percentages are of shares outstanding). The structural incentive is to pump a thin-float name into a binary event; the content should be read as a hypothesis to disconfirm, not evidence to bank.
