# Lumentum First $1B Quarter FQ4 2026 — PhotonCap

## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices FQ4 as a $1B pluggable/EML confirmation already in the multiple (stock +2% after hours, still >20% below the $1,085.68 52-week high) and reads the $7.2B GAAP hole as an operating wipeout → PhotonCap treats the print as first-S-curve cash already discounted and argues the real news is five in-rack signals (1.6T production, OCS first $100M-plus quarter, UHP CPO lasers, first ELS purchase order, broadening NPO) that open a dated 2H27–2028 TAM floor while the GAAP loss is a $7.8B non-cash convert-equitization footnote on $1.1B of debt retired. [G-13] the $1.25B / ~40% OM staircase is what the price already embeds; [Semis #8] copper-to-optics inside the rack is the unpriced duration claim; [VLM §1/§4] merchant OCS + ELS-module step-up is the emerging-layer test, not the $1B headline.

## Summary
PhotonCap's 2026-08-12 post-call note (released the evening of [[Theses/LITE - Lumentum]]'s FQ4'26 print, quarter ended 2026-06-27) is not a business description and is not a $1B-celebration. The author's lead is that the $1,006.3M revenue figure (+24.5% QoQ / +109.3% YoY off a $480.7M year-ago base) is the eighth consecutive growth quarter and the third in a row above 20% sequential, stacking ~$200M on the May $808M print — and that this is still the less-interesting number [1×: LITE IR / PhotonCap]. Non-GAAP gross margin crossed 50% for the first time at 50.4%, a threshold management had modeled around a $2B quarterly revenue run-rate and hit at half that revenue; non-GAAP operating margin printed 36.6% (+440 bps QoQ / +2,160 bps YoY) with operating expenses pinned at 13.7% of sales, so incremental dollars drop through [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. FQ1'27 guidance of $1.225–1.275B revenue, 39.5–40.5% non-GAAP OM, and $4.05–4.35 EPS puts the OFC target model ($1.25B quarterly) more than a quarter early, and the CFO lifted the long-run OM range by 100–200 bps so that 42% is "more like the midpoint" of what used to be a 38–42% band at $2B quarterly [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. The GAAP net loss of $7.2B is a $7.8B non-cash loss on debt extinguishment from proactively converting ~$1.1B of convertibles (~35% of the outstanding book) into equity after the stock's multi-fold rise; non-GAAP net income is $326.3M / $3.23 per share [1×: LITE IR / PhotonCap]. Dilution is real and permanent; cash did not leave. The August 11 regular-session close was $820.59 (+0.87%); after-hours the stock was up ~2% at a $63.8B market cap, more than 20% below the $1,085.68 52-week high against a $111.20 low [web: stockanalysis.com via PhotonCap]. PhotonCap's market-structure read: optics now has standalone theme products (Roundhill LYTE listed five days earlier; Tema LAZR late June), so a print of this magnitude being met with a 2% after-hours move is the tell that the first S-curve is priced [web: Roundhill / Tema via PhotonCap].

Demand that doubled revenue is the hyperscaler network sitting next to the GPU. PhotonCap points back to late-July big-four capex upgrades: every incremental GPU cluster still requires rack-to-rack and site-to-site optics — transceivers, the InP laser chips inside them, and the pump lasers in the long-haul spans between campuses — all on Lumentum's list. The call's scale anecdote: for one major hyperscaler, the network capacity connecting *two* AI data-center sites is double the entire global backbone that company laid over the prior decade [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Pump lasers are sold out after >80% YoY shipment growth, with a plan to quadruple output over the next several quarters, underwritten by three-year long-term agreements that are often take-or-pay and contain contractual price-increase levers [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. The July Aurelion/PhotonCap IR conversation already had EML capacity up eightfold in two and a half years and still short; FQ4 is the first quarter-close where that claim prints as a $1B P&L ([[Research/2026-07-29 - LITE Aurelion PhotonCap Management Meeting - deep-dive]]). The CEO press-release paragraph does not linger on today's SKUs. It strings five products that sit *inside* the rack — OCS, 1.6T, ultra-high-power CPO lasers, a first ELS module order, NPO — and closes with "optics are starting to penetrate in-rack connectivity" [1×: LITE IR / PhotonCap]. Until now optics connected rack-to-rack and DC-to-DC; copper owned the short scale-up links. GPU generations that force multiple racks to behave as one computer pull that floor from copper to optics and open a second addressable market. PhotonCap's claim-scope is explicit: the five signals are products *outside* the existing pluggable transceiver TAM, all timed to land in revenue between 2H27 and 2028, lining up with the July IR answer that "copper hits the wall in late 2027."

Both reported segments ran together. Components $649.4M (+21.8% QoQ / +102.7% YoY) and Systems $356.9M (+29.7% QoQ / +122.6% YoY) each more than doubled year over year [1×: LITE IR / PhotonCap]. Inside Components: EML printed another record, still volume-centered on 100G per lane, with 200G EMLs now >25% of EML revenue and expected to pass half of EML *volume* by mid-2027, supply still trailing demand [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. CW is the section PhotonCap "stared at the longest": Japan-fab output beat plan, surplus wafers were steered to CW, a substantial die shrink narrowed the margin gap versus EMLs, and a tight spec distribution produces high yields in customer transceiver lines, supporting a premium to market price [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Irrational Analysis's physics read, which PhotonCap adopts but does not re-model, is that the shrink is a shorter cavity at the same linewidth requirement — more chips per wafer if wavelength purity holds ([[Research/2026-08-12 - LITE Irrational Analysis Q4 FY26 Call Alpha - deep-dive]]). The 1.6T generation's SiPh share rise makes the dual-hand (EML *and* CW) the hedge: whichever modulation architecture wins, the light source is still Lumentum's. Pump lasers sit on take-or-pay 3-year LTAs. On Systems: 800G transceivers hit record shipments, 1.6T production shipments started on plan, OCS shipments doubled quarter-over-quarter, and transceiver-line margins improved as higher-priced initial 1.6T volume landed on yield and utilization gains [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. One-line compression PhotonCap uses: today's cash (EML, 800G) and tomorrow's cash (CW, 1.6T, OCS) grew in the same quarter.

The five signals are the reservation list for the second S-curve. (1) 1.6T production shipments have started; adoption is expected to intensify from FQ1'27 through 2027; management claims in several cases they reached the market ahead of larger competitors — a vendor claim, not an audited share print [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. (2) OCS doubled sequentially and next quarter's guide includes the first-ever $100M-plus OCS quarter, "meaningfully above" that mark. The existing $400M 2H26 OCS guide is *tracking rather than exceeding*, which PhotonCap reads as slack left in the later quarter after early-ramp supply-chain losses. Competitive language: even against the customer that builds OCS in-house (publicly Google), Lumentum says it will overtake that internal volume by early 2027 and become the number-one supplier, and that it is effectively the only merchant OCS vendor; higher- and lower-port-count variants plus in-tray products aimed at 2028 sit on the roadmap [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. (3) Ultra-high-power CPO laser demand has accelerated since the last update; the lead CPO customer's production plans remain on track (on the public record [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] has named Lumentum an ELS vendor); HVM shipments 2H27, customer scale-up deployments 2028; the CFO put high-power laser revenue through the $50M level late this calendar year and the first $100M quarter in FQ3'27 [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. (4) The first ELS-module purchase order is in, delivery 2H27; ASP is "meaningfully higher" than discrete laser chips; margin sits above company average but below standalone lasers; management framed ELS as the product that "opens up CPO and NPO for customers who do not know how to handle laser chips" [1×: LITE IR / FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. (5) NPO engagements are broadening — board-adjacent optical engines as a faster-to-build stepping stone that gives up power and cost versus CPO; customers other than the lead, *and* the largest CPO customer for specific use cases, are in design discussions; the light-source approach derates the 400 mW-class high-power design to 120–150 mW mid-power; this fifth signal is still pre-PO, with revenue timed late 2027 into 2028 [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. PhotonCap layers Irrational Analysis's reliability frame onto (3)–(5): integrating the laser into the optical engine makes drive-current and thermal environments harsher, so preventing mode hops is harder than in an external module, and a dead laser forces whole-engine replacement; ELS swaps the module. With InP scarce, area-efficient integration and reliability-driven externalization are in a tug of war; the light-source vendor that has proven a wide stable operating range in production statistics wins either way — the July IR thesis that "the specs that do not show up on datasheets are the real moat" [1×: Irrational Analysis via PhotonCap].

Guidance is half Components, half Systems, so the 1.6T and OCS ramps are already inside the next three months of reported numbers; PhotonCap's three-month check is whether section-5 signals are talk or P&L. The muted tape is read as "numbers of this level were already largely in the stock," with the June SemiAnalysis-driven CPO-timing correction as the bar the call is rebutting: "the lead customer's demand signal has grown" and "the first ELS PO" did not exist three months ago [1×: PhotonCap]. The August 2 AXT-LTA checkpoint ([[Research/2026-08-02 - AXTI LITE InP Substrate LTA Prepay Chain - deep-dive]]) is updated from the transcript, not the press release. Citi asked the ~30% EML supply-gap question: CEO said for EML, no change — neither wider nor narrower, still shipping behind demand — but the weight of the answer sat on high-power lasers, where demand accelerated and supply fell further behind; ramp execution is on plan, the demand signal outran it [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Shortage gravity is shifting from EML toward high-power CW. Wolfe asked why AXT after a quarter of "substrate supply is fine": a surge in high-power laser demand arrived over the prior three months and the AXT deal was the response; adding AXT to the existing Japanese supplier covers them *for now*, with an explicit "that judgment could change a quarter or two from now" if the demand vector continues [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. No additional substrate LTAs have been signed; management previewed new arrangements around Greensboro capacity with multiple customers, "something to announce within a few quarters." China InP competition: "no impact is visible in the numbers," and the customer-yield advantage from spec-distribution tightness "will keep protecting the premium"; some Chinese suppliers "throwing out big numbers are not actually delivering into the market yet" — company-internal comparison, not externally verified [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. PhotonCap's one-sentence close: under the first-$1B headline, the next-cycle reservation list (1.6T, OCS, CPO, ELS, NPO) got physical evidence across the board for the first time. Wrong if OCS or 1.6T slips in the November guide (second S-curve pushed at least two quarters), if the lead CPO customer's 2028 deployment wobbles again (June correction marked right), or if Chinese InP is confirmed in actual shipments rather than announcements.

## Framework / Mental Model
PhotonCap names three interlocking typologies on this call, plus two borrowed physics frames it does not re-estimate.

**Five Signals (PhotonCap original).** A checklist of products *outside* the pluggable transceiver market that must appear together to justify an in-rack / scale-up TAM expansion: (1) 1.6T production start, (2) OCS first triple-digit quarter, (3) UHP CPO laser demand acceleration with a dated HVM path, (4) first ELS-module PO, (5) NPO engagements broadening. Scoring is binary on evidence state (shipping / guided / PO / engagement) and on calendar (all five clocked to 2H27–2028 revenue). Signal 5 is explicitly still engagement-stage; treating it as a PO is a mis-score.

**Two S-curves.** First S-curve = EML + 800G cash that printed the $1B quarter. Second S-curve = the five-signal reservation list. The analytical claim is that a same-quarter print of *both* is the information, not either curve alone.

**Judgment-date ladder.** PhotonCap converts the call into a dated falsification calendar rather than a narrative: mid-August 2026 AXT FQ2 ($87M prepayment receipt + expansion progress); early November 2026 LITE FQ1'27 (first $100M+ OCS quarter, 1.6T takeoff, 39.5–40.5% OM delivered); December-quarter 2026 (EML unit shipments +50% YoY; high-power laser revenue at the $50M level); early 2027 (overtake the customer's internal OCS volume); mid-2027 (200G EMLs >50% of EML volume); 2H27 (HVM high-power lasers + first ELS deliveries; first $100M high-power quarter in FQ3'27); early 2028 (first Greensboro revenue; customer scale-up deployments). Slippage on the November gate pushes the second-S-curve read at least two quarters.

**Borrowed, not re-modeled.** Cavity-length interpretation of the CW die shrink, and the mode-hop / ELS-swap reliability frame, are attributed to Irrational Analysis's Lumentum Q4 FY26 piece [1×: Irrational Analysis via PhotonCap]. PhotonCap states it does *not* use IA's BOM-model margin estimates for high-power lasers and the ELS module (those are IA's numbers, not company-confirmed).
