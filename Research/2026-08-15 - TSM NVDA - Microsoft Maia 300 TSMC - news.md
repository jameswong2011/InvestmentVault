## Summary
GIGAZINE (13 August 2026 08:00:00, log1h_ik; AI / Hardware) relays The Information ("Microsoft's Homegrown AI Chip Effort Shows Signs of Life After Slow Start") and Reuters ("Microsoft plans to unveil next-generation AI chip in September, The Information reports," 10 August 2026). Same-story English cluster used for additional facts already flagged in the triage note: Quartz (updated 10 August 2026), TNW, RuntimeWire (primary source: The Information; person with direct knowledge of the TSMC discussions). TSMC did not respond to requests for comment (Quartz / triage).

According to The Information, Microsoft plans to announce its next-generation AI chip, Maia 300, as early as September 2026 (also framed as "this fall" / "this autumn"), and is in negotiations with Taiwan's TSMC to manufacture more than 300,000 chips by 2027 (Quartz: "upward of 300,000" targeting delivery sometime in 2027; RuntimeWire: capacity for more than 300,000 Maia 300 chips "next year"). NVIDIA holds an overwhelming market share as a manufacturer of AI chips, but major technology companies are trying to reduce their dependence on NVIDIA by developing their own AI chips. Google has developed its own processor called TPU, specifically designed for AI processing, and Amazon is manufacturing its own AI chip called Trainium. Microsoft announced its own AI chip, the Maia 100, in 2023 (Quartz / RuntimeWire: first Maia introduced November 2023) and the second generation, Maia 200, appeared in 2026 (Quartz / TNW / RuntimeWire: Maia 200 arrived / started rolling out in January 2026). Microsoft is lagging Alphabet, which began recording TPU sales revenue in its second-quarter 2026 earnings report (Quartz: milestone in the quarter that closed in June), and Amazon, which has started negotiations to sell its Trainium chip to other data centers (GIGAZINE cross-link: moving from AWS-exclusive use to other data centers).

Only tens of thousands of the previous generation Maia 200 were manufactured, and as of late July 2026 they were only operational in two data centers in the United States. RuntimeWire adds: Microsoft delayed Maia 200 after early tests missed internal targets; Microsoft remains the only known user; Maia 200 was introduced in January as an inference chip built on TSMC's 3-nanometer process, with 216 gigabytes of HBM3e memory and support for lower-precision FP4 and FP8 calculations; by April, Microsoft said Maia 200 was operating in its Iowa and Arizona data centers; Microsoft claimed the accelerator delivered more than 30% better tokens per dollar than the newest silicon then running in Microsoft's fleet — an internal comparison, and Microsoft has not published enough customer testing to establish whether Maia can deliver similar savings across outside workloads. Quartz: Maia 200 relies on TSMC's 3-nanometer process and incorporates substantial SRAM — a memory technology that gives AI systems a throughput edge when fielding heavy user traffic.

Microsoft aims to eventually produce more than one million Maia 300s and reportedly plans to acquire major cloud customers, including AI development company Anthropic. The Information points out that constraints such as component shortages and ongoing negotiations with TSMC will be obstacles to achieving this goal (Quartz: the 1 million figure may prove difficult given parts availability and where talks with TSMC currently stand; TNW: TSMC's advanced packaging capacity is tight through 2027; component supplies could constrain the >1 million-unit target). Andrew Wall, General Manager of Azure Maia at Microsoft, commented: "Microsoft continues to invest in custom silicon as part of its long-term AI infrastructure strategy. We do not disclose production volumes, but the reported figures do not reflect the scale of our program." RuntimeWire: Wall declined to discuss production volumes in a statement to The Information; said the reported figures did not reflect the scale of Microsoft's program; said Microsoft expects Maia deployments to support AI demand measured in gigawatts; did not provide a chip count or timetable. TNW: a company manager said the ultimate goal is to produce "gigawatts' worth" of Maia chips.

Additional same-story English coverage (TNW / Quartz / RuntimeWire), kept in full:

Microsoft's custom chip programme started years after Amazon and Google. The 300,000-unit order for Maia 300 is Microsoft's attempt to close that gap before its cloud customers start choosing Amazon or Google specifically because of their custom silicon (TNW). TSMC's advanced packaging capacity is tight through 2027 (TNW). Microsoft recently told employees to stop "tokenmaxxing" and set division-level AI budgets, a sign that compute costs are becoming a constraint even at the company spending $35.8 billion per quarter on infrastructure (TNW). All three cloud giants are chasing AI accelerators that cost less and run more efficiently than Nvidia's GPUs; Nvidia's chips remain the industry standard, but high prices and chronic short supply have turned custom silicon from a nice-to-have into a strategic necessity (TNW / Quartz). If the 300,000-unit 2027 bet works, Azure gets a cost advantage over Nvidia-dependent competitors; if it does not, Microsoft will have spent billions on a chip programme that is still a generation behind (TNW).

Microsoft has been working to place Maia chips with major cloud customers. The company was in early talks to supply Maia 200 chips to Anthropic for use in the AI startup's Claude models, though no deal had been finalized (Quartz; RuntimeWire: talks about using Maia). Anthropic agreed to use Amazon Trainium in a 10-year arrangement and announced plans to use Google TPUs (Quartz). RuntimeWire sizes the gap: AWS says nearly 1 million Trainium2 chips are training and serving Claude through Project Rainier; Alphabet separately told investors that Anthropic planned to access up to 1 million Google TPUs; those deployments give Amazon and Google a major independent model developer building directly on their custom accelerators — software tooling, model optimization, and reliable access to large clusters can tie customers to a chip platform over successive hardware generations — so Maia 300 has to arrive with usable capacity and a mature software stack, rather than benchmark gains alone. The Information cited Morgan Stanley estimates that Google planned to make more than 3 million TPUs in 2026 and 5 million in 2027; Microsoft's discussions for more than 300,000 Maia 300 chips would still represent an order-of-magnitude increase from Maia 200 production and would remain smaller than rival programs (RuntimeWire). TNW: Anthropic is building its own chip team and has deals to use one million Amazon Trainium chips and Google TPUs.

Microsoft can use Maia even without Anthropic. Maia runs OpenAI models and Microsoft's own MAI models, which support Copilot products, though The Information reported that Nvidia hardware still handles most of those workloads (RuntimeWire). Microsoft's fallback is to direct more internal inference to Maia while reserving costly Nvidia systems for Azure customers willing to pay for them. Microsoft told investors in July that Maia 200 was 30% to 40% cheaper to operate than cutting-edge Nvidia chips when running OpenAI and Microsoft models, according to The Information; internal testing has shown further gains from Maia 300, the report said, without providing a figure (RuntimeWire). A 2022 email later released in court showed Satya Nadella worrying about Microsoft's thin infrastructure layer above Nvidia and its lack of control over both the chips and OpenAI's intellectual property (RuntimeWire). Maia 300 is the first planned production ramp large enough to address that dependency. The September unveiling will establish Microsoft's technical claims; the consequential test comes in 2027, when Microsoft must secure the components, deploy hundreds of thousands of chips, and convince at least one large customer to use them (RuntimeWire).

GIGAZINE related-post cluster on the same page (not independently verified here; retained as on-page context): OpenAI's first in-house custom chip slated for 2025; Microsoft developing Athena in-house since 2019 and already testing with OpenAI LLMs; TSMC capacity limits prompting Google, NVIDIA, AMD, Tesla and others to consider Samsung; Microsoft purchased 485,000 Nvidia Hopper GPUs (double its rivals' total) while ByteDance and Tencent each purchased around 230,000 and Meta 225,000; Anthropic signed with Google and Broadcom to deploy Google TPUs at large scale; OpenAI signed a strategic partnership with Broadcom for in-house AI chips (contract value estimated at hundreds of billions of yen; 10 gigawatt-class chips); OpenAI reportedly begins using Google TPU for ChatGPT inference.
