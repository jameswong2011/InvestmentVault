# NVDA Superposition — Open Weights Expand the Compute Market

## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices open weights as a binary for [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] — either a CUDA-kill (Bear Case share fade 75%→60% via ASIC/ROCm) or automatic Jevons volume (Bull Case: open-source model proliferation raises infra demand) → Superposition Part 1 (fact cutoff 2026-08-11) prices NVIDIA capture as **N × A × S × V**: openness can enlarge the deployer/task/intensity set while exposing execution share and stack premium, so the live variable is qualification-time-and-cost on AMD / TPU / Trainium / XPU versus CUDA’s moving production-cost gap, not model-download counts. Hypothesis: if major open-weight models reach qualified production on alternative backends faster and cheaper, S and V compress even if N and A grow; if demos proliferate while feature/scale/ops gaps persist, early-phase demand concentrates on NVIDIA. [G-13] [G-14] [semis #2] [VLM §2 falling-switching-costs]

## Summary
Open weights expand the set of organizations that can self-deploy a model, the experiments that can become recurring production workloads, and the execution paths that can be *attempted* — they do not automatically raise net accelerator-hours. Superposition’s central sentence is the claim the rest of the essay is built to keep from collapsing: open weights can create new compute demand, but they do not determine either its net magnitude or the architecture on which it will run. Two doors open at once and they face opposite directions. **Demand-side fungibility** is the ability to reassign the same AI-factory capacity across customers, workloads, or operators without a major loss of economic value — which customer can fill a given box after the current job ends. **Silicon portability** is the ability to move the same model or workload across NVIDIA, [[Theses/AMD - Advanced Micro Devices]], TPU, Trainium, or custom XPU ([[Theses/AVGO - Broadcom]]) at an acceptable engineering and operating cost — which box a given workload can run on. NVIDIA sits inside both effects: it signed the 2026-07-24 *Open Weights and American AI Leadership* letter, and FY2026 Form 10-K says the company accelerated release cadence of open AI model platforms including Nemotron and Cosmos; the same filing warns that high-quality open-source foundation models deployed on competing platforms could reduce demand for NVIDIA products and services. The filing’s category is “open-source foundation models”; the essay asks how that disclosed risk materializes inside the narrower category of open weights.

Demand-side fungibility is a system property, not a model attribute. Physical capacity (accelerators, networking, power, cooling) can exist while economic value collapses if the operator cannot place another paying workload after the current one ends. The July letter — a collective policy claim by signatories that include NVIDIA, not an empirical demonstration that aggregate compute consumption has already risen on a net basis — defines open-weight models as models organizations can download, inspect, modify, and run on their own infrastructure, and argues they let buyers skip training every model from scratch, choose efficient specialized models, and promote competition across cloud, chip, application, and service layers. Superposition splits the resulting “growth” into three processes that must be measured separately. **Net-new creation**: workloads that could not exist under external API pricing, data-control requirements, regional regulation, or security constraints become viable via self-deployment — sovereign AI, industry-specific systems, local inference, internal-data applications. **Relocation**: a workload already running through a closed API moves to self-hosted infrastructure or another cloud; one factory’s demand rises, market-level accelerator-hours may not. **Compute-intensity change**: agentic loops, long reasoning traces, multimodal processing, and synthetic-data generation raise compute per task; smaller models, quantization, distillation, caching, batching, and serving optimization cut it. Open weights therefore expand the *possibility set* first; net accelerator consumption is the combined result afterward. Utilization is not determined by the number of published models. A replacement customer can use the same servers only if memory, network topology, latency, data access, security certification, and SLAs are compatible. A cluster tuned for large-scale post-training is not a low-latency inference service; power, cooling, and operating software can make physically similar accelerators economically different assets. GPU-hours are not a homogeneous commodity. The operator question is: when the current customer or workload disappears, how quickly can the same capacity be placed with another paying workload, and at what price discount and conversion cost? That is the same remarketing variable [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] DDTL/re-rent analysis already treats as the second-cycle credit test.

Silicon portability is neither a necessary nor a sufficient product of published weights. A model owner can run multiple architectures without releasing weights: Anthropic states Claude is trained and served across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, and NVIDIA GPUs, with workloads allocated by platform characteristics, and that holding the same quality standard requires platform-specific optimization and validation. The distinctive effect of open weights is elsewhere: they let a broader set of third parties develop and validate alternative execution paths without a separate bilateral collaboration with the model owner. Even then the legal/artifact gate comes first — execution and modification rights plus architecture, tokenizers, serving code, custom ops, quantization methods, validation materials. OSI distinguishes open weights from a fully open-source AI system; weights are a critical artifact, not automatic opening of architecture, training process, execution code, or all redistribution rights. After the gate, Superposition scores three levels that the market routinely flattens. **Formal portability**: the model loads and produces output on another backend (a compatible framework and compilation path can start this). **Technical portability**: required quality, functionality, scale, and SLA — supported ops, numerical stability, distributed execution, validation. **Economic substitutability**: a real operator has reason to leave the incumbent — TCO, equipment supply, engineering talent, operating risk, cloud availability, existing contracts, durability of the alternative ecosystem. Portability is not performance parity, and performance parity is not economic substitutability. Common layers shorten pieces of the path without making backends identical: StableHLO as a portability layer between ML frameworks and compilers; vLLM across CUDA and AMD ROCm. AMD HIP documentation states HIP is not a drop-in CUDA replacement and porting can still require manual coding and performance tuning. AWS Trainium’s Neuron 2.30 (May 2026) ended PyTorch/XLA support for Trainium *training* and moved toward native PyTorch/TorchNeuron (Neuron 2.31, PyTorch 2.12); PyTorch/XLA remains supported for inference. Native PyTorch, standard distributed APIs, and an official vLLM Neuron route lower the code-entry barrier; version compatibility, custom kernels, model-specific functionality, compilation behavior, and production performance still require separate validation. Open weights do not produce complete hardware substitution. They make the execution market more *contestable*. Whether contestability becomes a shift in execution share is a separate question.

NVIDIA’s public support for openness and its 10-K competitor-deployment risk are one business problem, not a contradiction. NVIDIA does not need to own every important model to benefit from an expanding compute market: outside developers and enterprises creating more models and applications, and more post-training and inference entering production, enlarge the potential demand pool for accelerated computing. The more widely models can be deployed and modified, the easier it is for competing accelerator vendors and cloud operators to build their own optimization paths. An open model does not create NVIDIA demand first; it creates *contestable* compute demand. Superposition labels the next sentence an analytical inference, not declared intent: disclosures and product strategy can be read as using openness at the model layer to expand the market while preserving NVIDIA as the default execution path at the infrastructure layer. The objective is not necessarily to keep every model closed. It is to remain the fastest, most mature, lowest-risk route when a model moves from experiment into production. As the model layer opens, competition at the execution layer becomes *more* important, not less. CUDA’s moat is not API syntax or developer-count theater. Code compatibility is one layer; production cost accumulates above it — porting (custom ops, compilation graphs, memory layouts, collectives, quantization, distributed runtimes), optimization (batch, memory, kernel fusion, cache, comm/compute overlap, tensor/pipeline/expert parallelism), validation (numerical quality, quantized/multimodal correctness, long-running distributed reproducibility; in high-failure-cost industries this can dominate purchase-price gaps), and operating cost (monitoring, fault diagnosis, security patching, capacity planning, autoscaling, supply contracts, hiring, incident-response routines). The stack Superposition writes is: model artifacts → frameworks → compilers and graphs → kernels and runtimes → networks and clusters → validation procedures → operating organizations → production economics. Small lower-layer compatibility gaps propagate upward as reoptimization, revalidation, and operational risk. The moat is also not a permanent wall. Porting/optimization work paid once and absorbed into an open-source project or common compiler is not paid again by the next user; if NVIDIA ships new hardware, libraries, and networking faster than that catch-up, the optimization target competitors must match keeps moving. CUDA’s moat is a cost differential repeatedly recreated by the gap between NVIDIA stack velocity and the rate at which alternative ecosystems cut productionization cost. Testable prediction: if major open-weight models reach qualified production on AMD, TPUs, Trainium, or custom XPUs progressively faster and the required engineering/validation expense declines, CUDA’s effective switching cost weakens; if demonstrations and formal support proliferate while gaps in new functionality, large-scale distributed execution, and operational reliability remain, open-weight expansion can *concentrate* execution demand on the most production-ready NVIDIA path. [G-3] [G-6] [semis #2/#14] [VLM §1 interface-control + switching-costs; §3 infrastructure overlay]

What does not matter: how many open-weight models have been released; whether a model has run once on competing silicon; how many NVIDIA accelerators have been installed. What does: whether models become recurring production workloads; whether demand is net-new, relocated, or intensity-shifted; time and total cost for alternative silicon to reach qualified production at the same quality, functionality, scale, and SLA; NVIDIA’s share of *actual* production workload and stack value captured per unit of that workload. Pretraining, post-training, and inference are different markets; frontier labs, clouds, ordinary enterprises, and sovereign buyers have different switching costs, supply constraints, and bargaining power. The most favorable NVIDIA scenario is genuinely new production tasks plus rising compute intensity while CUDA holds S and V. The least favorable is limited net-new N, existing demand migrating to competing backends, and common software layers compressing both execution share and stack premium. Failure modes Superposition names: production-conversion failure (downloads/pilots, few paid production tasks); efficiency dominating (N up, A down faster); relocation mistaken for growth; formal portability stalling before production; supply and contracts dominating technical choice; the moat keeps moving (alts cut current-generation port cost while NVIDIA changes the target). August 10, 2026 compute-financing MOUs with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR — a proposal to mobilize more than $500B of third-party capital over time, not a funded single pool — do not discuss open weights, but they are the financial translation of the same remarketing clock: when a customer or workload disappears, speed and price of placing capacity become inputs to utilization, cash flow, and residual value. Apollo’s initial $35B capital solution for the Broadcom AI XPV platform, linked to more than 1 GW of Anthropic training and inference infrastructure, foregrounds contracted cash flows. Part 2 ([[Research/2026-08-11 - NVDA Superposition AI Factory Underwriting - deep-dive]]) asks what a lender is actually underwriting: the ability to find another customer after the current one leaves, or a contract designed to keep the current customer from leaving. Part 1 leaves the unresolved tension: will growth in net-new production workloads and compute intensity outpace the decline in alternative silicon’s production-qualification cost and the compression of NVIDIA’s stack value per unit?

## Framework / Mental Model
**Two doors / two time variables.** Demand-side clock: time to place capacity with another paying workload, inside an acceptable price discount, after the current workload disappears. Silicon-side clock: time and total switching cost for an alternative backend to reach qualified production at the same quality, functionality, scale, and SLA. Execution share is the result that emerges above those processes.

**N × A × S × V** is a heuristic decomposition, not an accounting model, valuation formula, or demand forecast. Variables are not independent: lower compute prices can move both N and A; software quality can move both S and V. The formula’s job is to separate growth in AI activity from NVIDIA’s share of the resulting economics.

| Factor | Definition | What open weights do | What they do *not* do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N** | Successful *production* task classes operated repeatedly over a defined period — not downloads or one-off experiments | Lower experimentation cost; enable self-deploy under security/regulatory/economic constraints an external API cannot satisfy | Guarantee pilots convert; prevent relocation of existing API demand from being counted as new N |
| **A** | Normalized accelerator workload per successful production task (invocation frequency × compute per execution) over a defined period | Direct effects land in post-training, fine-tuning, evaluation, quantization, optimization, inference, serving. Pretraining compute of an already-released model is sunk | Guarantee more tasks ⇒ more total compute. Agentic/multimodal/long-context/synthetic-data raise A; smaller models, quantization, distillation, caching, batching, serving cut A |
| **S** | NVIDIA *execution share* of actual production workload after normalizing for model quality, functionality, throughput, latency, SLA — not installed accelerators or nominal FLOPS | Raise the likelihood that S is contested and redetermined; early in a rapidly spreading open model, demand can concentrate on NVIDIA because its production path is already the most mature | Imply automatic S decline from openness. S is governed by alt-silicon qualification time/cost, hardware supply, cloud availability, network performance, contract-renewal cycles, existing asset lives, NVIDIA release cadence |
| **V** | Economic value NVIDIA captures per normalized unit of NVIDIA-executed workload via hardware, networking, systems, and software — not market cap or stock price | If best production performance arrives first on NVIDIA libraries/systems, full-stack integration value can strengthen | Translate 1:1 into reported revenue/GP without product-mix and contract assumptions. If common compilers/serving layers conceal hardware differences and cut switching costs, part of the vendor premium migrates into standardized layers |

**Portability ladder** (weights + artifacts are the entry ticket, not the finish):

| Level | Test | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal | Model loads and produces output on another backend | Possibility | Feature completeness, performance, stability, scale |
| Technical | Required quality, functionality, scale, SLA | Production-shaped capability | That a switch is economical |
| Economic substitutability | Operator leaves the incumbent on TCO + supply + talent + risk + contracts + ecosystem durability | A real share shift | Nothing automatic from a demo or a compiler flag |

**CUDA production-cost stack** (moat = accumulated cost above code, recreated by cadence vs catch-up):

`artifacts → frameworks → compilers/graphs → kernels/runtimes → networks/clusters → validation → operating orgs → production economics`

**Two independent paths** (a cluster can be high-fungibility / low-portability, or the reverse):

| Path | Sequence |
|---|---|
| Demand-side | Execution/modification rights → more deployers and hosts → broader replacement-demand pool → changes in remarketing time and price discount after customer loss |
| Silicon-portability | Execution rights + model artifacts → third-party ports/kernels/runtimes → validation of function/performance/scale → changes in time and total cost to qualify an alt backend → changes in S at new deployment, expansion, and contract renewal |

**Title meaning.** “Market expansion” in the title is first an expansion of the *possibility set*: organizations able to self-deploy; use cases that can become production workloads; hosts able to offer the model; silicon paths capable of executing it. Net growth in accelerator consumption is tested separately as creation + relocation + intensity.

## Contradiction Check
**Supports** [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] §Key Non-consensus Insights #1–#2 (CUDA as general-purpose production stack; 100–1000× integration-cost differential vs TPU/Trainium/ROCm) and Insight #4 (open model/platform strategy as “Android” conversion of challengers into participants). Superposition supplies the missing *mechanism* those insights assert: the cost that does not travel is not kernel syntax, it is the accumulated porting + optimization + validation + operating stack, and NVIDIA’s disclosed >50% software-engineer mix plus >7.5M CUDA-tooling developers (10-K company claims; thesis also cites 5.9–6.0M at GTC 2026 — different vintage, same direction) are inputs to that cost stock, not proof every developer is equally locked. The 10-K risk line is the thesis’s own ASIC Outstanding Question restated from inside the filer: open-source foundation models on competing platforms as a demand reducer. Hypothesis to test [semis #2 design-lock-in / VLM interface-control]: CUDA remains a toll layer *if* production-qualification cost on alt silicon stays high relative to NVIDIA cadence; the source’s testable prediction is the kill switch the thesis lacks a Conviction Triggers section to hold. [G-6] [G-13]

**Challenges** the same thesis’s §Bull Case line that “open-source model proliferation *increases* infrastructure demand via Jevons” as an automatic, architecture-agnostic volume win, and the §Outstanding Question / §Bear Case collapse of efficiency vs Jevons into a single net-GPU-demand scalar. Superposition’s [G-14] decomposition says Jevons can fire on N (new self-deploy task classes that APIs could not serve) while A falls on quantization/distillation/serving and S leaks on economic substitutability — so “open weights are good for NVIDIA” is not a well-posed question. It also challenges the street binary the thesis already fights (open weights kill CUDA vs open weights are irrelevant) without licensing the bull’s stronger claim that NVIDIA “wins on volume regardless of which model company captures end-user revenue”: volume is N×A; NVIDIA’s economic outcome is N×A×S×V. The share-path debate (thesis 87%→75% maturation vs Bear 75%→60%) is exactly Superposition’s S, and the source refuses to infer S’s direction from openness alone — including the counter-intuitive early-phase concentration on NVIDIA when it is the only qualified production path. Single falsifier the source names: major open-weight models reaching qualified production on AMD / TPU / Trainium / XPU *progressively faster* with declining engineering/validation expense. That is the same surface as [[Theses/AMD - Advanced Micro Devices]] Insight #3 (ROCm as step-function via vLLM/SGLang/Triton, not an asymptotic 12–24 month lag) and Outstanding Q5 (training parity vs inference-only): formal/serving-layer support is the source’s *formal* rung; MLPerf Training submissions and Meta/OpenAI production training on ROCm would be the *technical* rung; take-or-pay conversions and renewal losses would be *economic substitutability*. [G-10] [semis #13/#14/#18]

**Cross-ticker.** [[Theses/AVGO - Broadcom]] XPU/Anthropic is the closed-weight existence proof that silicon portability does not require open weights, and the $35B Apollo XPV print is the contracted-cash-flow pole of Part 2 — complementary, not substitutable, with this note’s fungibility clock. [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] §Insight on second-cycle re-rent / DDTL covenants *is* Superposition’s demand-side fungibility question under a credit wrapper: replacement-demand pool, recontract time, and price discount after the current workload ends. Thesis has **no Conviction Triggers section**; if one is written, the source’s pair belongs there — → LOW if time-to-qualified-production on two or more non-NVIDIA backends for the top open-weight production models compresses through a full quality/scale/SLA bar; → HIGH if open-weight production N and A rise while S holds on renewal/expansion prints.

**Disconfirming check (models agreed, so invert).** [G-3] trend, [G-6] switching-cost monopoly, [semis #2] qualification-gate, and VLM infrastructure-toll all lean the same way (CUDA cost stock holds; openness expands N). That agreement is the cue to hunt the bear: OSI-grade artifact release + StableHLO/vLLM/HIP/TorchNeuron collapsing the *next* user’s cost, while NVIDIA’s own Nemotron/Cosmos releases become the optimization target competing backends compile against. Outside view: no vendor has held >80% of a $400B+ accelerator market through a customer-verticalization plus open-runtime wave; the thesis must beat that base rate with production-qualification cost, not with developer-count or letter-signatory status.
