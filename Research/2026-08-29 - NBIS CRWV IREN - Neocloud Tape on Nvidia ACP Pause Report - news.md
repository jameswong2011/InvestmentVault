# Neocloud Stocks Fall as Nvidia Shelves Revenue-Sharing Deals

## Thesis Delta
Consensus tape on Friday priced reduced Nvidia credit-enhancement and revenue-share support for neoclouds after WSJ 'shelved' / 'paused' headlines on the 1 July AI Compute Partnership, even though an Nvidia spokesperson in the same TipRanks piece said the model remains in place and continues to evolve. Transmission into Full-weight [[Theses/NBIS - Nebius Group]] (and peers [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]], [[Theses/IREN - IREN Limited]]) is equity beta to ACP credibility as a financing channel, not a cancelled offtake print or a new GW disclosure: we treat this as a sentiment and funding-optics datapoint that complements, rather than replaces, the same-day denial note. Street still prints Moderate Buy on all three names and a $77.44 IREN target (about 91% upside), so the price action is the story; the article itself does not change contracted capacity or the $5.75B convert close.

## Summary
TipRanks is a Friday-morning tape recap, not the primary WSJ or Nvidia-denial source: CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN all fell early Friday after reports that Nvidia had put on hold the revenue-sharing deals with AI cloud companies announced on 1 July, which the piece says likely triggered fears of reduced support for the industry. IREN dropped the hardest, more than 7% toward about $38, but TipRanks attributes most of that move to IREN's Q4 FY26 earnings released late Thursday (the story highlights say late Wednesday; the body says Thursday), a quarterly loss of $0.41 and revenue of $137.23 million, down 27% year on year, so the ACP headline is an overlay on an already-ugly print rather than a clean single-factor gap-down.

The deals sit under Nvidia's AI Compute Partnership. Nvidia's quarterly filing this week said commitments under the programme totalled $36 billion and typically last six years. WSJ, as relayed here, said Nvidia shelved the plan last week: the chipmaker had offered credit support to AI cloud firms that offer services on its chips, and in return took a share of product and cloud revenue, including a structure in which Nvidia would take half of revenue once sales passed a preset level, deals that appeared tailored for smaller AI cloud firms, with Sharon AI (SHAZ) and Firmus named as the first two partners. Analysts have been raising circular-financing concern (Nvidia providing credit support to the same AI firms that buy its chips), and the piece flags that CoreWeave, IREN, and Nebius are financing capacity expansion with both debt and equity while also leaning on big-tech counterparties, citing CoreWeave's March $8.5 billion syndicated loan backed by its Meta contract and the 2025 leftover-capacity purchase Nvidia agreed at an initial $6.3 billion. TipRanks notes Nvidia might rework the plan or fold it into another initiative, quotes the spokesperson that the new business model is still in place and continues to evolve, and still rates all three names Moderate Buy with IREN showing the largest Street upside at an average $77.44 target.
