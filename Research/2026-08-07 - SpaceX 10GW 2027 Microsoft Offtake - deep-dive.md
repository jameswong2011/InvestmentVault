# SpaceX 10GW in 2027 — Why It’s Real, Microsoft as Largest Offtaker

## Thesis Delta
Consensus still treats Musk’s first-earnings 6–8GW (and “well above +10GW”) 2027 add as PR theater, prices Azure as a decelerating cloud print, and reads 90-day CSA exits as a reason a hyperscaler *cannot* sign GW-scale paper → SemiAnalysis argues [[Theses/SPCX - SpaceX]] is on a credible path to **~10GW nameplate by YE2027**, that **Microsoft is the natural largest offtaker** of a 3GW / $50B-per-GW-year block because the April-2026 OpenAI rewrite dropped the old 20% revenue share and Azure can re-accelerate from ~42% to **>100%** if it captures $100M/MW-year inference, and that the **90-day cancel is what makes the deal signable for Amy Hood** (zero balance-sheet risk), not what blocks it. Distinct from [[Research/2026-08-10 - SPCX PhotonCap First Earnings 10GW Hardware Chain - deep-dive]] (Q2 print → hardware-chain value) and [[Research/2026-08-05 - SPCX Shanaka Capital Recovery Clock - deep-dive]] (duration vs D&A): this piece is the *why-the-GW-count-is-buildable / why-Azure-clears-the-3GW* argument — [G-13] priced variable is vapor-vs-real not duration; [G-4] $300–500B 2027 capex as Perez frenzy; [G-7] <1-year payback at $30–50M/MW-year; Semis #1 near-term large-scale compute; VLM §2 campus still a silicon/turbine **layer-renter**.

## Summary
Dylan Patel / SemiAnalysis open on the first SpaceX earnings shock: Musk “conservatively” aims to **build and deliver an incremental 6–8GW in 2027**, with upside “well above +10GW.” At **$50B per GW** that is **$300–500B of 2027 capex**, sized against what the authors expect from AWS and Google — an “unbelievable” number for a firm they describe as far less profitable than rival hyperscalers. Their claim is that the number is *operationally* real: they have already scored every site they judge suitable for SpaceX (full list in the Datacenter Model) and the quarter-by-quarter availability of gas-generation kit across **30+ turbine, engine, and fuel-cell suppliers** (Energy Model), and they say they published that equipment book before the market treated it as live. The piece is a feasibility-plus-offtake note, not a Q2 walk-through.

The economic engine underneath the GW count is dollar-to-watt math, not launch cadence. Large-scale *and* near-term compute is treated as a scarce combo priced at a premium of **up to $50B/GW/year**. The Tokenomics Model plus Inference Simulator, run at realistic tokens/sec per GPU on a GB300 cluster, says both OpenAI and Anthropic can generate **>$100B/GW/year** of API-inference revenue — more than a year of GB300 rental at current neocloud prices. Cost side is pinned at **~$12B/GW/year** using a conservative **$3/GPU-hr** rental, with token production taken from a frontier-class architecture plus AgentX (InferenceX), which blends input / cache-read / cache-write / output tokens at the authors’ production-coding-trace ratios. Serving frontier tokens is framed as an extremely high-margin business that sophisticated investors have only just accepted as consensus: SemiAnalysis says it called inference gross margins **>60%** to Tokenomics subscribers in January, then in June put **Opus 4.8 at 85%+** (now the default Anthropic cite), with a leaked DeepSeek investor call (10-month GPU payback) used as a ballpark check. The authors themselves flag the remaining hole: a single firm-wide inference GM is the wrong object; the object is GM for every (model, accelerator) pair along the throughput-vs-latency Pareto (Opus 5 Fast on Trainium3 vs Fable 5 on TPUv7). The Fable 5 GB200-vs-GB300 teaser table is referenced and **not present in the archived body**.

Microsoft is introduced as the **third** entity on earth that can print those economics per GW: full access to OpenAI models, same revenue and margin per MW, **none of the training cost**. Satya “nailed” the April 2026 rewrite that **dropped the old 20% OpenAI revenue share**. That, plus Codex demand and OpenAI ARR acceleration, is the incentive to procure as many MW as possible as fast as possible. SemiAnalysis says much of Microsoft’s current datacenter capacity still goes to OpenAI at **~$14M/MW/year**, and Microsoft has room to improve that mix toward Foundry/Copilot — the highest revenue-per-MW surfaces, which the October 2025 **$250B / ~7GW** OpenAI IaaS deal (Tokenomics estimate) starved. The once-in-a-generation print they underwrite is **Azure revenue growth from ~42% to >100% next year**, and SpaceX is “incredibly well positioned to serve” the near-term hole. A 3GW SpaceX block at **$50B/GW/year** is admitted to “sound insane” and then defended on two facts: (1) Microsoft has already signed **10GW / >$300B TCV** of binding contracts year-to-date (leasing + neocloud + self-build starts + PPAs/ESAs) — *but those contribute late-2027 and 2028 capacity, so a near-term gap remains*; (2) a **90-day cancellation**, matching the Anthropic and Google SpaceX deals, is **zero balance-sheet risk** and therefore easy for Amy Hood given the revenue opportunity. The authors’ Datacenter Model history is used as credibility: they called Microsoft’s leasing **pause in December 2024** before it was consensus; the same tracker now shows the “awakening.”

SpaceX financing is the other half of “why 10GW is payable,” not just buildable. Two items: **Nvidia vendor financing** that cuts upfront cash — offered as the reason Elon declared **Nvidia-exclusive** on the earnings call after the Accelerator Model had tracked xAI/SpaceX actively evaluating **TPU and AMD** — and **operating-cash-flow financing** from industry-high pricing enabled by the fastest timelines. SpaceX will keep selling large-scale compute on **3–5 month lead times** at **$30–50M/MW/year**, which “pays back the capex in less than a year” (cross-ref their Meta Compute piece). That stack produces a path to **$300B ARR by YE2027**, on the explicit assumption that **only 50% of 2027 incremental compute is monetized** and the rest is reserved for Grok and Cursor training with **no inference revenue modelled**. The title’s **$500B ARR** is the un-haircut headline (10GW × $50B/GW/year arithmetic); the body number is the 50% case. Elon’s commercial move, in the Meta Compute framing they reuse, is **value-based pricing** of GPU clusters against surged lab margins, not cost-plus.

The construction argument is a *different playbook*, not a claim that SpaceX builds *better* shells. Speed citations: **122 days to Colossus 1’s 300MW**; **six months to 200MW at Colossus 2**; an onsite generation plant **1 km across a border** to dodge permitting; Southaven expanding from **27 turbines / ~495MW (Feb 2026) to 69 turbines / 1.7GW (July 2026)**; “MiniHard” vertical construction in March 2026, **450–500MW in ~5 months**. Colossus 1 is still scored as the largest fully operational single-coherent cluster (~200,000 H100/H200 plus ~30,000 GB200 NVL72), Google excepted. The constraint-bypass list is the load-bearing mechanism: switchgear and large power transformers sold out two years → **buy Chinese power modules** and **skip LPTs** by feeding medium voltage from on-site gen into widely available low-voltage transformers; GEV turbines on **5yr+ backlog** → use the Energy Model’s **30+ other gas-gen makers** that already have large datacenter orders, plus a **surging secondary market** (every turbine originally slated for **Oracle’s New Mexico** site is for sale; prices are high and “Elon can pay”); labor as the last constraint → parallelize, cut commissioning, preassemble, with Colossus 2 peak daily labor ~**3,000** workers, stated as well below other GW-scale sites (source phrasing garbled). Most operators optimize for efficiency/quality because that is how one wins a **15–20 year take-or-pay** hyperscaler contract. SpaceX optimizes **speed above all**: a **500MW cluster in three months with a 90-day cancel** is framed as one of the scarcest assets on earth while token margins stay extreme, and “typical quality metrics don’t matter.” The proof offered is that **Google — “the most vertically integrated infra company in the world” — signed with SpaceX**. Retrofit remains on the table (Colossus 1/2); 2027 is also the first true greenfield cycle, so the authors expect the next shells to be faster. Building **10+GW in a single year** still requires scouting land with easy permitting and gas; they judge the option set large enough and say the ramp “will, naturally, extensively rely on onsite gas generation.”

Site color in the archived body is a sample, not the full Datacenter Model book. Two warehouse retrofits surface via **liens against MZX (Elon)** showing **Darana Hybrid** electrical/mechanical work: an **863k sqft Olive Branch, Mississippi** building owned by **ElmTree Funds (BlackRock-owned)**, the same landlord that sold the Macrohard facility to MZX; and a **474k sqft Southaven, Mississippi** building, listed but not ElmTree-owned, sized by the authors at **~1GW or more**. After Elon’s “quiet **APR** acquisition,” **Pampa** is scored as a **2GW onsite-generation** candidate: Kinder Morgan’s **NGPL** interstate mainline sits about a mile away, and the just-announced, fully subscribed **Panhandle expansion** (cited to datacenter demand) is “the most likely firm-supply route” for the permanent plant. **No documents confirm Pampa.** The archived inbox file ends there; the complete site catalog is not in this clip. SpaceX YE2026 compute is conceded as **“only” 2GW** — the 10GW question is 2027, not this year.

## Framework / Mental Model
SemiAnalysis runs a named **dollar-to-watt / Tokenomics** stack rather than a DCF. **Tokenomics Model** converts MW of a named accelerator into $ of inference (and, separately, into the OpenAI IaaS GW equivalent — the $250B October 2025 deal is mapped to **~7GW**). **AI Cloud TCO** is the cost leg. **Inference Simulator** is the revenue leg that used to be “unknowable”: a roofline plus a realistic per-operation timing model that executes a real workload trace on simulated silicon, validated across accelerators, and used to forecast unbuilt parts from design specs. **AgentX** (inside InferenceX) supplies the workload shape — >$1M of the authors’ own agentic coding traces, forced to first-party interactivity and TTFT. Output is not one GM number; it is a (model × accelerator × serving-config) surface along the throughput–latency Pareto. Complementary ops models cited as already-built books, not derived here: **Datacenter Model** (quarterly leasing, neocloud contracts, self-build starts, binding PPAs/ESAs), **Energy Model** (30+ turbine/engine/fuel-cell suppliers, kit available by quarter), **Accelerator Model** (xAI/SpaceX TPU and AMD evaluations before the Nvidia-exclusive declaration).

Two named commercial typologies sit on top of that stack. **Value-based vs cost-plus cluster pricing**: once lab inference GM cleared 60–85%, Elon stopped selling compute as a marked-up BOM and started selling it against the buyer’s $100B/GW-year revenue. **Speed-vs-quality construction**: the 15–20-year take-or-pay industry optimizes PUE, LPT pedigree, and commissioning completeness; SpaceX optimizes months-to-energize and will take Chinese power modules, skipped LPTs, secondary-market turbines, and a 90-day cancel to do it. The 90-day clause is classified here as a *product feature that clears the offtaker’s balance sheet*, not as a credit defect — the opposite typology from [[Theses/SPCX - SpaceX]] §Key Non-consensus Insights (capital-recovery clock) and from [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] multi-year take-or-pay.

## Contradiction Check
**Supports the GW-count half of [[Theses/SPCX - SpaceX]] §Bull Case and the YE26 2GW floor inside §Conviction Triggers → HIGH** (source independently writes YE26 compute as “only” 2GW and puts the 10GW fight in 2027). **Challenges §Key Non-consensus Insight #2 (demand printed, capital recovery not) and #3 (Musk-Starlink-playbook transfer / MSFT 3GW unverified) on mechanism, not on disclosure.** SemiAnalysis treats the **same 90-day clause** the thesis uses as the duration bear — and that Shanaka clocks against GPU/turbine D&A — as the *feature* that lets Amy Hood sign 3GW at $50B/GW-year with “zero balance sheet risk.” That is a typology collision, not a new fact: the source does **not** print a named, long-duration Microsoft CSA, and it never claims the 3GW block is signed. It argues it is *rational and signable*. [[Theses/SPCX - SpaceX]] §Outstanding Questions (“Is a 90-day CSA an Azure substitute at GW scale?”) and the **→ HIGH** requirement for “CSA duration/payback prints (matched cohort or long-duration non-cancellable amendment)” therefore remain the live test. A 3GW Microsoft headline on 90-day paper would **confirm this source and still fail the thesis HIGH trigger**. **→ LOW** (YE26 <2GW same-definition, or first CSA termination cascade) is the source’s own unstated kill: if 2026 cannot clear 2GW, the 2027 10GW playbook is a story; if Anthropic/Google exercise the 90-day the authors advertise, the “zero BS risk” feature is the tenant’s option, not SpaceX’s.

**Challenges §Summary’s line that MS $300 “on the SemiAnalysis 10GW / MSFT-largest-offtaker path” is already priced as if proven.** This source *is* that path: $300B ARR at 50% monetization, Microsoft as largest offtaker, Nvidia-financed exclusive silicon. The vault’s variant — Connectivity is the printed annuity, AI is a cancellable renter pool, printed tenants are Anthropic/Google/Reflection — is not rebutted by site liens and a Datacenter Model 10GW Microsoft *commitment* book that the authors themselves date to **late-2027/2028**. [G-13] the operating variable this piece wants re-priced is **2027 energization + Azure MW mix**; the thesis says the variable is **CSA duration**. Both can be true; they are not the same trade. [G-10] outside view: one-year 8–10GW greenfield+retrofit against a 2GW 2026 base is an extreme outlier versus the datacenter reference class; the source’s answer is the constraint-bypass playbook, not a base-rate argument. [G-14] Jevons is used on the *token* side ($100B/GW-year as cost-per-token falls) and is **not** a claim that SpaceX owns the scarce complement the way Falcon owns launch — VLM §2 / thesis Insight #3 still read the AI campus as a **layer-renter** on [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] silicon, HBM, optics, and turbines. Automation §2 Lens B / §6 semis overlay: selling GW of GB300 hours is a compute-adjacency, not ownership of the decision-context layer; Microsoft’s claimed edge here is *model access without training cost*, which is a contract, not an operator-readiness story.

**[[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] §Summary (no Conviction Triggers section exists).** Supports the hardware-demand / “new hyperscaler-class buyer” leg: 6–10GW of 2027 incremental GB300/Rubin-class clusters, Nvidia-exclusive by *financing* rather than by a completed technical bake-off (TPU and AMD were evaluated). That is a different CUDA-moat story than the thesis’s software/Physical-AI stack — vendor financing as the closer is [G-8] capital-allocation / [G-7] ROIIC on Nvidia’s book, and it **weakens** any read that the exclusive declaration was a technical rejection of ASICs. Semis #10: SpaceX as an incremental anchor is bullish units and **raises** customer-concentration on a single well-financed but cancellable neocloud. Semis #1/#8: if 2027 GW actually energizes, the binding constraint moves from “is there a sixth hyperscaler” to CoWoS/HBM/power, which is [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] §Insight #1 (CoWoS annuity) demand, not a TSMC-specific claim in this source.

**[[Theses/BE - Bloom Energy]] §Insight #1 (time-to-power arbitrage) and → HIGH (turbine backlogs stay >4 years).** Energy Model’s “30+ turbine, engine, **fuel-cell** suppliers” plus “GEV are 5yr+ backlogged but there is plenty of other capacity if you will work with new names” is double-edged. It supports the *existence* of a non-GEV kit market Bloom sells into. It **challenges** treating the GEV/Siemens backlog as a sealed 2028–2030 annuity: secondary-market Oracle-NM turbines and a long-tail of other OEMs are exactly the supply response that compresses Bloom’s wasting-asset window. SpaceX “can pay” high secondary prices is a bid, not a SOFC design-win.

**[[Theses/VRT - Vertiv Holdings]] §physics-limited moat / 15–20-year quality contract.** The source’s explicit claim that “typical quality metrics don’t matter” and that Chinese power modules + skipped LPTs + 90-day paper beat a 15–20-year take-or-pay shell is a **playbook competitor** to Vertiv’s integration premium. It does not name Vertiv. It does say the industry that buys Vertiv-class plants is optimizing a different objective function than SpaceX. If that speed playbook stays SpaceX-only, VRT is untouched; if it proliferates, the OCP/15–20-year quality rent is the casualty. Semis #18: do not read one firm’s speed exception as a structural end to quality-gated plants.

**[[Theses/ORCL - Oracle Corporation]] §Insight #2 (RPO quality / Project Jupiter).** Oracle NM turbines “all on the market” is a physical tell that at least one Oracle site’s generation kit was released — consistent with a build that slipped, resized, or lost its gas path (New Mexico permitting is already a Bloom/Oracle live issue), not proof OCI demand died. Treat as a kit-market datapoint, not an RPO verdict.

**[[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] §Summary (multi-year take-or-pay vs levered derivative).** SpaceX inverts the category on both tails in the way the SPCX thesis already states: shortest paper, fastest MW, highest $/MW, strongest ability to pay secondary-market kit. This source adds that **Google and (hypothesized) Microsoft will buy that product** — i.e. the duration discount is the *reason* the hyperscaler can sign, which is the opposite of CRWV’s IG-backlog product. A world where 90-day GW-scale paper becomes the clearing contract is a **rate/renewal** problem for CRWV’s 5–7 year DSCR math, not a volume problem.

Disconfirming check (READING PROTOCOL): SemiAnalysis, the SPCX bull tape, and MS $300 all point the same way (10GW real, Microsoft offtakes, Nvidia exclusive). That agreement is the cue to hunt the single falsifier, not to raise conviction. The source’s own holes: 3GW Microsoft is argued, not shown; Pampa is unconfirmed; the full site list is outside this clip; 2026 is only 2GW; 50% of 2027 MW is reserved for internal training with $0 inference; payback <1 year assumes $30–50M/MW-year holds after the first 90-day anniversary. [G-3] mis-applying trend continuation to a cancellable rental book is the expensive error if token margins mean-revert and the speed premium collapses.
