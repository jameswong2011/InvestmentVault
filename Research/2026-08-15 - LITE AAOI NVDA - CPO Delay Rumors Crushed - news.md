## Summary
Hassan Mujtaba (Wccftech Senior Editor, hardware; dated on-page as the CPO-partners story of 14 August 2026) writes that NVIDIA’s co-packaged-optics partners have said first-server-solution deployment remains on track. The rumor mill had CPO adoption “delayed for years”; on recent investor calls both Lumentum and Coherent stated that CPO production remains on track. The piece is a secondary recap, not a transcript: it does not print LITE Q4 FY26 $1,006.3M / Q1 FY27 $1.225–$1.275B, Coherent OCS TAM, ELS-module PO dollars, or yield/WPE plots. Those live in the 11–13 August vault notes. Do not treat this clip as independent confirmation of call-only figures that are not on this page.

Lumentum’s named color: demand for ultra-high-power CPO lasers is gaining momentum; in addition to CPO, several NPO engagements are “underway”; volume shipments for scale-up commence in the second half of 2027; first customer deployments are expected in 2028. That is the same lead-customer calendar Damnang attributed to the 11 August Lumentum call (HV scale-up 2H 2027 / deploy 2028; initial ELS-module order; NPO engagements live). This page does **not** restate the ELS-module purchase order, the $1.01B / $1.25B guide, OCS >$400M backlog, or Cloud Light 1.6T mix.

Coherent’s named color (investors call “this week”): scale-out CPO revenue begins in the second half of 2026 and is explicitly tagged rack-to-rack; scale-up CPO revenue is expected to follow in 2027; Coherent is engaged with multiple customers on CPO and NPO offerings. Damnang’s 12 August Coherent recap already had “no CPO demand push-out,” the same 2H26 / 2027 split, and multi-customer CPO+NPO. This page does **not** restate Coherent’s “no push-out” quote, the >$4B OCS market call, or Texas-fab CHIPS dollars.

Both companies are framed as having one major client: NVIDIA. In March, NVIDIA announced a multi-year “strategic partnership” with Lumentum Holdings to accelerate optics technologies and a $2 billion investment in Lumentum to support R&D, increased capacity, and operations as Lumentum expands its US manufacturing landscape with a brand-new fab. Wccftech reprints the partnership rationale in full: optical interconnect technology and package integration are critical for continued scaling of AI factories, improving energy efficiency and resiliency of large-scale AI networks; the expanded collaboration draws on NVIDIA’s leadership in AI, accelerated computing and networking and Lumentum’s leadership in optics and advanced manufacturing; the investment enables Lumentum to scale manufacturing capacity and R&D for future AI data centers. That $2B / Greensboro-US-fab lock-out is already the LITE thesis non-consensus (capacity lock-out mirroring HBM/packaging monopolization; mandate to build a new US fab). This page does not name Greensboro, convertible-preferred structure, or purchase-commitment terms.

Coherent “recently broke ground” on its new Texas plant, “where the world’s first 6-inch indium phosphide fab will be built for volume production.” InP wafers are described as the fundamental technology that enables transfer of data between chips, servers, and data centers at the speed of light through optical networks. Vault already has Coherent’s 6-inch InP platform as launched across four facilities in August 2025 (IQE / LITE industry context) and NVIDIA breaking ground *with* Coherent on a $2B CHIPS-backed Texas laser fab (2026-06-18, LITE Mental Models). Treat “world’s first 6-inch InP fab” as Wccftech phrasing, not a new first-of-kind IR claim — the vault’s August 2025 four-facility launch and the June 2026 Texas groundbreaking are the dated primary events. NVIDIA also invested $2 billion in Coherent in March to develop optics technologies to scale next-gen data center architectures (the dual-supplier $2B+$2B hedge already in LITE Risk #5 / IQE industry context).

The copper-break mechanism is the page’s incremental architecture sentence. NVIDIA stated that Vera Rubin NVL576 servers will connect eight NVLink racks of 72 Rubin Ultra GPUs into a singular 576-GPU domain. That server “will have to break past the traditional copper networking solution to carry signals across vast distances, and that’s where CPO comes into play.” An on-page NVIDIA slide is described as showing Blackwell, Rubin, and Feynman hardware architectures with NVLink models and CPUs for 2024, 2026, and 2028. This is the same NVL576 domain already in [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] §Computer 1 (NVL72 rack-scale, NVL576 multi-rack) and the 8-Hi HBM4 despec note that relocates the binding constraint from local HBM density to NVL576 interconnect; the new clause is the explicit “break past copper / vast distances ⇒ CPO” coupling. It sits next to — and does not replace — the 13 August OCP APAC note ([[Research/2026-08-13 - NVDA LITE AAOI TSM - Nvidia Spectrum-6 CPO Shipping - news]]): Shainer already has Spectrum-6 CPO in production and shipping to CoreWeave / Lambda / Meta / Microsoft / OCI, copper kept on NVLink72 / NVLink144 if dimensions allow, and CPO required for multi-rack (NVLink 1152) beyond ~25 cm at 448G PAM4. This Wccftech clip does not name Spectrum-6, COUPE, first-adopter roster, ASE test-ecosystem gap, or Bechtolsheim’s 18-month / 49.6T pluggable ceiling.

Competitive follow-on: “others are also following suit.” AMD is expected to tap GlobalFoundries to leverage Co-Packaged Optics on next-gen AI servers featuring Instinct MI500 GPUs. The same MI500 GPUs are positioned against Rubin Ultra, with both coming out in the second half of 2027. NVIDIA’s Feynman GPUs are expected to go one step ahead of Rubin Ultra on the CPO front when they arrive in 2028. GFS+MI500 CPO is not in the 13 August LITE/COHR/Spectrum-6 notes; TSMC COUPE risk production with AMD (February 2026) is already in LITE / IQE / AAOI industry context — do not collapse GFS-for-MI500 into COUPE-with-AMD. AVGO Bailly / Tomahawk-6 Davisson is not named on this page.

Wccftech’s close restates the calendar: recent earnings comments from Lumentum and Coherent have swept aside delay rumors; ultra-high-power CPO laser demand is rising; scale-out revenue starts in H2 2026; scale-up volumes follow in 2027; customer deployments are expected in 2028; multi-billion-dollar NVIDIA investments accelerate capacity and R&D at both suppliers; CPO is set to power the next generation of AI factories, from Vera Rubin onward, “right on schedule.” Page chrome includes a related-story box on the FCC mulling a ban on China-sourced optical transceivers (Innolight 27% global share; Coherent and Lumentum “prepare to pounce”) — already vaulted as [[Research/2026-08-11 - LITE PhotonCap Innolight FCC Ban Optics Chain - deep-dive]] / [[Research/2026-08-05 - AXTI LITE IA Chinese Transceiver Ban Memo - deep-dive]]; not a new print here.
