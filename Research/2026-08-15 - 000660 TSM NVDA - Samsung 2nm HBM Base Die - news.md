## Summary
Ramish Zafar (Wccftech, more than a decade covering semiconductor fabrication; bachelor's in Finance and a micromasters in supply-chain management from MIT) reports that Korean chip giant Samsung might repurpose a production line at an under-construction research and development facility to manufacture base logic dies for its HBM memory-chip production. The report comes courtesy of industry sources quoted by ZDNet, and it adds that since the facility's opening is roughly scheduled in two years, the plans are subject to revision.

The design of HBM memory chips, used primarily in AI computing applications, requires multiple DRAM memory chips stacked on top of each other. As the performance requirements of computing infrastructure grow, the design of these chips is also evolving. Along with the number of DRAM layers stacked on top of each other, another key factor that is evolving is the base die on the HBM memory chips.

The first update to the base die occurred when manufacturers advanced to HBM3 and HBM3E memory chips. This upgrade saw manufacturers jump to IC foundry node processes for the base die for the first time as data rates and pin speeds increased significantly from the previous generation of memory chips. As a result, the base die started to rely on logic nodes ranging from 20-nanometers to 12-nanometers.

The next shift occurred with the HBM4 memory chips that are currently used in the latest AI products, such as NVIDIA's Rubin chips. It was necessitated by high data rates achieved by HBM3E memory and wider buses and more through-silicon vias (TSVs). These shifts saw foundries, such as TSMC and Samsung Foundry, take over the role of manufacturing the HBM chip base die.

A report from ZDNet claims that Samsung is interested in repurposing a production line at its under-construction research and development facilities in Korea's Giheung industrial district. This line can be used to manufacture HBM base dies through the 2-nanometer chip manufacturing technology. The base dies might be destined for NVIDIA's AI chips, and the line that might be used for this purpose is the NRD-K Line 2.

Earlier this year, Samsung announced that it would use the 4-nanometer manufacturing technology for the HBM4 memory chips. Along with Samsung, SK hynix will also work with a foundry for its HBM chips, as the firm has teamed up with TSMC. TSMC offers both the mature N12 node and the advanced N5 node as options for building HBM memory chips. The third player in the memory industry, Micron, has also partnered up with TSMC for its HBM4 memory chips.

**ZDNet Korea primary (장경윤, 2026-08-14; Wccftech's cited source — keep the facts Wccftech compressed).** Samsung Electronics will change the use of the next-generation semiconductor R&D complex it is building at Giheung. As of 14 August the line has settled on being used to expand foundry production capacity. Investment as a DRAM mass-production line was also discussed; that plan was recently revised. The mass-production product being targeted is a 2-nanometer base (logic) die for next-generation HBM. The read is a preemptive capacity grab as HBM supply expands under NVIDIA and other core customers.

Industry sources say Samsung is pursuing a plan to use NRD-K Line 2, under construction at Giheung, as a foundry line rather than R&D. NRD-K is Samsung's most advanced integrated R&D complex, built to seize future semiconductor technology. The company plans to invest about KRW 20 trillion by 2030 to build three lines; the first line was completed at the end of 2024. Preparations for NRD-K Line 2 began this year. The old Comprehensive Technology Institute (종합기술원) building on the Giheung campus is being demolished, and site preparation / foundation work is underway.

The use of NRD-K Line 2 is highly likely to change substantially from the original plan. Samsung is recently pursuing using the line as a foundry **send-fab** (센드팹): an auxiliary fab that receives wafers from other mass-production lines and performs specific process steps rather than the entire flow. The main mass-production product under review is a 2nm base die for HBM to be supplied to NVIDIA. The base die sits under the vertically stacked DRAM core dies and supports data transfer between HBM and system semiconductors such as GPUs; it is one of the factors that determines HBM performance.

HBM demand is expected to keep expanding on aggressive AI-infrastructure investment by global big tech. Samsung in particular plans to apply the industry's most advanced 2nm process to future commercial custom HBM and to HBM5 (8th-generation HBM) to secure a performance advantage versus competitors, and to use the new Giheung fab to lock related capacity quickly.

A person familiar with the matter said Samsung is building NRD-K Line 2 with a second-half 2028 opening target and has settled on using it as a foundry fab, and that because NRD-K Line 2's fab size is relatively small it will contribute to leading-edge capacity expansion via the send-fab method. Industry view: with about two years until fab open, the use of NRD-K Line 2 could change again.

Another source said the plan is internally at a near-finalized level, but actual equipment purchase orders (POs) still need more time, and that because semiconductor market conditions can change at any time, the specific investment direction and scale could still be revised.

In fact, through last month Samsung also reviewed using NRD-K Line 2 as a DRAM send-fab, and the Hwaseong memory division had drafted a related investment plan. After Samsung revised the plan this month, DRAM mass production is more likely to be concentrated at the Pyeongtaek campus.

**TrendForce same-day recap (cites ZDNet, Seoul Economic Daily, ETNews).** Samsung is weighing a shift for the new Giheung facility ahead of growing NVIDIA-driven HBM demand; NRD-K Line 2 would primarily target mass production of 2nm base (logic) dies for next-generation HBM; Samsung plans to use its 2nm process for future custom HBM and HBM5. NRD-K Line 2 targeted to open 2H28; relatively small, so send-fab. Seoul Economic Daily: Samsung unveiled HBM5 for the first time in June. ETNews: HBM5 base dies will use Samsung's GAA-based 2nm process, as the new generation is expected to require over 50% higher operating speeds than HBM4E. With around two years before open, role could change again; plan close to finalized internally; equipment POs not yet placed. NRD-K: ~KRW 20 trillion, three lines by 2030, first completed late 2024; Line 2 preparations began this year; initial construction including site preparation now underway.
