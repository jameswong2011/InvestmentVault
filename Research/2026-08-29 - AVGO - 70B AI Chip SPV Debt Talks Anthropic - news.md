# Broadcom (AVGO) Nears $70B Debt

## Thesis Delta
Consensus still prices [[Theses/AVGO - Broadcom]] as a custom-ASIC plus Ethernet cash-compounder whose $73B AI backlog and >$100B 2027 chip target are customer-funded offtake, not vendor-financed demand. This Yahoo Finance clip of the 21 August 2026 CNBC story says Broadcom is negotiating $70–80B of special-purpose-vehicle debt (Bloomberg path up to ~$100B; senior ~$45B / junior ~$35B, fluid) so a vehicle can buy custom chips and lease them to labs including Anthropic, extending the June $35B Apollo/Blackstone package that sat under a 20 GW by 2028 platform for Anthropic and OpenAI, with Broadcom guaranteeing part of the senior paper. That is T5 vendor-support demand enablement ([G-4] frenzy-phase financial capital funding the substrate; Industry #10 Anthropic as the named offtaker), not a new XPU win, and it adds contingent leverage on the same customer seat the thesis already treats as a concentration test. No conviction change.

## Summary
CNBC's David Faber confirmed Thursday 21 August 2026 that Broadcom is in talks to raise $70 billion to $80 billion of debt for a chip-financing deal aimed at AI companies including Anthropic, with the senior tranche (paid back first) around $45 billion and the junior around $35 billion, numbers the sources called fluid; Bloomberg the same day had floated a total package toward $100 billion and named Blackstone and Apollo among participants. Yahoo's Insider Monkey wrap of that CNBC story fills the mechanism the two-paragraph wire leaves implicit: a special-purpose vehicle, not Broadcom's corporate balance sheet, would raise the debt and lease the chips back to customers, so the growth capital stays off Broadcom's books in its rawest form while Broadcom still 'guarantees part of that debt', which CNBC's Kristina Partsinevelos said is 'where the risk sits' if Anthropic or another financed lab underperforms. The raise extends the June partnership in which Broadcom, Apollo and Blackstone committed $35 billion to expand Anthropic infrastructure on Broadcom custom chips, the same June announcement that described a new AI platform designed to enable 20 gigawatts of compute for Anthropic and OpenAI, and the Yahoo piece treats this expansion as a step toward that 20 GW by 2028 target rather than a one-off transaction.

Chipmakers are tapping debt markets at historic scale to fund the buildout, and CNBC placed the Broadcom talks next to two Nvidia prints from the same week: up to $105 billion to finance a new OpenAI data centre in Ohio (securities filing) and, a week earlier, a partnership with six large asset managers on a $500 billion financing push designed to treat compute infrastructure as a new asset class. Yahoo's bear case then scales the guarantee pattern: Bank of America estimates guarantee-related exposure across this kind of AI financing could reach $370 billion by 2029, Nvidia is pursuing a similar structure above $500 billion, and if demand slows the guarantees could be tested together; the reported ticket itself has already grown from over $60 billion to $70–80 billion to as much as $100 billion, which the piece reads as execution risk if credit markets tighten before close. Broadcom designs custom chips for Alphabet, Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI; shares rose about 1% on the news (Yahoo) and a little more than 1% on Friday (CNBC). Terms are not final.
