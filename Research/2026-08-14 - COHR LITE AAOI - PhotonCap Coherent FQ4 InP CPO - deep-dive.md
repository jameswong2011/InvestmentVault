## Summary
PhotonCap's 2026-08-14 post-call note (published two days after Coherent's FQ4'26 release, quarter ended 2026-06-30, after the close on August 12) is not a business description and is not a beat-celebration. The author's lead is that $2.05B revenue landed on the exact top of the $1.91–2.05B range given three months earlier, non-GAAP EPS of $1.74 cleared the $1.72 top end, and FQ1'27 guides $2.2–2.4B (~+12% QoQ at the midpoint) — and the stock still closed the first post-earnings session down 8% at $327.23 after having rallied nearly 9% to ~$356 in the regular session on the 12th, giving the entire pre-release move back [1×: COHR IR / PhotonCap; web: stockanalysis.com via PhotonCap]. FY2026 printed $7.12B (+22.5%); non-GAAP gross margin 40.2%; EPS +74% year over year. Datacenter & Communications did $1.6B-plus in Q4, up close to 60% year over year and now ~80% of the company; Industrial slipped to the $430M level. The AI data-center side is carrying the whole P&L [1×: COHR IR / PhotonCap]. PhotonCap's three candidates for the reversal: expectations already loaded into a name trading close to four times its 52-week low; the cash burden of the buildout; or a bar that had formed above the guide. The second candidate is checkable against CapEx and cash-flow later in the piece.

The public takeaway was one word, InP. PhotonCap's claim is that the load-bearing news sits between the lines: where the bottleneck has moved, what six-inch does to margins, and what the company is worried about instead of demand. On the call Coherent stated flatly that transceiver assembly and test capacity are not constraints today — the biggest constraint is InP production [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. That line made the author pause. PhotonCap has long treated PIC test as the single biggest bottleneck in optical devices (and holds the related test names); the author's own [PC101] Lecture 4 covers why PIC test is harder than EIC test. The read offered here is not that test stopped mattering — it is that the InP bind is severe enough for management to name production, not test. June-quarter InP laser output grew ~80% year over year, and the company's own 800G and 1.6T transceiver demand is absorbing all of it: everything made goes into its own modules [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Counting transceiver units therefore understates scarcity; the visible layer is EMLs, CW lasers, and below them InP wafer and substrate capacity. The Lumentum call the day before pointed the same direction: EMLs still shipping behind demand, and on high-power lasers supply fell further behind as demand accelerated ([[Research/2026-08-12 - LITE PhotonCap FQ4 2026 First 1B Quarter - deep-dive]], [[Theses/LITE - Lumentum]]). Two reports one day apart, same upstream layer. Expanding fab capacity and securing usable InP substrate remain two different problems ([[Research/2026-08-02 - AXTI LITE InP Substrate LTA Prepay Chain - deep-dive]]).

Six-inch is the supply-side mechanism. The CFO: "Four times, but it's at half the cost" — a six-inch wafer yields about four times as many devices as three-inch at around half the cost per device, and yields for EMLs, CW lasers, and photodiodes are already higher on six-inch than on three-inch [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. The normal large-diameter sequence is lose yield first; this line says they are past that stage. As InP capacity grows, transceiver shipments can rise while the cost of the most critical internal component falls. PhotonCap had flagged this as a core monitor in the May Q3 piece ($20B+ New SAM / +27% pro forma); this call made the six-inch margin thesis sharper. In this cycle, volume growth and margin expansion do not have to move in opposite directions.

The demand-side lines are what PhotonCap thinks the −8% missed. "We have seen absolutely no push-out of CPO demand" — demand increased, some customer requests pulled in — while NPO engagement grew sharply over the past three to six months [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. The sentence the author marks as the most important in the section: "The amount of content that we would have in an NPO application versus a CPO is very comparable, very similar." For Coherent, NPO and CPO are two form factors, not two investment theses. PhotonLink bundles the laser, the external laser module, isolators, polarization-maintaining fiber, fiber attachment, and the SiPh PIC into one integrated optics platform; whether the optical attach point sits next to the package or on the board, the optical content is similar, and the company said so. PhotonCap's prior architecture note (DSP / LPO / NPO / CPO as a staged optical transition) is the frame: even if CPO takes longer, LPO and NPO fill the gap. The question that matters for Coherent is how much of scale-up converts from copper to optics, not which acronym wins.

OCS is held at more than $4B of opportunity — not a new number this call, the scale the company has been laying out through the year [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. What is widening is the application area, from existing scale-out toward DCI and other network domains. Lumentum doubled OCS shipments quarter over quarter and guided its first $100M-plus OCS quarter [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap] — shipment actuals versus an opportunity estimate, different kinds of figures, same layer. PhotonCap's read: optical switching is spreading across multiple layers of the AI network hierarchy. CPO/NPO turns electrical links into optical links; OCS reconfigures the optical fabric those links create, rather than adding another electronic switch. Same direction: more network functions moving into the optical domain.

Communications revenue grew 56% year over year in Q4. Products management singled out: ZR/ZR+ transceivers, pump lasers, high-end optical subsystems; Multi-Rail has sampled to multiple customers with a revenue ramp expected in the first half of 2027, and the company explicitly connected Multi-Rail to the "scale-across AI networking" opportunity [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Coherent does not monetize scale-across through a single component — transceivers, pump lasers, amplifiers and subsystems, and higher-level transport. PhotonCap's prior piece framed scale-across as coherent DCI, not CPO. The Lumentum call's scale anecdote is reused here: one large hyperscaler's network capacity connecting two AI data centers is twice the entire global backbone that company deployed over the past decade [1×: LITE FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Once clusters spread across buildings and campuses, optical content is no longer a transceiver-count problem — pump lasers, amplifiers, coherent DSPs, transceivers, subsystems, and fiber travel with it.

The single strongest line on the call, per PhotonCap: "Fiscal 2027 is basically completely booked out." Immediately after: "What we are seeing now is customers now booking into calendar 2028." Purchase orders for specific products are already coming in for 2028; multiple LTAs run to the end of the decade; contracts carry "pricing-related commitments" and "minimum demand guarantees from our customers, or sometimes you refer to those as take-or-pay agreements" [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Cancellation terms on individual POs and the minimum-purchase levels in each LTA are not disclosed. Direction: the problem Coherent has to solve is how fast it can turn demand it can already see into capacity, not whether demand shows up. NVIDIA's investment in Coherent is read in the same context — less a simple equity bet than a multi-year commercial relationship and optical capacity lock-in [1×: NVIDIA Newsroom 2026-03-02 via PhotonCap]. Cycle order: customer commitment → capacity expansion → revenue realization.

Q4 CapEx jumped to $556M from $290M in Q3; FY2026 CapEx $1.1B, more than double the prior year's $441M, supporting Data Center and Communications capacity [1×: COHR IR / FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. FY2026 operating cash flow fell to $79.5M from $633.6M a year earlier; inventory swelled from $1.44B to $2.58B [1×: COHR IR / PhotonCap]. Cash is tied up in the buildout and the ramp. The CFO put payback on data-center capacity investment at around 18 months — a forward estimate, not a realized return [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap]. Across the optical chain, customers have started locking future capacity in advance: Lumentum tying up AXT substrate with a prepayment-backed LTA; Tower taking $290M of customer prepayments from SiPh customers for capacity reservation ([[Theses/TSEM - Tower Semiconductor]], [[Research/2026-08-02 - AXTI LITE InP Substrate LTA Prepay Chain - deep-dive]]). PhotonCap's watch-list is not CapEx itself but what CapEx sends back: (1) six-inch InP ramp pushing actual gross margin higher, (2) comparable CPO/NPO content converting to revenue, (3) OCS spreading across more network layers, (4) Multi-Rail becoming a system-level scale-across stream, (5) booked FY27 demand carrying through to the stated targets of $3B+ quarterly revenue and 42%+ gross margin [1×: COHR FQ4'26 call / PhotonCap].

The May Q3 checkpoint played out as written on the print: Q4 guide was $1.91–2.05B / 39.0–41.0% non-GAAP GM / $1.52–1.72 EPS; actuals $2.05B / 40.2% / $1.74 — revenue at the top, EPS through it, PhotonCap's then-Base-Case (revenue within the guide plus sustained datacenter acceleration) hit [1×: COHR IR May 6 + Aug 12 / PhotonCap]. Of the six monitoring items: six-inch yields already exceed three-inch but six-inch share of total InP capacity was again not disclosed; 1.6T mix answered only indirectly (internal 800G/1.6T absorbing all InP); OCS $4B+ held, still no quarterly revenue breakout; CPO "absolutely no push-out," revenue recognition still a next-quarter question; some LTAs carry take-or-pay to the end of the decade, NVIDIA-specific terms and the NVIDIA-attributed revenue curve still invisible; LITE non-GAAP GM 50.4% vs COHR 40.2%, gap still ~10 points, six-inch one of COHR's main levers for closing it [1×: LITE IR 2026-08-11 + COHR IR / PhotonCap]. None of the six broke the wrong way. About half confirmed with numbers; the other half rolled to "next quarter" again.

Compression of the call: Coherent's story is no longer "how many 1.6T transceivers can it sell." It is how many layers — InP through lasers, integrated optics, fiber, OCS, and transport systems — Coherent can keep in-house while optics expands across scale-out, scale-up, and scale-across at the same time. So far in this cycle the company's biggest problem has not been finding demand. It has been making light fast enough. Judgment dates and kill conditions are in Evidence and Source Excerpts.

## Framework / Mental Model
PhotonCap names interlocking typologies on this call; they are the reusable machinery, not the print.

**Constraint-location shift.** The analytical move is to stop counting transceiver units and locate the bind one layer down: module assembly/test (unconstrained, per management) → InP production (named bind) → usable InP substrate (separate problem, AXT/LTA chain). Scoring rule: if a vendor says "test is not the constraint," treat that as a statement about *relative* severity, not as a claim that PIC test physics changed.

**Six-inch dual story.** Capacity and margin are the same object: 4× devices / ~½ cost per device, *and* yields already above three-inch on EML, CW, and photodiodes. The falsifiable claim is that in this cycle volume growth and margin expansion need not be opposites. The missing disclosure — six-inch share of total InP capacity — is the score the framework cannot fill from this call.

**CPO vs NPO as form factors, not theses (PhotonLink content-parity).** Axes: attach point (package-adjacent vs board) × optical content (laser, ELM, isolators, PM fiber, fiber attach, SiPh PIC). Coherent's claim is content ≈ comparable across attach points. The more important axis is copper-to-optics conversion in scale-up, not CPO-beats-NPO. Distinct from Evidence: Evidence records the quotes and the "no push-out" denial; this section is the typology that lets a later call be re-scored if content diverges.

**Optical-domain migration.** CPO/NPO (electrical link → optical link) and OCS (reconfigure the optical fabric rather than add an electronic switch) are the same direction: more network functions moving into the optical domain. OCS opportunity ($4B+) is a TAM claim; LITE's doubled shipments / first $100M+ quarter is a shipment claim — do not add them.

**Scale-across stack, not a single SKU.** Transceivers + pump lasers + amplifiers/subsystems + Multi-Rail transport. FST (Ciena concept, OFC): 16 × 1.6T lanes at 300 GHz spacing, 25 Tb/s from a single line port — the transport-box counterpart to CPO/NPO pulling client optics next to the ASIC.

**CapEx-return checklist (five items).** (1) six-inch GM, (2) CPO/NPO content → revenue, (3) OCS layer-spread, (4) Multi-Rail system revenue, (5) FY27 book → $3B+ quarterly / 42%+ GM. CapEx dollars are the input, not the score.

**Six May monitors + judgment-date ladder.** Carry-forward items that did not get a number this call stay on the next-call list. Dated gates: FQ1'27 early November 2026 ($2.2–2.4B / 39.5–41.5% GM, first CapEx report card); within 2026 (first OCS revenue contribution disclosure; CPO revenue recognition — PhotonCap checkpoints, not company commitments); 1H 2027 (Multi-Rail ramp, management timeline); during 2027 (six-inch share of InP disclosed; GM toward 42% — first is PhotonCap's checkpoint); 2028 (calendar-2028 orders realize; 18-month payback prints as numbers).

## Contradiction Check
**No COHR thesis exists.** This note cannot "support existing conviction" on Coherent. The live question is whether the vault opens a COHR thesis as a multi-layer optics platform (PhotonCap's compression) or keeps COHR as the named 6-inch / dual-source challenger inside [[Theses/LITE - Lumentum]] and the assembler-competitor inside [[Theses/AAOI - Applied Optoelectronics]].
