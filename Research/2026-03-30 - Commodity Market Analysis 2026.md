# Commodity Market Analysis 2026

I’m pulling together the current Iran/Hormuz supply-risk backdrop and wider market structure, then mapping the direct energy links plus second-order inflation and supply-chain effects across major listed commodity benchmarks. After that I’ll rank the main commodity complexes and turn it into five clear long/short trade ideas.

---

The first-cut hierarchy is taking shape: strongest upside sensitivity sits in LNG, middle distillates, crude, fertilizer, petrochemical feedstocks, and some substitutes like coal and uranium. The clearest downside is in hogs, some other livestock, and cyclical industrial metals where input costs and demand destruction collide.

---

The ranking is settling into three buckets—direct supply-loss winners, substitution/inflation beneficiaries, and recession-demand losers—with aluminum a notable exception inside metals and uranium looking more second-wave than immediate. The trade list is therefore skewing toward relative-value expressions, and I’m checking grain/livestock spreads to make the futures setups more actionable.

---

As of **March 30, 2026**, this is no longer a generic “Iran risk premium.” Reuters and the IEA are describing an effective Hormuz choke, attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure, and the largest oil-market disruption on record. Roughly **25% of global seaborne oil trade** and **19% of global LNG trade** normally pass through the Strait of Hormuz; oil has only limited bypass capacity and LNG effectively has none. Brent is around **$115–116/bbl**, Asian LNG prices are up **143%** since February 28, Dutch TTF is about **70%** higher, and Asian diesel/jet benchmarks have more than doubled. citeturn603837view0turn491827view0turn775515news38turn199833news45

The transmission channels are straightforward: direct lost barrels/cargoes, extreme freight and insurance costs, substitution from gas into coal and eventually nuclear, and second-round fertilizer/feed/freight inflation into crops and livestock. Reuters reported VLCC freight from the Middle East to China at about **$423,736/day**, while LNG tanker rates jumped more than **40%**, so this is as much a logistics shock as a pure supply shock. citeturn161043view0turn603837view0

### Bottom line

The **best direct longs** are still **JKM LNG, diesel/gasoil, Brent, urea, naphtha/polyolefins and aluminium**. The **best second-round longs** are **thermal coal, corn/wheat and uranium**. The **cleanest shorts** are **lean hogs, feeder cattle, cotton**, and—counterintuitively in this episode—**gold/silver/PGMs on a near-term basis**, because the market is treating the Iran shock more as an inflation-and-rates event than a classic safe-haven event. citeturn603837view0turn359038news20turn374826view4

### Five trades I would prioritize

These are **scenario trades, not tailored advice**.

1. **Long JKM / short Henry Hub.**  
   This is the cleanest LNG stress expression: Hormuz normally carries almost a fifth of global LNG, there are no alternative routes, Qatar supply is impaired, and U.S. liquefaction cannot fully backfill the gap, so offshore gas should stay bid harder than domestic U.S. gas. citeturn603837view0turn199833news45

2. **Long gasoil or diesel crack / short Brent.**  
   Refined products are tighter than crude in this shock. IEA says middle distillates have little spare flexibility, and Reuters shows the biggest realized move has been in gasoil and jet, not just in flat crude. citeturn603837view0turn530156view2

3. **Long Brent / short WTI.**  
   Same oil bull thesis, but cleaner than outright crude because Brent captures the seaborne shortage and WTI is buffered by much lower direct U.S. Gulf dependence. This is the best core crude relative-value trade here. citeturn491827view0turn624184view0turn775515news38

4. **Long Urea U.S. Gulf futures.**  
   This is the best second-order inflation trade. More than 30% of global urea trade uses Hormuz, there are no fertilizer stockpiles comparable to oil reserves, and planting-season demand makes the timing especially bullish. CME has listed fertilizer futures/options if you can access them. citeturn603837view0turn374826view5turn280196view1

5. **Long aluminium / short copper.**  
   Aluminium has a direct Gulf supply squeeze; copper has more macro-demand and dollar sensitivity than direct Hormuz exposure. It is a good way to stay long the geopolitical supply shock while short the recession beta embedded in industrial metals. citeturn747713view0turn530156view3turn318455search0

Best alternates: **long corn / short soybeans** for an ag expression, and **short lean hogs** for a pure downstream user-margin short. On your examples specifically: **uranium is a good longer-horizon long, not my first-wave Iran trade**, and **feeder cattle is a cleaner short than live cattle**. citeturn515906view0turn374826view4turn219998view4

The biggest kill-switch for all front-line longs is a real reopening of Hormuz. On **March 29, 2026**, Pakistan-hosted talks were explicitly focused on reopening the strait, and Iran had already said “non-hostile” ships could transit if they coordinated with Tehran. That makes headline risk two-sided. On the other hand, Reuters’ analyst poll still sees oil elevated across scenarios and potentially much higher in a further escalation.
