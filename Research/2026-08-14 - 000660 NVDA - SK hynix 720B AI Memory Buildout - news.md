# SK Hynix $720B AI Memory Buildout — Chey Tour, 58% HBM, Nvidia $500B SK Group Deal

## Summary
Katie Tarasov and Jeniece Pettitt report the first on-camera tour of the Yongin Cluster (production “in February”) and Cheongju cleanrooms. SK Hynix, market cap “more than fivefold in the past year” and “now tops $1 trillion,” is investing $720 billion on what it calls the largest network of memory factories in the world, betting AI demand outruns the classic boom-bust. Counterpoint’s MS Hwang: SK Hynix 58% of HBM in Q1, Samsung and Micron 21% each. Funding: $26.5 billion Nasdaq listing in July, “the most money ever raised by a foreign company listing on U.S. markets.” U.S.-listed shares peaked near $195 on 14 July and closed Wednesday at $154.41 (−21%), coinciding with AI-chip volatility and a Korean market that “gets about half its value from SK Hynix and Samsung.”

President Lee Jae Myung’s June plan aims to double Korean memory production in five years, with a chip support package “worth at least $22 billion” and multiple new Samsung megafabs. Yongin Fab 1 walls were halfway up a structure SK Hynix said will reach 50-story height, with six stacked cleanrooms rather than a single-story Arizona-style TSMC/Intel footprint. Chey: “It’s like a war… Everybody wants to buy the memory chips.” He said prices “went up too fast” and that the buildout is meant to help. Ten long-term agreements with major customers; Nvidia “secured a stable supply of HBM” and agreed to co-develop next-generation memory as part of a $500 billion SK Group deal that includes SK Telecom data centers by 2027. Chey showed a wafer inscribed by Jensen Huang: “Please make more.” Hotels near the Korean memory expansions are “fully booked” (Hwang).

China: three SK Hynix fabs cannot sell leading-edge HBM there under U.S. export controls; CXMT “just had a blockbuster debut on the Shanghai market.” Hwang: “the counterparty of the race is China.” U.S. peers: Micron $50B two-fab Boise (wafers 2027) and $100B Clay, NY campus (up to four fabs). SK Hynix’s first U.S. site is packaging, not front-end — “$4 billion” West Lafayette, construction “set to conclude in 2028” (the companion Korea Herald piece dates groundbreaking 27 August at $3.87B). Solidigm (Intel NAND, $9B, 2020) plus a $10 billion “AI Company” launched in January; Chey is “studying possible sites” for more U.S. investment, including a memory production facility if power/water/labor/supply-chain line up. Custom HBM co-designed with AI processors, accelerating into HBM5, is Chey’s “turning point” that “changes the memory chip’s status.”
