# Memory to Account for 68% of Cloud Giant CapEx by 2027 — DRAM and NAND Share of CSP Spend

## Thesis Delta
Consensus still models AI factories as a GPU-plus-networking BOM with memory a high-teens slice — this 25/26 August Evertiq recap of a TrendForce release is the **BOM-share inversion**: **DRAM + NAND = 47% of major CSP capex in 2026 and 68% in 2027**, driven by both bits and price (server DRAM **+64% in H2 2025 and ~+270% in 2026**; enterprise SSD **+~35% H2 2025 and ~+235% in 2026**; HBM contract **+70–140% in 2027**). It is **not** a Micron-CEO reprint: the mechanism is TrendForce's CSP-capex math plus a QoQ contract-price table (Server DDR5 +93–98% in 1Q26, decelerating to +3–8% in 4Q26F; HBM4 **0% QoQ** through 4Q26F). **Supports** [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] L1 contracted-scarcity and the "whole DRAM complex, not HBM alone" rewrite in §Business Model. **Supports** [[Theses/SNDK - SanDisk]] / [[Theses/285A - Kioxia]] NAND as a co-equal capex sink, not a leftover. Second-order [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]]: TrendForce says elevated memory costs "give chip suppliers such as Nvidia greater justification for raising product prices" — consistent with Rubin rack inflation already in the NBIS thesis, not an NVDA trigger. No HIGH/LOW/CLOSE fire.

## Summary
Evertiq (citing a TrendForce press release): combined DRAM and NAND Flash will be 47% of major cloud service providers' total capex in 2026 and 68% in 2027. CSP capex itself is modelled +98% YoY in 2026 and +50% in 2027. Price path: server DRAM +64% cumulative in H2 2025, ~+270% in 2026; eSSD +~35% H2 2025, ~+235% in 2026. Some LTAs from Q2 2026 include price ceilings that could cap further increases; HBM contracts are still expected +70–140% in 2027.

Bit mix: HBM + RDIMM = 51% of DRAM bit supply in 2026 as suppliers steer scarce wafers to servers. 2027: process migration + new-fab ramps → +27% combined server DRAM and HBM bit supply. Second-order: memory inflation lets NVIDIA raise product prices; CSPs either lift capex to hold GPU unit targets or **cut memory per system** to manage cost and supply — the latter is the demand-destruction / 8-Hi-density-peak channel already in the 000660 20 August Damnang note. QoQ table (CSP DRAM): Server DDR5 +93–98% / +53–58% / +13–18% / +3–8% across 1Q–4Q26; LPDDR5X +58–85% / +80–85% / +8–13% / +0–5%; HBM4 blanks then 0% QoQ in 2Q–4Q26F — i.e. HBM contract prices modelled **flat sequentially** even as HBM 2027 is modelled +70–140% (annual, not QoQ).
