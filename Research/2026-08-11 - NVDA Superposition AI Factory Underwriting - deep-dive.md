## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices AI infra financing as simple “GPU collateral loans” → Part 2 asks what lenders actually underwrite: **remarketing/fungibility after customer loss** vs **contracts that prevent the customer from leaving** (take-or-pay / guarantees). Reframes NVDA’s multi-sponsor financing platforms and Apollo’s Broadcom/Anthropic $35B structure as credit design around contracted cash flows—not just silicon scarcity. ## Summary
Building on Part 1’s fungibility concept, Part 2 analyzes AI-factory financeability: utilization, cash flow, and residual value when workloads disappear. NVDA’s Aug 10, 2026 compute-financing MOUs (Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, GS, KKR) aim to mobilize >$500B third-party capital over time—not a single funded pool. Company claim: compute redeployable across customers/workloads; software updates extend economic life. Contrasted with Apollo-led $35B Broadcom AI XPV solution tied to >1GW Anthropic infra, which foregrounds contracted cash flows. Essay separates risks absorbed by contracts from risks left to remarketing capacity. Investor implication: underwriting standards will shape which AI campuses get cheap capital—and therefore which accelerator stacks win deployments.
