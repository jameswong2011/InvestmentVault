# AI "Dark Output" — Why AI's Economic Value Is Invisible in GDP (SemiAnalysis)

## Thesis Delta

The most-cited AI-bubble bear argument — "AI output isn't showing up to justify the capex" — is partly a measurement artifact, not an absence of value. SemiAnalysis's "Dark Output" framework names and fully develops the "phantom GDP" concept the vault first flagged via [[Research/2026-04-24 - Dylan Patel on AI Token Supply and Demand - video-transcript]]. It shifts the investor question from "is AI output real?" (yes, but structurally unmeasurable in the service sector) to "who *captures* it?" — only firms with pricing power convert AI cost-deflation into visible margin; competitive layers leak the value to users as untracked consumer surplus and it vanishes from both GDP and corporate earnings. The framework reinforces, not resolves, the J.P. Morgan $650B/yr monetization gap in [[AI Bubble Risk and Semiconductor Valuations]]: output is real, *capture* is the binding constraint.

## Summary

AI produces real economic value that national accounts cannot see — SemiAnalysis labels it "Dark Output," after the dark energy that is visible only through its effects on everything else. The asymmetry is the entire point: AI's costs (GPUs, electricity, water, token spend, displaced jobs) are fully visible, headline-friendly, and measured in dollars, watts, and gallons, while its output stays invisible unless sold at a visible price. Token spend enters GDP; the value the tokens produce does not. Because most AI value lands in the service sector — where statistical agencies have a longstanding inability to capture productivity — the boom can read to the macro data as a bust, and "critics can dismiss AI as a bubble" on the strength of data that is structurally blind to the output side of the ledger.

The mechanism is that services, unlike goods, give statisticians nothing to count. Manufacturing automation produced physical units: a single iron screw fell more than 99% in real price over six centuries while global quantity rose on the order of 10 billion times, and real GDP correctly booked that as growth and productivity. Services have no unit — "no metric ton of literature reviews, and no barrel of consulting" — so service GDP backs into an implied "quantity" by dividing current-dollar spend by a price index. When AI collapses the cost of a task, receipts fall and the accounts read the drop as an *output decline*; surveyed prices can simultaneously *rise*, because the cheapest tasks get automated first and the average price of the human work that remains goes up. The same data print therefore reads as inflation plus falling productivity even as real output explodes. There is no "mind power" metric to do for AI what horsepower did for steam — a million tokens can yield junk, a useful email summary, or a decision that changes a company's strategy, so the value depends on the output, never the token count.

SemiAnalysis splits the invisible value into three buckets — substitution, new, and captured — and the third carries the investment signal. Value only re-enters GDP (and corporate margins) where a firm has enough market power to hold price as its cost of production falls; that is "Captured AI Output." Everywhere else, AI cost-deflation leaks to users as consumer surplus and disappears. The widely-quoted $1.5T figure is "exposed labor," not "missing output" — tasks tied to ~$1.5T of labor cost that sit in categories where current AI has credible displacement potential, resting on Tier-4 market evidence (tools claimed in production). No Tier-5 (court-defended) or Tier-6 (insurer-underwritten) evidence exists yet, which the authors flag as a deliberate caution against AI boosterism; most evidence to date points to augmentation, not wholesale replacement.

The macro and policy stakes are explicit. Incoming Fed Chair Kevin Warsh (December 2025) conceded that anyone "looking at the data" is "backward looking… going to be late" and will "have to make a bet" on non-inflationary AI growth the statistics can't yet see. As AI funding transitions toward active capital-market financing, any measure that fails to show AI results "will be scrutinized for signs of a bubble" — so the measurement gap is itself a bubble-narrative risk independent of the underlying reality. The precedent is a 2013 GDP methodology revision that capitalized R&D and intellectual-property investment, lifting 1990s production by ~$3.6T (nearly 30% of full-year-2000 GDP) yet barely moving the growth rate because it was spread evenly; SemiAnalysis argues AI's measurement problem dwarfs that revision, and clusters in exactly the service-sector blind spot the accounts handle worst.

## Framework / Mental Model

The source advances four reusable analytical structures.

### 1. Dark Output taxonomy — three buckets of invisible value

Dark Output = AI-enabled economic value that exists but is absent or badly distorted in GDP, prices, labor statistics, or industry accounts. Apply the bucket test to any AI use case to predict how (and whether) it surfaces in the data.

| Bucket | Definition | GDP / data treatment | Worked example | Investor read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Substitution** | Work previously done by humans, now done by AI | Receipts vanish into tokens; surveyed prices may *rise* (simplest tasks automated first) → reads as output decline + inflation | Simple legal doc drafted by AI not a lawyer; ~$1.5T tasks exposed | Margin only if price held; otherwise value leaks out |
| **New** | Work that didn't happen before AI made it cheap | No wage bill disappears (no one paid for it before); only token cost is traced | Literature review before every project ($2,000→$2); email-thread summaries | Pure demand for compute/tokens; no displaced incumbent revenue |
| **Captured** | Work shifted to AI but still charged the same price | Surfaces *correctly* as a margin explosion — requires genuine market power | External $10,000 HR service → AI HR provider still billing $10,000 | The only bucket that reaches shareholders as visible earnings |

The capture distinction is the framework's core investing output: identical work, two outcomes. A $10,000 external service replaced by an AI provider that still charges $10,000 keeps the output in GDP and only deletes the wages/workers; the same service pulled in-house for $10 of tokens removes $9,990 from GDP for identical work. New Dark Output is likely much larger than substitution in the long run.

### 2. The Evidence Ladder — grading market signals, not benchmarks

SemiAnalysis rejects expert benchmarks (expensive, slow, subjective, backward-looking, and gated by scarce expert time) in favor of what firms reveal about their own practices. Displacement does not require beating the best lawyer or engineer — only being "good enough, cheap enough, and reliable enough" to aid or replace the worker who would have done the task at prevailing wages. Strength rises as a third party puts something real at stake:

| Tier | Signal | Strength / use |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Benchmark — model completes the task under test conditions | Weakest; used only to estimate the *cost* of AI doing the task |
| 3 | Public claim a product/company "can do the work" (the "hype layer," also human-verified benchmarks) | Low — assertion, not deployment |
| 4 | A business states the tools are in production use | The $1.5T exposed-labor estimate is built on Tier-4+ |
| 5 | A firm successfully defends AI-produced work in a court fight | Not yet observed |
| 6 | An insurer underwrites the AI failure mode | Strongest — a third party has priced and absorbed the risk; not yet observed |

Read as a continuous ladder, not a binary. The absence of any Tier-5/6 evidence is the authors' own discipline against over-claiming.

### 3. Four ways the statistics break

Treating every failure as "GDP missing AI" oversimplifies; the errors are distinct and land in different datasets.

| Mechanism | What happens | Data symptom |
|---|---|---|
| **Boundary Shift** | Market-bought work moves inside the firm/household (paid brief → internal prompt; contractor → employee) | Transaction that made value visible disappears |
| **Price Collapse** | No separate quantity vs. quality measure in services; receipts fall, junior staff displaced | Reads as higher inflation + falling productivity/output |
| **Sector Misrouting** | AI creates value in one sector; the transaction is booked in another | Hospital paperwork value shows up as AI-vendor revenue (NAICS 5415) while physician offices (NAICS 6211) look stagnant — "counting the screws while missing the houses" |
| **New Work Invisibility** | Net-new task with no receipt beyond tokens | Real work visible only at the token cost |

### 4. Reid's delegability test (1934, via Care/Feminist Economics)

Margaret Reid's diagnostic: if work could be delegated to a paid third party, it is productive. Hire a housekeeper → housekeeping enters GDP; do it yourself → it does not; the act is identical, only the cash flow differs. AI makes virtually every information task delegable (draft a legal brief, analyze a statement, triage a complaint, generate code), so it pushes a large fraction of work across the production boundary into the "produced but unpriced" region — the same constructed, politically-contested boundary that Feminist Economics documented decades ago. SemiAnalysis concedes its own framework inherits the exclusion: Displacement Dark Output measures only paid market labor (BLS wages, BLS employment, O*NET activities) and is blind to household/unpaid AI use, which only enlarges the unpriced economy further.

## Evidence

**Substitution fingerprint (trackable macro signal):** junior staff displaced from routine work first → average wages in exposed occupations *rise* because the lowest-paid workers leave the sample ("No one got a raise, and yet wages rose"). Employment in the most AI-exposed sectors is falling relative to the broader economy while those same segments show relative wage *increases* — the diagnostic mismatch SemiAnalysis tracks on its Dark Output Monitor. This is a measurement artifact of displacement, not a direct output measurement, but it is falsifiable and observable now.

## Contradiction Check

**Assumption affected:** "AI output not appearing in GDP / corporate revenue ⇒ AI demand is hollow / the capex is a bubble." Dark Output decouples *output* from *revenue* — much of the value is real but accrues to users as surplus, leaving no trace beyond tokens.

**Supports** the bull-side reframe already in [[AI Bubble Risk and Semiconductor Valuations]]: the soft macro/productivity prints partly reflect a blind accounting system, and Warsh's Fed acknowledging the lag supports a "look through weak GDP, stay accommodative" stance that is favorable to long-duration AI-capex equities — *if* the Fed bets on unmeasured productivity.

**Does NOT resolve** the J.P. Morgan $650B/yr monetization gap — it sharpens it. That threshold is about revenue and returns to capital; Dark Output says a large share of AI value will never become revenue to anyone because competitive markets compete it away to users. So the framework *reinforces* the bear-side observation that capture lags, while reframing the output side as genuinely real. For equities the binding question is no longer "is the output there?" but "which layer captures it?":
- **Captured-AI beneficiaries (bull):** pricing-power enterprise software that holds price as AI cuts its cost of production — [[Theses/NOW - ServiceNow]], [[Theses/PLTR - Palantir]] (the macro note already frames these as deployment-layer "picks and shovels" that benefit if infra is oversized). The "Captured AI Output" bucket is the formal mechanism behind that bull case: margin expansion that *is* visible in GDP and earnings.
- **Value-leakage warning (bear):** competitive/commoditizing AI layers where price collapses — the frontier-model API war already at −65-90% in the macro note — surface as consumer surplus, not shareholder value. New Dark Output (agentic, net-new workflows per [[Macro & Technology/Agentic Internet]]) drives token/compute demand but does not, by itself, prove anyone monetizes it.

**Tension to keep honest:** "real but unmeasurable" is convenient for justifying capex and risks unfalsifiability. The article's own guardrails — no Tier-5/6 evidence, augmentation > replacement in the data so far — are the discipline; an investor should treat Dark Output as a reason the bear's GDP/revenue evidence is *weaker than it looks*, not as positive proof of capture. Conviction on the AI-bubble base case ("digestion phase") is unchanged; the framework strengthens the "output is real" pillar and the capture-layer (software pricing-power) bull case, while leaving the monetization-gap arithmetic intact.
