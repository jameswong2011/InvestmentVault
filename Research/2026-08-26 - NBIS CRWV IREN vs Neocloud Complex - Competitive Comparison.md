# NBIS vs CRWV vs IREN vs the Neocloud Complex: Competitive Comparison

Scope, per user selection: the listed pure-plays (CRWV, NBIS), the listed miner-pivot landlords and full-stack (IREN, APLD, CIFR, WULF, CORZ), and the unlisted pure-plays (Lambda, Crusoe, Nscale, Together AI, Fluidstack). Captive builders (SPCX / xAI, ORCL OCI), CBRS and the sovereign tail are excluded. Only NBIS, CRWV and IREN carry theses; the other nine are web-supplemented and receive no vault writes. The comparison is run as a test of the frame in [[Website/2026-08-22 - Neoclouds - The Property Developers of Compute]], which is the argument behind the Live Portfolio's Full-weight position in Nebius.

## Thesis Delta

**The property-developer frame survives the twelve-name test as a description of the mechanism and fails as a description of which layer keeps the return.** A developer earns its spread by stabilising a non-depreciating asset onto long leases with an anchor tenant who funds the build; Nebius stabilises a seven-year asset onto five-year paper and keeps the residual, which is the serviced-office model, not the developer's. The names the frame actually describes are the powered-shell landlords (APLD on 15-year hyperscaler take-or-pay, CORZ on 12-year CoreWeave plus 15-year AMD, WULF on 20-year Anthropic, CIFR on 15-year AWS guaranteed by Amazon.com), where the tenant owns the GPUs and holds the residual, and IREN, the one full-stack operator that owns the power layer beneath its fleet. Those names trade at roughly 4.5–10x EV / annualised contracted revenue with BB-range project paper, and none is in the portfolio.

For [[Theses/NBIS - Nebius Group]]: conviction unchanged (medium). The external check the vault did not author cuts both ways. Rates rose two quarters running (SemiAnalysis H100 one-year index +40% Oct 2025 → Mar 2026; CoreWeave list prices +25% in July against Nvidia's +17% input rise; a Nebius auction cleared 15% above its highest prior Blackwell rate), which supports the essay's "equilibrium, not spike" claim on the near end and says nothing about the 2028–29 re-rent step. Nebius disclosed no active-MW figure through the 12 Aug Q2 call; Korolenko reframed ("there is a gap between the connected power and the revenues generation") and no analyst pressed. Every in-window sell-side action on NBIS was a raise (BofA $310, Goldman $286, Citizens $270, DA Davidson $250); short interest 25.6–27%. The Full weight is ahead of both the medium conviction and the disclosure.

For [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]]: conviction unchanged (medium, monitoring). Coordination proof strengthened: 1.5 GW active at 30 Jun after adding ~500 MW in Q2 (300 MW in June alone), YE2026 active target raised to >1.85 GW, and the only operator that has printed price above input cost. Credit weakened in the same window: the $2.6B Anthropic-linked facility repriced +100–125bp and took a 1.35x DSCR maintenance covenant with full amortisation before it would sell (covenant-lite is over for GPU paper); the only two rating downgrades in the set (Jefferies and Citi, Buy → Hold, mid-July, on the Q1 miss and >17M shares of insider selling); CDS round-tripped from 452bp in June toward ~855bp in July [T2, single source]. Net: unchanged.

For [[Theses/IREN - IREN Limited]]: conviction strengthened at the margin, level unchanged (medium). Horizon 1 (50 MW IT / 75 MW gross) was accepted by Microsoft on 13 Aug, the only in-window event where a neocloud converted contracted megawatts into billing megawatts with hyperscaler sign-off; Futurum's efficiency ledger (17 Aug) ranks IREN first at ~$40M contracted revenue per MW against CoreWeave's ~$28M; Bernstein holds $100 Outperform. The ~$21B funding-gap bear is untouched, and Q4 FY26 prints 27 Aug (consensus revenue $130M, −30% YoY), so this note is stale on IREN within 24 hours.

## Summary

The twelve names sort into four tiers by what is actually sold and on what tenor, and the market prices them as one correlated tape: every listed name is Buy-rated, short interest runs 16–27% across the listed set, and the 29 July credit-swap scare took CRWV −9% and NBIS −10% on the same day. The tiers differ on the one variable the tape ignores, which is who holds the residual value of the GPU when the first contract rolls. Full-stack operators (CRWV, NBIS, IREN, Lambda, Nscale) sell GPU-hours on four-to-five-year take-or-pay and keep the box; landlords (APLD, CIFR, WULF, CORZ) sell powered shell on twelve-to-twenty-year leases and hand the box to the tenant; Crusoe sells the build itself with project-level debt against real generation; Fluidstack and Together assemble capacity on other people's shells and other people's credit. In a famine every tier prints demand; in a glut the residual lands on the full-stack operator, the rent keeps flowing to the landlord, and the landlord's problem becomes the credit of the operator paying it.

The essay's coordination claim is testable in two places, energised megawatts per quarter and price realised above input cost, and on both CoreWeave is currently the operator with the evidence. It added roughly 500 MW of active power in a single quarter, more than Nebius's entire YE2025 base, and it raised list prices 25% in July while Nvidia raised GPU prices 17%, which Truist models as a nine-point margin uplift on long-duration deals. That is the first hard datapoint against the vault's pure layer-renter read: the layer below priced, and the operator kept eight points. Nebius prints neither number. Its premium is a funding premium (roughly 70% of Q2 deals prepaid, >$9B of 2026 upfront funding expected, $8B of cash, converts at 0.50% and 4.50%) that the sell-side has already paid for, which is why the stock sat at or above the average target after the August round-trip. IREN sits between them: the smallest energised base of the three, but the only one with an in-window hyperscaler acceptance and the highest independently ranked revenue density per contracted megawatt, on a power stack it owns rather than rents.

The landlords match the essay's frame and do not yet earn under it. Billing megawatts today are CORZ 437, APLD 175, WULF 102 and CIFR zero (Black Pearl commenced in August, two months early); interest as a share of revenue runs CIFR 269%, WULF 126%, CORZ 14.5% and APLD 4.1%, the last flattered by capitalised construction interest. Their credit is rented: Amazon.com guarantees CIFR's Black Pearl rent (notes at 6.125%) while Google backstops Fluidstack's lease at Barber Lake (7.125%, Fitch BB−), Google holds ~14% of WULF and ~5.4% of CIFR through warrants taken in exchange, and CORZ carries negative book equity and a 187M → 508M fully diluted share count. Two of the four (CIFR, WULF) are levered to Fluidstack, a tenant whose own equity is a levered option on the spread between what Anthropic pays it and what it owes the miners. Applied Digital spun its GPU-cloud arm into ChronoScale in May because hyperscale tenants would not lease from a competitor, which is the clearest statement in the set that landlord and full-stack cannot coexist in one entity. The developer that survives the glut is the one that presold to an investment-grade anchor on a fifteen-year lease; CORZ (AMD, >$14B, coupon already covered), CIFR (AWS with parent guarantee) and APLD (65% of contracted MW with an IG hyperscaler) are that developer, and WULF's Anthropic lease, at twenty years and ~$19B, is the longest paper in the complex written against an unrated counterparty.

The unlisted tier shows where the cycle is. Nscale is marketing a September IPO at up to $25B on $51B of "contracted revenue" against a ~$450M run-rate, a ratio near 110x where CoreWeave's is ~10x, with the firm-versus-optional split and the Microsoft share undisclosed. Lambda's listing has slipped to 2027 while it borrows $926M against GPUs to buy Nvidia chips that Nvidia, a shareholder, leases back. Fluidstack's takeout by Alphabet is structurally pre-arranged through ~$6–7B of lease backstops and warrant positions. Crusoe is the name most mis-shelved as a neocloud: ~4.9 GW contracted, GE Vernova and PROENERGY turbines procured at scale, a factory building modules, project-level debt against Oracle and Microsoft leases, and a $30B raise in talks. It is the closest thing in the complex to the property developer the essay describes, and it is private.

### Dynamic Analysis

**1. Market share trajectory.** Energised megawatts are the share metric, and the trajectories diverge by tier: CoreWeave 1.5 GW → >1.85 GW by YE2026 on a demonstrated ~500 MW-per-quarter cadence; Nebius ~170 MW → 800 MW–1 GW guided with the numerator undisclosed; IREN 50 MW IT → 200 MW IT in four accepted tranches this year; the landlords CORZ 437 → ~1.1 GW by 2028, APLD 175 → 1.41 GW, WULF 102 → 839 MW, CIFR 0 → 700 MW by 2028–29. The constraint that decides the pace has moved from silicon to the electrical layer, transformers, switchgear and interconnect batch audits (ERCOT Batch Zero, the New York moratorium), which is structural rather than cyclical and favours owners of pre-queued power (IREN, the miner landlords) and builders that bring their own generation (Crusoe's ~1.65 GW of turbines, IREN's Sweetwater) over operators that lease colocation. Nebius's >75% owned share of its 5 GW is the right answer to that constraint if it converts; its silence on conversion is the open question.

**2. Pricing power divergence.** The rate reversed upward for every tier in the last two quarters, so the divergence sits in tenor rather than direction. Full-stack operators reprice every five years and therefore capture both the famine uplift (CoreWeave's +25%, Nebius's +15% auction) and the 2028–29 re-rent step; landlords lock twelve-to-twenty-year escalators at $1.2–2.3M per MW per year and forgo both. CoreWeave passing Nvidia's +17% through at +25% is the datapoint the sector note's layer-renter frame did not have: the layer below priced, and the operator kept a margin above it. Whether that margin is a temporary coordination rent or a durable one is exactly the essay's question, and it is answered by the first aging-Hopper re-rental prints, not by anything in this window. Nvidia's backstop programme, which floors GB300 at an average $2.36/hr over six years against a $6.75 year-one rate, is the vendor writing down the re-rent curve in public.

**3. Technology trajectory.** Rubin ships H2 2026 with CRWV, NBIS, IREN and Nscale as launch partners, and IREN carries Nvidia Exemplar Cloud status on GB300 NVL72. Hyperscaler silicon (MAIA, MTIA, Trainium, TPU) attacks full-stack demand on a 2027–30 timeline from the two anchor tenants that fund Nebius; the landlords are silicon-agnostic by construction (CORZ signed AMD; TPUs reach third-party shells through Fluidstack, where Google is the strategic holder). The right side of the platform shift is the layer indifferent to which chip sits in the rack: owned power and powered shell. Nebius's software stack is the counter, a CSP in gestation, and Together's ~45% gross margin on a 60–70% rental mix is the evidence that the serving layer is not yet the margin driver anywhere in the complex.

**4. Logical tension.** The essay needs the coordination premium to be durable, roughly twice the prior generation's cash rate as an equilibrium. The landlords need tenants to keep signing fifteen-year take-or-pay at fixed $/MW regardless of the GPU rate. Both hold in a famine. In a glut the full-stack operator takes the re-rent, the landlord keeps the rent, and the landlord's problem becomes the operator's credit: CoreWeave's success is APLD's and CORZ's rent, CoreWeave's failure is their credit event, and the Fluidstack leases at CIFR and WULF are Alphabet's problem before they are anyone else's. The CRWV–CORZ episode is the category's price discovery, shareholders rejecting $9B of CoreWeave stock in October 2025 and the same asset attracting >$14B from AMD nine months later, which says the shell layer is worth more standalone than captive. IREN's version of the tension: if the power floor is real, the GPU fleet on top is a funded option; if the fleet melts faster than owned power helps, IREN is a levered renter with a nicer cost of electricity.

**5. Scenario divergence.** Under the 2028–29 capex digestion the vault's macro base case dates ([[Macro & Technology/Sustainability of AI Capex]]), the underdog that wins is the landlord holding an investment-grade counterparty on fifteen-year paper (CORZ / AMD, CIFR / AWS, APLD's hyperscaler) and the owner of pre-queued power with a revert-to-arbitrage floor (IREN), because the residual lands on the tenant and the rent survives the rate. Under continued scarcity into 2029–30, which [[Macro & Technology/AI Supply-Demand Considerations to 2030]] supports by moving the binding gate from HBM to power in 2027, the built base (CoreWeave) and the largest owned pipeline (Nebius at 5 GW) earn the most, and the landlords' fixed escalators look like the wrong tenor. The vault's two macro notes split on the timing, so the comparison cannot lean on either; the portfolio's aggregate exposure (NBIS Full, SPCX High, NVDA Medium) is a single bet on the second scenario.

**6. Customer and supplier overlap.** Microsoft underwrites Nebius (~$17–19B), CoreWeave (67% of FY25 revenue, 36% of Q2), IREN ($9.7B), Nscale (~$23B), Lambda and Crusoe's 900 MW campus: one counterparty across the whole full-stack tier, which is where the concentration in this complex actually sits. Fluidstack is the tenant at CIFR (300 MW) and WULF (~530 MW across Lake Mariner and Abernathy) on the same Google backstop; CoreWeave is the tenant at APLD (400 MW) and CORZ (590 MW), so owning CoreWeave alongside either landlord is the same megawatt from both sides. On the supplier side Nvidia allocates to every full-stack name and sits on every cap table except Fluidstack's (Google's), Dell carries IREN's $5.8B GPU bill, and the ODM working-capital layer beneath all of them runs on a concentrated Taiwanese bank group. Pricing power in the relationships: Nvidia raised 17% and CoreWeave re-priced 25%; Microsoft buys priority at Nebius with a 40% prepay that sells the operator roughly eight points of contract PV; the miner landlords sell Google and Amazon warrants for the credit they cannot earn.

## Framework / Mental Model

Lenses applied ([[Generalist - Overview]] READING PROTOCOL: hypotheses to test, not verdicts).

- **[G-13] price-implied variable.** Nebius at ~$64B EV implies both that the 800 MW–1 GW connects and converts and that the contracted rate survives the re-rent; the essay's 5–6.5x 2028–29 EV/EBIT at $12 is the price if both hold. The single mispriced variable is active MW in November; nothing in this window moves it.
- **[G-10] outside view, run adversarially.** Credit-funded capacity booms break where the marginal builder's interest crosses 20–25% of revenue; CoreWeave prints 24.9%. The landlords' 126–269% are construction-phase ratios on pre-revenue projects, a different animal but the same tranche, and CORZ at 14.5% with the coupon covered is the only landlord already past the line. The IREN preference must beat the base rate that builders of the durable layer dilute through the build ($21B gap, 26% short, founder RSUs).
- **[G-7] ROIIC × runway.** The essay's 22% pre-tax ROIC at $12 on a 5 GW runway is the full-stack case; Needham's CORZ 2028E (~79% adj EBITDA margin on fifteen-year paper) is the landlord case, lower return on tenant-funded capital over a longer runway. Which is worth more depends entirely on the re-rent step.
- **[G-4] Perez.** The frenzy-phase over-build funds the substrate; the builders that fund it are usually not the harvesters. The complex's own structure now encodes that: shells securitise ($61B of DC ABS outstanding, A/A− cap), contracts wrap in SPV notes, GPUs stay in private credit. The landlord layer is the dark fibre; the question is whether its owners survive to lease it twice.
- **[[Lens - Value Layer Monopoly]].** Full-stack: layer-renter (rents silicon above, grid below) with a contract wrapper as a temporary layer; CoreWeave's +25% / +17% passthrough is the first evidence of margin above the layer, held as a famine hypothesis. IREN: owns the grid layer (STRONG on capital-scale and interconnect, WEAK on pricing evidence until Q4 FY26 prints $/MW at owned-power cost). Landlords: own the shell layer, rent the credit layer (Google / Amazon), which makes the guarantor the layer-owner. Nscale: layer-renter marketing a 110x backlog / run-rate into an IPO.
- **Disconfirming check.** The lenses cluster toward "owned power and powered shell beat the melting-asset renter", which is also the sector note's standing trade-construction view, so the cluster is the cue to argue the other side. The bull for Nebius the models understate: 77% gross margin, $4.5B of 1H26 operating cash flow, ~70% prepaid deals, 5 GW contracted with >75% owned, two-quarter rate reversal, and the widest re-rating in large-cap markets if the build lands. The bear for the landlords the models understate: pre-revenue P&Ls, rented credit, a 5x revenue-density disadvantage, AWS moving toward owned sites, the New York precedent, and two of four levered to one thin intermediary.

## Contradiction Check

- **Rates rising supports the essay, and every datapoint is famine-vintage.** The +40% index move, the +25% list increase and the +15% auction are all shortage prints; the essay concedes the same about the re-rent record. The Nvidia backstop floor ($2.36/hr average over six years against $6.75 year one) is the vendor's own re-rent curve and it is steep.
- **CoreWeave's passthrough contradicts the vault's pure layer-renter frame.** The sector note holds that pricing power is upstream-pinned at Nvidia; an operator re-pricing 25% against a 17% input rise kept eight points. Held as a famine hypothesis; the frame needs the datapoint in view.
- **The landlords out-contract and do not out-earn.** Bajarin's ~5x density gap and the CIFR / WULF pre-revenue P&Ls mean the property-developer frame is right about durability and wrong about near-term cash; a landlord preference is a 2028 preference.
- **Landlord durability is upstream of full-stack credit.** Where the tenant is CoreWeave or Fluidstack, the landlord's fifteen-year lease is only as durable as the tenant or its guarantor; the frame's clean separation of layers holds only for the IG-direct leases.
- **Nebius's non-disclosure is absence of evidence, not evidence of a miss.** The November print resolves it in either direction; the flag is on weight-versus-disclosure, not on execution.
- **Metric mismatch.** Futurum's contracted TCV per MW (~$40M IREN), Bajarin's annual revenue per MW ($1.2–2.3M landlord / $7–13M full-stack) and ElectronEconomics' ~$10M per active MW are three denominators and cannot be stacked.
- **Price and vintage risk.** CRWV metrics are as of 12 Jul against NBIS 15 Aug; NBIS's price prints $216 in the vault and $187.77 at TipRanks on an unstated date; IREN reports 27 Aug. The multiples table is directional and stale within a day.
- **The CDS figure rests on one secondary source.** The 29 Jul price action is corroborated; the ~855bp / ~50% five-year default read is not. The $2.6B facility's covenant package is the load-bearing credit fact.
- **The IREN preference has to beat the dilution base rate.** A $21B gap, 26% short, founder RSU grants and a Q3 FY26 revenue miss of −34% are the outside view; the owned-power floor is a narrative until Q4 prints $/MW at owned-power cost.
