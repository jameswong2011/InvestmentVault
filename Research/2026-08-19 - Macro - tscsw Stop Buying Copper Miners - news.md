# Stop Buying "Copper Miners" — TSCSW

## Thesis Delta

Consensus still prices "copper miners" as one tightness trade after a record COMEX print and a 42-session LME warehouse draw — the same basket TSCSW itself called too cheap in June, when Marimaca's study deck was being bought at about $4/lb with spot already above $6. This 19 August 2026 free TSCSW letter (Gmail `1a01b011c815f961`; web fetch matched, not a teaser) retires that number. Bloomberg Intelligence's 17 August reverse-engineered residuals (Alon Olsha / Salma Belmaachi) imply Lundin $12,050/t, KGHM $13,080, Ivanhoe $10,880, Hudbay $14,100 — the last of those equal to LME spot, i.e. a mid-cap priced as if the record price is permanent. Consensus assumes/prices a single copper-beta re-rate; this source implies four different bets bought as one thing: a COMEX–LME tariff/currency spread, a developer-vs-producer lag (Marimaca +2.5%, Hot Chili −16%, Hudbay/Arizona Sonoran 30% takeover already done), an acid-seller option at Ivanhoe the market still discounts as a broken mine-plan, and one name (Hudbay/Cactus) whose bull case is "if everything goes right, you get what you paid for." Live-book Holdings TABLE (17 names, last refresh 2026-08-12) contains no copper miners. Cached [[Theses/CCJ - Cameco]] and [[Theses/LNG - Cheniere Energy]] checked and not forced — copper residuals do not touch uranium fuel-cycle or LNG toll-road handles. No conviction/status change (none available to change on a live copper-miner thesis; flag-only elsewhere).

## Summary

TSCSW's June letter treated copper developers as mispriced decks, not mispriced metal: Marimaca's own study was worth $709 million at $4.30 copper and $347 million at $3.50, so the equity was a ~$4/lb purchase against spot already above $6, with two payoffs — a slow price catch-up, or a bid. By 18 August, through the highest copper price ever recorded, Marimaca had only gone C$7.50 → C$7.69 (+2.5%) and Hot Chili A$2.00 → A$1.68 (−16%). The M&A route did pay: Hudbay completed Arizona Sonoran the day after the June post, at the 30% premium struck in March. Producers repriced; the named developers did not. That split is the setup, not a rounding error.

The physical tape that did the re-rating is dated. Front-month COMEX settled a record $6.7030/lb on 5 August; the September contract traded as high as $6.7415 on the 17th. LME cash went $34 over 3-month metal at end-July, $207.50 over on 11 August, and $535 over by the 17th on official prices. Warehouses drew for 42 straight sessions through 14 August — the longest run since 2014 — down to 204,975 tonnes, then jumped to 223,550 by the 18th as the cash premium more than halved. Q2 filings cited in the letter sit in a 29 July–5 August window; the letter does not reproduce those filing tables. The live question is which copper you own, not whether "copper miners are too cheap."

BI's 17 August work solves for the long-term copper price each mid-cap equity needs, forever, to justify the share price. In LME terms, with spot at $14,100/t (COMEX nearer $14,800/t; the gap is the tariff premium): Lundin ~$12,050/t, KGHM $13,080, Ivanhoe $10,880 (marked down until consensus decides whether Kamoa-Kakula fully returns), Hudbay $14,100 — spot. TSCSW's load-bearing caveat, which BI's cheap-Ivanhoe read does not cancel: an implied copper price is a solved-for residual. It soaks up every other model assumption, the same way an implied discount rate does; it is not a séance on what shareholders believe. Even so, a valuation that only works if the record price never ends has used up its arguments before a share is bought. Hudbay's premium is attributed by BI to Cactus. TSCSW's read: that is the entire bull case.

BI concludes the opposite on Ivanhoe ($10,880 = cheap; real vs nominal unspecified). TSCSW refuses the basket. Ivanhoe-to-Hudbay is a 30% gap in embedded copper inside one sub-sector on one method, yet the market re-rated them as one thing. The Ivanhoe discount is no longer an information lag: the revised mine plan (called in November as a backfill-methodology failure, not an act of God, with reserve-sterilization risk) has been public for five months, cut reserves 25%, and published a path back above 500,000 tonnes a year from 2028. What remains is belief in a plan from a management team whose last plan broke. Meanwhile the Kamoa smelter is running, guidance reads in anodes, and Ivanhoe is an acid seller in a region of leachers paying up — "additional margin via significant by-product sulphuric acid sales," with Friedland calling the acid "ever more valuable." The market is discounting a mid-cap whose chairman is openly gloating about the acid squeeze. They aren't one thing.
