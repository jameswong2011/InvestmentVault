# SK hynix Indiana $3.87B HBM Packaging Groundbreaking Set for 27 August

## Thesis Delta
Consensus treats [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] U.S. presence as Solidigm NAND plus slideware packaging — this 13 August 2026 Korea Herald report dates the West Lafayette advanced-packaging + HBM R&D groundbreaking to 27 August, restates the 2024 $3.87 billion envelope, CHIPS Act $450 million grant plus $500 million loan, and mass-production in 2H 2028, with Chey and CEO Kwak expected and Huang’s attendance unconfirmed. That is Industry #15 (subsidized multi-region footprint) and a packaging-not-wafer print: it does not add 2026–27 HBM bits and does not fire Rubin-allocation triggers. Hypothesis: a U.S. HBM stack/wire site is political insurance and customer-proximity, not a substitute for Yongin wafers.

## Summary
Jo He-rim reports SK hynix will hold a groundbreaking ceremony on 27 August in West Lafayette, Indiana, for a $3.87 billion advanced chip packaging plant and research center for high-bandwidth memory. Invitations have gone to major customers and partners. SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won and CEO Kwak Noh-jung are expected; Jensen Huang’s attendance is not confirmed, though the two have met several times as HBM and AI-infrastructure ties deepened.

The project was announced in 2024 near Purdue University. Mass production is planned for the second half of 2028. The site will package HBM — stacked DRAM for AI-processor bandwidth — not run front-end wafer fabs. The U.S. government committed up to $450 million in direct funding and $500 million in loans under the CHIPS Act. Site preparation and concrete work began earlier this year; 27 August is framed as the start of full-scale structural construction.

Chey said after the July U.S. depositary-receipt listing that SK hynix was considering additional U.S. investment beyond Indiana, including a memory production facility if power, water, labor, and supply-chain infrastructure were in place. That option remains conditional. The companion CNBC tour (same day) called the U.S. site “$4 billion” with construction “set to conclude in 2028” — same project, rounded.
