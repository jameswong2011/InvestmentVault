# Optical Investing, Round Two: Which Names to Watch Now

## Thesis Delta

Consensus treated the June–July optical drawdown as a CPO-timeline break (SemiAnalysis “Massive Delay” pushing NVIDIA scale-up CPO past 2029; AEC/copper-in-rack; Optical I/O shrinking the module TAM) and priced the August bounce as a dead-cat on still-triple-digit multiples → Damnang’s paid piece implies the fundamentals did not break, August prints *raised* guidance, and the investable screen is a two-axis map (form-factor-agnostic content + bottleneck ownership) that still ranks L2 lasers first. For the vault: consensus that “CPO slipped so LITE/COHR duration compressed” is the error; this source implies [[Theses/LITE - Lumentum]] §arms-dealer / CPO-role and [[Theses/AEHR - Aehr Test Systems]] §SiPh/WLBI kicker are being re-armed by primary-company disclosure, not by a new physics story. [G-3]/[G-13] the mispriced lever is which layer keeps dollars if pluggable, NPO, *or* CPO wins, not the single CPO year; Semis #1/#8/#18 (bottleneck location, architecture remap, cycle-vs-structural) and VLM §1 high-power CW/ELS fire on L2 and L1, not on the module assembler.

## Summary

Damnang’s 13 August 2026 paid post is the second-act companion to the April optical investment map and the 8 August scale-up CPO TAM piece already in the vault ([[Research/2026-08-08 - LITE COHR Laser Market Repriced by Scale-Up CPO - deep-dive]]). The free WebFetch teaser stopped at the yellow-highlight table; the Gmail body is the full article, including the name-by-layer screen. The author’s claim scope is personal ranking, not a buy list: yellow names are “priority watch” by his criteria; absence is not a short.

The June–July correction is attributed to three stacked forces, not an earnings break. SemiAnalysis’s CPO note used low yields and cost to push scale-up CPO mass production from late 2028 to beyond 2029 and argued copper plus pluggables keep the upper hand until then. AEC shipment growth then locked a consensus that in-rack connectivity stays copper for a while, and the Optical I/O argument (E/O conversion moving inside the GPU package shrinks the transceiver TAM) returned. Triple-digit P/Es plus profit-taking and index rebalancing widened the drawdown. The author’s counter is that this priced a *single CPO timeline* as if it were optical demand.

August is the rebuttal from primary companies. On 11 August Lumentum said ultra-high-power CPO laser demand is increasing, it had an initial ELS-module order, several NPO engagements are live, and for the lead CPO customer high-volume scale-up shipments begin 2H 2027 with customer deployment in 2028. On 12 August Coherent said it had seen *no* CPO demand push-out and that interest had increased; scale-out CPO revenue (rack-to-rack) begins 2H 2026, scale-up (multi-rack inside a cluster) follows in 2027, and it is engaged with multiple customers on both CPO and NPO. Damnang treats those two calls as falsifying SemiAnalysis’s “NVIDIA scale-up CPO past 2029” claim. OCS is the second August print: Google moving from in-house to external sourcing; Lumentum >$400M backlog from three new customers and ~$400M shippable in 2H 2026; Coherent already at initial revenue and, on 12 August, a >$4B OCS market call. Aehr is the third: FY2027 revenue guided $130–150M (2.6–3× prior year) with $100.6M effective backlog already in; lead silicon-photonics customer on an unmanned automated line; a follow-on production order in early August; a new customer ordered four systems within months of first contact and took delivery. Test-equipment orders are framed as a leading indicator one-to-several quarters to more than a year ahead of the customer ramp.

The “why optics again” section is valuation, not a new demand story. Coherent’s FY2027 Q1 guide has a low end above the quarter just printed and gross margin is heading up. What fell was the multiple. Optical connectivity still scales with AI cluster size; the content opportunity is similar on CPO or NPO — the same point the 8 August piece made. Supply remains the bottleneck: InP wafer capacity is expected to grow 20–30× through 2029 and still not keep up (JX Advanced Metals / JPMorgan color via Zephyr); Lumentum’s last-quarter revenue and operating margin rising together is read as the bottleneck converting to profit. Sentiment is back: 4 August FCC draft-rule report on new Chinese optical-transceiver imports (already vaulted as [[Research/2026-08-04 - LITE COHR Optical Trade Phase Two Geopolitics - deep-dive]]); LYTE, an optics-only ETF, traded $72M on its 6 August first day — bullish flow and a crowding signal.

The selection thesis has two parts. First, how much exposure a name has to which *content* among pluggable, OCS, NPO, CPO, and scale-across, and whether that exposure grows. Second, whether it holds the *bottleneck* needed to produce that content. Axis one is “do not lose the dollar whichever form factor wins.” Axis two is “can set price when demand rises.”

L2 lasers are the best fit on both axes. Lasers are needed if pluggable lasts, if NPO wins, and *more* so as high-power external sources if CPO wins; OCS is incremental demand on top. All four highlighted names — Lumentum, Coherent, AAOI, Sivers — are treated as same-theme laser beta in round two, with Lumentum and Coherent as the core pair to accumulate on dips and stand aside on spikes. Coherent is watched slightly more closely for vertical integration (already argued in the 8 August deep-dive). AAOI’s CPO tell is the 400 mW-class high-power laser from its own Sugar Land epi fab: CPO/NPO share one external source across channels, so output must cover splitting loss, and sample-to-volume is what separates “CPO supply-chain” from “transceiver assembler.” US production is the FCC-optionality sleeve. Sivers is the small-cap most tightly tied to CPO/NPO spread: Glasgow InP DFB arrays are the ELS-module core; multi-wavelength from one device into the engine; ELS work with O-Net, Enablence, and POET; prior Ayar Labs light-source supply. Caveats are explicit: AAOI datacenter revenue more than doubled in a year but GM is below 30% and GAAP still a quarterly net loss; Sivers’ revenue base is small versus ELS assets and volume conversion is unconfirmed. AAOI is an accumulate-on-dips name; Sivers is approached as short-term trading.

L6B (fiber/connectivity) is the cleanest form-factor-agnostic layer: fiber is needed if pluggable lasts, and under CPO fiber moves inside the switch box with count and unit price up; scale-up across racks adds rack-to-rack links. Corning: own fiber and CPO connectors; NVIDIA partnership to expand US capacity 10×; multi-year Amazon supply; optical communications 44% of core revenue, but the rest is display glass, mobile cover, auto, solar polysilicon. Amphenol: “interconnect neutrality”; Q2 IT datacom 43% of revenue, +89% YoY; AI-related revenue >$10.5B annualized run-rate; CommScope CCS optical-interconnect wholesale, that book’s IT datacom doubled in a year. Breadth is wider than Corning’s because copper-in-rack still pays; the risk is undisclosed optical share inside IT datacom and CCS integration. Fujikura is named and not highlighted — datacenter optical exposure not separately confirmed. L6A (modules) is where 800G/1.6T pluggables print profit *now* and where some sockets survive NPO/scale-up. AAOI is re-used from L2; Fabrinet is the FCC-spillover watch into the 17 August print. L6C (scale-across / DCI / coherent transport) is the long-horizon pie: cluster → pod → campus → DC grows DCI regardless of whether the engine sits in-package or out; Coherent named DCI/scale-across as a communications growth driver. Ciena and Nokia are hold-for-duration, low optical purity, telecom-cycle contamination; a promised later deep-dive.

L3/L4: Marvell is “the company that internalized every ingredient… the company NVIDIA needs most” — pointing at the vault’s owned electro-optics franchise rather than the rented ASIC seat. Credo is the L3 favorite: AEC if copper lasts; optical DSP and SiPh PIC if optics accelerates; DustPhotonics internalization; FY2027 optical revenue guided >$600M. Astera Labs is viewed favorably and *not* highlighted: PCIe retimers / scale-up fabric move independently of the optical argument; optics is fiber-to-chip via aiXscale with no revenue yet, versus Credo already printing optical dollars. Semtech via March HieFo (InP gain chips and DFB) is an L2-direction name: high-single-digit dollars of TIA/driver in an 800G module versus ~$80 in a 3.2T module once InP lasers and gain chips attach — ~10× content per module per generation; datacenter still low-20s percent of company revenue, so mix shift is the re-rate.

L1/L5/L7 are bottleneck seats, not device makers. One name per layer: AXT (InP substrate — the bottleneck itself), Tower (SiPh wafer volume regardless of transceiver type), AEHR (KGD / WLBI across CPO and HBM; optical angle is known-good-die sort before package, orders leading the customer ramp). AXT’s 30 July print is the InP number: Q2 revenue $47.59M (+164% YoY, +77% QoQ); InP $30.7M, a record, ~65% of total; GM 8.0% → 44.9%; swing to $11.13M net income; InP backlog >$100M; Q3 guide ~$66M (+39% QoQ). Capacity plan: quarterly InP to $60M by end-2026 and ~$130M by end-2027. Coherent $22.3M advance on a three-year 6-inch InP supply agreement; Lumentum up to $87.5M on a six-year capacity reservation. The risk is China production-entity and export-permit pacing; Q3 guide includes only permitted or permit-not-required volume. Tower 11 August Q2: SiPh revenue +270% YoY to a >$680M annualized run-rate, $1B Q4 run-rate target, $1.3B of 2027 revenue locked with $290M prepayments, >50 SiPh customers. Risks: foundry-low margins, contract-to-shipment conversion; author says optical catalysts are already in the price and the work from here is watching quarterly run-rate versus contracted volume. AEHR is “holders’ territory” after a >4× move from the March piece — do not chase; use volatility.

## Framework / Mental Model

Damnang’s reusable object is a two-axis layer screen on top of the April L1–L7 optical map.

**Axis 1 — form-factor-agnostic content.** Score a name by whether dollars survive (or grow) across pluggable, OCS, NPO, CPO, and scale-across. Best scores: L2 lasers (needed in every architecture; high-power ELS *rises* under CPO; OCS is extra) and L6B fiber/connectors (count and ASP up when fiber moves inside the box and when scale-up adds rack-to-rack).

**Axis 2 — bottleneck ownership.** Score whether the name can set price when demand rises (InP substrate, high-power laser process, SiPh wafer, KGD/WLBI) versus assembling a contestable module.

**Layer map (this piece’s application).** L1 materials (AXT) → L2 lasers (LITE, COHR, AAOI, SIVE) → L3 connectivity IC (CRDO, SMTC; ALAB not highlighted) → L4 PIC/platform (MRVL) → L5 SiPh foundry (TSEM) → L6A modules (AAOI, FN) / L6B infra (GLW, APH) / L6C transport (CIEN, NOK) → L7 test (AEHR).

**Yellow-highlight rule.** Priority watch ≠ buy. Unhighlighted ≠ uninvestable. Fujikura and Astera Labs are the explicit “good company, wrong axis this round” examples.

**How this framework can be wrong.** If scale-up CPO really does slip past 2029 *and* NPO/OCS/pluggable do not backfill the same laser and fiber dollars; if InP 20–30× capacity actually arrives on time and clears the high-power band before volume; if LYTE + FCC narrative is crowding rather than a fundamental re-rate.
