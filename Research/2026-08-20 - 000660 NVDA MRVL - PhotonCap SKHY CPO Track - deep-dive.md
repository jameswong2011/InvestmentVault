## Summary

On 20 August 2026 SK hynix’s newsroom posted that a co-packaged-optics roadmap paper had appeared in Nature Electronics, framed as AI competition shifting from chips to systems. PhotonCap treats the post as a positioning signal rather than a CPO product schedule. The paper is the Review “Co-packaged optics for high-performance computing and artificial intelligence,” submitted 18 November 2025 and accepted 2026-07-08 (DOI 10.1038/s41928-026-01681-6). Corresponding authors named by the company are Seunghoon Hong, AI Infra team lead at SK hynix, and Professor Kyusang Lee of the University of Virginia. Collaborating institutions listed in the Korean newsroom are UIUC, NTU, MIT and Yonsei; the official X post on the afternoon of 19 August local time (Korea-date 20 August) keeps only “@SKhynix and University of Virginia researchers” and drops MIT/UIUC even though their author share is not small. AI Infra was stood up in January 2024 and, by the company’s own description, groups global sales and marketing, the HBM business, and Memory System Research (pathfinding beyond HBM). The newsroom footnoted what a corresponding author is — principal authors who oversee planning through submission, peer-review response, and post-publication questions — which PhotonCap reads as a tell for where the weight of the announcement sits. CPO here means putting the optical transceiver inside the same package as the processor. The author opened the 15-page paper next to the post, then walked the public record of all twelve authors. The track did not start with the paper. The company post has not one line about the step before it. What holds up is that the technology choice was already marked in money, with all three memory makers leaving that mark side by side.

The paper’s own term for the bottleneck is bandwidth wall (memory wall appears once, in a reference title). Scaling on the same axis: every two years compute throughput 3x, DRAM bandwidth 1.6x, interconnect bandwidth 1.4x, while AI-model training compute jumped 8.8x. The newsroom quotes only 3x and 1.4x and drops the DRAM 1.6x curve — a curious edit from a memory company. The paper’s sentence: gains are confined to intra-package communication; once data goes beyond the memory-logic boundary, electrical interconnects incur resistive and capacitive losses that greatly increase with frequency and distance. HBM’s TSV-stack terabit-class gain does not follow the data out of the package. PhotonCap has run this diagnosis before (three routes around the memory wall; optical memory pooling). A memory company putting its name on that chart and issuing a release is the part that changed. The company post summarizes three technical targets that are also in the paper: more than 100 Tb/s of bandwidth per node, energy below 1 pJ per bit, chip-to-chip latency under 10 ns. Table 2 in the paper — representative industrial CPO and optical I/O platforms — mixes shipped product, demonstrated prototypes, and some projected figures. Ten rows, nine companies: Broadcom Bailly and Davisson, NVIDIA Quantum-X Photonics, Intel OCI, Ayar Labs TeraPHY, Lightmatter Passage M1000, Celestial AI Photonic Fabric, Ranovus, Avicena, Microsoft MOSAIC. Switch companies, accelerator companies, and optical I/O startups are in; memory makers are out. A later figure puts a memory pool and an XPU pool facing each other across a single photonic interposer; the company post ran that diagram. The side with no name in the table takes up half of the future architecture. The word CXL never appears (full-text search). UCIe shows up five times; NVLink and PCIe twice each. Die-to-die and network standards are covered; the current electrical standard for memory pooling is the one that is missing. PhotonCap will not push a missing word into a deliberate exclusion. Readers should account for the gap. The right reading is that the review’s scope sits on the optical-architecture side.

This announcement is the first partial answer to a July monitoring item: whether the memory-system CPO reviewed at SK hynix Future Forum 2025 turns into an actual R&D program or a partner disclosure. The forum was 2025-09-11 at the Icheon campus. Third agenda: KAIST Professor Kyoungsik Yu and SK hynix Vice President Hoyoung Son, head of Advanced PKG Development — a package-development organization, not strategy — on “A future connected by light: CPO technology evolution and its ripple effects on the memory industry.” Content, per the newsroom: silicon-photonics technology types, application inside and around the memory system, and preparation across Architecture, Design, Device and Package. An academic partner is now disclosed and a company employee is a corresponding author. That is still not confirmation an R&D program is running, so the item stays half filled. Walking the twelve-author list shows the collaboration’s character. MIT’s Jeehwan Kim pioneered remote epitaxy (2D material underneath so a single-crystal film can be peeled). Corresponding author Kyusang Lee was in that lab around 2016–2017 before Virginia (PhD Michigan, postdoc MIT, faculty 2017). UIUC’s Hyunseok Kim, also corresponding, was in the same lab 2019–2023, joined UIUC spring 2024, and picked up a Samsung GRO grant on micro-LED epitaxy and integration. Young Jin Yoo, one of six co-first authors, is still a postdoc in that lab on integrated photonics. Three of six corresponding authors are Jeehwan Kim and two alumni; a current lab member is co-first. NTU’s Sanghoon Chae passed through MIT in Jing Kong’s group and Columbia in James Hone’s; Georgii Zograf is nonlinear nanophotonics out of the Chalmers line. The academic list sits closer to heterogeneous integration — peeling films and moving them onto another substrate — than to silicon-photonics foundries. It looks less like a broad industry-academia collaboration and more like SK hynix attaching itself to one lab’s network. Transferable membrane photonic components, remote epitaxy and layer transfer sit in the paper’s near-term Outlook paragraph; the optics-centric structure, photonic interposer and optical TSV sit on the long-term side. The authors’ own line of work lands in the near-term box. Among the twelve there is no academic author whose day job is DRAM devices or memory circuits on public record. The memory share is one seat, SK hynix. Career composition cannot settle which direction the knowledge traveled.

The track actually starts on 2025-05-14. Avicena, micro-LED chip-to-chip optical interconnect, announced a $65M Series B led by Tiger Global with SK hynix on the investor list; Lam Research and Hitachi Ventures are said to be in the same round. Rene Do, Senior Director of Venture Investment at SK hynix, is quoted on disaggregated memory fabrics, the reach limit of high-speed electrical interconnects, laser-based links being too power hungry, and microLED-based interconnects like Avicena’s LightBundle as a promising low-power solution for future memory fabrics — plus excitement to join the round and potentially collaborate on next-generation memory interconnects. “Disaggregated memory fabric” is the name the company can put on the paper’s Fig. 4b memory-pool / XPU-pool drawing, fifteen months before the paper. “Potentially” keeps the third sentence short of a confirmed partnership; the company is marking a spot between a pure financial position and a development partnership. Dates in order: 2025-05-14 Avicena Series B; 2025-09-11 Future Forum CPO agenda; SK AI Summit 2025 in Seoul 3–4 November, where Kyusang Lee presented “Evolution Into Next-Generation CPO-based Connectivity Technology for Implementing Massive Computational Networks”; paper submitted 18 November 2025. The newsroom introduces SK hynix-organized session speakers as Vice President Kyoung Park (Biz. Insight), Vice President Youngpyo Joo (System Architecture), and Professor Kyusang Lee together; Senior Vice President Hoshik Kim of Memory System Research appeared separately on a keynote panel with David Patterson and Philip Wong. Lee is the outside speaker placed in the same row as company executives. Which of the two summit days he presented is not in the newsroom, so the gap to submission is 14 or 15 days. PhotonCap does not read that order as coincidence and also will not claim the schedules were coordinated. The only university that survived in the X wording is the same Virginia professor SK hynix put on its own stage. Lee flagged laser heat and wavelength drift and raised micro-LED sources as an alternative (summit newsroom); the August newsroom interview named micro-LED again as the next research direction. Three public touchpoints: an investor release, a company-event newsroom, and this announcement. The paper spends space there too: Avicena LightBundle a 304-channel micro-LED array on 16 nm CMOS for roughly 1 Tbps at about 1 pJ per bit; Microsoft MOSAIC’s 460-channel scaling outlook; at 800 Gbps the micro-LED approach draws 3.1–5.3 W per end against 9.8–12 W per end for laser-based active optical cables. One micro-LED reference is the co-authors’ own 2023 Nature paper on vertically stacked full-color micro-LEDs via 2D-material layer transfer (Jeehwan Kim corresponding). A review pointing at its own camp is common; readers should factor it in. Public signals reach into the micro-LED path. Nothing lets you read it as dropping lasers. Interest showed up first in an investment fifteen months ahead of the paper.

Wind the Avicena list back and Samsung Catalyst Fund, Cerberus, Clear Ventures, and Micron Ventures as a strategic investor are on the $25M Series A of 2 August 2022. Micron described it then as a strategic opportunity to support innovation in HPC architectures. All three memory makers put names on this company’s investor list: Samsung and Micron in 2022, SK hynix in 2025. Holdings are not disclosed, so whether any of them still hold is unverifiable; round participation is on the record. On the same micro-LED interconnect bet, SK hynix came in 2 years and 9 months later. The company that is ahead in HBM is the late one on this layer. Overlay the Samsung GRO micro-LED grant to UIUC’s Hyunseok Kim and Samsung money is inside the academic lineage too. On 3 September 2025 Avicena named Marco Chisari CEO — former EVP who headed Samsung Foundries and the Samsung Semiconductor Innovation Center; founder Bardia Pezeshki moved to CTO. SK hynix is listed as a member of the IOWN Global Forum (set up 2019 by NTT, Intel and Sony; subject optical disaggregation). Venture investment, forum membership and paper co-authorship are activities at the standards layer rather than product programs. PhotonCap’s interpretation, labeled as interpretation: SK hynix is organizing the board late — late on the investment, so it takes a position on the standards and roadmap side instead. No source has a sentence tying the investment, the executive hire and the paper into one decision. That Seunghoon Hong’s day job is long-term strategy does not contradict it. Material the other way: the Future Forum speaker was the head of Advanced PKG Development, so the strategy side is not the only place in the company that has talked about CPO in public. Across fifteen months, venture investment, package development and long-term strategy each showed up on a public stage with this subject. That is the heaviest fact on this track. An earlier PhotonCap piece mapped listed-ticker exposure to the micro-LED light source; this piece adds the demand-side axis where three memory makers line up.

Is this something that attaches to next year’s earnings. The paper’s numbers make that hard. A rack draws 30–100 kW; to hold interconnect power under 10% the requirement is getting below 1 pJ per bit. Reported current optical I/O chiplet values: Intel OCI about 5 pJ per bit, Ayar Labs TeraPHY under 5, Lightmatter Passage 4.6 on a wall-plug basis. Against pluggable transceivers at 15–30 that is a long way down, and the target line is still several times away. What holds the gap open: residual DSP and FEC, laser wall-plug efficiency, thermal regulation and SerDes power, none of which shrink just because photonic devices get better. That is where the micro-LED path gets its appeal — the device modulates directly, so the high-speed electrical conversion step can come out. The price: emission spectrum over 10 nm wide against under 1 pm for a laser, beam spread requiring custom micro-optics, reach in the tens of meters. The paper’s position is that these constraints do not matter much inside CPO because chip-to-chip distances are short. On the memory side the paper lists three long-term tasks as equally necessary, first among them “optical memory interfaces capable of preserving coherence and minimizing latency.” Coherence protocols are extremely sensitive to round-trip delay. Going optical buys bandwidth but adds conversion stages and link latency. The target is under 10 ns chip to chip; in real systems, once switch hops, host SerDes, residual FEC and network-protocol overhead pile on, end-to-end round trip lands in the microsecond regime. A fast optical link and faster memory access remain two different things. Semiconductor laser emission wavelengths shift by tens of picometres per kelvin; the paper writes that this drift becomes a problem when the CPO laser shares a thermal envelope with the ASIC or accelerator. What happens when HBM enters the same budget is not settled. DRAM is temperature-sensitive to begin with; PhotonCap flags that combination as the author’s guess, not the paper’s claim. Rene Do’s lasers-too-power-hungry line and Lee’s summit point on heat and wavelength drift point at the same problem. Lee assessed the technology as having left the laboratory and entered the early stage of industrialization — not volume production. The paper puts chiplet-based heterogeneous integration on the near-term path and the photonic interposer and optical TSV after that.

Limits first. A review paper and a press release are not a product-roadmap filing, not a mass-production promise, and carry no development-stage or schedule information. Series B participation is not an adoption decision. “Potentially” is still attached to collaboration, and there has been no follow-up that collaboration started. How much SK hynix put in, and whether it is also in other optical-interconnect companies, is undisclosed. The paper carries no SK hynix data or demonstration results; a Review by format summarizes other people’s results. March 2026 domestic reporting said SK hynix was developing a structure that puts optical devices inside the HBM package; the company officially denied it. The report rests on anonymous expert comment, so it is not used as evidence. What was denied then and what the company has put out under its own name are different layers: product-development claim denied; investment and roadmap paper on its own channels. Documents pointing the same way came out three times in three forms — money, company-event stage, academic journal — inside fifteen months, aimed at the same technology. The optical memory interface is a spec that foundries making interposers, logic companies making accelerators, and companies selling optical engines decide together with the memory company, so the side that got directly priced by this investment is the optical-interconnect vendor. Until an interface spec shows up as a document, this announcement alone is not something that moves a price target on SKHY. Falsifiers the author will pull timing forward on: an optical-interface paper carrying SK hynix’s own measured data at the next conference, or an optical I/O line written explicitly into an HBM roadmap announcement. Falsifier the other way: Avicena does not name a memory customer in its next round or production announcement, and no SK hynix name turns up in primary CPO material beyond this paper and this release through the first half of 2027. Dated checkpoints: SK hynix next-generation HBM technology session at SEMICON Taiwan in September 2026; memory-interface session at ISSCC in February 2027; SK AI Summit 2026 likely around November (official schedule not out) — who takes the outside-professor seat this year is the one the author is watching most; Avicena naming a memory customer, on the release rather than a calendar. The highest-value mark in the piece is the May 2025 collaboration possibility turning into an actual joint-development announcement.
