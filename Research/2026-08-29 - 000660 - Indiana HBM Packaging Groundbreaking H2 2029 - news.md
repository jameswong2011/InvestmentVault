# Groundbreaking Ceremony in Indiana for SK Hynix HBM

## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices US HBM onshoring as 2027–28 bit-supply relief and the 14 August Korea Herald dated Indiana mass production in 2H 2028; the official 28 August 2026 SK hynix release firms a >$4 billion West Lafayette advanced-packaging, test and R&D hub whose cleanroom opens by October 2028 and whose first 'Made in USA' next-generation HBM mass production begins only in H2 2029, with cutting-edge wafers still produced in Korea and shipped to Indiana for back-end work, so the US print is packaging proximity for later-decade custom HBM rather than a wafer-fab that loosens 2026–27 scarcity. That is a ~one-year slip versus earlier plan chatter and supports [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] long-cycle customer intimacy and Industry #15 multi-region footprint without touching Rubin-allocation HIGH/LOW/CLOSE or near-term HBM bit supply; conviction unchanged.

## Summary
SK hynix announced from Seoul on 28 August 2026 that it held the groundbreaking ceremony on the 27th (local time) at Holloway Gymnasium, Purdue University, West Lafayette, Indiana, for its first US next-generation High Bandwidth Memory advanced-packaging production base, framed as 'beginning a new future for US-Korea AI' and as a Korea-integrated HBM hub rather than a front-end wafer fab. The company puts total expected investment at over $4 billion, schedules the cleanroom to open by October 2028, and initiates mass production of next-generation HBM in the second half of 2029, with a workforce of approximately 1,000 personnel during anticipated commercial operations and a claim that the site grows into a key US HBM production hub; cutting-edge wafers produced by SK hynix in Korea will be delivered to Indiana for advanced packaging and testing, after which 'Made in USA' products are supplied to US customers, so the onshore content is back-end stack/test, not DRAM wafer starts. An 'Advanced Packaging R&D Testbed' is to be built alongside the production lines so that customers, universities and business partners can jointly develop next-generation packaging technologies and execute prototype fabrication and performance validation, and the release defines advanced packaging as back-end technology that combines multiple chips into a single package or stacks them vertically to raise performance once micro-fabrication has hit physical limits. Attendees named on the official page are Indiana Governor Mike Braun, US Senator Todd Young, Purdue University President Mitch Daniels, Korea-US Alliance Foundation Chairman Robert Abrams, South Korean Ambassador Kang Kyung-wha, SK Group Vice Chairman Yu Jeong-Joon, SK Group Vice Chairman Lee Hyung-hee, and SK hynix CEO Kwak Noh-Jung.

The operating argument in the release is ecosystem and alliance, not 2026–27 bit relief: more than 100 companies are being considered as suppliers for materials, components and equipment at West Lafayette; approximately 7,000 direct and indirect jobs are claimed with local and national companies across construction and subsequent commercial operations; a Memorandum of Understanding with Purdue commits both sides to joint research on system integration and advanced packaging technologies including HBM, plus Midwest talent cultivation; and SK Group will participate as a hiring corporation in the 'USFK Veterans Job Platform' operated by the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry and the Korea-US Alliance Foundation, honouring Indiana's deployment of approximately 140,000 troops during the Korean War. Senator Young ties the project to the CHIPS and Science Act and to rebuilding the US semiconductor industry; Governor Braun casts it as regional jobs and 'industries of the future'; Ambassador Kang calls it the first AI-memory production facility and R&D centre in the United States rather than a mere manufacturing spend; Kwak says the United States is the epicentre of AI innovation (premier customers, top-tier R&D, partners) and that the Indiana fab will be a gateway to deliver the first 'Made in USA' HBM products, with a typo ('proudcts') left in the source quote. The release's own disclaimer marks the dates and dollar envelope as forward-looking and unverified; the page does not name HBM4E, a Q3 2029 start, wafer starts per month, or CHIPS grant/loan dollars.
