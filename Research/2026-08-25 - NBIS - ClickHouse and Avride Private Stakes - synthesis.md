# NBIS — ClickHouse and Avride: what the two private stakes are worth to Nebius, and when

## Thesis Delta

The 'self-funding reserve' in Insight #3 fails on size, timing and use. Nebius's own balance sheet carries ClickHouse at roughly $1.5B at 30 June 2026 (the $89.7M cost basis plus $50M of Series C warrants and two ASC 321 step-ups of $597.4M and $780.6M), which is 35–39% of the $3.75–4.2B headline pro-rata the thesis uses, and the same ~39% ratio held at the Series C mark, so the discount is structural rather than a one-off judgement. Avride has never had a priced third-party round: the '~83%' in the stake table is Nebius's holding net of a 17% employee award pool, Uber's money is a $100M SAFE plus a $75M milestone tranche carried as a Level 3 liability, and the $2.2–2.3B is an analyst construct with no transaction behind it. Neither asset can produce cash inside the 2026–27 capex window (ClickHouse guides an IPO 'within the next few years' and Nebius has said it will wait for that event; Avride consumed $82.7M of adjusted EBITDA in 2025 on $1.3M of revenue and took $200M from Nebius in October), and the revealed preference is now on the tape: about $15.7B of external capital raised in 2026 through March converts, NVIDIA warrants, the ATM, the ABL and the $5.75B of converts that closed on 24 August, against zero stake sales and $250M flowing the other way. Conviction unchanged (medium): at 3–13% of a ~$59B capitalisation depending on the mark, the stakes cannot decide an energisation binary either way. Insight #3 should be re-graded from 'self-funding reserve' to long-dated venture optionality that is pro-cyclical to the core, and the August converts put the LOW tripwire (>15% dilutive raise in six months) within reach on a converted basis, which needs a formal /conviction-audit rather than a judgement here.

## Summary

Nebius does not mark ClickHouse to the headline. Having lost 'significant influence' in 2022 (the 20-F's phrase; the year ClickHouse Inc. put distance between itself and its Yandex parentage), the stake sits under the ASC 321 measurement alternative: cost, less impairment, stepped up only when an orderly transaction in a similar security gives an observable price, and then only to the fair value of the securities Nebius actually holds, which a back-solve allocates across the capital structure and discounts for illiquidity. The Series C step-up ($597.4M, May 2025) took the original stake to $687M against a pro-rata $1.78B at $6.35B (39%); the Series D step-up ($780.6M, Q1 2026) took it to roughly $1.5B including the warrants against $3.75–4.2B at $15B (35–39%). Two readings fit that stable ratio and both are unfavourable to the thesis's number: either the fully diluted stake is materially below the 28% that has been repeated since the 2021 spin (four preferred rounds, six stock acquisitions and pool refreshes later, 18–22% is the arithmetic), or the common Nebius holds is worth a large discount to Dragoneer's preferred once preference, lock-up and marketability are priced. Nebius itself now discloses only a 'significant minority equity stake', which we read as the wording a company chooses when the percentage no longer flatters, and the auditors flagged the valuation as a critical audit matter. We take the truth to sit between the two marks, and note that the $50M of Series C warrants are exercisable only on an IPO, sale or transfer, a structure that says Nebius is holding for the liquidity event and not for a secondary.

As a business ClickHouse is the strongest asset in the group after the AI cloud itself, and the Series D price already assumes that. Annualised revenue reached $250M in May 2026, roughly tripling year on year, on ~4,000 cloud customers (from 3,000 in January), fewer than 100 quota-carrying reps and no SDR or CSM layer: the distribution is the open-source engine (47,000+ GitHub stars), the Yandex-era engineering reputation and, increasingly, the fact that frontier models recommend ClickHouse when asked for a real-time analytics store, a channel the CEO calls new and which we read as an emergent form of standard control. The AI labs are the anchor logos (OpenAI ingests petabytes of logs a day into a 90-shard cluster growing 20% a month; Anthropic runs an air-gapped copy of the ClickHouse Cloud architecture with ClickHouse's team and credits it as instrumental to shipping Claude 4), with Tesla, Meta, Cloudflare, Shopify and Capital One alongside a long tail of AI-native start-ups (Lovable, Harvey, Sierra, Cursor, Polymarket). At $15B the round paid ~60x ARR, which per point of growth (0.24x) is cheaper than Databricks at 27x run-rate and >80% growth (0.34x) or Snowflake and Datadog at ~21x and ~25%; the mark is defensible, but it discounts two further years above 100% growth. The base rate for that is thin but not empty (Snowflake grew 124% and 106% through the same ARR band), so a 2027 IPO on $600M–1B of ARR at 20–27x gives $12–27B: the Series D is in effect a forward mark, and the stake's upside from here is 1–1.8x in the good case rather than the 3–5x the more excitable sell-side notes imply. The 'high-nine-digit' year-end ARR target reported in May requires a re-acceleration to ~4x a year from the observed January–May pace, which annualises to $450–550M; the thesis's '$700–900M guided' carries that optimism forward without the caveat.

Avride is the opposite case: real operating progress, no external mark, and two gating items that are regulatory and competitive rather than financial. The fleet went from 80 cars in December to more than 200 Hyundai Ioniq 5s in May (most of them R&D), with over a million autonomous miles in the first five months of 2026 and 60,000 commercial Uber rides in Dallas since the 3 December launch, all with a safety operator in the seat; at Uber's 25–30 trips per vehicle per day that is the output of 10–15 vehicle-equivalents, which is where a nine-square-mile map with monitors aboard lands. No-vehicle-operator service is the whole valuation question, and it now runs into NHTSA's preliminary evaluation (opened 6 May) of 16 crashes between December and March whose common thread is the driving policy itself, lane changes into traffic and failure to slow for stopped vehicles, rather than a component; and into Waymo, which opened fully driverless public service in Dallas on 4 August through its own app with Avis running the depot. Avride's only commercial market thus has the category leader driverless while Avride is still supervised, and Uber, whose roster now carries vehicle commitments of 35,000 (Lucid/Nuro), 25,000 (Waabi), 10,000 (Rivian) and 5,000 (Stellantis/NVIDIA) against no number for Avride, is structurally a buyer of cheap options on many suppliers, which is why its money came as a $100M SAFE with a $75M milestone tranche at a 20% conversion discount rather than a priced round. The robots are the part that works today (316,000 US deliveries in 2025, over 600,000 since inception, 1,200 a day at Ohio State, a Chartwells master agreement in July and Starship's June exit from US campuses handing over the channel), but Serve Robotics, the listed comparable with 2,000 robots, cut its 2026 revenue guide from $26M to $9–10M this month on weak Uber Eats volumes and trades at $433M, which brackets what a robot fleet is worth. A priced third-party round, which the 20-F says Nebius is 'actively pursuing' and which 'may cede control', is the realistic 2026–27 event: it would set a first mark somewhere in the Wayve ($8.6B) to May Mobility ($0.7–1.3B) corridor, most plausibly $1.5–3B, dilute Nebius toward 55–65% after the SAFE converts, deconsolidate roughly $100M a year of losses, and deliver no cash to the parent unless a secondary is attached. The '$10–20B exit by early 2028' in the thesis is a scenario from analyst notes rather than a management target; it requires no-operator service at scale in several cities inside eighteen months against Waymo, Tesla and a Lyft entry in the same city.

For the NBIS position the arithmetic is simple and the framing change is the point. At $218 and 271.9M shares (the 6-K count; FMP's $52B uses only the ~240M Class A) the capitalisation is ~$59B: the stakes are 12–14% of it at the thesis's headline, 2.7% at book and 5–13% on our realisable ranges, and every path to cash runs through 2028, after the 2026–27 build that the thesis says decides the name. Management's behaviour confirms the reading: in 2026 Nebius has raised ~$15.7B externally (March converts $4.34B, NVIDIA pre-funded warrants $2B, ATM $2.85B at $223.6, ABL $775M, and $5.75B of converts on 24 August at $313–325 conversion prices, 40–45% above the $223.90 spot), taken $7.9B of customer prepayments onto the balance sheet as deferred revenue, and sold nothing; it put $50M into ClickHouse warrants and $200M into Avride instead. Volozh's 52% voting control makes that patience rational, since a holder who believes ClickHouse compounds should issue converts at a premium rather than sell common at a pre-IPO discount, but it means the reserve is inert exactly in the window the thesis assigned it. The stakes are also not a hedge: ClickHouse's growth is a derivative of frenzy-phase AI spending (log volumes at OpenAI, AI-native start-ups as customers), and Avride's value rests on Uber's demand and NVIDIA's compute, so the scenario that widens the capex gap violently (a risk-off in AI infrastructure) is the scenario that shuts the ClickHouse IPO window and cuts its customers' spend. We therefore re-grade Insight #3 from 'self-funding reserve' to 'long-dated, pro-cyclical venture optionality worth 5–13% of the cap', keep Outstanding Q#5 but re-date its answer to 2028, and flag that the August converts, added to the Q2 ATM and the March notes, put ~22% of the capitalisation through the primary market in six months; whether that fires the LOW tripwire depends on whether the trigger counts converts before conversion, which it should now define.

## Framework / Mental Model

Held as hypotheses per the READING PROTOCOL in [[Generalist - Overview]].

- **Value Layer Monopoly · ClickHouse.** Layer: the real-time analytical engine for machine-generated event data (logs, traces, product events) at petabyte scale. Structural cluster STRONG: non-rivalrous software, data-gravity switching costs at petabyte scale, and a new standard-control channel in frontier models recommending the engine by default. Durability cluster WEAK: Apache 2.0 makes the layer forkable and self-hostable (ByConity, Tinybird, Altinity, Aiven, the Chinese clouds; OpenAI runs its own cluster), and the pitch is cost (1/20th of Datadog on storage, '200x' on ClickStack), so pricing power is capped by the company's own positioning. Disqualifiers: commoditising-layer risk from the open core; a political ceiling from Yandex provenance (the 2022 loss of 'significant influence' was the distancing). AI overlay: infrastructure-adjacent (agent telemetry through Langfuse and ClickStack), moat widening if agents generate the event data, contested from above by Snowflake and Databricks converging on real-time plus Postgres (ClickHouse answered with its own Postgres service) and from below by DuckDB/MotherDuck. Verdict for a ClickHouse investor: WEAK-to-STRONG emerging fit, already priced at 60x. Verdict for the NBIS holder: the layer question is beside the point; NBIS owns a diluting minority whose realisable value is set by the IPO window, not by the moat. Kill criteria: cloud ARR growth below 100% for two consecutive quarters; a hyperscaler-native managed ClickHouse; a licence change (which would signal the open-core moat failing).
- **Value Layer Monopoly · Avride.** No layer. It rents demand (Uber, ~20% take and the customer relationship), the vehicle (Hyundai), and compute (NVIDIA), and competes on the contested policy layer against Waymo's ~3,800 of ~4,100 paid US robotaxis. NO FIT; layer-renter three times over. The robots are a niche operator in campus food delivery where Starship's exit gives share and Serve's guide cut shows the ceiling.
- **[G-7] ROIIC × runway.** ClickHouse is an old mine from the Yandex era compounding on ~$140M of Nebius capital (cost basis plus warrants); Avride's incremental capital ($200M in 2025) earns negative ROIIC until no-operator service; the group's new mine is the AI cloud, and the stakes do not extend its runway.
- **[G-10] base rates.** Persistence of >100% growth from $250M ARR is rare but has data-infrastructure precedent (Snowflake FY2021–22). Independent robotaxi start-ups without a hyperscaler or OEM parent reaching $10B+: Wayve at $8.6B is the only live case, and it sells software to OEMs rather than running a fleet; Cruise and Argo are the base-rate reminders.
- **[G-13] expectations.** At $218 the price embeds the stakes somewhere between book and headline, a ~$7B swing on a $59B cap. The operating variable that matters is unchanged (energised MW); the stake variable that matters is the 2027 IPO window, not ClickHouse's product.
- **[G-4] Perez.** Both stakes are installation-phase beneficiaries of the same AI surge as the core (ClickHouse's AI-native customer base and lab log volumes; Avride's Uber and NVIDIA dependence). Optionality that correlates with the core is not a reserve.
- **Management & Culture [MC-1] / [MC-2].** The optionality feed is real and dated (ClickHouse spun out in 2021 at $2B, $15B by 2026; Toloka's external round 2025; the Avride–Uber agreement 2025), which supports Insight #4's provenance argument. Founder control (LASTAR ~52% vote) explains the refusal to sell at a pre-IPO discount. Execution on Avride ([MC-1c]) is the weak link: 16 supervised crashes and no-operator service later than Waymo in the same city.
- **Automation Lens B.** ClickHouse sits in the execution path for agent telemetry, the enterprise-data-infrastructure battleground in §6. Toloka's data-labelling layer is the one frontier labs most actively in-house or synthesise; Scale AI's client exodus after the Meta transaction is the precedent, though Toloka's Q2 equity-method income of $12.7M to Nebius suggests the business is currently profitable.

## Contradiction Check

**Challenges:**
1. Insight #3 ('a self-funding reserve the market discounts as a conglomerate distraction'): the market is not discounting the stakes; sell-side SOTP work carries them at headline pro-rata, above Nebius's own marks. If there is a mispricing it runs the other way, and the reserve cannot be drawn in the window that matters.
2. §Business Model stake table: '~83% following the Uber-led round' is pre-Uber and net of the 17% pool; the '$2.7B post' has no transaction behind it; 'guided $700–900M' ARR needs the pace caveat; 'OpenAI ... customers' overstates what is disclosed (OpenAI runs the open-source engine; the commercial relationship is not public).
3. §Key Metrics 'Non-core stakes ~$7–8B': add the book figure (~$1.6B) and a realisable range with dates.
4. §Outstanding Q#5 ('before or after the capex gap bites?'): the answer is 'after', 2028 at the earliest, unless a secondary appears; re-date rather than close.
5. §Conviction Triggers, HIGH leg 3 ('a ClickHouse or Avride monetization event (IPO filing, secondary, or strategic sale)'): an Avride priced round is capital into Avride, not a monetisation, and an IPO filing is not cash. Suggested leg: cash proceeds to Nebius of ≥$1B from any stake.
6. §Catalysts 'Avride monetization progress (toward 2028 exit)': rename to 'third-party priced round (first external mark; deconsolidation of ~$100M/yr of losses)'.
7. §Conviction Triggers, LOW: the 24 August converts, with the Q2 ATM and the March notes, are evidence-touched on a converted basis; the trigger should define whether converts count before conversion.
8. §Bear Case #5 ('the optionality is marked, not realised'): strengthened; the two marks diverge by ~2.5x.
