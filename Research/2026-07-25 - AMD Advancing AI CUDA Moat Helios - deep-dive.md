# Can AMD break the CUDA Moat? AMD Advancing AI 2026

## Thesis Delta
Consensus prices Advancing AI 2026 + Anthropic 2GW + OpenAI/Meta 6GW warrants as proof that ROCm is approaching CUDA parity and that Helios is a rack-for-rack [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] Rubin alternative (AMD Insight #3 H2-2026 framework-native parity + Insight #2 sole merchant full-stack, already in the multiple). SemiAnalysis upgrades AMD from “non-zero chance” to “great chance *if* two risks are solved,” then shows both are live at the keynote — llm-d/Pollara Kubernetes inference nightly gating **0% vs ConnectX-7** (Advancing AI ETA missed), Helios in flyover-cable / ~550-retimer ramp hell — so the **~105% OpenAI / ~85% Meta equity rebate at a $600 share hurdle** can print negative-cost tokens without CUDA displacement ([G-13] mispriced variable is gating + DI composability, not peak FP8; [G-10] first-attempt next-gen software/training parity is rare).

## Summary
SemiAnalysis’s Advancing AI 2026 piece is a third mark on its own AMD-software probability path, not a keynote recap. First AMD software article: **0% chance** of closing the Nvidia AI-accelerator gap — stack broken, SemiAnalysis the top ROCm bug submitter for months. AMD 2.0, six months later, at rock-bottom sentiment: upgrade to a **non-zero / meaningful chance**, because Lisa Su took the call, implemented suggestions, and replaced committee leadership with urgency even though destination was still far. This piece moves again: **great chance of success if two named risks are solved** — Helios rack-production ramp, and stable internal GPU clusters for CI plus agentic coding. Share gain is not framed as an Nvidia-revenue kill. The pie grows fast enough that Nvidia still “massively grow[s] revenue”; the competitive claim is that AMD is now a software-front threat, so Jensen must cut bureaucracy and flatten internal-approval layers. [G-3] That is a trend-continuation read on the compute TAM with a mean-reversion read on CUDA’s *kernel-level* exclusivity — the source’s own next sentence is that the moat has already moved to disaggregated multi-node systems.

Customer announcements are treated as confirmation of a prior Accelerator Model call, not as new demand discovery. Anthropic publicly commits **2GW** of AMD chips (SemiAnalysis flagged Anthropic-as-AMD-customer three months earlier and on socials a week before the keynote); first gigawatt is **1H27 Helios**, paired with a multi-year engineering collaboration that points Claude at ROCm and a **up-to-$5B AMD cash investment** released on deployment milestones. Microsoft, which dropped AMD after MI300X on Samsung 2023 HBM unreliability plus poor software and then skipped both MI325X and MI355X, reverses onto **MI455X Helios**, with OpenAI read as the main Azure end-customer. AMD also announces a Cerebras partnership for prefill/decode disaggregation and ultra-fast interactive inference — explicit analogue of the Nvidia–Groq LPX move. Open-source compiler and kernels are argued to position AMD better than Nvidia for the agentic-coding age *except* for one bottleneck the rest of the article spends its length on: agents consume GPUs in the test loop, and AMD does not have the clusters.

The two risks are first-principles, not vibes. Risk 1: Helios is AMD’s first AI rack-scale system and is **not cableless**. Rubin Oberon has already pivoted off the flyover-cable design that punished GB200/GB300; Helios froze too late to follow. Weak SerDes plus a flexible-I/O PHY ambition means **up to 85% of Meta’s scale-up backplane must be retimed**, **>550 Broadcom Ethernet retimers per rack**, plus backplane reliability failures during “rack production ramp hell.” Risk 2: the chief internal complaint is a persistent lack of **stable GPU clusters** for software-development teams and for automated CI. Leadership still treats clusters as a fungible, migrate-able pool that can be yanked from the vLLM team to backstop a customer or a temporary CSP. That philosophy now collides with agentic coding: each human used to need a couple of nodes; each human now runs dozens of agents, each of which needs GPUs and a tool-use test loop. Even **+2,000 MI355X this month** and **+6,000 MI325X/MI355X later this year** leave AMD **more than an order of magnitude** below Nvidia’s stable long-term internal clusters. gfx1250 (MI455) is a **different ISA** from gfx950 (MI355), so every kernel path must be tested twice. The audacious goal — open-source vLLM/SGLang nightly CI on MI455X by Advancing AI 2026 — **slipped to October 2026**, with a left-shift attempt to August/September.

Silicon is the part SemiAnalysis is willing to praise without a qualifier. MI455X is framed as the most advanced chip leaving a fab: first **2nm datacenter silicon** (compute tiles *and* Venice CPU on TSMC N2; every competing accelerator still on N3), largest shipping **CoWoS-L at 5.5× reticle**, only vendor adopting **SoIC-X hybrid bonding**, **3,470 mm²** of logic in package. Floorplan: eight N2 XCDs hybrid-bonded onto two reticle-sized base dies (SRAM, HBM controllers, compute fabric) plus two identical flexible I/O dies (72 lanes each: 212G UALoE, 64G Infinity Fabric, PCIe Gen 6, 128G UALink, xGMI4). Peak marketed dense FP8 is **20 PF vs Rubin 17.5 PF** — a mild advantage bought with far more silicon, because CDNA 5 still trails Nvidia microarchitecture and **lacks Rubin SM107’s 3-bit LUT tensor cores**. Memory is the cleaner win: **12 HBM4 stacks / 432 GB** vs Nvidia/Google **8 / 288 GB**, **23.3 TB/s** vs Rubin **22 TB/s**. The bandwidth gap is tiny despite a 50% wider bus because Nvidia raised HBM4 pin speed to **10.7 Gbps vs AMD 7.6 Gbps** (~40% faster, higher-quality bin, supplier rework, Rubin output slip). SemiAnalysis’s verdict on that gambit: it closed Rubin’s memory shortfall, and **Vera Rubin will still be first to deliver tokens at scale** versus MI455 Helios. Hidden under the interposer, MI455 is read as TSMC’s first **active LSI** (local silicon interconnect) ship — ISSCC 2026 repeater-in-the-bridge, PHYs shrink, shoreline returns to compute/memory — with the TSMC demo floorplan matching MI455 (two base dies, 12 HBM4, two I/O dies). MI455 is the test vehicle.

The rack is where the silicon lead is spent. Helios is AMD’s first **switched** scale-up (MI300X–MI355X were 8-GPU point-to-point meshes): **72 MI455X** through **12× 102.4T Broadcom Tomahawk 6** in a single-tier all-to-all, **1.8 TB/s** uni-directional scale-up per GPU, **432 of 512** TH6 lanes used because AMD bought a merchant switch rather than a co-designed NVSwitch. Scale-out walks Pollara 400G → Vulcano 800G, two NICs the dominant config at **1.6 Tbit/s** per GPU, third NIC optional. That is a real architecture step. It is also a **partial co-design**: Venice CPU, Pensando Vulcano NIC, Pensando Salina DPU are first-party; the scale-up switch is not. Broadcom is the only merchant 100T / 200G-SerDes switch, so AMD’s rack roadmap is hostage to one partner’s execution and to multi-vendor bring-up. LPDDR5X that used to sit as ~1 TB of second-tier memory on each EAM is **gone** (memory-supply despec). Compute-tray Genesis flyovers (up to **12 Molex cables per 1U tray** next to liquid-cooling and power) plus **1,728** switch-side flyovers plus **10,368** differential pairs of copper backplane are the manufacturing tax Nvidia already paid and then designed out. Backplane + flyover content is costed at **$68,928/rack**. Meta, one of the two warrant anchors, then **refuses the full SKU**: TBD Lab inherits a recsys-team cut-down (6/8 XCDs, 8-Hi HBM vs 12-Hi, one I/O die, **900 GB/s** scale-up vs Rubin **1,800 GB/s**, ~25% less compute) that SemiAnalysis says will “nuke AMD’s volume at Meta” because TBD will take Rubin instead. An 3 August 2026 edit updates that custom config from new information; the recommendation is that AMD force the full MI455 onto TBD, not celebrate the custom win.

Software is the longer half of the argument, and the claim is precise: **ROCm is no longer “broken”; the competitive frontier moved**. Single-node recipes, docs, and reproducibility are real (AITER/vLLM **up to 18×** Kimi K2.5 1T MXFP4 interactivity in <30 days; AMD’s own write-up **1.08–1.2×** throughput over baseline frameworks; official vLLM blog **1.2–4.4×** from latest orchestration; MiniMax M3 catching B200 via ATOM). Upstream posture is the right playbook — Anush’s developer-first push, ROCm in upstream vLLM (stable January 2026, nightlies shortly after), SGLang two-node 1P1D disagg nightly in June then DP-attention / EP8 / MTP / Kimi K2.6 in July. George Hotz’s 2025 diagnosis is quoted as still operative: Lisa answers email; the open question is culture shift to *deliver* software. What has not landed is the metric that actually gates merges. **Gating/blocking tests** (PR cannot merge unless green) are the quality bar; non-gating pass rates are what leadership shows non-technical audiences. Kubernetes-inference Pollara first-party NIC CI is **0% of Nvidia ConnectX-7 nightly** — not because engineers refuse the work, because CI capacity is under-bought — and the planned Advancing AI 2026 parity ETA was **missed**. The same week as the event, vLLM gating that had been marching toward a **90% CUDA gating-parity** goal **regressed** after leadership pulled clusters off the internal vLLM team. SemiAnalysis’s instruction to Lisa Su, Mark Papermaster, Sharon Zhou, Anush Elangovan and Vamsi Boppana is capacity-planning philosophy, not another kernel.

The new moat, named in the GTC 2026 recap and restated here, is **composable distributed inference**: disaggregated prefill/decode, WideEP, KV transport, expert routing, scheduler/orchestration, and now attention/FFN disaggregation (AFD) plus Nvidia’s Groq 3 LPX SRAM decode island inside the Nvidia rack. CUDA open-ecosystem shipped disagg+WideEP from early 2024; AMD’s first public PD-disagg + WideEP recipes landed on InferenceX in **January 2026**. AFD’s payoff wants superpod-class interconnect, which AMD does not ship until MI455X. Individual pieces now sometimes compose — SGLang MI355X nightlies cover DeepSeek-V4 disagg with DP-attention, EP8 and MTP; four-node V4 and Kimi WideEP16 have been hardware-validated — but composition is **model- and topology-specific**. Kimi DP8/EP8 was excluded after a GPU memory fault; WideEP16 int4 decode must disable HIP graph capture; WideEP16 CI is still under review. Accuracy is the tell: before March 2026, DeepSeek-R1 disagg + DP-attention scored **~0 GSM8K** while no-DPA passed **>95%**; that composition bug is fixed. What is not fixed is SGLang+MoRI expert-parallel at concurrency 64, where GSM8K falls to **~80% vs ~94%** at every other concurrency; the catastrophic 0-score cousin was an uninitialized reduce buffer in AITER’s FP4 MoE kernel (patched June), and AMD is declining to prioritize the residual 80% cliff as a numerical corner case. Helios (gfx1250) software, layer by layer, repeats the same shape: arch-enablement and KV-transfer plumbing land; WideEP, validation, and wave32-tuned kernels do not. PyTorch gfx1250 support merged mid-July as build-time plumbing behind unreleased ROCm 7.14+, no gfx1250 CI, high-value GEMM/SDPA/FP8 grouped-GEMM/int4 paths filtered or hard-error, attention a “Tech Preview,” wave32 kernels unwritten. SGLang’s 22 July gfx1250 nightly **publishes an image and runs no test job**, pins MoRI to a June commit that predates gfx1250, so MoRI-EP WideEP does not run. vLLM bring-up is four models on FFM simulator plus early silicon, ATOM-parity pass for gpt-oss reverted “until AITER is ready,” **MoRI removed from the build**. ATOM itself is disaggregation-first and expert-parallel-later: MoRI-IO write-push KV + UALink fabric backend exist, e2e validation ran on **eight MI355X nodes and a UALink vPOD, not MI455X**, and every MoRI-EP PR still targets gfx942/gfx950. Bottom to top: **arch enablement + first half of disaggregation, WideEP nowhere**.

Agentic kernel generation is the source’s candidate solvent for Nvidia’s headcount moat, and also the reason CI-cluster poverty is existential. SemiAnalysis’s own 2.5-person team now uses Claude Code/Codex agents with GitHub Actions plus physical-runner access to stand up day-0 InferenceX sweeps, diagnose engine errors, and few-shot upstream fixes (listed TRT / NIXL / MiniMax / ROCm PRs). That loop “would simply not have been possible 3–6 months ago.” AMD productizes the same loop as **ROCm.ai**: GEAK (mini-SWE-agent writing Triton/HIP/FlyDSL kernels), Hyperloom (profiles a serving workload, throws GEAK/GEMM agents at bottlenecks, gates on end-to-end A/B; pulls its target off InferenceX), Magpie, TraceLens, Apex (trajectory → RL-flavored training, plus tamper detector against `print("PASS")` and banned-library reward hacks), AgentKernelArena. GEAK’s learned notes already log a verified **~+21.8% e2e** from an MXFP8 decode-bound dense-linear rewrite on MI355X, with grouped-MoE GEMM stalling around a **1.1×** ceiling. This industrializes last year’s CUDA-engineer room; it does not, by itself, defeat the CUDA moat. It does change the production function of software catch-up — **if** there are GPUs for the agents to test against. InferenceX’s new AgentX scenario (replay of ~3 months of SemiAnalysis Claude Code/Codex traces; median ISL **140k** / OSL **396**, median cache hit **99.2%** on infinite cache, subagent + tool-use think-time) is the benchmark the source wants TaaS and frontier labs to use instead of 8k1k. MoRI — modular RDMA, MoRI-EP for expert dispatch/combine, MoRI-IO for KV transfer, China-based first-principles tiger team — is called a real win whose H2 2026 roadmap (tiered HBM→DRAM→NVMe KV, SHMEM v2, EP v2, FlyDSL mega-kernels, Helios enablement, SGLang/vLLM upstream) sits on the same **five or six engineers** whose handles dominate merged PRs. Two-batch overlap, table stakes on the SGLang 2025 H1 roadmap, only merged MoRI-EP support on **20 February 2026** and broke CI days later. NIXL is the other cultural tell: at GTC 2025 Nvidia said it would not help AMD’s comms fork; at GTC 2026 Dynamo maintainers said on the record they would take AMD’s RIXL the way they took Trainium’s Neuron fork. SemiAnalysis spent April connecting Anush to NIXL maintainers. Andy Luo’s ROCm/HIP PR opened **15 May**, merged **4 June**, follow-up **#1647 hit 341 Gb/s** cross-node RDMA on two MI355X with AMD AINIC RoCE and no Mellanox NIC. RIXL is now fully ported; Dynamo core has started taking AMD patches. The remaining gaps — nixlbench HIP, ROCm Dockerfile/CI, an AMD GPU CI runner still an offer not a workflow, rocSHMEM a future plugin — are the same “ingredients land late and fragile” pattern.

Part 3 is TCO plus the warrant. HBM ASPs are expected to gap-up in 2027 as memory suppliers chase commodity-DRAM margins; AMD passes HBM through with markup but **below corporate incremental margin**, dragging MI455X package GM. Last year’s BOM “spam” (**138 TB** DRAM across LPDDR5X + DDR5 MRDIMM) has been despec’d to far less RDIMM and **zero LPDDR**; DRAM inflation is partly offset by cutting content, residual price-up is HBM + non-HBM chip cost. Hyperscaler ticket size sits **between GB300 NVL72 and VR NVL72**. Operating power does not: Helios **~240 kW server / ~257 kW all-in** vs GB300 **~142 / ~159 kW**. On marketed dense FP8 TCO and TFLOPs/W, MI455X looks competitive with even VR NVL72 — **if** one takes AMD’s PFLOPs at face value. SemiAnalysis does not: AMD “historically overstated TFLOPs” and **MFU is suspected quite low**, so effective training throughput is the missing haircut. The “one chart to rule them all” (cost-based Neocloud rental floor at **~15.6% project IRR** vs value-based renter ceiling vs the prior generation) is extended from the earlier VR-vs-GB300 note: GB300→VR marketed dense FP8 goes **5,000 → 17,500 TFLOPs (3.5×)**, which is what props VR’s theoretical max rental; MI455X’s lower TCO per marketed PFLOP lets it clear the same 15.6% IRR at a **lower $/hr** than VR, with both offering headroom versus GB300. That is a *marketed-FLOPs* chart. Stack the OpenAI/Meta warrants on top and the chart becomes financial engineering. Warrants issued **October 2025 (OpenAI)** and **February 2026 (Meta)**, **160M shares each (~10% of AMD each)** at **$0.01**, vesting on purchase/deployment of **6GW** Instinct with price hurdles stepping to **$600**. At $600 the implied rebate is **~$16.0B/GW**. Against partner prices of **~$15.2B/GW (OpenAI)** and **~$18.7B/GW (Meta)** that is **~105% and ~85%** — OpenAI can book a profit by buying servers and exercising, *if* AMD is at $600 across issuance tranches and OpenAI holds. Meta’s warrant wraps the **cut-down recsys SKU**; OpenAI buys off-the-shelf MI455X and successor SKUs. The warrants are an **incentive, not a commitment**. Orders remain contingent on delivering MI455X silicon and the Helios system; **OpenAI can back out of the first gigawatt at multiple milestones**. SemiAnalysis’s justification for the structure: AMD is fighting a two-front war (CUDA merchant GPU *and* TPU/Trainium custom ASIC externalization) and needs frontier labs inside the tent to bridge the software gap. Neocloud rhyme: AMD’s **$300M Crusoe loan guarantee (February 2026)** plus a takeout rental if no customers appear let Crusoe print debt at **6%**; Nvidia’s backstop program scaled **$860M (October 2025) → $3.5B (January 2026)** and is engineered as a rental floor plus upside share — bankability without a complacency-inducing return.

Part 4 is a short prescription, not a ship announcement. For MI500 AMD “should” pivot to **co-packaged copper (CPC) and near-packaged optics (NPO)**: at least **six 6.4 Tbit/s** optical engines (**≥38.4T** scale-up), **57.6T** argued as the radix that actually supports a large scale-up world size; OCI MSA; each engine **32 × 200G** lanes, **8 lambdas** (4 Tx / 4 Rx) at **50G NRZ**; ELSFPs as the light source at matching 6.4T. Topology stays flat one-tier, **576 GPUs across 8 racks**, one-hop, tail-latency limited. At 57.6T the sketch is **288 switches** (Tomahawk 6 in a 115.2T SKU rather than 102.4T) each touching all 576 GPUs at 200G/lane. Inbox source ends on that diagram description.

Net claim scope: SemiAnalysis is not calling a CUDA break at Advancing AI 2026. It is calling a **conditional path** — silicon + open-source + agents + warrant-distorted TCO can take share *if* Helios manufacturing and CI-cluster philosophy are fixed — and then documenting that both conditions failed their event-dated ETAs. Rubin still ships tokens first. Meta’s volume is at risk from a self-inflicted cut-down SKU. The rebate can print “negative cost per million tokens” as a spreadsheet identity without a single extra gating test turning green.

## Framework / Mental Model
The source runs four named schemes. They are how SemiAnalysis wants the event read; they are not vault verdicts.

**1. AMD software-probability path (0 → non-zero → conditional-great).** Three dated states on the same object (ROCm vs CUDA in AI accelerators). State 1: 0%. State 2 (AMD 2.0): non-zero / meaningful, published into rock-bottom sentiment, justified by leadership that “can create change” rather than committee leadership. State 3 (this piece): great chance **if and only if** two enumerated risks (Helios ramp; stable CI/dev clusters) are solved. Methodology: the authors are daily ROCm users on MI300X/MI325X/MI355X and the #1 quarterly bug reporter; probability is updated from observed CI, recipes, and cluster politics, not from keynote demos. Share-gain is explicitly decoupled from Nvidia-revenue destruction.

**2. Gating vs non-gating CI (the metric that matters).** Gating/blocking tests are the only quality bar because a red test blocks the merge. Non-gating pass rate is the number leadership can show a non-technical audience while the merge gate stays open. Parity is measured against Nvidia’s ConnectX / CUDA vLLM nightly gates, not against AMD’s own yesterday. Kubernetes inference (llm-d, AMD a founding partner) is the deployment layer that “most inference deployments across the world use,” so Pollara-vs-ConnectX-7 nightly is the scoreboard, not a niche NIC test.

**3. Software composability problem (named in InferenceX v2, restated here).** AMD can land individual optimizations — disagg, FP4, WideEP, DP-attention, MTP, cache offload — and still lose, because combining them breaks the stack. The competitive frontier has left single-node aggregated inference; Nvidia’s next moat is disaggregated systems including AFD. A win counts only when FP4 + WideEP + disagg + cache offload + speculative decoding run together in default vLLM/SGLang across models and topologies. ATOM/AITER are scored only insofar as they improve those mainstream engines; an AMD-only island is a failed strategy.

**4. “One chart to rule them all” + equity-rebate overlay.** Two axes already used on VR vs GB300. Vertical dotted line: **cost-based** rental floor = minimum $/hr for a Neocloud to clear a standard project IRR (here ~15.6%). Horizontal dotted line: **value-based** rental ceiling = price at which a renter is indifferent between the new rack and the prior generation. Apply to MI455X vs VR NVL72 on marketed dense FP8; then stack the OpenAI/Meta warrant as a contingent rebate (~$16B/GW at a $600 AMD share) that can push partner TCO through zero. Methodology caveat, from the same authors: the chart takes marketed PFLOPs at face value; they simultaneously say AMD overstates TFLOPs and that MFU is low. Read the chart as an upper bound on price-competitiveness, not as measured tokens/$.
