# Oklo Groves Criticality — 5th SMR Validated Since June, Part 57 Opens Mass Production

## Thesis Delta

Consensus (the [[Theses/BE - Bloom Energy]] bear case and [[Theses/CCJ - Cameco]] Outstanding Question #4) treats advanced nuclear as a 2030+ story that *slips* — NuScale-style delays keep on-site SMRs off the table for the acute 2026–2029 AI-power gap. This source implies the SMR timeline is compressing faster than priced: five reactor types reached criticality in ~2 months (June–Aug 2026) after a 52-year drought, and the NRC's pending **Part 57** rule would let a developer mass-produce a *licensed prototype with no further approval*, cutting reviews from >10 years to 6–12 months. The non-consensus read cuts two ways — bearish-confirming for BE (the "time-to-power arbitrage is a wasting asset as SMR supply normalizes 2028–2030" leg gains evidence) and counter to the CCJ bear's SMR-slippage fear — but with a load-bearing caveat: Oklo runs on recycled EBR-II fuel and HALEU, not Cameco's natural-uranium/conversion products, so faster deployment of *this* reactor type dilutes rather than amplifies the natural-U pull the CCJ thesis banks on.

## Summary

Oklo's (OKLO) Groves Isotope Test Reactor reached criticality on Aug 5, 2026 at Lockhart, Texas — the fifth advanced-reactor type validated in the US since June, after none for ~52 years. The sequence: Antares Nuclear's Mark-0 (Jun 4, first since 1973), Aalo Atomics' Aalo-X (Jul 4, fourth), then Oklo's Groves (Aug 5, fifth). All fall under a DOE reactor pilot program (10 companies, 11 "first-mover" designs) authorized by four May 2025 Trump "Nuclear Renaissance" executive orders targeting 10 new reactor licenses by 2030 and 4x nuclear capacity by 2050. Energy Secretary Chris Wright expects as many as seven criticalities by year-end; the five-year goal of 10 validated small-reactor types is already half-done.

The investable mechanism is regulatory, not technological. The NRC is expected to adopt Part 57 this fall (final rule no later than early 2027) — a micro-reactor framework that compresses license reviews to 6–12 months and lets a licensed prototype be mass-produced without further approval. Oklo built Groves greenfield to "substantial completion" in 227 days. Its 75 MW Aurora Powerhouse (Idaho National Lab) targets commercial operations by 2028, and it holds a Sept-2025 contract to power a 200-acre Meta datacenter campus in Pike County, Ohio "by the early 2030s," plus an Nvidia fuel partnership at Los Alamos. Claim scope is deliberately forward: criticality of a *test/isotope* reactor is a licensing milestone, not commercial gigawatt-scale datacenter power — which on Oklo's own timeline remains a 2028–early-2030s event.
