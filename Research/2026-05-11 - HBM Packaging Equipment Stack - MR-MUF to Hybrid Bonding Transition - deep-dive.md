# HBM Packaging Equipment Stack — MR-MUF to Hybrid Bonding Transition

## Thesis Delta

## Summary

The source is an institutional deep-dive synthesising trade-press, primary-source, and supply-chain channel material spanning early 2024 through May 2026 on the HBM packaging stack. Its central argument is that **JEDEC's HBM4 max-height extension from 720 µm to 775 µm bought microbump-based stacking another full generation of life**, pushing the hybrid-bonding transition from HBM4 (which would have forced it) to HBM4E 16-Hi (mixed) and HBM5 20-Hi (~2029, mandatory). The investment consequence: the equipment opportunity bifurcates cleanly into a **near-term TCB/MR-MUF cycle (2026-2028)** favouring Hanmi, ASMPT, Towa, Disco, and Resonac/Namics; and a **longer-cycle hybrid bonding inflection (2028-2030+)** favouring BESI, Applied Materials, ASMPT (again — bridge supplier), and Lam/Tokyo Electron on the front-end-like flow. SK Hynix has explicitly told investors it will retain MR-MUF through HBM4 and HBM4E 16-Hi; the $13B P&T7 Cheongju packaging mega-fab announcement (January 2026, construction April 2026, full ops late 2027) is the structural commitment.

The source's second-order argument — and the most differentiated insight relative to current vault coverage — is that **MR-MUF's competitive moat is materials chemistry, not process equipment**. The liquid Epoxy Molding Compound supplied by Namics (Japan) under a de facto exclusive arrangement with SK Hynix is reportedly approaching contract expiration; Nagase ChemteX is the parallel LMC second source; Resonac (formerly Showa Denko + Hitachi Chemical) is the dominant generic EMC supplier and the alternative Samsung is courting. Ex-SK Hynix VP Shim Dae-yong is quoted explicitly: "Without stable access to optimized underfill and EMC, 3D stacking becomes a yield nightmare. That is not something you fix with capex alone." The contract-expiration timing is what is shaping SK Hynix's hybrid-bonding migration urgency — not equipment availability.

The third argument frames the HBM packaging-vendor dual-sourcing pattern as a recurring structural compression of incumbent pricing power across every layer of the stack. **Hanmi's share of SK Hynix TCB orders has fallen from ~100% (pre-2024) to ~40-50% (2025)** with ASMPT at ~50% of the deployed base and Hanwha Semitech entering with multiple 2025/2026 contracts. Macquarie has cut Hanmi's FY26 revenue forecast from KRW1.51tn to KRW0.82tn (-46%) and FY27 from KRW2.184tn to KRW1.045tn (-52%), with the price target cut from KRW170,000 to KRW90,000 and a downgrade to Neutral. The same dual-sourcing pattern is migrating to materials (Namics → +Nagase → +Resonac), molding (Towa → +ASMPT/BESI), and ultimately hybrid bonding (BESI → +ASMPT → +Hanwha → +SEMES). The investment implication is that **near-monopolistic incumbents (Towa at 66% molding share; Disco at 70-80% grinding/dicing share; Hanmi at 71% TCB share) all face the same incumbency-compression trajectory** — though Towa and Disco are protected by consumables annuities and product-cycle leadership that Hanmi lacks.

The fourth strand is the bifurcation between Samsung's aggressive hybrid-bonding (HCB) roadmap and SK Hynix's deferred MR-MUF-first posture. Samsung has demonstrated HCB at GTC 2026, claims 33% higher stacking and >11% base-die temperature reduction at 12/16-Hi, is establishing a dedicated HCB line at Cheonan with equipment from BESI (development), Onto Innovation (non-destructive ultrasonic inspection), and SEMES (captive). Samsung CTO Jae-hyuk Song has positioned HBM4E as a TCB/HCB hybrid generation with full HCB at HBM5. **Samsung HCB qualification at Nvidia for HBM4E in 2027 is not the source's base case** given three consecutive prior HBM3E qualification failures, 1c-nm DRAM yield ~50% (vs target 70%), and 4nm base-die yield ~40%. If Samsung's HCB execution disappoints, MR-MUF's window extends another generation — a directionally bullish scenario for SK Hynix, Towa, and Disco; mildly bearish for BESI's near-term order pace; neutral for AMAT/LRCX (whose HBM exposure is incremental, not gating).

## Framework / Mental Model

The source advances five named frameworks, each useful as a re-applicable analytical scaffold for tracking the HBM equipment cohort beyond this specific note:

**1. Three-vendor HBM4 packaging-structure framework.** As of May 2026, the three IDMs' HBM4 architectures are locked across three axes — DRAM node, base-die fab, and bonding process:

| Vendor | DRAM node | Base die | Bonding | Backup path |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SK Hynix | 1b-nm | TSMC ~12nm | Advanced MR-MUF | Fluxless TCB evaluated 4Q 25, rejected; BESI Kinex order Mar 2026 for HBM5 |
| Samsung | 1c-nm (~50% yield) | In-house 4nm Foundry (~40% yield) | TC-NCF | HCB on parallel Cheonan line for 16-Hi HBM4/HBM4E samples |
| Micron | 1β-nm | TSMC custom | TC-NCF | BESI as sole HBM4 TCB vendor (Hong Kong domicile disqualifies ASMPT) |

**2. Bump-pitch roadmap with packaging-tier classification.** The source codifies a clean bump-pitch progression that maps to which packaging tier the bonding tool must support: legacy C4 ~130 µm → copper pillar ~50 µm → HBM3E microbumps 20-30 µm → HBM4 microbumps target 10 µm → hybrid bonding sub-10 µm in production (with sub-2 µm demonstrated by TSMC SoIC R&D and IMEC at 400 nm pitch / 150 nm overlay). The framework: anywhere above ~10 µm pitch, TCB/MR-MUF works; below ~10 µm, hybrid bonding is the only path. Microbump plating uniformity and solder reflow variability are the technical reason for the floor.

**3. Equipment-vs-materials moat framework.** Equipment selection has multiple credible suppliers (Hanmi ↔ ASMPT ↔ Hanwha for TCB; BESI ↔ ASMPT ↔ Hanwha Semitech ↔ SEMES for hybrid bonding; Towa ↔ ASMPT for molding). Materials chemistry does not: liquid EMC for MR-MUF is sole-sourced (Namics) with two credible alternates (Nagase ChemteX, Resonac); NCF film for TC-NCF is sourced from Resonac, Sumitomo Bakelite, and Nagase; ABF substrate dielectric has Ajinomoto controlling ~98% of IP licensing with six downstream substrate makers. The implication: **equipment incumbents lose pricing power through dual-sourcing; materials incumbents retain it through chemistry-cycle-length contracts**. The Namics contract expiration is the source's specific flag for when the materials moat compresses.

**4. Vendor dual-sourcing waterfall framework.** SK Hynix's demonstrated pattern: introduce a sole-source supplier at category genesis, prove out the technology, then add 2nd and 3rd sources to compress pricing once the category is stable. Applied to TCB: Hanmi 100% (pre-2024) → +ASMPT (Q4 2024) → +Hanwha Semitech (early 2025). Applied to materials: Namics-exclusive → talks with Nagase ChemteX (2024) → Resonac as Samsung-aligned generic. Applied to molding: Towa-dominant → ASMPT/BESI optional secondaries. Applied to hybrid bonding (forward-looking): BESI sole-source mass-production March 2026 → ASMPT 2nd-gen HB platform → Hanwha Semitech SHB2 Nano (first article shipped Mar 2026 for qualification) → SEMES at Samsung. The framework predicts pricing-power compression on a ~2-3 year lag from sole-source qualification.

**5. Equipment-beneficiary bifurcation framework (the highest-level investment scaffold).** Two distinct cycles with non-overlapping equipment beneficiaries:

| Cycle | Window | TAM trajectory | Winners |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near-term TCB/MR-MUF | 2026-2028 | TCB bonder: $542M (2025) → $1.5-1.6B (2027-2028) | Hanmi, ASMPT, Towa, Disco, Resonac/Namics |
| Long-cycle Hybrid Bonding | 2028-2030+ | HB tooling: ~$152M (2025) → ~$2B (2028) per News1 Korea/Yole | BESI, AMAT (Kinex), ASMPT, Lam, Tokyo Electron |

Disco and Towa are positioned as the "lowest-debate, highest-quality compounders" — they benefit from HBM intensity regardless of MR-MUF/TC-NCF/HCB mix.

## Contradiction Check

The source **supports and quantifies multiple existing conclusions** in [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] with greater technical depth:

- **SK Hynix Insight #2 (MR-MUF is a process moat, not architectural)**: directly supported — Hanmi Chairman Kwak's "TC bonders are sufficient for HBM4 and HBM5" comment and SK Hynix's own March 2026 BESI Kinex mass-production order confirm that even SK Hynix treats MR-MUF as time-bounded. The framework labels SK Hynix "a fast follower on hybrid bonding, not a leader" — same conclusion, sharper phrasing.
- **SK Hynix Insight #4 (CXMT bear case, wrong timing)**: directly supported — industry consensus including ex-SK Hynix VP places the China memory gap at "more than 5 years"; CXMT's HBM3 (using MR-MUF) targets end-2026 but China remains material-chemistry-dependent on Japanese Resonac/Namics/Nagase. Confines the CXMT disruption to mainland AI accelerators (Huawei Ascend, Biren, MetaX, Iluvatar) through the 2026-2028 horizon.
- **SK Hynix Bear Pillar #3 (MR-MUF lead erodes at HBM5 transition)**: supported with timing — industry consensus (TrendForce, Counterpoint, Yole, KAIST) points to HBM5 20-Hi (2029) as the first generation requiring hybrid bonding at scale, with W2W for premium variants.
- **BESI thesis (HBM5 inflection)**: data-confirmed — SK Hynix's first commercial mass-production hybrid-bonding order (BESI/AMAT Kinex, March 2026) is direct evidence that the inflection is starting. BESI Q1 26 revenue +28.3% YoY and H2 25 orders +63.6% over H1 are the early-cycle marker.

The source **introduces new content not in current thesis coverage**:

- **NEW RISK on SK Hynix**: the Namics liquid-EMC exclusivity contract is reportedly approaching expiration. Materials chemistry is the actual MR-MUF moat (not process equipment), and contract expiration timing is what is shaping SK Hynix's hybrid-bonding migration urgency. Samsung is in talks with Namics. This is an unmodeled 2026-2027 materials-moat erosion vector distinct from the Samsung HCB execution timeline.
- **NEW SUPPLIER-COMPRESSION FRAMEWORK**: SK Hynix's dual-sourcing pattern is now visible across every layer of the stack (TCB, materials, molding, hybrid bonding). Compresses pricing power for ALL incumbents over the 2026-2028 window — applies to Hanmi (already in vault narrative) but extends to Towa, Disco (less so given consumables annuity), Namics, Nagase, BESI on a 2-3 year lag from sole-source qualification.
- **NEW FRAMING on HBM4E**: vault treats HBM5 as the hybrid-bonding inflection; source frames **HBM4E 16-Hi as the mixed/transition generation** (Samsung pushes HCB on selected SKUs; SK Hynix evaluates fluxless TCB and hybrid bonding as backup). The inflection is bracketed by HBM4E (2027-2028, mixed) and HBM5 (2029-2030, mandatory) rather than a single HBM5 step.
- **NEW DATA on AMAT/LRCX HBM exposure**: AMAT etch crossed $1B/quarter on advanced DRAM wins in late 2025; LRCX DRAM mix rose from 14% → 16% of systems revenue in Q3 2025 on HBM investment; LRCX advanced packaging is guided +40% in 2026 vs WFE growth; LRCX Aether dry-resist is an HBM "tool of record" win.
- **NEW CONFIRMATION on Vera Rubin split**: Counterpoint and TrendForce both cite SK Hynix ~70% / Samsung ~30% / Micron ~0% — multiple-source corroboration of the central catalyst tracked in [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] §Catalysts.
