## Summary
Datacenter construction has shifted from stick-built concrete to LEGO-like assembly: finished wall panels, wired mechanical/electrical rooms, and sometimes full halls arriving by truck. Hyperscalers, colos, and AI labs now treat modular/prefab as the default playbook for speed. SemiAnalysis’s Modular Tracker (inside the Industrials Model) quantifies >61GW modular capacity and 1,000+ sites, with modular share expected >30% of live capacity by YE2028. The analytical core is taxonomy: “modular” today conflates radically different products (skids vs volumetric halls vs tents), so vendor claims must be stress-tested. Case studies include Meta’s tent strategies and AWS’s Houdini approach; Vertiv is highlighted for ~2× content uplift per MW as modularization pulls more factory scope into power/cooling packages. Net investor takeaway: the binding constraint set is migrating from pure site labor toward **factory throughput, logistics, and standardized interfaces**—favoring vendors who own those modules.

## Framework / Mental Model
**Modular universe taxonomy (SemiAnalysis):** classify offerings by what actually ships prefabricated (structure / power room / cooling skid / full hall), who integrates (hyperscaler self vs colo vs EPC), and whether speed claims survive site-specific utility and permitting constraints. Apply by mapping each vendor’s SKU to category before accepting MW or timeline marketing.

## Key Segments
### Why construction looks like LEGO now
Speed-to-power for AI campuses forces offsite manufacturing; concrete/MEP arrive as finished assemblies.

### Taxonomy / wild-west problem
“Modular” is overloaded—vendors describe incompatible scopes; SA’s map is the antidote.

### Hyperscaler case studies
Meta tents and AWS Houdini illustrate extremes of temporary vs factory-integrated approaches.

### Vendor/content implications
Equipment makers with factory-built power/cooling skids (e.g., Vertiv) capture more $ per MW as modularization rises.

## Contradiction Check
Challenges “labor shortage permanently caps AI GW” as a single-variable thesis—modularization is the workaround. Supports industrials exposure in AI infra portfolios; does not remove grid/interconnect as the deeper bottleneck (see SA’s separate BTM/grid work).
