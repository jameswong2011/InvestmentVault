# AI Datacenter Financing Mechanism Design

Every live AI-factory financing is one machine with three interchangeable parts: a **revenue underwriting** (whose cash flow services the debt), a **residual claim** (who owns the box when the contract ends or the tenant leaves), and a **loss waterfall** (who pays, in what order, when both fail together). The template names in the press — SPV, JV, DDTL, prepay, vendor backstop, financing platform — are permutations of where those three parts sit. This note is the deal-level mechanism manual: how each structure works, what each party buys and sells, and the permutation grid across templates. The macro layer — how much leverage exists (~$0.8–1.1T complex debt-like claims incl. ~$200–350B of SPV/vendor-finance/prepay structures), tranche funding mix, the race-to-the-financing-ceiling game, historical cycle analogs — lives in [[Macro & Technology/Sustainability of AI Capex]] §XI and is not repeated here.

## Why structures proliferate now

The marginal capex dollar crossed from cash flow to structure in mid-2026: hyperscaler IG issuance ran $194B YTD to Jul-7 vs $108B in all of 2025, interest cover compressed <2x from ~5x in February, and 86% of those bonds trade wider than issue; Alphabet's Q2 was the first quarterly crossover ($44.9B capex vs $39.1B OCF); Oracle's FY26 FCF was ~-$24B; Meta's Q2 FCF fell to $784M against $349.31B of non-cancellable commitments and $278.99B of leases not yet commenced. 2026 AI-related debt issuance projects to ~$570B against ~$850B of DC capex. When internal funding caps out, every incremental structure is engineered to do some weighting of three jobs:

| Job | What it buys | Who pays for it |
|---|---|---|
| **Balance-sheet relief** | Rating preservation, capex→opex conversion, VIE deconsolidation | Sponsor pays a spread + writes residual-value support |
| **Risk transfer** | Moves utilization / counterparty / residual risk to whoever prices it cheapest | Lender/insurer AUM absorbs tail; sponsor keeps first loss via guarantee |
| **Demand enablement** | Vendor financing that lets under-capitalized customers keep buying | Vendor descends its own loss waterfall (margin defense dressed as credit support) |

Every template below is a different mix of the three. Reading a deal = identifying the mix.

## The underwriting frame every structure must answer

From [[Research/2026-08-11 - NVDA Superposition AI Factory Underwriting - deep-dive]] — the credit grammar underneath all templates:

**Two sequential questions.** Performing state: does CFADS (contract/lease/merchant revenue − power − opex − maintenance − taxes − required reinvestment) cover scheduled debt service (DSCR), under normal and stressed conditions? Default state: what is *net* recovery after downtime, dismantling, transport, porting, requalification, legal enforcement, and price discounts — and who absorbs first loss? Heuristic EL = PD × LGD × EAD, with the AI-specific pathology that the three are **correlated**: one AI-demand shock simultaneously raises customer PD, cuts rack remarketing value (LGD), and lands when EAD is highest (just after rapid deployment).

**Two ideal underwriting poles, one connective layer.** No live deal is a pure type:

| Pole | Performing-state assumption | Default-state assumption | What still must be underwritten |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fungibility / residual-value** (NVDA public framing) | Factory reassignable to other customers/workloads | Re-lease or sale reduces loss severity | Paying customers *now*; CFADS before anyone leaves |
| **Offtake / contracted-cash-flow** (AVGO XPV framing) | Current customer stays obligated long-term | Debt service continues without immediate replacement buyer | Collateral value *after* customer default; termination and acceptance terms |

Between them sits the **credit-support layer** — guarantees, backstops, escrow, lease assumption, residual-value support — which allocates the interval between contract failure and asset recovery. It is not a third underwriting type; it is what turns ideal types into signable paper, and it is where the interesting design choices live.

**Four-layer asset map** (why "GPU collateral" is the wrong unit): (1) site-fixed — land, shell, interconnection, substations, cooling; long life, one power market; (2) movable equipment — accelerators, servers, racks; relocatable only with destination power/cooling/networking + requalification; (3) intangible operating layer — licenses, monitoring, certifications, operator; title can transfer without the rights to run the box; (4) contract & credit-support layer — the legal layer that converts 1–3 into CFADS and into a loss waterfall. Each template finances a *different subset* of these layers, which is the first permutation axis.

**Redeployability is three nested tests, not one**: technical versatility (can run many models) → operational redeployability (moves without excessive downtime/requalification/capex) → **bankable redeployability** (returns to a *billable* state within bounded time and cost, proceeds material to lender recovery). Vendor marketing occupies the first test; loan pricing requires the third; no downturn print yet exists for any of them.

## The waterfall grammar

Standard project-finance priority of payments, which the live AI paper adapts:

1. Project revenue (lease / take-or-pay / merchant) →
2. Power + opex + maintenance →
3. Senior debt service (interest + scheduled amortization) →
4. Reserve accounts / covenant tests (DSCR triggers, cash traps) →
5. Mezzanine / subordinated →
6. Equity distributions.

The **loss waterfall** runs the other direction on default: equity → credit-support layer per its written triggers (RVG shortfall payment, vendor backstop, lease assumption) → subordinated/unsecured → senior. What is confirmed in live AI paper: Beignet sized to **1.12× DSCR flat through maturity** (S&P); CRWV DDTL covenants at **1.15–1.20× DSCR** with amortization *inside* the contract term. What is not disclosed anywhere yet: reserve mechanics, cash-trap triggers, lien priority among platform participants (Apollo vs banks vs Blackstone vs Broadcom in XPV is explicitly undisclosed), claim/cure procedures, and commencement-of-pay vs completion terms. The waterfall everyone is pricing — first loss vs last loss when PD and LGD move together — is exactly the part with no public print.

## Template catalog

### T1 — Hyperscaler corporate balance sheet

No SPV; the "waterfall" is the corporate capital structure. MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN/META fund from OCF + IG bonds + finance/operating leases; Google added an $85B equity raise (incl. $10B Berkshire). Cheapest capital in the complex, zero structural risk transfer: utilization, residual, and counterparty risk all stay on the franchise, absorbed by ads/search/cloud incumbency cash flow. The constraint is ratings math, not project math — which is why the crossover (capex > OCF from Q3 2026) pushes the marginal dollar into T2–T6. Meta's disclosure shows the migration in one balance sheet: $784M quarterly FCF, yet $349.31B commitments + $278.99B uncommenced leases + ~$68B July leases + $46.03B Louisiana maximum-exposure perimeter — the burden is dated, not instantaneous, and increasingly not on the GAAP balance sheet at all.

### T2 — Site-layer SPV JV: short lease + declining residual-value guarantee (the Hyperion template)

The hyperscaler off-balance-sheet workhorse, reconstructed in [[Research/2026-08-08 - META Shanaka Clocks on One Building DC Finance - deep-dive]]:

**Structure**: External capital owns 80% of a project SPV (Blue Owl at Hyperion, ~$27B; BlackRock-managed funds at El Paso, ~$14B/1GW), hyperscaler keeps 20%, constructs, and occupies 100%. SPV issues amortizing notes (Beignet Investor LLC, ~$27.3B due May 2049, S&P A+ — one notch below Meta's AA−). Tenant signs a **4-year initial lease** with options to 20 years, plus a **declining residual-value guarantee** (~$28B threshold at Hyperion) running the first 16 operating years: if the tenant walks and a marketed sale clears below the contractual floor, tenant pays the shortfall — conditions-precedent, not automatic on departure.

**The design trick is the duration stack**: 4-year lease / 20-year options / 16-year RVG / 23-year debt. The RVG is the bridge instrument that converts a 4-year lease into 25-year A+ debt capacity — S&P rates the *guarantee-plus-forced-sale-process* (sized to 1.12× DSCR, sale must start promptly, guarantee proceeds on a short timetable), not campus liquidity. Flagged rating weakness: no direct asset pledge.

**The consolidation trick is a control sacrifice**: Meta fails the VIE primary-beneficiary test *because* it gave up power over remarketing, successor negotiation, and property sale — the activities most significant to SPV economics. Surrendering remarketing control IS the deconsolidation mechanism. Recognized investment $2.92B vs $46.03B maximum exposure.

**Loss waterfall on non-renewal**: noteholders → made whole by RVG before market-clearing value is discovered; tenant → holds the gap to the declining floor; SPV equity → holds unsupported residual above the floor. Nobody holds a *price*: a public search 2015–Aug 2026 found zero qualifying prints of a vacated single-tenant >100MW hyperscale campus sold at arm's length (Cyxtera was multi-tenant colo; King Mountain was BTM bitcoin; Core Scientific→CRWV was powered-site conversion). The template's four clocks — credit recovery, accounting control, physical succession, workload continuity — are four different outcomes that cannot substitute; first renewal cluster ~2033 is when the first clock can strike.

**The template now has a curve** (web pass, Aug-15): Beignet priced 2025-10-16 at **6.581%** (Pimco anchored ~$18B, BlackRock >$3B); the El Paso twin priced Aug-2026 as [Sopaipilla Investor LLC](https://www.ifre.com/bonds/2462406/priced-sopaipilla-investor-llc-us-12547235000-22.3yr-at-t287.5bp) — **$12.547B amortizing notes due Nov-2048 at 7.534% / T+287.5bp, S&P A+** — ~40bp of spread wider than Hyperion ten months earlier, on reported soft demand with no tightening from price talk. Identical structure, identical rating, wider print: the first hard evidence the SPV curve reprices as supply arrives. El Paso equity mechanics: the BlackRock side includes GIP and HPS; Meta contributes land + construction-in-progress (~$2.3B) against ~$4.9B of consortium cash and takes a **~$1B one-time distribution at close** — the sponsor monetizes development margin at signing, before first rent.

**Why the sponsor pays up — the T1-vs-T2 calculus.** T2 is *more expensive money* than T1: the SPV borrows at A+ — one notch below the tenant's own AA− — at 6.581%/7.534%, 80% of equity economics go to the partner, and in the renewal state (20 years of rent covering 1.12× DSCR plus partner equity returns) T2 costs more in total than self-funding. Nobody pays a premium to hide leverage that is disclosed in their own 10-Q ($46.03B maximum exposure, printed). The premium buys four goods, ranked:

1. **Debt-capacity preservation + investor-pool arbitrage** — project paper taps insurance/annuity AUM for 20yr+ amortizing A+ credit (Pimco anchored ~$18B of Beignet) without supply-pressuring the corporate unsecured curve or consuming capacity that also backs buybacks, M&A, and the next AI round. Binding as the complex crosses capex>OCF and corporate cover compresses <2x.
2. **Reported-number shaping** — the 80% never enters capex guidance; expense arrives as rent at lease commencement (2029) rather than as D&A into the 2028 wave ($170B→$260–300B); the 4-year initial term minimizes the lease liability booked at commencement (PV of 4 years, not 20). In a sell-the-guide tape (Alphabet −7% on a capex *raise*) the label is a stock-price variable.
3. **The 2033 walk option — the real risk transfer** — non-renewal caps the tenant at the declining-RVG shortfall after a marketed sale, versus owning 100% of a possibly-stranded campus for 25 years under T1. An option premium paid via above-self-funding rent: full freight in the good state, capped exposure in the bad state.
4. **Capital velocity + development-margin recycling** — 80% partner equity lets one sponsor dollar seed five campuses' 20% slices instead of one campus outright; El Paso monetized at signing (~$2.3B land+CIP in, ~$1B distribution out). Required to run the [G-4] race past internal funding without corporate-debt tripwires.

Off-balance-sheet treatment is the *weakest* leg, not the point: post-Enron consolidation rules force a real control sacrifice — deconsolidation obtains only because the tenant genuinely surrendered remarketing / successor-negotiation / property-sale power — and the exposure is disclosed regardless. The accounting outcome protects only against mechanical consumers: leverage screens, covenant math, headline capex guidance [G-1]. Disconfirm to carry [G-13]: the sponsor bought a 4-year exit on a 25-year asset — a revealed confidence interval on its own AI-asset-duration conviction — and the market has started charging for the structure (Sopaipilla ~40bp wider, no tightening from talk). If the third campus prints wider still, the optics premium is competing away and T2 converges back to plain cost of funds.

### T3 — GPU-collateralized delayed-draw term loan (the CoreWeave template)

**Structure**: SPV holds GPUs + assigned customer contracts; lender commits a delayed-draw facility that funds as racks deploy (EAD path matches deployment); amortization is front-loaded *inside* the anchor contract term; DSCR covenant 1.15–1.20×. [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] DDTL 4.0 (closed Mar-31): **$8.5B, first investment-grade-rated GPU-collateral facility (Moody's A3 / DBRS A-low), fixed ~5.9% / floating SOFR+225, matures Mar-2032, non-recourse** — collateral is a single SPV (CoreWeave Compute Acquisition Co. VIII) holding the fleet plus one IG customer contract (reported as Meta backlog), Blackstone Credit & Insurance anchor.

**Mechanism**: the IG rating is manufactured from the *contract*, not the asset — take-or-pay from OpenAI ($22.4B through May 2031) and Meta ($14B+) makes the paper a secured corporate exposure to the offtaker with GPU salvage as secondary. Collateral is valued at contracted/acquisition-cost rates during the term; at the **amortization cliff** the residual is whatever re-rent the market gives. The capital structure prices this split explicitly: ~5.9% senior contract-backed vs **9.625% unsecured notes** funding the residual claim — the 370bp gap is the market's live quote on GPU residual risk. The stack compounds quarterly (web pass, Aug-15): [DDTL 5.0 ($3.1B, May-18)](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Closes-3-1-Billion-Loan-Facility-Expanding-Access-to-Public-Markets-for-GPU-Backed-Financing/default.aspx) and continued draws took total principal debt from $25.15B (Mar-31) to **~$35.6B at Jun-30** with $10B undrawn; a further [$2.6B JPMorgan/MUFG facility closed Aug-10](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260810690198/en/CoreWeave-Closes-$2.6-Billion-Loan-Facility-Expanding-Financing-Flexibility-for-AI-Infrastructure) (due 2031, reported SOFR+550, oversubscribed). Q2 net interest $640M ≈ **24.9% of $2.575B revenue** — still pinned to the 20–25% historical vendor-finance break line ($6.07B of 2026-scheduled principal per the Q1 10-Q; Q2 schedule not yet surfaced). One issuer now quotes a full credit curve — SOFR+225 (A3, contract-backed) → SOFR+550 (Aug-10 marginal facility, unrated) → 9.625% unsecured — a 325bp step between the contract-wrapped and marginal GPU paper. Backlog $104B at Jun-30, ~$129B including >$25B of early-Q3 signings; top-three revenue concentration 36%/26%/10%. The CRWV thesis Mental Models log carries the systemic point: **~$120B of sector AI debt has moved off-books via SPVs** — reported leverage understates the complex.

### T4 — Customer prepayment / customer-as-lender (the Nebius–Oracle template)

**Structure**: the customer finances the vendor's capex, positionally senior to every external lender because the cash arrives *before* the asset exists. Variants: cash prepay ([[Theses/NBIS - Nebius Group]]: Microsoft ~$7B upfront on a ~$17.4–19.4B deal — ~40% upfront, ~$51K/GPU ≈ 28% of Vera Rubin per-GPU capex of $183K; Meta $27B = $12B dedicated + $15B flexible); in-kind prepay ([[Theses/ORCL - Oracle Corporation]]: **$75B of the $638B RPO is customer-prepaid or customer-supplied GPUs**; Oracle's FY27 plan nets $20–25B of customer prepays from $90–95B gross capex); deposits-against-shipments in the component chain (AXT–Lumentum $87M InP deposits, the late-2000s solar-polysilicon shape); and power-side queue prepays (10–15% behind-the-meter deposits now standard to hold interconnection slots).

**Mechanism and price**: from [[Research/2026-08-05 - NBIS CRWV - Rubin Fleet Economics - Rate Sensitivity Model - deep-dive]], prepay value to the operator is PV = q·TCV + (1−q)·TCV·A(r,60); a 40% upfront lifts 5-year contract PV ~8% at an 8% discount rate — the customer is selling the operator ~8 points of PV in exchange for capacity priority and price. What the customer buys back is delivery risk: a prepay claim in operator insolvency is trade-credit-shaped (unsecured unless escrowed — Meta's $10.80B restricted MMFs under infrastructure purchase-agreement escrow shows the mitigation form). What the operator buys is escape from T3: NBIS Insight #5 treats prepay/contract-secured funding as the anti-DDTL — offtake underwriting with **no residual-value put written on the box** — and the thesis carries → CLOSE if capex migrates to primarily GPU-collateralized debt. Prepay share of new deals is therefore a *structure-quality* signal, not just a funding line. NBIS's Q2 (Aug-12) turned the template into an operating model: **>70% of new deals carry upfront payments, >$9B of 2026 prepayments expected, offsetting 50–60% of related capex**, target funding mix ~60% prepay / 40% equity+debt across a raised 5GW contracted-power target — and the Microsoft upfronts ($6.96B) are **milestone-gated to deployment/availability rather than lump-sum at signing**: the customer writes its completion-risk protection into the prepay schedule itself, T4's native answer to underwriting criterion #1.

### T5 — Vendor financing & credit support (the NVDA / AVGO templates)

The vendor descends its own loss waterfall to make customers financeable. Four NVDA instruments, escalating in commitment:

| Instrument | Terms on record | Layer financed | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer equity | ~$40B+ AI portfolio; ~13% of CRWV (~$4.7B) | Whole-co | Live |
| Site-lease guarantees | **$3.5B max aggregate, 5–7y terms, $712M partner escrow**; remedy: take over lease / occupy / sublease | Site layer only (land, power, shell — not compute) | Live (FY26 10-K) |
| Residual-value support | "Up to 25% of an opportunity," selected deals; denominator, legal form (guarantee vs put vs repurchase), trigger, valuation date, priority — all undisclosed | Movable-equipment layer | Offered, none entered |
| $500B platform MOUs | Aug-10 MOUs with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman, KKR; independent project-level underwriting; >$500B mobilization ambition *over time* | Market-building | Uncommitted |

Three formulations are false on the public record: NVIDIA has not raised $500B; no single $500B vehicle exists; NVIDIA has not guaranteed the amount. The 10-K states financing requests were received and *none entered* — the $3.5B site guarantees are a separate, narrower instrument than the RV support Huang "may" offer. Reported and unsettled (Jul-27, unconfirmed by either company): NVDA weighing **~$250B of lease/construction-debt guarantees** to let OpenAI lease SB Energy's 10GW Piketon campus, plus discussions on up to **$350B of chip financing** — two orders of magnitude above the live $3.5B layer; any closed version converts vendor first-loss from footnote to capital structure.

AVGO's XPV is the disclosed contrast — vendor-financed *offtake* paper: Apollo assumed the rack purchase agreements + customer leases (Jun-8 10-Q); **Broadcom backstops 5 years of customer lease obligations, maximum exposure $29B, rising as racks deploy and falling as the customer pays**; post-default remedies: assume the leases or sell the racks. The $35B initial Apollo-led capital connects (per press, not the filing) to >1GW of Anthropic capacity from mid-2026 at Fluidstack sites, inside a >20GW-by-2028 ambition. $29B is a dynamic EAD path, not a guarantee of the $35B. Sell-side dimensionalized the scaling path Aug 11–14: BofA cut its AVGO credit view and modeled tranche-one RVG exposure peaking ~$26B in Sep-2027 (max loss ~$2.9B) — and at the full >20GW ambition, **maximum RVG exposure ~$370B by mid-2029 with loss scenarios up to ~$42B**; AVGO fell ~6% on the framing. The market began pricing the vendor's waterfall seat before a single default.

**Mechanism**: vendor support fills the interval between contract failure and asset recovery so a third party will fund the purchase — demand enablement priced as credit support. The correlation problem is squared for the vendor: the same AI-demand shock that raises customer PD and rack LGD also hits the vendor's own P&L (AVGO's top-5 end customers ≈ 45% of revenue), so the support is weakest exactly when drawn. Reference class: Lucent/Nortel 1999–2001 vendor finance; Stratechery's 1873 Jay Cooke/Northern Pacific overlay — financing innovation as margin defense, not demand proof.

The instrument set mutated through 2025–26 (web pass, Aug-15). **De-escalation**: NVDA's $100B OpenAI LOI (Sep-25, one tranche per GW deployed) stalled and resolved into a **$30B straight equity ticket** inside OpenAI's $122B round (closed Mar-31-26 at $852B; Amazon $50B, SoftBank $30B) — the loudest circularity headline ended in the *weakest* instrument: unsecured equity, no deployment gating, Huang calling the full $100B "probably not in the cards." **Escalation elsewhere**: the [Valor Compute Infrastructure fund](https://www.apollo.com/insights-news/pressreleases/2026/01/apollo-backs-5-4-billion-valor-and-xai-data-center-compute-infrastructure-transaction-with-3-5-billion-capital-solution-3214463) bought ~100k GB200s for $5.4B — Apollo $3.5B debt, **NVDA as anchor LP (~$1.9B)** — and triple-net leases the compute to an xAI subsidiary, inside a reported $20B program ($7.5B equity incl. up to $2B NVDA + ~$12.5B debt): the vendor sits in the equity of the vehicle financing its own product, one seat below the backstop. The sponsor's own curve explains why: xAI's Jun-25 corporate package priced at **12.5%** (secured notes and term loan; TLB SOFR+725 at 96 OID) — the wide end of the complex — so the SPV manufactures cheaper credit than the sponsor's name can. Two mirror instruments complete the set: NVDA paid Crusoe **~$150M to hold the Abilene expansion on NVDA sockets** after the anchor tenant walked (a vendor capacity option — paying the developer rather than financing the buyer), and AMD granted OpenAI a **160M-share penny warrant** (~10% of AMD at $0.01) vesting per-GW against a 6GW commitment plus AMD share-price targets to $600 — vendor financing inverted: the vendor pays the customer synthetic equity for demand.

### T6 — Infra-platform / sovereign offtake wrap (the Theseus template)

**Structure**: infrastructure GP + sovereign wealth own the platform and fund majority equity (Theseus = Macquarie AM funds + GIC); the AI lab anchors under long-term leases (Anthropic, US-first) with novel indemnities (Anthropic covers consumer electricity-price increases from the sites — a political-risk absorption clause with no precedent in DC leasing); the chip-lease leg is confirmed (Bloomberg, Jun-9): a **Compute SPV bought ~1GW of TPUs for ~$35B and leases them to Anthropic, with Google backstopping the lease payments at five datacenters — off both Google's and Anthropic's balance sheets**; Blackstone is in early talks on a second ~$36B Ironwood-TPU package that would take Google-hardware chip-lease credit to **~$71B**, against Anthropic's ~$19B/yr compute spend and a confidential S-1 draft (Jun-1). The sponsor backstop is the vendor backstop's mirror image — the platform selling the chips guarantees its customer's leases so third-party credit funds the purchase; same waterfall seat as AVGO's $29B, entered from the customer side. Adjacent prints: BlackRock AIP targeting up to $100B incl. debt; GIP's $40B Aligned acquisition closed Jul-21. The capital pool is the point: Apollo (~$1.05T), Blackstone (>$1.3T), Brookfield (>$1T), BlackRock (~$15.3T) appear across *both* NVDA and AVGO platforms — the financing layer is silicon-agnostic and rents to every stack.

**Stargate runs the grid at maximum depth** (web pass, Aug-15): topco equity reported — never confirmed by the parties — as SoftBank/OpenAI ~40%/40% ($19B each) with Oracle/MGX ~20%; underneath, each site is its own stack. Abilene: a Blue Owl / Crusoe / Primary Digital JV owns the shell, financed by JPMorgan construction loans ($2.3B, then $7.1B; ~$15B committed) → **Oracle leases the site for 15 years and owns the 450,000+ GB200s** → OpenAI consumes under its 4.5GW Oracle agreement. The end customer's credit never touches the SPV — Oracle's corporate wrap interposes, which is exactly where [[Theses/ORCL - Oracle Corporation]]'s $638B RPO risk concentrates. The chain [fractured in March](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-06/oracle-and-openai-end-plans-to-expand-flagship-data-center): Oracle and OpenAI scrapped the ~600MW Abilene expansion after unresolved financing talks and shifting demand forecasts — the first marquee break in a flagship chain, with Meta in talks for the freed capacity. A duration lesson sits inside it: a 15-year operator lease over a 4.5GW consumption agreement still fractured at the *expansion* decision, where no instrument had locked anyone in.

**Below the SPV sits a fifth tranche** ([[Research/2026-08-14 - TSM NVDA - TSPA AI Servers Bottleneck Money - deep-dive]]): ODM working capital. Foxconn/Quanta/Wistron/Wiwynn fund rack builds on bank credit; +$5B of AI-server sales adds $0.5B (capital-efficient) to $1.5B (capital-heavy) of WC; Taiwan banks are hitting *group-concentration* caps, not liquidity caps; consignment vs ODM-owned GPU inventory is the swing on who carries the float. A financed factory can miss a quarter because a bank concentration limit binds two layers below the SPV.

**The anti-template — merchant short-tenor**: SpaceX sells compute on **90-day cancellation at $30–50M/MW/yr** (vs the 15–20-year take-or-pay standard), claiming <1yr payback. Extraordinary price, zero debt capacity: 90-day paper underwrites no waterfall, which is why the model runs on equity + vendor support (NVDA vendor financing is the stated basis of SpaceX's NVDA exclusivity). The power-market version of the same trade: ~$5,000/MWh on 90-day terms vs ~$271/MWh on 20-year take-or-pay. Near-term willingness-to-pay and financeable duration are different commodities. The long end is now printing too: Anthropic signed a **20-year, ~$19B lease** at TeraWulf's 401MW Hawesville miner-conversion (Jul-26, online 2H-27) and Bitdeer a 16-year $4.7B lease at Tydal — converted-power sites supplying the first 15–20-year offtake prints, the far end of the barbell the 90-day merchant model anchors.

### T7 — Powered-shell ABS: the layer that already standardized

**The securitization frontier runs exactly along the four-layer map's layer-1/layer-2 boundary** (web pass, Aug-15). Site-fixed DC paper is a functioning asset class: US DC ABS issuance $2.4B (2020) → $15.5B (2025) with 2026 pacing a record; outstanding $4B → **$61B** (Barclays, YTD-26), projected ~$180B by YE-2028; 69 ABS deals / 19 issuers / $34.4B through Sep-25 (Sabey most active; DataBank largest at $3.2B). Conventions are settled: master trusts, ~5-year anticipated-repayment dates against 30-year legal finals, senior tranches capped at **A/A−** (84% of S&P-rated issuance at A), mean senior spreads ~138–145bp over 5-yr UST, 2026 curve AAA ~100–160bp → BBB 230–300 → BB 320–410. Fresh prints: [Aligned $1.183B (Jul-28, upsized ~30%)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/28/3334210/0/en/aligned-data-centers-completes-1-18-billion-securitization-financing.html), Vantage's EMEA sterling tap (Jan-26), and the first big stabilized cap-rate mark — Digital Realty buying Blackstone's 64% of three fully-leased NoVA campuses at $7.8B gross, **>6.5% cap rate** (Jun-29). Methodology exists at every agency — S&P (Jun-24, rev. **What has never priced: a single GPU- or compute-backed ABS** ([SFA, Jul-26](https://structuredfinance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/SFA-Research-Corner_How-Data-Center-ABS-and-CMBS-Fit-in-a-Broader-Financing-Ecosystem.pdf) — equipment financings "may emerge as a complementary sector"). Two structural reasons: no agency methodology covers equipment residuals, and scale mismatch — average DC ABS is ~$600M (CMBS ~$1.2B) against Beignet's $27.3B, so mega-projects route through T2 SPV notes and private credit. Morgan Stanley's channel split of the ~$1.5T external financing need through 2028: $200B corporate debt, $150B securitized, **$800B private credit**, $350B other. The hierarchy is observable: shells securitize, contracts wrap in SPV notes, compute stays private — each layer priced by a different market at different disclosure.

## Reading a new deal — the screen

Four criteria, in order (Superposition):
1. **Completion** — when do power, cooling, racks, networking reach commercial operation, and who bears delay and overruns? (Payment usually starts at acceptance; pre-completion risk sits with sponsors/vendors/contractors. Energy expansion is multiyear; a single missing component stalls the project.)
2. **First performing-state repayment source** — long-term contract, diversified market revenue, or mix; does CFADS cover debt service, stressed?
3. **Bankable redeployability** — time and cost to a *billable* state with a replacement user; net recovery after discounts, requalification, relocation, enforcement, downtime.
4. **Duration alignment + loss waterfall** — do contract term, vendor-support period, loan maturity, and technology economic life align, and who takes first loss / last loss?

Headline hygiene, from 2026's live errors: an MOU is not a fund ($500B); a dynamic maximum exposure is not a guarantee of deal size ($29B ≠ $35B); "up to 25% of an opportunity" with no denominator is not $125B; a press-release counterparty is not a filing counterparty (Anthropic appears in the XPV announcement, "customer" in the 10-Q); an A+ on SPV notes rates the guarantee-plus-sale-process, not the building; current GPU rental prices are not collateral value at maturity.

## Standardization test — does factory paper become an asset class?

Aircraft finance (EETC), CMBS, and powered-shell DC ABS each became asset classes when five inputs standardized: loss-waterfall conventions, completion conditions, collateral rights, performance measurement, and secondary-market pricing. The shell layer now has all five (T7); compute-layer paper has none, and the missing empirical inputs are dated:

| Missing input | What exists instead | First possible print |
|---|---|---|
| Empty-campus clearing price | Zero qualifying vacated single-tenant >100MW transactions, 2015–Aug 2026 | Whenever a tenant first walks; Hyperion renewal cluster ~2033 |
| GPU residual in a downcycle | Acquisition-cost collateral marks; 4-y-old H100s at $2.29–3.12/hr rental, $12–22K resale | First Hopper re-rent prints 2026–27 (CRWV → HIGH if >70% of contracted rate) |
| Platform economics | MOU headlines; no committed tickets, spreads, LTVs, maturities | First closed ticket under the NVDA platforms |
| RV-support contract form | "Up to 25%" with undefined denominator/trigger/priority | First signed platform agreement |
| Correlated-downturn waterfall print | Untested; PD/LGD/EAD correlation is theoretical until it isn't | First neocloud restructuring or backstop draw |
| Settled rating methodology for GPU collateral | Shell-layer methodologies published at four agencies (S&P Jun-24, Moody's Feb-25, KBRA final Jan-26, Fitch exposure draft Jul-25 consulting on residual-value relevance) — none covers compute equipment; the reported Fitch "GPU depreciation consultation" did not verify against any agency and is likely a garble of the exposure draft | First agency methodology naming GPU/equipment collateral |

Until these print, the complex is running a **credit experiment at installation-phase scale**: the structures are functioning as capital-mobilization machinery (the [G-4] frenzy mechanism working as designed), while the loss-allocation machinery — the actual product being sold to pension and insurance AUM — has never run once. Both readings of the same fact are live: standardization succeeding would cut WACC across every thesis in the complex; the first bad print into an oversupplied re-rent market would reprice all six templates simultaneously, because they share anchors.

## Mental Models

Applied per the READING PROTOCOL in [[Generalist - Overview]] — hypotheses to test, not verdicts:

- **[G-4] Perez frenzy-phase machinery** — the templates ARE financial capital's transmission into physical build; T3/T5/T6 carry the installation-phase signature (return-agnostic capital, vendor credit, narrative-priced equity). Hypothesis: if economic life exceeds financing maturity and oversupply stays limited, the same loop cheapens deployment; if capital outruns demand, "highly redeployable" assets chase the same replacement buyers. Test: duration-alignment axis per deal, not aggregate capex.
- **[G-3] Reflexivity as credit math** — correlated PD × LGD × EAD is reflexivity written into a loss formula: cheap credit → deployment → apparent demand → cheaper credit, reversing all at once. Test: does any new structure *break* the correlation (true third-party demand diversification) or only relabel it?
- **[G-10] Outside view** — reference class is specialized-lending books on short-cycle equipment with correlated offtake (Basel object finance; 1999–2001 vendor finance; 1880s→93 railroad receiverships resolved by Morganization). Base rate: such books get larger, then tighter — not cheaper — as the installed base scales into a boom; consolidation, not construction, is where returns accrued. The vault's own agreement across templates ("finance unlocks deployment") is the cue to hunt the single falsifier: one downturn print where PD and LGD move together and a waterfall is disclosed.
- **[G-13] Priced variable** — the market prices headline capital ($500B) and FLOPS; the actual value driver is the loss waterfall and duration stack. Expectations edge = tracking the undisclosed terms (spreads, LTVs, denominators) as they surface, ahead of consensus re-rating.
- **[G-7]/[G-1] ROIIC optics** — structures exist partly to keep reported ROIC and leverage clean while claims migrate off-book ($2.92B recognized vs $46.03B exposure; ~$120B sector debt in SPVs). Quantitative-screen investors consume the flattered numbers; the edge is reading the footnotes as the real balance sheet.
- **[VLM] financing-layer test** — NVDA sells lenders an infrastructure-toll story (CUDA + installed-base remarketing) while stepping *down* into first-loss via guarantees and RV support: a layer-owner voluntarily descending the stack is a durability test of the toll, not proof it is widening. The silicon-agnostic AUM platforms (Apollo/Blackstone on both stacks) are candidates for the actual new layer — capital that taxes every silicon outcome. CRWV/NBIS remain layer-renters whose WACC is set by whichever underwriting pole their paper uses.
- **[Automation lens, §3]** — the duration mismatch that every template must bridge is organizational: supply installs on financing clocks (quarters), demand matures on enterprise-readiness clocks (years). The templates are all, at bottom, devices for carrying that gap; none of them closes it.
- **[semis #5, #10]** — the credit regime is the economic-cycle overlay (a spread shock compresses the cycle regardless of bottlenecks); anchor concentration makes each template's "diversified" book a single-anchor survival test.
