**Underlying generalist principles that apply most directly to this sector**:
- *Mean reversion vs trend continuation*: the hardest meta-question in every position is which applies. Misapplying mean reversion to compounders (selling NVDA in 2017) or trend continuation to cyclicals (chasing SanDisk at memory cycle peaks) is the single most expensive equity research mistake — and semis is where this mistake compounds fastest because the cycles are sharp and the compounders look indistinguishable from cyclicals at low resolution.
- *S-curve location*: every technology and every segment sits on its own adoption curve — invisible R&D, early adopters, chasm, mainstream, saturation. Pre-chasm = binary outcome distribution, high failure risk; mid-chasm = S-curve secured but timing uncertain, the highest-edge zone for long-term capital; late mainstream = peak earnings, falling growth, mean-reversion regime. Investors consistently extrapolate early-adopter growth as if it represented saturation behavior, or declare saturation when an enabling technology has actually shifted the asymptote upward.
- *Pricing power and moats*: distinguish between direct network effects, two-sided marketplaces, data network effects, scale economies, switching-cost lock-in, and qualification-gate monopolies. Most claimed moats don't actually qualify under scrutiny — and the moat type determines durability (direct networks succumb to bundling; two-sided marketplaces to disintermediation; qualification-gate monopolies are typically the most durable in this sector).
- *Power-law portfolio returns and barbell construction*: public market returns are power-law / lognormal rather than normal — 2–3 names in a 30-stock book typically drive >70% of returns over a decade. Mean-variance optimization systematically underweights asymmetric upside; Kelly-adjusted concentration on highest-conviction names dominates. The barbell (80–90% in resilient compounders + 10–20% in downside-bounded convex bets) resolves the vol-vs-concentration tension. The >90% NAV concentration in semis is intentional under this logic.
- *Long-horizon edge*: quant + L/S funds have wrung out short-term (<1mo) and short-to-medium-term (1–6mo) alpha. The market's capacity to assign value to 3–5+ year datapoints is structurally weaker than ever, because trading volume is concentrated in shorter horizons. Long-horizon thesis-driven investing is where alpha persists in this sector.

## A. Pricing Power & Bottlenecks

#1 - Emerging bottlenecks drive asymmetric pricing-power shifts; identifying them is the dominant alpha source in this sector. Absolute alpha exists investing in emerging bottlenecks and instances where shortages drive extremely asymmetric shifts in pricing power. This requires predicting two conditions simultaneously: (a) demand meaningfully outstripping supply, typically AI-driven and often catalyzed by a technology or architecture shift; AND (b) supply response being inelastic — either because demand-forecasting uncertainty makes suppliers conservative, or because suppliers in oligopolistic positions choose to exercise pricing power and let demand fall rather than expand capacity. The bottleneck location moves between cycles (logic wafers in 2021, CoWoS in 2024, HBM4 + advanced packaging in 2026–2027) — always re-identify which segment is currently binding rather than anchoring to last cycle's bottleneck. Live examples: [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] (CoWoS), [[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] (HBM), [[Theses/BESI - BE Semiconductor Industries]] (hybrid bonding).

#2 - Qualification-gate monopolies are hidden behind share data. Absolute discernment in product quality differentiation may be a serious edge, requiring engineering/physics-level analysis. Qualification to enter the supply chain for failure- or yield-sensitive end markets demands extremely precise and high-grade product, and there are opportunities where the incumbent with highest market share but multiple small competitors is perceived as not having pricing power due to the smaller competitors — but adjusted for the product grade achievable, the incumbent is an absolute monopoly. Three flavors to distinguish: (i) capacity scarcity independent of grade — cycle-dependent pricing power ([[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] CoWoS); (ii) qualification gate that only one vendor crosses — structural pricing power even at low share ([[Theses/2802 - Ajinomoto]] ABF film, [[Theses/KLA - KLA Corporation]] inspection, ASML EUV, [[Theses/BESI - BE Semiconductor Industries]] hybrid bonding); (iii) design lock-in via switching cost or software dependency ([[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]] CUDA, EDA triopoly).

## B. Capital Cycle Mechanics

#3 - The semi cycle is a variation of the general industrial cycle, sharpened by inventory-as-commodity dynamics. Capital goods and consumables in this industry can be hoarded and trade like commodities, especially during shortages when speculative buying for customer inventories takes over. This incentives a capital investment spree that drives a delayed supply response, often calibrated to inflated prices — and when supply catches up and underlying demand separates from speculative inventory buildup, a demand pocket emerges, leading to a downward price spiral, production shuttering, and complete halt in factory starts. The cycle's asymmetry — fast-up, slower-down with terminal capitulation — is why timing entries near capitulation is the only reliable approach to true cyclicals like commodity DRAM and NAND.

#4 - The tech-curve race compounds the cycle. Generational improvements in product specs or industrial production processes drive product-obsolescence risk and cost-per-effective-output advantages for the vendor at the top of the technology curve. During supply additions a "race" emerges to remain at the competitive end of the technological cost curve — supply-disciplined firms are outcompeted eventually by aggressive firms that take the opportunity to upgrade their industrial capacity technology, while firms too aggressive in upgrading go bankrupt. The middle path (capacity + tech upgrade calibrated to demand visibility) is rare and is the operating signature differentiating [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] from Samsung Foundry and from [[Theses/INTC - Intel]] Foundry.

#5 - The economic cycle underpins everything. Underpinning all the semi-specific cycle dynamics is the economic cycle, which drives coordinated volatility in demand profile across the entire industrial and consumer supply chain — most of it driven by discretionary credit policies at commercial banks or monetary policy at central banks. Semi cycles overlay on this base layer, so a tight monetary regime can compress a semi up-cycle by 18+ months even when segment-specific bottlenecks remain unrelieved.

#6 - Peak margins don't mean-revert symmetrically when qualification stickiness is high. Industrial-cycle intuition expects margins to round-trip from peak to mid-cycle to trough as new capacity arrives, but in segments with qualification gates and multi-year design cycles the downcycle margin compression is shallower than the industrial template predicts — the qualified-supplier base doesn't expand quickly enough to compete pricing back to commodity equilibrium. Failure mode: applying industrial-cycle margin-reversion templates to qualification-gated segments. Most visible in WFE primes ([[Theses/AMAT - Applied Materials]], [[Theses/LRCX - Lam Research]]) where 2019–2020 trough margins exceeded prior-cycle peaks.

## C. Demand Architecture

#7 - Unit growth vs price growth divergence tells you the cycle phase. Units up + prices up = shortage; units up + prices flat = healthy expansion; units down + prices flat = inventory disposal; units down + prices down = capitulation. The most useful signal is the second derivative — when prices start firming with units still falling, the cycle has bottomed even if no one calls it. Forward indicators: distributor lead times, OEM inventory days, and "allocation" language reappearing in supplier earnings calls.

#8 - Architecture transitions remap the bottleneck location. Monolithic → chiplet shifted scarcity from leading-edge logic wafers to advanced packaging substrates and capacity. PCIe Gen5 → Gen6 + CXL adoption shifts memory architecture from local to disaggregated. NRZ → PAM4 → PAM6 + co-packaged optics shifts optical scarcity from PCB to photonic integration. NVL72 → NVL576 → rack-scale shifts system-level scarcity from individual GPUs to interconnect bandwidth + power. Reading an architecture announcement before it is fully priced is the highest-leverage way to forecast where the next bottleneck materializes.

#9 - Inventory propagates through the chain with predictable lag. Inventory builds at distributors first, then OEMs, then contract manufacturers, then spot markets. It unwinds in reverse — spot tightens first, contract follows 1–2 quarters later, then OEMs, then distributors. The psychological turn point (sell-side capitulation) typically arrives 1–2 quarters after spot signals have already turned — that gap is where long-horizon edge lives, since quant + L/S funds have wrung out the shorter-duration alpha and the market's capacity to price 3–5+ year datapoints is structurally weaker than ever.

## D. Competitive Dynamics

#10 - Anchor customer concentration is a binary survival test, not a diversification weakness. Each non-monopoly leading-edge foundry, each follower HBM vendor, and each emerging packaging line survives on 1–2 anchor customers rather than a diversified book — loss of a single anchor is existential, not "concentration risk to monitor." Apple → [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] N2 ≥50%, NVDA → SK Hynix HBM3E, Tesla AI6 → Samsung Taylor 2nm $16.5B through 2033, AWS + Microsoft → Intel 18A. The right analytical frame isn't "what's the customer concentration" but "what happens if this single anchor fails to renew" — the answer is typically "the vendor exits the segment."

## E. Classification & Reclassification

#13 - Compounder / semi-cyclical / true cyclical classification is the most expensive call in this sector. Misclassifying a compounder as cyclical (selling at trough mid-compounding) or a cyclical as a compounder (paying peak multiples at cycle top) is the highest-cost error a generalist equity investor can make, full stop — and this sector is where the mistake compounds fastest because the cycles are sharp, the compounders look indistinguishable from cyclicals at low resolution, and the multiple rerate on correct classification is large. Structural compounders are characterized by qualification-gate monopoly + customer lock-in widening each generation ([[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] leading edge, ASML EUV, [[Theses/KLA - KLA Corporation]] inspection, [[Theses/2802 - Ajinomoto]], [[Theses/BESI - BE Semiconductor Industries]], [[Theses/NVDA - Nvidia]]). True cyclicals are characterized by contestable capacity + commodity output ([[Theses/SNDK - SanDisk]] NAND, commodity DRAM, mature-node foundry). The semi-cyclical middle category is the most common and most misread — WFE primes, HBM at qualified leaders, advanced packaging, test equipment, custom silicon.

#14 - Reclassification triggers flip the category and rerate the multiple. A compounder losing a qualification drops to semi-cyclical (Samsung Foundry post-NVDA-HBM3 loss; [[Theses/INTC - Intel]] IDM losing leading edge 2018–2024). A cyclical winning a structural anchor upgrades to semi-cyclical compounder ([[Theses/000660 - SK Hynix]] post-NVDA HBM monopoly 2023–2024; [[Theses/LITE - Lumentum]] post-NVDA NVLink optics qualification). Architecture shifts move the bottleneck — reclassify source AND destination simultaneously. Cycle-cliff in a commodity tier can force consolidation that repromotes survivors (potential NAND consolidation: [[Theses/285A - Kioxia]] + [[Theses/SNDK - SanDisk]] + WD).

## F. Geopolitical Overlay

#15 - Subsidized multi-region oligopoly bakes in a decade of margin dilution. The post-2022 transition from cost-optimized global supply chain to subsidized multi-regional oligopoly means capacity duplication across US (CHIPS Act), EU (€43B), Japan (METI + Rapidus), and Korea (national support) — the qualified-supplier base expands faster than end demand. The investment implication: peak-cycle margins in this regime should be discounted, not extrapolated, particularly for foundry and memory incumbents operating geographically duplicated fab footprints.

#16 - Geopolitical bifurcation creates parallel markets with independent cycles. SMIC, CXMT, ChangXin, YMTC operate on supply / demand curves disconnected from TSMC / SK Hynix / Micron / Kioxia. The Chinese-domestic stack — Huawei Atlas 950DT SuperCluster as the first non-NVDA + non-TSMC hyperscale AI architecture — permanently removes a ~$40–50B AI-compute bucket from the Western supply chain. Western incumbents shouldn't model Chinese-domestic capacity as future Western competition; it's a parallel market with permanent walls. The implication: Western leading-edge concentration in [[Theses/TSM - Taiwan Semiconductor]] is structurally higher than aggregate share suggests, because Chinese-domestic share is non-contestable.

## G. Common Anti-Patterns

#17 - Assuming new entrants materialize at tight prices. Multi-billion fab capex + 5–7yr lead times mean the supply response in semis is structurally inelastic vs the industrial benchmark. Sell-side models that assume capacity arrives "in line with demand" systematically miscalibrate up-cycle duration. The right base case: supply lags demand by 18–30 months at every major node transition, with persistent shortages until the next architecture shift relocates the bottleneck.

#18 - Conflating cycle and structural shifts. Reading a cycle inflection as structural ("memory is dead" at 2019 trough) or vice versa ("AI demand is forever" at 2025 peak) is a recurring error. Cycle and structural shifts coexist — the skill is decomposing observed pricing/volume moves into cycle component + structural component, not picking one frame. Two diagnostic questions to ask in tandem: (1) is the observed move mean-reverting or trend-continuing — i.e., does the segment's history support reversion or is the move driven by a regime that won't unwind; and (2) where on the technology S-curve does the underlying segment sit — pre-chasm (binary outcome distribution), mid-chasm (S-curve secured, timing uncertain, highest-edge zone for long-term capital), or late mainstream (peak earnings, falling growth, mean-reversion regime). The answer to (2) heavily disciplines the answer to (1).

#19 - Reading equipment orders as a direct demand signal. Orders can pull forward or push out by 6+ months from utilization-driven need depending on customer capex policy, vendor lead times, and corporate optionality. The cleaner signal is utilization × wpm capacity at the foundry — book-to-bill is a noisy proxy that lags the underlying demand it claims to track.

**L1 - DRAM sector will no longer be as cyclical as historical cycles.**  Historically, memory triopoly discipline is rented pricing power. Samsung / SK Hynix / Micron have repeatedly shown that triopoly discipline holds for 4–7 years then breaks when one player chooses share over price, or when a fourth entrant (currently CXMT) gains scale. Pricing power in commodity DRAM is rented, not owned — the model that works is short-cycle trading around discipline-vs-share inflections rather than long-term ownership at peak multiples. Distinct from HBM, where qualification gates create genuine pricing power for the qualified supplier (per #2). Going forward, memory triopoly likely no longer compete as intensively on generational node upgrades as HBM becomes a greater share of revenues where consumer HBM enter into long term supply contracts with manufacturer. Shift to contracted markets from spot commodity markets shifts competitive dynamics in favour of volume and pricing discipline. Further, technology delta between HBM vendors around yield and defect and speed rates as well as bifurcation in design principles likely to lead to winner take most dynamics in DRAM (similar to NVDA vs. AMD in GPUs) where current winner SK Hynix takes 70% of overall volume share for NVDA and MU takes none in Rubin generation products. DRAM best of breed likely trades from 7-10x mid-cycle earnings to 15-25x mid-cycle earnings as a result. 

**L2 - WFE re-rates as structurally non-cyclical high moat industrial monopolies (TDG etc.)** - WFE primes are picks-and-shovels with structurally rising floors. Each cycle's WFE trough floor is structurally higher than the last because (a) more process steps per node — GAA needs more deposition layers than FinFET; (b) more customers globally — geopolitical region-duplication expands the customer base; (c) higher capital intensity per wafer — leading-edge fab costs have risen ~30–40% per node. As end markets become less cylical, equipment demand also becomes more predictable. Furthermore, end markets in a period of structural shortage results in equipment vendors with predictable, growing book of revenues that appear to not follow any cyclical pattern, or alternatively, high troughs cycles. 

**L3 - Further thoughts**

- Industry previously needed to compete based on technological process advantage and thus needed to invest in capex to remain competitive in every generation, depressing pricing as volume expands rapidly, however this is no longer as pertinent because the industry has consolidated, and the playbook of 'pushing the last viable competitor to oblivion' no longer works because there is now a competitive equilibirum
- Related point, industry pricing has traditionally been highly uncertain so players tended to price low using spot market for commodity-like products because there are too many names to respond to, you go aggressive and at whatever margin sustains yourself rather than collaborate to maintain pricing power 
- Related point, as demand growth has been steady and incremental in the age of AI, and the fact that industry volume is capped by various factors like fab capacity (TSMC N3, CoWoS, HBM), power, land, permitting, and various bottlenecks means we are not likely to get a 'glut' anytime soon due to supply not needing to ramp up to respond to a massive demand peak (historically when demand was high and volatile, you needed to ramp up supply quickly in order to not get left behind on the technology node race).
- There is, in most markets in semiconductors, a 2-3 player race where there is a technology leader, earning very high margins, and a technology laggard, earning reasonable but healthy margins. Traditionally in semis when there were companies with similar product specs (i.e. a commodity dynamic), the market share laggard can lower prices to destroy industry pricing to gain share, which is rational. But in this dynamic the technology laggard is generally supported by customer orders preserving a second choice option (AMD in GPUs, MRVL in Switches) to remove absolute pricing power from the dominant technology and market share leader, this is a stable equilibirum game theoritically where neither side wants to budge on pricing.
- The rise of TSMC and foundry businesses that are disconnected from fabless semi companies removed the volume vs. technology leadership dynamic from product pricing. Historically,  non-fabless semi houses all fought to secure volume so that they dont lose the technology race in process node leadership. Now manufacturing is done via fabs, it has equalled the playing field and market share increments are no longer critical to support a huge capex burden and technology migration economies of scale leadership, ensuring more disciplined pricing.
