#### English Script
Over the next few weeks, I'm breaking down one of the most consequential companies of the AI era: TSMC.

TSMC doesn't design the chips inside an iPhone or an AI server. It manufactures designs created by customers such as Apple, Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom. That makes it a pure-play foundry: a factory partner that promises not to compete with the companies trusting it with their most valuable blueprints.

The model matters because advanced chip design has separated from advanced manufacturing. A customer can spend years and billions of dollars creating a processor, then rely on TSMC to turn that design into millions of working chips. TSMC earns revenue from the wafers it processes, and it earns more when customers use newer, harder, and more valuable manufacturing technologies.

The AI boom has shifted the mix sharply. TSMC reported $40.2 billion of Q2 2026 revenue, up 34% in US dollars from a year earlier, with a 67.7% gross margin. Technologies at 7 nanometers and below produced 77% of wafer revenue. Those numbers show that TSMC is not selling commodity factory time; it is monetizing the scarce manufacturing layer behind leading computing.

The counterpoint is concentration. AI and high-performance computing now dominate growth, while a small group of customers controls a large share of demand. If their spending slows, TSMC cannot instantly fill the same expensive capacity with ordinary chips.

TSMC is the neutral factory layer beneath competing chip designers, and AI has made that layer more valuable and more concentrated at the same time.

#### English Script
Welcome back—this is video two in the TSMC Investment Case.

A foundry does not really sell a finished wafer. It sells working chips delivered on time. Three variables drive the economics: how many wafers run through the factories, the price charged per wafer, and yield—the percentage of usable chips produced from each wafer.

Yield changes everything. Imagine two factories processing the same design. If one produces ninety working chips and the other produces sixty, the first factory gives the customer more saleable products from the same expensive materials and equipment. A high quoted wafer price can still be cheaper for the customer when the effective cost per working chip is lower.

The factory itself has huge fixed costs. Lithography machines, cleanrooms, engineers, power, and depreciation continue whether a production line is full or half empty. High utilization spreads those costs across more wafers, so incremental revenue can create disproportionate profit. This is why demand forecasting and capacity discipline matter as much as transistor physics.

Breadth improves the loop. In 2025, TSMC manufactured 12,682 products for 534 customers using 305 technologies. Each ramp produces process data, supplier learning, and operating routines that can improve later ramps.

The counterpoint is capital intensity. A leading-edge fab takes years to plan and costs tens of billions of dollars. If demand arrives late, changes technology, or disappears, depreciation remains while utilization and margins fall.

TSMC's economic product is dependable output per invested dollar, and its profit compounds only while yield, utilization, and capacity judgment stay aligned.

#### 中文脚本（普通话，简体）
欢迎回来，这是“台积电投资逻辑”的第二条视频。

晶圆厂真正卖的不是一片加工完成的晶圆，而是按时交付、能够正常工作的芯片。经济模型由三个变量决定：工厂处理多少晶圆、每片晶圆收多少钱，以及良率，也就是一片晶圆上最终可用芯片的比例。

良率会改变全部成本。假设两家工厂生产同一款设计，一家得到九十颗可用芯片，另一家只有六十颗。前者用相同的昂贵材料和设备，为客户提供更多可以销售的产品。所以，即使它的晶圆报价更高，每颗可用芯片的实际成本仍可能更低。

#### English Script
Video thirteen in the TSMC Investment Case gives the bear case its full weight.

TSMC's dominant risk is not losing five points of market share. It is losing access to the productive asset.

The leading-edge ecosystem remains overwhelmingly concentrated in Taiwan: fabs, advanced packaging, engineers, suppliers, power, water, and logistics. A blockade does not need to destroy a cleanroom to stop output. It can interrupt energy, chemicals, replacement parts, employee movement, and exports. Customers cannot immediately transfer a qualified design because a replacement process requires redesign, validation, and capacity that may not exist.

Scenario analysis estimates that an invasion, destructive shutdown, or long blockade could cause 85% to 95% permanent equity impairment. That is not a probability forecast. It describes severity if production is lost and customers rebuild supply chains elsewhere.

Arizona is the strongest counterargument. TSMC now discusses $265 billion of total US investment, including advanced logic and packaging, and customer pressure can accelerate the schedule. Geographic capacity also raises the political cost of abandoning TSMC.

But resilience is not duplication. Through the current investment horizon, overseas capacity remains a fraction of Taiwan's leading-edge scale and costs more to operate. The business also has commercial concentration: industry estimates place Nvidia and Apple together near two-fifths of revenue. A demand shock can therefore arrive before geographic diversification is complete.

The bear case is not that TSMC suddenly forgets how to manufacture; it is that geopolitics, utilities, or concentrated demand prevents its unmatched manufacturing system from operating.

#### English Script
This is video fourteen and the conclusion of the TSMC Investment Case.

TSMC does not maximize price because its objective is to maximize the lifetime value of the manufacturing ecosystem. Customer profit funds new designs, moderate increases reduce the incentive to qualify rivals, and trust secures multi-year capacity commitments. Restraint is rational while returns remain high.

Its competitive lead needs three clocks. Intel is close on selected process technology but still lacks major external high-volume proof. Samsung has a comparable 2-nanometer label and an integrated memory-plus-packaging stack, but not comparable disclosed merchant economics. Chinese foundries remain at least two major generations behind in independently confirmed commercial production, although subsidies can accelerate learning without acceptable private returns.

The bull case says AI complexity deepens TSMC's role across wafers, packaging, and design enablement. The bear case says high expectations, record investment, customer concentration, overseas dilution, and Taiwan can overwhelm even the best operating moat.

Two observables decide the classification. First, gross margin should remain around 63% to 64% or better after N2 matures and overseas costs are separated. Second, Intel or Samsung must put a named independent customer's leading-edge product into sustained high-volume production; an internal chip or risk-production announcement is not enough.

The evidence currently favors a durable compounder with deliberately restrained pricing and a merchant ecosystem lead measured in years, not an untouchable monopoly. Taiwan risk prevents that business conclusion from becoming a risk-free investment conclusion.
