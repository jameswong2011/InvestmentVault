# Pricing the End of the Semiconductor Cycle

**A structurally less cyclical semiconductor industry deserves two things the market historically denied it: a higher multiple and a more visible earnings base.** If qualification gates, service annuities, contracted HBM volumes and industry concentration keep the next trough above prior peaks, the right framework is not a commodity multiple on peak earnings. It is the **30–35 times multiple paid to high-quality industrial monopolies, applied to earnings only slightly above a defensible trough**. The reclassification matters twice: trough earnings are higher, and each dollar of those earnings is worth more because it is visible through the cycle.

**Even very conservative earnings growth leaves material upside outside equipment.** Start with the July 2026 forward multiples, assume only **5% annual EPS growth through 2028**, then haircut that earnings base to **85% for equipment, foundry and logic leaders** and **55% for memory**. The first haircut assumes a mild downturn; the second assumes memory still loses almost half its record earnings. Applying 30 times as the conservative industrial-monopoly case and 35 times as the high-quality case produces the following scenario math, not price targets:

| Segment / name | July 2026 forward P/E | Normalized earnings used | Return at 30x | Return at 35x |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| WFE equipment | 40–46x | 0.94x 2026E EPS | **−39% to −30%** | **−29% to −18%** |
| TSMC | ~23x | 0.94x 2026E EPS | **+22%** | **+43%** |
| Nvidia | ~21x | 0.94x 2026E EPS | **+34%** | **+56%** |
| Memory leaders | 5–6x | 0.61x 2026E EPS | **+203% to +264%** | **+254% to +325%** |

The implication is direct: **the less-cyclical reclassification alone supports roughly 20–55% upside for the foundry and logic monopolies, and a three-to-four-times outcome for memory if its first tamed trough arrives. Equipment already trades above the benchmark, so the same framework implies no re-rating upside there.**

**A tamed supply cycle does not guarantee that the stocks rise.** From here, the key determinant is whether **compute intensity across the economy — compute consumed per unit of GDP, revenue or labour — keeps rising faster than expectations already embedded in hyperscaler capex, AI monetization and semiconductor earnings**. Less cyclicality raises the earnings floor and improves visibility; it cannot create positive revisions if compute intensity merely matches, or falls below, the expected path. The equity bet therefore has two independent legs: supply must stay disciplined through a downturn, and compute demand must beat expectations.

The July 2026 price sheet still answers the cyclicality question three ways. Wafer-fab equipment trades at 40–46 times forward earnings, as though the cycle is dead. Memory trades at four to six times record earnings, as though nothing changed since 1996. TSMC and Nvidia trade near the index multiple on 40–85% growth, as though the cycle survives just long enough to stop them. The dispersion shows where the market has accepted the reclassification and where it still demands proof.

Between the June 22 record and July 24, roughly $2.1 trillion came off the listed semiconductor complex and the SOX entered a bear market. Samsung fell 7% on the largest quarterly profit in its history. Micron slid a quarter from its peak with 2026 supply sold out at agreed prices. TSMC fell 5% on the strongest quarter any semiconductor company has reported. Records were printed and records were sold. The marginal investor still runs the old model — whether that is loyalty to a fifty-year base rate or failure to notice a regime change is the trade.

## The machine that made the violence

Semiconductors became the most cyclical major industry on earth because four mechanisms compounded one another. Each has been removed or structurally weakened.

| Historical mechanism | What it did | What replaced it | 2026 evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dozens of undercapitalised producers | Downturns were fought to bankruptcy; predatory pricing carried a prize — the victim's share | DRAM consolidated to three scaled survivors; most other segments to two or three | No global DRAM entrant has reached scale in 30+ years outside China's walled ecosystem; bankrupting a peer now costs years of losses with no absorbable victim left |
| Spot-market commodity pricing | Every inventory swing transmitted directly into price; sellers priced to survive, not to collaborate | Multi-year contracted volume, led by HBM | 2026 HBM sold out with price and volume pre-agreed; Micron holds long-term agreements with 16 customers covering ~20% of DRAM and ~a third of NAND volume, a book management floors at $100 billion |
| The technology-race capex compulsion | Market share funded node economics, so volume wars were rational and permanent | The foundry model severed volume from process leadership for every fabless designer | TSMC runs ~92% of leading-edge output; share shifts among its customers no longer fund or forfeit anyone's node race |
| Demand spikes met by supply races | Overshoot was rational — miss the ramp and lose the node — so gluts were built into every boom | Supply capped by constraints capex cannot quickly clear: advanced packaging, HBM, power, land, permitting | CoWoS capacity roughly quadrupled in two years and still runs ~10% short; TSMC's CEO: packaging is "so tight that now it's limiting my customers' growth" |

A fifth mechanism is quieter and more durable. Most semiconductor product markets now hold a technology leader earning exceptional margins and a laggard earning healthy ones — AMD behind Nvidia in accelerators, Marvell behind Broadcom in switching. Customers deliberately preserve the laggard's volume to keep a second source, so it does not need to attack price to live; the leader prices below its ceiling to keep the laggard unthreatening and regulators uninterested. Neither side moves. The old configuration — interchangeable products, existential share incentives — made price war the rational default; the new one makes discipline the rational default. The payoff matrix flipped sign.

## Floors are rising where the cycle used to bottom

The dampening has printed.

Equipment ran the cleanest natural experiment. In 2008–09, wafer-fab equipment spending fell 31%, then a further 46%. In 2023, memory producers cut equipment purchases 39% — an old-cycle collapse in the most cyclical segment — and total WFE held between flat and a mid-teens decline depending on definition, because logic, foundry and geographic duplication absorbed the hole. A full memory bust occurred inside the equipment market; the equipment market shrugged.

Underneath tool sales sits the annuity. Lam's customer-support business printed its first $2 billion quarter — 36% of revenue, growing 24% a year at a record ~50% gross margin — on an installed base of ~90,000 chambers that consume spares and upgrades regardless of the order book. KLA has grown services for 52 consecutive quarters across 50,000+ tools with twenty-year lives and frames over three-quarters of revenue as recurring. ASML's installed-base management ran near 30% of last quarter's sales. This is an aerospace-aftermarket P&L grafted onto a toolmaker — sole-source consumption on a captive fleet — and it grows through downturns.

Memory's version is newer and weaker as evidence. HBM has gone from 8% of DRAM industry revenue in 2023 to ~41% forecast for 2026, sold forward under long-term agreements and qualified into specific accelerator platforms rather than swapped like a commodity. All three vendors passed HBM4 certification for Nvidia's Rubin generation in June; allocation is not symmetric — supply-chain estimates put SK hynix near 60–70%, Samsung a quarter to a third, Micron the remainder. Winner-take-most economics inside a contracted market is the structure of an equipment industry, not a commodity one. HBM also consumes roughly three times the wafer capacity per bit of conventional DRAM, so the fastest-growing product tightens the rest of the market as it grows — the opposite of every previous memory transition.

The limit: dampened, lengthened and decoupled is not eliminated. One form of cyclicality has been concentrated rather than removed. The old cycle spread demand across desynchronised end markets — PCs, phones, autos, industrial. The new structure funds the marginal wafer from a single line item: five hyperscalers' capital budgets, up from ~$410 billion in 2025 to $700–725 billion guided for 2026. The supply side has been domesticated. The demand side has been concentrated.

## What a visible trough changes

If the complex is becoming structurally less cyclical, the benchmark is the set of businesses that made that transition a generation ago: aerospace aftermarket and high-end automation — cyclical end markets, premium multiples, held through downturns.

| Company | Fwd P/E | EV/EBITDA | Consensus growth (3yr) | What earns the multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TransDigm | 28x | 20x | ~11% rev / ~12% EPS | ~90% proprietary, ~80% sole-source parts; aftermarket ≈ 55–60% of revenue, ~3/4 of EBITDA; $1 of OEM sales seeds ~$100 of lifetime aftermarket |
| Rolls-Royce | 38x | 22x | ~9% rev / ~20% EPS | Pay-per-flying-hour LTSAs; £6.0bn of 2025 flying-hour receipts on a captive engine fleet |
| HEICO | 44–55x | 38x | ~12% rev / ~17% EPS | FAA-certified sole-alternative parts; 19% organic aftermarket growth |
| Keyence | 34x | 26x | ~12% rev | 51% operating margin; fabless, direct-sales, value pricing |
| Fanuc | 31x | 21x | ~7% rev | Installed-base robotics; priced at the *start* of an orders recovery |
| Rockwell | 33x | 18–24x | ~6% rev / ~16% EPS | North American automation incumbency, software mix |
| ABB | 28x | 25x | ~11% rev / ~14% EPS | Electrification + automation portfolio |

The benchmark says three things. **Through-cycle visibility, not headline growth, earns the premium:** captive fleets, mandated maintenance and sole-source parts keep trough cash flow visible even when orders fall. **The benefit is a higher floor, not immunity from drawdowns:** TransDigm and HEICO still fell sharply when growth disappointed. **For semiconductors, 30–35 times slightly-above-trough earnings is the useful anchor:** it is below the benchmark's most aggressive 40–55 times observations and avoids applying a peak multiple to peak profits.

## The scorecard: three verdicts in one industry

Set the semiconductor complex against that benchmark.

| Segment | Names | Fwd P/E (Jul 2026) | Current growth | Historical norm | Verdict embedded in the price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equipment | Lam ~44x, KLA ~46x, Applied ~40x, ASML high-40s | 40–46x | ~10–25% revenue; SEMI forecasts records through 2027 | ~15–20x; industry 5-yr median ~25x | Cycle dead; full quality-cyclical treatment, at a premium to most of the benchmark set |
| Foundry / logic leaders | TSMC ~23x 2026E; Nvidia ~21x fwd; Broadcom ~mid-30s | ~20–35x | TSMC guiding >40% for 2026; Nvidia +65% FY26, +85% last quarter | TSMC ~22x trailing avg; Nvidia fwd ~32–40x over 2–5 yrs | Profits real, duration denied; growth priced to stop |
| Memory | Samsung 4.8x, SK hynix 5.3x, Micron ~6x fwd | 5–6x | Micron revenue roughly doubling in FY26; DRAM industry revenue +51% 2026E | Record lows — cheaper than at any prior peak | Old cycle returns with certainty; peak earnings erased |

The market extended credit in reverse order of evidence. Equipment has completed one stress test and prints its service annuity every quarter, so it is fully reclassified. Memory's contracted regime has not completed a cycle, so it is priced as if the hypothesis were already disproven. TSMC has the strongest single-company monopoly evidence and still receives roughly the index multiple. Fifty years of memory drawdowns set the prior; CXMT keeps it live.

## Where the classification remains mispriced

**Equipment: the operational thesis is strong; the reclassification has been paid for.** Service annuities and rising troughs support durable earnings, but 40–46 times already exceeds the 30–35 times framework. Returns now require earnings delivery; a less cyclical business can still be a poor stock from a full entry price.

**Foundry and logic leaders: the best risk-adjusted expression.** TSMC and Nvidia combine the strongest layer ownership with multiples that still deny duration. The conservative front-page math leaves 20–55% upside without assuming their current growth rates persist; the thesis only needs the next trough to remain high and compute intensity to beat the path in expectations.

**Memory: the largest convexity and the weakest evidence.** The three-to-four-times scenario requires the first cycle in listed memory history where contracted HBM, reverse scaling and triopoly discipline hold normalized earnings near half of peak. Eight prior cycles and CXMT argue the other side. Treat it as option-sized exposure until the trough is observed, not as a compounder on narrative alone.

The opportunity is therefore not "semiconductors re-rate" in the abstract. It is the narrower classification spread between already-priced equipment, under-duration foundry and logic monopolies, and memory's unproven trough.

## What would restore the old cycle

Every mechanism in the first table shares one load-bearing dependency: **economy-wide compute intensity must keep rising faster than the market expects**. Absolute compute demand can grow while semiconductor stocks fall if usage, monetization or capex grows more slowly than the path already embedded in estimates. The no-glut argument works because bottlenecked supply meets a queue of demand; if the queue shortens relative to expectations, the bottlenecks become inventory. [[Macro & Technology/Sustainability of AI Capex]] shows the gap: hyperscaler capex is approaching the operating cash flow of the companies funding it while end-market monetization still has to catch up.

Hyperscaler capex is growing 77% into 2026. Analysts count more than $800 billion of circular or interlocking financing across the AI complex; OpenAI carries ~$1.15 trillion of decade-long vendor commitments against a projected $14 billion loss this year; Nvidia has discussed guaranteeing the financing of a customer buying its own hardware. None of this proves a bust. It describes a demand base that could digest — and a digestion would test contracted volumes at their first renegotiation and triopoly discipline under falling prices for the first time, with CXMT's subsidised wafers arriving on a schedule set by policy, not returns.

The base rate: roughly eight DRAM upcycles since 1995, every one ending in a 40–60% equity drawdown. The most bullish bank on the street compares this boom to the 1990s supercycle — a comparison that cuts the other way, since that supercycle ended with DRAM prices down 90% and a generation of producers exiting. Morgan Stanley calls the July selloff a compelling entry point and forecasts memory contract prices peaking in the fourth quarter of 2026. The sell side is split against itself.

The reflexive risk runs through the multiples, not the businesses. Equipment revenue floors can hold as designed while a 44-times multiple meets a capex digestion — an old-cycle drawdown in the stock without an old-cycle drawdown in the company. July's tape — records sold at TSMC, Samsung and Micron — shows the marginal holder trades the old model first and checks the structure later.

## The tests that settle it

1. **The next memory correction is the experiment.** SK hynix and Micron holding margins far above prior troughs — troughs resembling old peaks — confirms the reclassification, and the multiple follows; new capacity returning them to old trough economics means the structural story was an unusually strong boom. A vendor holding above 10 times forward earnings *through* a price decline would be the market conceding in real time.
2. **The HBM share-versus-price diagnostic.** Two consecutive quarters of HBM prices flat or rising while one vendor loses five-plus points of share would prove qualification pricing power detached from volume — never observed in DRAM history. Its absence at the first HBM oversupply would argue HBM is commodity memory with extra steps.
3. **The equipment trough test.** A downturn where total WFE declines less than ~20% while service revenue grows through it confirms the floor at the revenue line; a trough below 2023's kills the thesis at 40-plus times. Watch the multiple separately — the thesis can pass operationally and still fail as an investment from this entry.
4. **Compute intensity versus the expected path.** Track accelerated compute consumed per unit of economic output, agent and inference usage inside paid workflows, fleet utilization, GPU rental rates versus depreciation, and hyperscaler capex versus operating cash flow. Absolute demand growth is insufficient; these measures must beat the path embedded in estimates. A miss falsifies every segment simultaneously — the complex traded one diversified cycle for one concentrated dependency.
5. **The fourth player.** CXMT past ~400–500,000 wafers a month, or DDR5 qualified at scale with Western OEMs, re-arms the mechanism that broke every previous memory oligopoly: a subsidised entrant that does not need returns.

## The cycle will be judged at its trough

The industry is less cyclical than the one that trained three generations of investors. Fewer producers than at any point since the 1970s. A third of memory revenue and a third of the equipment P&L under contract or annuity. Supply capped by constraints no competitor can outbuild on a cycle timescale. Technology races nationalised into a single foundry. Payoff matrices that reward discipline over aggression. **That should mean 30–35 times slightly-above-trough earnings and materially better visibility through the cycle.** It does not, by itself, make the stocks go up: from here, compute intensity must continue rising faster than the demand curve already in expectations.

This is the configuration Laniakea Partners looks for — not a hidden fact but a mispriced classification, held in plain sight because the correct answer requires disbelieving either a fifty-year base rate or a two-year-old structure. The next downturn is the referendum. Peaks prove nothing. What SK hynix's margins, Lam's service line and TSMC's utilization do at the trough will test the supply thesis; what economy-wide compute intensity does versus expectations will test the demand thesis. Both must hold. Equipment has already collected its verdict in advance; the remaining upside belongs to the segments still being asked for proof.
