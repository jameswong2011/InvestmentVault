# NBIS - Nebius Group

## Summary

Nebius is the same neocloud trade as CoreWeave with the risk vector inverted: CRWV is a credit bet on a fully-built base, NBIS is an execution bet on a base that barely exists yet. The market prices the two as correlated twins (NBIS at a ~116% valuation premium to CRWV), but the binary that decides each is orthogonal — CRWV's thesis turns on counterparty credit and DDTL re-rent economics; NBIS's turns on whether it can physically energize 800MW–1GW of data-center power by YE2026 off a ~170MW YE2025 base, a >4x build in twelve months, to convert a fully-contracted ~$50B Microsoft + Meta backlog into revenue. Demand is de-risked (capacity sold out, contracted through 2031); supply is not. The non-consensus case is that NBIS is the *higher-quality* expression of a sector the vault rates bearishly ([[Sectors/Neoclouds & GPU-as-a-Service]]: "neoclouds are levered NVIDIA derivatives, not independent businesses") — cleaner balance sheet ($9.3B cash, ~$7–8B of non-core stakes, customer-prepayment funding instead of GPU-collateralized debt), genuine full-stack software differentiation inherited from Yandex, and embedded venture optionality (ClickHouse ~$4.2B, Avride ~$2.2B) that can self-fund the buildout without the leverage that makes CRWV a credit-risk position. Conviction is medium, not high, because the stock is priced for a flawless 4x build (~110x trailing sales, trading past even bullish sell-side targets) and the 2026 capex–cash gap is large; medium, not low, because the funding structure and optionality genuinely de-risk it relative to CRWV. Status: draft (sized as a quality-and-optionality position with a hard execution catalyst path, distinct from CRWV's credit framing).

## Key Non-consensus Insights

**1. NBIS and CRWV are priced as twins but carry orthogonal risk — NBIS is an execution bet, CRWV is a credit bet. The decorrelation on the actual binary is the mispricing.** The market's entire differentiation between the two public pure-plays is the valuation spread (NBIS ~116% premium to CRWV) and a vague "cleaner balance sheet" narrative. The structural reality is sharper: CRWV's investment case is decided by counterparty credit (67% Microsoft) and second-cycle DDTL re-rent economics on 250K+ GPUs already deployed — a *demand and financing* question on built supply. NBIS's case is decided by whether ~170MW of active power at YE2025 becomes 800MW–1GW by YE2026 on a back-end-weighted (Q3-onward) schedule — a *supply construction* question on de-risked demand (sold-out, contracted). Two assets whose outcomes hinge on different, largely uncorrelated variables should not trade as a high-correlation pair. When the sector next dislocates, the question "is this a build problem or a credit problem?" separates them — and only one of them is exposed to each shock.

**2. The "cleaner balance sheet" edge is conditional, not structural — it survives only if NBIS closes a ~$11–16B 2026 funding gap without GPU-collateralized debt.** Consensus cites $9.3B cash and minimal traditional debt as NBIS's advantage over CRWV's 14x liabilities/equity. But 2026 capex is guided to $20–25B (up from $5B in 2025) against $9.3B cash — even crediting ~$7B of Microsoft upfront prepayment and the $4.3B convertibles already raised, NBIS must source another ~$11–16B this year. The balance-sheet-quality thesis is real *only if* that gap is closed via customer prepayments plus non-core stake monetization rather than the SOFR+450 GPU-collateralized debt that turned CRWV into a credit-risk position. This cuts both ways and that is precisely why it is non-consensus: bulls under-model the funding gap; bears under-model that NBIS holds prepayment and ~$17B of cash-plus-stake funding levers CRWV simply does not have. The thesis is a wager on *which* funding path NBIS takes, and management has so far chosen the non-dilutive-where-possible one (prepayments, converts, NVIDIA equity).

**3. The non-core stakes are a self-funding reserve the market discounts as a conglomerate "distraction."** ClickHouse (~25–28%, ~$4.2B implied at the $15B Jan-2026 Series D), Avride (~83%, ~$2.2B post the Uber-led round), Toloka and TripleTen together represent ~$7–8B of pre-AI-cloud balance-sheet value against a ~$58B market cap. Sell-side treats the portfolio as Yandex-legacy noise warranting a conglomerate discount. The non-consensus read inverts it: these are a liquidity reserve that can plug the capex gap (insight #2) without DDTL-style leverage. ClickHouse is the highest-quality piece — $250M ARR scaling to a guided $700–900M, with OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and Tesla as customers, IPO-track, and CEO Volozh explicitly flagging it as "a source of several billion dollars" in a liquidity event; Avride targets a $10–20B exit by early 2028. The $780.6M non-cash ClickHouse mark that produced Q1's headline $621M "net income" is dismissed by skeptics as low-quality earnings — but it is a real mark on a real, monetizable asset, and the option to convert it to cash is what structurally de-risks NBIS's dilution path. The market is pricing the stakes at a discount to a self-funding mechanism.

**4. Yandex provenance is the *source* of NBIS's full-stack differentiation, mispriced purely as a governance liability.** The market applies a discount for the Russian-asset-separation optics (completed 2024, but secondary perception persists). The vault's own sector view holds that "no operator has differentiated technology — all run NVIDIA reference architectures with comparable networking, cooling, and rack densities" ([[Sectors/Neoclouds & GPU-as-a-Service]] §Competitive dynamics). NBIS is the one credible exception, and the reason is the provenance: Volozh built one of the world's largest non-US AI/search infrastructure stacks, and that inheritance is why NBIS ships native managed services, MLOps, an inference platform (Nebius AI Studio) and agentic tooling (TractoAI) — capability CRWV had to *acquire* for $1.7B (Weights & Biases) and the miner-pivots lack entirely. The software-stack depth that is genuinely the sole differentiator in a commoditized hardware layer is discounted as a liability rather than priced as the moat-adjacent asset it is.

**5. Customer prepayment moves NBIS out of the sector's DDTL trap — the single best reason it deserves a different quality rating from CRWV inside the same bearish frame.** The vault's dominant neocloud bear angle is "the DDTL collateral mechanic is broken": GPU rental rates have fallen 50–70% from peaks while CRWV's debt covenants reference contracted or acquisition-cost rates, creating an amortization-cliff at second-cycle re-rent ([[Sectors/Neoclouds & GPU-as-a-Service]] §Investor heuristics; [[Theses/CRWV - CoreWeave]] insight #2). That critique is structurally CRWV-specific. NBIS funds buildout substantially with Microsoft's ~$7B upfront prepayment and the Meta deal's $12B dedicated tranche — customer cash that carries no DSCR covenant and cannot trigger a credit event. Same upstream NVIDIA dependence, same Microsoft/Meta counterparty concentration, same long-term in-housing risk — but a structurally superior funding source the market does not distinguish when it marks the two pure-plays to the same risk model. The DDTL bear case that anchors the vault's CRWV thesis largely does not transmit to NBIS, and that asymmetry is unpriced.

**6. The Rubin transition decouples reported earnings growth from incremental ROIC — the market reads NBIS's 45% EBITDA margin and >50%-QoQ ARR as a high-return compounder while the capital actually being deployed earns a near-hurdle return NVIDIA can compress further.** The generation now coming online (first Vera Rubin NVL72 rack validated in Finland, Jul 2026) has re-priced violently since underwriting: a VR NVL72 rack now runs **~$7.8–9.1M (Morgan Stanley / Foxconn est.), ~2× the Blackwell GB300 it replaces**, on a **~435% memory-price surge** (HBM4 + LPDDR5X ~$2–3.2M of the rack; GPUs ~$4M) — voiding the June "One Chart" premise that Rubin capex/watt was "barely up" ([[Research/2026-06-03 - AI Value Capture and GPU Rental Economics - deep-dive]]). At Foxconn's **~$47B all-in per 1GW** (≈ NBIS's whole YE2026 target), the tranche's return reduces to one variable: the **rate multiple Rubin contracts achieve over current Blackwell contracts (~$6/GPU-hr)**, against Rubin's ~3× performance. Corrected model (rack/shell depreciation split, take-or-pay hours): lifetime ROIC **~14% at 1.5× · ~22% at 2.0× · ~30% at 2.5× · ~39% at 3.0× (performance-parity)** — the 15% hurdle needs **~1.6×**, and the power-slot test makes deployment rational only above **~1.8×** (below that, sweating Blackwell beats installing Rubin). The rational pricing corridor (**~1.8–3.0×**) therefore clears the hurdle throughout; the bear case requires contracted pricing below the deployment-rational floor — a vintage risk (deals struck at pre-memory-surge rates), not an equilibrium one ([[Research/2026-08-05 - NBIS CRWV - Rubin Fleet Economics - Rate Sensitivity Model - deep-dive]]). Yet revenue and EBITDA keep hypergrowing (base ×4, ~3.5× FP8 FLOPS/GPU), and because **EBITDA excludes the depreciation that just doubled**, the metric the market anchors on (45% AI-cloud margin, $7–9B exit ARR) stays blind to the capital burn. Perceived ROIC (inferred from margin + growth) and real incremental ROIC ([[Mental Models/Generalist - Overview|G-7]]: return on the new "mines") diverge exactly where forward capital concentrates — and NVIDIA, the layer owner, harvests the Rubin value gap through its memory markup (SOCAMM ~60% GM) while NBIS the **layer-renter** carries the depreciation. *Held as hypothesis, not verdict:* the open variable is which multiple the contracted tranches embed — value-based pricing (sold-out capacity lets NBIS bank the performance uplift as $/GPU-hr), customer prepayment shrinking *net* invested capital (Microsoft's ~$7B + ~$4.8B deferred revenue), and useful-life extension ([[Research/2026-04-24 - Dylan Patel on AI Token Supply and Demand - video-transcript]]) all push realized returns toward the corridor's upper half; contracts struck at Blackwell-era rates before the memory surge would lock the sub-corridor outcome. The single falsifying datapoint to watch: NBIS's disclosed Rubin rental economics and whether the 45% AI-cloud margin holds or fades as Rubin scales through Q3–Q4 2026 (sharpens Outstanding Q#4; [[Research/2026-08-04 - NBIS vs CRWV - Competitive Comparison]] frames both peers as NVIDIA/grid layer-renters).

## Outstanding Questions

**1. Can NBIS physically energize 800MW–1GW by YE2026 off ~170MW at YE2025 — a >4x build, back-end-weighted to Q3?** This is the thesis's load-bearing variable. The entire revenue ramp ($3.0–3.4B FY26, $7–9B exit ARR) is a power-construction problem, not a demand problem (capacity is sold out). Grid interconnect queues run 24–48 months in tier-1 markets ([[Sectors/Data Center Power & Cooling]]), and the plan leans on a new 1.2GW Philadelphia campus, a second Pennsylvania gigawatt site, and Finland expansion. *Answered by*: Q2 2026 (Aug) and especially Q3 2026 (Nov) active-MW disclosure; any commissioning slip at Philadelphia/Finland is the cleanest early-warning signal. Track *active/firm/commissioned* MW and *delivered power cost per MW*, not announced or connected pipeline: the production-cost framework in [[Research/2026-08-05 - AI Data Center Power Markets and Electricity Pricing - deep-dive]] shows every added MW carries its own nodal cost (basis, marginal fuel, firmness), so a sold-out book cannot accelerate physical commissioning and each incremental MW's economics can leak to basis, curtailment, or emergency generation — the warning is falsified only if NBIS delivers the ramp without basis blowouts or materially higher capex per MW.

**2. How is the ~$11–16B 2026 funding gap actually closed, and at what dilution or leverage cost?** $20–25B capex against $9.3B cash and ~$7B Microsoft prepayment leaves a multi-billion shortfall. The entire balance-sheet-quality thesis depends on the answer. *Answered by*: terms of any 2026 equity/convertible raise; the first appearance of GPU-collateralized or asset-backed debt against the Meta contract — which would converge NBIS toward CRWV's credit profile and collapse insight #5.

**3. What is NBIS's true customer concentration and per-contract gross margin?** As a foreign filer NBIS does not publish CRWV-equivalent customer-level revenue concentration. Microsoft + Meta anchor ~$46B of the ~$50B backlog — concentration is high, merely spread across two hyperscalers instead of one. *Answered by*: any 20-F / shareholder-letter segment disclosure; whether the 45% AI-cloud margin is flattered by Microsoft prepayment economics versus the marginal contract rate.

**4. Is the 45% AI-cloud adj EBITDA margin durable or a small-base, sold-out-pricing artifact?** Margin jumped 24%→45% QoQ on a ~170MW base running sold-out at peak rental pricing. The source's most damaging data point is Oracle's GPU-rental business averaging ~16% gross margin over five quarters. *Answered by*: the margin print as the asset base scales 4x through 2026 — does 45% hold, or compress toward sector-normal as the mix dilutes and new capacity ramps below full utilization?

**5. Do the ClickHouse / Avride monetizations actually arrive, and before or after the capex gap bites?** The self-funding thesis (insight #3) requires liquidity events on a usable timeline. Avride's targeted exit is early 2028 — too late to fund 2026–2027 capex. ClickHouse is the only near-term lever. *Answered by*: a ClickHouse IPO filing or secondary sale; if the funding gap hits before any stake monetizes, NBIS dilutes or levers and the differentiation versus CRWV narrows.

**6. At ~$58B / ~110x trailing sales / ~116% premium to CRWV, what exactly is priced in — and what multiple survives a stumble?** The stock trades at or above the average sell-side target (~$220–230 vs ~$245–270 spot), i.e. it has outrun even its bulls. *Answered by*: the de-rate on any execution slip; whether the multiple holds if 2026 exit ARR lands at the $7B low end versus the $9B high end (a 22% ARR miss against a stock priced for the top of the range).

**7. How exposed is NBIS to the hyperscaler in-housing risk that caps the whole sector beyond 2027?** Microsoft (MAIA) and Meta (MTIA) are simultaneously NBIS's two anchor customers *and* the two most-incentivized builders of competing silicon. The Meta deal's $15B "flexible" tranche is option value, not a firm draw. *Answered by*: MAIA/MTIA scale disclosures; whether the flexible Meta tranche is drawn or quietly lapses as in-house ASICs ramp into 2028–2031.

## Business Model & Product Description

Nebius is a vertically-integrated AI cloud — it designs, builds, powers, and operates its own data centers and GPU clusters, then sells GPU-hours and managed AI services on multi-year contracts. The cleanest analogy is **"CoreWeave with a software layer it built rather than bought, a venture portfolio bolted on, and a European/US footprint instead of a US-centric one"** — or, framed by capital structure, *a neocloud that funds its buildout with customer prepayments and asset sales instead of GPU-collateralized debt.* It emerged in 2024–2025 as the carve-out of Yandex's non-Russian assets, re-listing on Nasdaq, which is why it carries both a full-stack software inheritance and a residual governance discount.

**Core: Nebius AI Cloud.** Bare-metal and managed NVIDIA GPU clusters (H100/H200/B200/B300, Vera Rubin deployment slated H2 2026) with InfiniBand/Spectrum-X fabric, direct-liquid cooling, and managed Slurm/Kubernetes — NCP Reference-Platform architecture, SemiAnalysis ClusterMAX Gold tier (one rung below CRWV's sole Platinum). Q1 2026 AI revenue $390M (98% of group), +841% YoY. The contract shape is multi-year dedicated capacity: Microsoft (~$17.4–19.4B, ~$7B prepaid) and Meta ($27B = $12B dedicated + $15B flexible, deployments from early 2027) anchor a ~$50B backlog.

**Software layer (the differentiator).** Nebius AI Studio (model inference-as-a-service), TractoAI, and native MLOps/managed services — built in-house from the Yandex stack, not acquired. This is the one credible exception to the sector's "no operator has differentiated technology" rule.

**Non-core portfolio (the optionality).** A venture book that doubles as a self-funding reserve:

| Asset | Stake | Implied value | Status / monetization path |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ClickHouse** | ~25–28% | ~$4.2B | Real-time analytics DB; $250M ARR → guided $700–900M; customers incl. OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Tesla; $15B Series D Jan 2026; IPO-track; $780.6M Q1 non-cash gain |
| **Avride** | ~83% | ~$2.2–2.3B | Autonomous vehicles + delivery robots; Uber-led round ~$2.7B post; robot deliveries +178% YoY, >600K cumulative; targeted exit early 2028 at $10–20B |
| **Toloka** | majority | smaller | RLHF/data labeling; Bezos Expeditions round May 2025 |

**Revenue segmentation (heuristic — group reporting is dominated by AI cloud).**

| Segment | ~% of group | Economics |
|---|---|---|
| AI cloud (GPU compute + managed) | ~98% (Q1 2026) | 45% AI-cloud adj EBITDA margin; sold-out; Microsoft/Meta anchored |

| Non-core (Avride/Toloka/TripleTen consolidated revenue) | ~2% and shrinking as % | Cash-consumptive today; value is in the equity stakes, not the P&L |

The economic point: the *income statement* is ~98% a GPU-rental business that looks exactly like CRWV; the *balance sheet* is where NBIS diverges — ~$9.3B cash + ~$7–8B of stakes = ~$17B of pre-AI-cloud value supporting the ~$58B cap, which is the structural reason to hold NBIS over CRWV inside an identical demand thesis.

## Industry Context

NBIS sits in the **non-hyperscaler GPU infrastructure layer** — between NVIDIA (chip supply) and a customer cluster of hyperscalers buying overflow + frontier labs buying training + enterprises buying inference. The full competitive landscape, pricing-power trajectory, and miner-pivot bifurcation live in [[Sectors/Neoclouds & GPU-as-a-Service]]; the thesis-relevant deltas for NBIS specifically:

| Layer | Player | NBIS relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Upstream chip supply | NVIDIA | Sole supplier; ~$2B equity investor (2026); "fleet management / inference / AI-factory design" partner; Reference-Platform NCP |
| Pure-play peer | **CoreWeave (CRWV)** | Direct comparator; NBIS = cleaner balance sheet + slower-built base, CRWV = levered + fully-built; ~116% premium gap is the market's only differentiation |
| Pure-play peers (private) | Lambda, Crusoe, Nscale | Compete for hyperscaler overflow + sovereign mandates; pricing-floor pressure from sub-$2/hr aggregators (RunPod, Vast.ai) |
| Miner-pivot landlords | APLD, CIFR | Structurally more durable (sell power/space, not GPUs) but not NBIS competitors |
| Downstream customers / future competitors | **Microsoft (MAIA), Meta (MTIA)** | Anchor customers today; building competing silicon — the 2027+ in-housing risk applies to NBIS's two largest counterparties |
| Component beneficiaries | [[Theses/VRT - Vertiv Holdings]], [[Theses/6981 - Murata Manufacturing]] | NBIS rack growth = demand for power/cooling (VRT) and MLCC content (Murata) |

**Pricing power is upstream-pinned at NVIDIA** — NBIS does not set GPU rental rates independently; its take-rate is bounded by NVIDIA chip cost on the supply side and Microsoft/Meta willingness-to-pay on the demand side. The 45% AI-cloud adj EBITDA margin is a *derived* margin and shares the same long-term compression vectors as CRWV (NVIDIA gross-margin trend, custom-silicon competition per [[Theses/AVGO - Broadcom]], hyperscaler in-housing per [[Sectors/Custom Silicon & Networking Semiconductors]]).

**Where NBIS's value-chain position diverges from CRWV:** (a) *financing* — customer prepayment + asset monetization instead of DDTL debt, which removes the covenant/re-rent failure mode that dominates the sector bear case; (b) *footprint* — Europe-centric (Finland flagship, UK, Israel, France, Spain) expanding into the US (Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Kansas City), positioning it for EU/sovereign-AI data-residency mandates that route demand away from US hyperscalers; (c) *software* — a built (not bought) full stack. None of these change the upstream NVIDIA dependence; all of them change the *quality* of the same beta.

## Bull Case

NBIS is the highest-quality balance-sheet way to own the one part of the AI-capex cycle that is supply-constrained through 2027, and it owns optionality CRWV cannot replicate. The setup that has to play out:

1. **The power build lands on schedule.** 800MW–1GW active by YE2026 converts the sold-out, fully-contracted backlog into the guided $7–9B exit ARR. Demand is not the question — execution is — and management has hit the cadence so far (3.5GW contracted, capex raised to fund it).
2. **Customer prepayment + stake monetization fund the buildout without DDTL leverage.** Microsoft's ~$7B prepayment, the $12B Meta dedicated tranche, $4.3B converts, $2B NVIDIA equity, and a ClickHouse liquidity event keep NBIS off the GPU-collateralized-debt treadmill that makes CRWV a credit risk. NBIS scales to hyperscaler size while preserving net-cash optionality.
3. **The 45% AI-cloud margin holds as the base scales.** Sold-out pricing + owned (>75%) capacity + the software layer (AI Studio, managed services) defend unit economics better than a pure bare-metal reseller, keeping NBIS structurally above the Oracle ~16% GPU-rental-margin floor.
4. **Optionality crystallizes.** ClickHouse ($250M → $700–900M ARR, OpenAI/Anthropic/Tesla customers) IPOs or sells at a higher mark; Avride exits 2028 at $10–20B. Either event is a multi-billion-dollar, non-dilutive funding source that also re-rates the stub.
5. **Sovereign/EU mandates and the European footprint open a demand lane CRWV can't serve** as EU AI Act/GDPR data-residency routes >$10B/yr toward non-US operators.

**Valuation framework.** Strip ~$17B of cash + stakes from the ~$58B cap → a ~$41B "AI cloud stub." Against guided $9B exit ARR that is ~4.5x ARR for a business growing AI-cloud ARR >50% QoQ at 45% margins — cheap *if* the build executes. Upside is "execute the 4x build, hold the margin, monetize one stake" → a re-rate toward 6–8x exit ARR on the stub plus stake crystallization = a path to $80–100B+ over 18–24 months. The bull case is "cleanest-funded neocloud compounding into a sold-out backlog with free venture options attached."

## Bear Case

NBIS is a ~110x-trailing-sales stock priced for a flawless 4x power build it has not yet executed, funded by a balance sheet that looks pristine today and could be levered or heavily diluted by this time next year. The drivers that break it:

1. **The power build slips.** A >4x energization in twelve months, back-end-weighted to Q3, against 24–48-month interconnect queues, with the largest sites (Philadelphia 1.2GW, second PA gigawatt campus) early in construction. A two-quarter slip means the contracted backlog converts slower than the stock's ~$9B-ARR-priced expectation, and a stock priced for the top of the range de-rates hard on the first miss.
2. **The funding gap forces the very leverage the thesis says NBIS avoids.** $20–25B capex vs $9.3B cash. If ClickHouse monetization is delayed and customer prepayments don't stretch, NBIS funds the gap with GPU-collateralized/asset-backed debt — at which point it *is* CRWV (insight #5 collapses) — or with dilutive equity into a richly-valued stock. The macro-capex frame ([[Macro & Technology/Sustainability of AI Capex]]) tightens this: its leverage ledger counts customer prepayments *among* the debt-like claims that put the neocloud tranche at ~90-100% of drawable leverage capacity — so NBIS's funding edge is covenant- and trigger-remoteness (prepayments carry no DSCR), not exemption from the tranche's aggregate fragility, nor from the 2028-29 capex digestion (or the deferred, sharper air-pocket if the complex races its financing ceiling first) that concentrates in exactly this lab-serving/merchant slice.
3. **The 45% margin compresses as the base scales.** New capacity ramps below full utilization; the sold-out, peak-pricing margin normalizes toward the Oracle ~16% precedent as the mix dilutes. Margin compression cascades just as capex peaks.
4. **Hyperscaler in-housing caps the back half of the backlog.** Microsoft (MAIA) and Meta (MTIA) are the two anchor customers *and* the two builders of competing silicon. The $15B Meta "flexible" tranche is optional; if MTIA ramps, it lapses, and ~$15B of headline backlog never converts.
5. **The optionality is marked, not realized.** The $780.6M ClickHouse gain is non-cash; absent an actual liquidity event the stakes fund nothing, and the Q1 "$621M net income" reverses to an adjusted ~$100M loss. The conglomerate discount the market applies may be correct if the stakes never monetize on a useful timeline.

**Downside scenario.** Exit-2026 ARR lands at $6–7B (build slips, low end), the margin prints low-30s%, NBIS raises $10B+ in dilutive converts/equity, ClickHouse monetization slips past 2027, and the multiple compresses from ~16x to ~6x FY-forward revenue. Outcome: a 50–70% drawdown from ~$58B toward $20–30B — the same Nortel-style late-cycle infrastructure de-rate the vault flags for the sector, with NBIS's premium-to-CRWV erased first.
