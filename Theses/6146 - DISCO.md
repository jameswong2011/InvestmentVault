# 6146 — DISCO

## Summary

DISCO is the closest thing semiconductors has to a true toll-booth monopoly that the market still prices as a cyclical: ~70–80% global share of wafer dicing, grinding and polishing, the mandatory back-end steps every die on Earth must pass through to be thinned and singulated, with a razor-and-blade consumables annuity (~30–35% of revenue) layered on an installed base no competitor can dislodge. The non-consensus tension is not whether DISCO is a good business (it is) but what you are actually buying: not the AI capex level (priced), but content-per-wafer compounding faster than the multiple de-rates. Every packaging architecture transition the vault already tracks independently (HBM stack height 12→16→24-Hi (each adds grind/dice passes), hybrid bonding (needs CMP-grade grinding), CoWoS→CoPoS panels (panel dicing), SiC/GaN via the Kabra laser) raises DISCO's dollar-content per wafer without it having to win a standard war or fend off a scaled rival (Accretech is ~¼ its size and a generation behind). The bear is the entry rather than the franchise. At ~48x trailing / ~34x forward earnings, on a stock whose 52-week range is ¥37,260–¥91,680, a ~60% peak-to-trough swing inside twelve months, the market has already demonstrated it treats DISCO as a high-beta AI-cycle instrument rather than a serene compounder, and there is no margin of safety at ~¥65,000. Medium conviction: a genuinely rare whole-industry monopoly with a consumables moat, diversified customers, ~70% gross margin and net cash, but bought here it is quality at a cyclical-premium price where the swing variable is hyperscaler-capex sentiment, not DISCO execution.

## Business Model & Product Description

DISCO Corporation (founded 1937 as a grinding-wheel maker; Tokyo Stock Exchange: 6146) is the world's dominant supplier of the three mandatory back-end wafer-processing steps captured in its corporate motto **KIRU•KEZURU•MIGAKU** ("cut, grind, polish"). Every semiconductor die manufactured must be (1) thinned (grinding/polishing the wafer backside) and (2) singulated (dicing the wafer into individual dies) before packaging. DISCO holds ~70–80% global share of the equipment and the consumables for both. The analogy: DISCO is the **"Gillette of the back end"**: it sells the razor (dicers/grinders/polishers) and owns the blade (precision consumables), and the AI packaging boom is buying more razors while running the installed base harder (more blades).

**Revenue structure (three streams):**

| Stream | What | ~% of revenue | Economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equipment** | Dicing saws (DAD/DFD), grinders (DGP/DFG), polishers, stealth-dicing laser saws (DFL), Kabra laser ingot slicing | ~55–60% | The cyclical swing line; ASPs rise with precision (HBM ultra-thin, hybrid-bonding prep) |
| **Precision tools (consumables)** | Dicing blades, grinding wheels: every DISCO machine consumes DISCO blades | ~30–35% | Razor-and-blade annuity; grows with installed base; resilient (beat while equipment missed in Q1) |
| **Other (service / apps)** | Field service, DISCO Application Labs (DAS) processing/rental | remainder | High-margin; the tacit-know-how layer |

**Key products and why they matter:**
- **Grinders (DGP8761, DAG810):** thin wafers to ~30µm for HBM 16-Hi stacking (SK Hynix HBM4); the thinner the target, the more grind passes and the tighter the tolerance. Sole-source at the leading edge.
- **Dicers (blade + laser):** singulate wafers. DBG (Dicing Before Grinding) and SDBG (Stealth Dicing Before Grinding) handle ultra-thin memory dies where blade dicing alone would crack the wafer.
- **Stealth Dicing (DFL7362, Φ300mm):** laser-based, kerf-free dicing for thin Si and low-k films, complementary to blades, adopted for advanced memory.
- **Kabra:** laser ingot slicing for SiC (roughly halves kerf loss vs diamond-wire), expanding to GaN and diamond, a structural share-gain vector as power-device wafers move to 200mm SiC.

**The content step-function is the model.** A standard logic wafer needs ~one grind + one dice. An HBM 16-Hi stack needs multiple thin-and-dice cycles; hybrid bonding adds CMP-grade grinding; CoPoS adds panel dicing. So DISCO's $/wafer compounds on complexity even if wafer-start units are flat, the Jevons / architecture-transition leverage that makes it a rare cyclical whose content secularly rises.

**Financial profile:** fabless-adjacent precision manufacturer with an unusually high consumables mix. FY ended March 2026 revenue ¥436.9B (+11% YoY), net income ¥135.5B (~31% net margin); TTM revenue ¥461B (+15.2%). Gross margin ~65–70% (among the highest in semicap; the consumables mix lifts the blend), operating margin ~38–40%. Net-cash balance sheet (large cash + securities, negligible debt). Sixth consecutive year of record shipments on generative-AI demand.

## Industry Context

**Value-chain position.** DISCO sits at the back end (post-fab), the thin/dice/polish layer between wafer fabrication and packaging/test. Upstream inputs (abrasives, laser modules) are commoditized; downstream, its customers are the entire chip industry (foundries, OSATs, IDMs, memory, power). DISCO's leverage is high because it is sole/dominant-source at the leading edge (ultra-thin HBM grinding, hybrid-bonding prep), switching means re-qualifying yield-critical recipes, and the consumables lock in the installed base. Q1 management framing: demand "exceeds or is close to exceeding supply capacity … a favorable environment to maintain pricing power."

**Competitive dynamics.**

| Player | Position vs DISCO |
|---|---|
| **Tokyo Seimitsu / Accretech (7729.T)** | The only scaled competitor: ~18–22% of dicing (older data as low as ~8–9%), a generation behind on precision, stronger in dicing than grinding, ~¼ DISCO's size. The credible-but-contained #2. |
| **ASMPT (0522.HK)** | Some back-end/dicing adjacency but primarily a bonding house, more complement than direct dice/grind threat. |
| **Chinese entrants (JSG et al.)** | Gaining in low-end/trailing dicing via price + local-procurement policy; no leading-edge capability yet (sector note: China has zero hybrid-bonding capability and a wide back-end precision gap). The double edge: China OSAT is a large current buyer (35% of Q1 mix) and the long-run competitive threat as self-sufficiency climbs. |
| **DISCO (6146)** | ~70–80% dice/grind/polish; leading-edge sole-source; consumables annuity; Kabra laser optionality in SiC/GaN/diamond. |

**Structural forces (each raises DISCO content).** (i) HBM stack-height escalation (12→16→24-Hi) adds grind/dice passes; (ii) hybrid bonding requires CMP-grade grinding: [[Theses/BESI - BE Semiconductor Industries]]/[[Theses/AMAT - Applied Materials]] Kinex is complementary, since bonding needs DISCO-thinned, flat wafers; (iii) CoWoS→CoPoS panel transition (2028–29, NVIDIA Rubin anchor) adds panel dicing/grinding, and DISCO is flagged a beneficiary in [[Macro & Technology/CoWoS-to-CoPoS Panel-Level Packaging Transition]]; (iv) power-device SiC/GaN 200mm transition (Kabra); (v) Jevons ([G-14]): cheaper/denser packaging → more chips → more dice/grind.

## Bull Case

- **The monopoly is real and widening at the leading edge.** ~70–80% dice/grind/polish; sole-source ultra-thin HBM grinding; Accretech ~¼ the size and behind. As precision requirements rise (30µm and below, hybrid-bonding flatness), the gap widens rather than narrows, and the qualification-gate flavor of the moat strengthens with each node.
- **Content-per-wafer compounds on every AI packaging transition.** HBM 16→24-Hi, hybrid bonding, CoPoS panels, SiC/GaN Kabra: each independently raises $/wafer. DISCO grows on complexity even at flat units. Sixth straight record-shipment year is the aggregate proof.
- **The consumables annuity de-cyclicalizes the P&L.** ~30–35% recurring, growing with the installed base, and it beat while equipment missed in Q1, the semi-cyclical-compounder reclassification (Industry-Semis #14) that would durably re-rate the multiple.
- **Diversified customers remove the binary risk that caps peers.** No single-anchor dependence; sells to the entire industry, structurally safer than ALAB, Hanmi, or any single-fab-levered back-end name.
- **Structural semicap re-rating.** Per the sector's de-cyclicalization thesis (Insight #7 / L2), back-end monopolies with annuities (DISCO, Towa, Advantest) are early in a cyclical→structural multiple transition; consensus Buy, avg PT ¥84,630 (~29% upside).
- **Valuation framework:** ~34x forward on a monopoly compounding content at ~15%+ with ~70% GM and net cash is not demanding if the annuity proves the cycle is dampened. The bull needs the de-cyclicalization to show through the next downturn.

## Bear Case

The world where this loses money is paying a compounder multiple for a business the tape still trades as a high-beta cyclical, not the franchise breaking.

- **The multiple has no margin of safety, and the drawdown is proven.** ~48x trailing, and the 52-week range ¥37,260→¥91,680 means DISCO already fell ~60% inside a year on AI-sentiment swings. A re-rate to even 25–30x (still a premium to its own history) is ~40–50% downside with zero thesis damage required. The ¥37,260 low is the market's own downside case (~43% below ¥65,470).
- **Back-end equipment is second-derivative cyclical.** Equipment (~55–60% of revenue) is the swing line and missed in Q1; it amplifies any AI-capex digestion. The consumables annuity dampens but has not been tested through a genuine downturn since the AI ramp; it may track utilization more than bulls assume.
- **China is a double-edged 35%.** China OSAT drove Q1 (35% of mix), a large, potentially pull-forward-inflated bucket that also funds the exact competitors (JSG et al.) China's self-sufficiency drive is nurturing to displace DISCO in low-end dicing, then climb.
- **The moat is know-how + consumables, not a standard.** DISCO owns no protocol/standard (Value-Layer-Monopoly WEAK fit on interface control); at trailing nodes dice/grind is contestable, and a "good-enough" Chinese or Accretech tool at 60–70% of the price can take low-end share and pressure blended margin.
- **Hybrid-bonding two-sidedness.** If W2W (wafer-to-wafer) bonding scales for premium HBM/logic, some singulation steps compress, a partial offset to the grinding tailwind at the very high end.
- **FX and access.** Yen depreciation erodes USD returns; the TSE listing adds a Japan-discount / liquidity overlay and access friction (OTC ADR / foreign brokerage) for USD investors.
- **Margin grind.** ~70% GM is a high bar to defend against China low-end mix and price competition; any compression pressures the earnings the multiple assumes.

## Risks

**Thesis risks (the investment case is wrong):**
1. **De-cyclicalization fails.** The consumables annuity proves utilization-linked, not installed-base annuity; DISCO trades as a pure back-end cyclical through the next downturn.
2. **China self-sufficiency reaches leading-edge dice/grind.** JSG/domestic tools climb from low-end, compressing share and margin (the sector's "good enough" commoditization vector).
3. **Content-per-wafer growth stalls.** HBM stack-height escalation slows, or W2W hybrid bonding reduces singulation steps, capping the complexity tailwind.
4. **Accretech takes disclosed leading-edge share.** The one scaled competitor closes the precision gap on HBM ultra-thin grinding or hybrid-bonding prep.

**Position risks (thesis right, stock still falls):**
5. **Valuation de-rate on AI-capex digestion.** At ~48x trailing the multiple compresses on any hyperscaler-capex deceleration headline regardless of DISCO prints; the ~60% 52-week drawdown is the template.
6. **Yen / FX.** USD returns eroded by yen depreciation independent of the operating result.
7. **Taiwan / concentration tail.** The whole leading-edge complex shares the TSMC/Taiwan kinetic-escalation impairment risk; DISCO's demand is downstream of the same nodes.
8. **Liquidity / access.** TSE listing, Japan-discount, and ADR/foreign-brokerage friction for USD investors.
