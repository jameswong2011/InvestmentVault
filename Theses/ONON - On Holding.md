# ONON - On Holding

## Summary

On has been re-priced from a hyper-growth compounder (~$54, ~30x forward earnings) to a decelerating premium sneaker brand (~$37, ~21-25x, near its 52-week low of $31.41) — yet the business that produced the de-rating grew Q1 2026 net sales +26.4% constant-currency, expanded gross margin 430bps to 64.2%, and took share while every large peer stalled. The sell-off was an expectations reset, not a demand event: FY2026 guidance of "≥23% cc" implied CHF 3.44B against CHF 3.67B consensus, and a CHF 173M non-cash FX loss cut reported FY2025 net income 15.9% even as operating profitability hit records. The investable question is narrow and unresolved — whether a founder-controlled, debt-free brand compounding 23%+ at a 64% gross margin off a ~CHF 3B base is a justified outlier to the brutal footwear base rate, or the first step of a taste-cycle fade now dressed up as "de-risked." At ~3x EV/revenue and ~16x EBITDA the price no longer demands the bull case, which is what makes the name interesting after a 32% drawdown; the founders re-took the CEO seats and bought stock at $36.64 into that drawdown. Medium conviction: the operating momentum, margin, balance sheet, and owner alignment are real, but the durability data that would earn "high" — cohort retention, LTV/CAC — is not disclosed, and low switching costs plus intensifying competition leave the base-rate bear fully alive.

## Key Non-consensus Insights

- **The de-rating priced a fade the numbers have not delivered — consensus repriced a beat-and-raise compounder as a fade candidate on optics (guide-vs-estimate + FX), not on a demand miss.**
  - **Consensus:** On's explosive growth is structurally cooling toward the footwear base rate; a 23% guide from a brand that grew 30%+ is the first visible step down, so ~21-25x forward is still not cheap enough for the deceleration risk.
  - **Variant:** 23% is On's characteristic sandbag, not a ceiling — it beat and *raised* margin guidance in Q1 (26.4% cc, gross margin +430bps), hit its 2023 Investor Day 18% EBITDA target a full year early, and the stock fell on a consensus that had over-extrapolated to 30%+, not on any softening of underlying sell-through. The de-rate to ~3x EV/revenue / ~16x EBITDA created the entry into a business still compounding mid-20s% with rising margins.
  - **First confirming observable [2026-08]:** Q2 2026 print delivers >23% cc growth with gross margin held ≥64% and no inventory build → the "cooling" narrative is optics, not fundamentals.
  - **Falsifier:** two consecutive quarters of sub-20% cc growth absent a macro shock (ties to → LOW trigger) — the fade is real and consensus was early, not wrong.

- **LightSpray is valued as a $310 marketing halo; it is an early-stage robotic, localizable manufacturing platform that directly hedges the tariff and FX drag now compressing the stock.**
  - **Consensus:** spray-on uppers are a PR stunt on a sub-1% halo racing shoe; On is structurally a Vietnam-sourced importer absorbing a 20% tariff ($70M in Q1 alone) with no manufacturing edge.
  - **Variant:** On just scaled LightSpray capacity 30-fold (Busan +32 robots vs. 4 in Zurich a year ago), and a near-zero-labor, near-zero-waste, one-piece automated process is the one credible path to *nearshoring production toward demand* — cutting labor, shipping, and tariff exposure while deepening the "Swiss engineering" brand story. This is the only place On owns a genuine process-technology lever rather than pure brand rent (held as a hypothesis to test, not a verdict — consumer/physical automation is the hardest, most bounded case per the Automation lens sector overlay).
  - **First confirming observable [2026-2027]:** a *mass-market* (not racing) model shipping on LightSpray at volume, with management disclosing a unit-cost or tariff/logistics benefit.
  - **Falsifier:** LightSpray stays confined to sub-1% halo volumes through 2027 with no mass rollout and no disclosed cost benefit → it is marketing, and the tariff/FX drag is unmitigated (ties to → LOW trigger on margin).

- **The market read the double CEO exit as instability; the founders re-took operational control and bought stock at the lows — an alignment upgrade, not a governance risk.**
  - **Consensus:** losing CEO Hoffmann (also CFO, 13 years) within a year of co-CEO Maurer's departure, plus a new CFO, signals strategic drift and shakes confidence in the next growth phase.
  - **Variant:** Allemann and Coppetti — who built On's premium-discipline culture and control >50% of votes on ~26% economic ownership — chose to step *back into* operating roles at the moment execution matters most, and each put ~$2.2M of personal capital in at $36.64 alongside co-founder Bernhard. Founder-led, founder-owned, founder-buying is the governance profile that beats the base rate; the market is discounting it as churn.
  - **First confirming observable [2026-11 / 2027-03]:** two clean quarters under the co-CEOs with no inventory glut, no step-up in discounting, and guidance met or raised.
  - **Falsifier:** founders prove operationally rusty after years as Chairmen — an inventory build, a discounting pivot, a missed guide, or a further abrupt executive exit (ties to → CLOSE trigger on governance breakdown).

- **Reported earnings understate the economics twice over — intangible investment is expensed and an FX loss masks record operating profit — so P/E screens misread the asset being compounded.**
  - **Consensus:** ~30x P/E on *falling* net income (−15.9% in FY2025) is an expensive, decelerating name.
  - **Variant:** the earnings decline was a CHF 173M non-cash FX loss; operating profitability set records (62.8% gross margin, 18.8% EBITDA margin). On top of that, the brand, the OAC athlete stable, Federer/Zendaya/LOEWE equity, the DTC build-out, and the LightSpray robot fleet all run through the P&L as expense — so reported invested capital and earnings understate the compounding intangible base ([G-11]). The screen that flags On as "expensive on falling earnings" is measuring FX and expensed growth investment, not economics.
  - **First confirming observable [ongoing]:** constant-currency operating metrics (cc revenue, gross margin, EBITDA margin) keep expanding while reported CHF net income stays FX-noisy — the divergence is the tell.
  - **Falsifier:** strip FX and *operating* margins also stall → the optics argument collapses and the deceleration is economic, not translational.

## Outstanding Questions

- **Where is the cohort data?** The entire durability case rests on repeat-purchase behavior in sticky franchises plus apparel lifting basket size — yet On discloses no cohort retention curves, LTV, CAC, or payback. Aggregate 26% growth blends new-customer acquisition (rising athlete/collaboration/retail spend) with existing-customer retention, and the two have opposite value implications ([G-12]). Answered only by On disclosing DTC cohort economics or by a third-party panel (e.g., card-spend) showing repeat rates — until then durability is an assumption, not a finding.
- **Is 23% a floor or a ceiling?** Management frames the guide as conservative and has beaten it; bears read it as the leading edge of a fade. Resolved by the trajectory of *constant-currency* growth across the next 3-4 quarters and by whether the US (Americas is 59% of revenue and the maturest market) holds double-digit cc growth or rolls to high-single-digits.
- **Can premium discipline survive a genuinely promotional market?** On has never faced a full demand air-pocket with Nike, Adidas, and Hoka all fighting for the same runner. Jefferies' bear framing — "premium positioning alone may not be enough to sustain price-led growth without risking demand and/or higher promotional activity" — is the crux. Answered by gross-margin trajectory and any observable rise in markdowns or non-compliant discounting.
- **Does LightSpray become a cost/tariff lever or stay a halo?** The 30x capacity build is a large bet; the payoff is a mass model at a disclosed unit-cost or nearshoring benefit. Answered by which SKUs move to LightSpray and whether management quantifies the P&L impact.
- **How exposed is the "status symbol" positioning to a taste-cycle roll?** On is now described as the running world's status symbol / office-to-track aesthetic. That is precisely the fashion-adjacent positioning that cooled Under Armour, Skechers, and Nike in prior cycles. Answered only in hindsight by whether franchise reorders and new-model sell-through hold as the brand saturates.
- **What is normalized earnings power ex-FX?** With a weak USD suppressing CHF-reported results and tariffs a live drag, the market lacks a clean read on through-cycle EPS. Answered by a stabilized FX environment or by On providing clearer cc-EPS bridges.

## Business Model & Product Description

On is a Swiss premium athletic brand that outsources manufacturing and centralizes engineering, design, and brand in Zurich — closer to a design-and-IP house (think a hardware-light Apple of running) than a vertically integrated manufacturer. The economic engine is full-price discipline: minimal discounting since inception, curated wholesale doors (it closes doors that dilute presentation or pricing), and a DTC shift that together produce a ~60-64% gross margin versus Nike's 43-46% and Adidas's 48-52% — a 15-20pt gross-margin premium that is the quantitative fingerprint of the model.

**Product technology.** The core IP is CloudTec — circular rubber "cloud" pods that compress on landing then lock into a firm platform for toe-off — extended by the Speedboard plate, the Helion / Helion HF supercritical superfoam, the new CloudTec Sphere geometry (Cloudboom Strike 2, launching 2026-07-30), and LightSpray, a robot-sprayed one-piece upper. LightSpray is the strategic wildcard: capacity scaled 30-fold in 2026 (Busan factory, +32 robots), positioned as automated, low-waste, and localizable manufacturing.

**Revenue architecture.** Product is organized as durable "franchises" (nine footwear franchises each >5% of revenue) updated iteratively rather than replaced, stretching product life beyond the industry's seasonal churn and compounding brand equity per model (Cloudsurfer, Cloudmonster, Cloudrunner).

| Segmentation axis | Mix (latest) | Trajectory / economics |
|---|---|---|
| Footwear vs. apparel+accessories | ~93% / ~7% | Apparel +57.5% YoY (Q1 2026); target >10%; skews DTC (~20% of DTC vs ~7% total) — a margin + acquisition lever |
| Channel: DTC vs. wholesale | 42% / 58% (from 25% / 75% in 2019) | DTC carries full assortment, higher margin; wholesale in only ~40% of Foot Locker/JD/DICK'S doors — "multiyear" white space |
| Region: Americas / EMEA / APAC | 59% / 25% / ~16-20% | APAC +61% cc (Q1 2026), China triple-digit; Americas is the maturity watch-item |
| Verticals | Running (core, fastest) + outdoor, tennis, training, all-day | Each extended into apparel; tennis originated by Federer, amplified by Shelton/Swiatek/Fonseca |

**Control structure.** IPO'd September 2021 (~$7B); founders hold ~26% economic and >50% voting via 10-vote Class B shares, insulating long-run brand-building from quarterly optics. Debt-free with >CHF 1.0B cash at year-end 2025.

## Industry Context

On sits in the design/brand layer of a ~$19-20B global running-shoe market growing ~4.8% CAGR, with performance running +8.9% YoY while lifestyle footwear declines −0.9% — a mix shift toward exactly On's technical positioning. It occupies the premium end alongside Hoka; the two are the only brands that held share through the 2022-24 industry bullwhip. **The reason matters, and the source contradicts the flattering read**: On avoided the glut because Vietnam/China lockdowns through H1 2022 mechanically capped its own over-ordering — the supply constraint, not the discipline, is why it had no excess inventory to clear ([[Research/2026-07-14 - ONON - On Holding Business Breakdown - deep-dive]]). "Refused to discount" and "never had to discount" are indistinguishable on the tape and worth multiples apart. Hoka's parallel resilience is the same setup (premium, lightly discounted, entering the period with momentum), so it corroborates the pattern without isolating the cause. On's one apparent stress test was a supply-constrained non-test — which is why §Outstanding Questions still records premium discipline as unproven in a genuinely promotional market.

**Competitive dynamics are intensifying precisely as On scales:**

| Competitor | 2026 posture | Read for On |
|---|---|---|
| Nike | Global sports-footwear share 22.9% (−3pts in 2025, 3rd straight decline), but running business +$1B over 5 quarters and +5 share points in running (W. Europe + N. America) in FY2026; turnaround pushed to 2027 | The most important threat — Nike is recovering *specifically in running*, On's core, even as it loses overall |
| Hoka (Deckers) | Decelerating to low-teens FY2026 (from 24%); Deckers stock −20% | Closest structural comp; its stall is both a warning (premium running is cyclical) and a share opportunity |
| Adidas | Record Q3 2025 (terrace/Samba-Gazelle trend) | Momentum in lifestyle; less direct in performance running |
| New Balance | Resurgent "quiet luxury" / Gen-Z; premium craftsmanship | Competes for the same premium, aesthetic-led consumer On targets |
| Asics | Performance-running strength | Direct performance competitor |

**Value chain and leverage.** On owns brand, design, and IP; it rents manufacturing (primarily Vietnam) and distributes through DTC + curated wholesale. Its leverage over wholesale partners is real (it can close doors), and its pricing power shows up as realized gross margin. But the layer it occupies is contestable: switching costs for a runner are near-zero, CloudTec/LightSpray are design-and-marketing differentiation rather than a controlled standard competitors must license, and the premium is brand rent, not a structural toll. The structural forces reshaping the industry — a shift to performance/technical product, DTC premiumization, and tariff-driven reshoring pressure — cut in On's favor on the first two and are the exact problem LightSpray is meant to answer on the third.

## Bull Case

On sustains 23-28% constant-currency growth through 2028 on four independent runways — APAC/China (triple-digit, "very much at the beginning"), apparel (+50%+, toward >10% of revenue), US white space (in only ~40% of key wholesale doors; ~75% of its own markets still don't know the brand), and DTC premiumization — while holding a 64%+ gross margin and pushing EBITDA margin above 20%. LightSpray scales from halo to mass models, localizing production and converting the tariff drag into a cost advantage. The "fade" fear proves wrong, and the market re-rates the multiple back toward its own consensus. Framework: if FY2027 revenue reaches ~CHF 4.2-4.5B (~$5.3-5.6B) at a 20%+ EBITDA margin (~$1.1B EBITDA) and durability re-rates the stock to 20-22x EBITDA, EV moves to ~$22-24B — roughly +90% from ~$11.3B EV, consistent with the $57-58 consensus target and the $70-85 bull-case targets (BTIG $70). The de-rating means the bull case now requires only that On keep doing what it is already doing, not a re-acceleration.

## Bear Case

23% is the first visible step of a fade, and the footwear base rate reasserts. Growth decelerates to mid-teens (2026) → low-teens (2027) → high-single-digits (2028) as the ~CHF 3B base scales and the "status symbol" taste cycle rolls over — the exact path that cooled Under Armour, Skechers, and Nike's own cycles. Competitive intensity does the rest: Nike's running recovery (+5 share points already), Adidas's momentum, New Balance's premium pull, and a defending Hoka force On off full-price discipline; markdowns rise, and the 64% gross margin unwinds toward the high-50s. FX (weak USD) keeps suppressing reported CHF results and sentiment; the double management change plus a new CFO produces an execution stumble under founders who have been Chairmen, not operators, for years; the Vietnam tariff stays a live earnings drag and LightSpray stays a sub-1% halo. In that world, the multiple compresses further — a decelerating brand loses its growth multiple. If cc growth falls to low-teens and the stock re-rates to ~15x P/E on ~$1.30 EPS, the shares are ~$20 (roughly −45%), and a broken growth narrative can take it below the $31.41 low well before that. The absence of any disclosed cohort/LTV data means neither On nor the investor can prove the retention that would refute this — the burden of proof sits with the bull.

## Catalysts

- **Q2 2026 earnings (~mid-Aug 2026)** — the key near-term test: Americas/US cc growth trajectory, whether ≥23% is beaten/raised again, inventory and discounting discipline under the new co-CEOs. Both directions.
- **LightSpray Cloudboom Strike 2 launch (2026-07-30)** and subsequent LightSpray mass-model signals — read on whether the platform moves beyond halo.
- **Q3 2026 earnings (~Nov 2026)** — holiday sell-through + China trajectory.
- **FY2026 results + FY2027 guide (~March 2027)** — first full year under founder co-CEOs; tests the post-2026 "20-25% growth / 20%+ EBITDA margin" ambition.
- **Insider activity** — further founder buying (bullish confirmation) or any selling (bearish).
- **Negative/macro:** USD/CHF moves, tariff escalation or refund resolution, competitor product cycles (Nike running push, Adidas), any further abrupt executive exits.

## Risks

- **Thesis risk — taste-cycle peak (central).** On is a contestable consumer brand with near-zero switching costs and no layer moat; if the brand cools, growth and margin fade to the footwear base rate. This is the single risk that breaks the compounder thesis.
- **Thesis risk — premium discipline breaks.** If intensifying competition forces On to discount to hold volume, the 64% gross margin (the load-bearing datapoint of the whole model) compresses and the premium-brand economics unwind.
- **Thesis risk — founder-operator execution failure.** A double leadership change plus a new CFO raises the odds of strategic drift, an inventory misstep, or a discounting pivot in the next 2-4 quarters.
- **Position risk — FX.** On reports in CHF; a weak USD suppresses reported results and sentiment regardless of underlying demand, and can drive the stock independent of the thesis.
- **Position risk — tariffs.** Vietnam sourcing concentration makes the 20% US tariff a live, ongoing earnings drag; escalation would hurt before LightSpray nearshoring can offset it.
- **Position risk — multiple compression.** Even if growth holds, a growth-brand multiple can de-rate further in a risk-off rotation.

## Conviction Triggers

- **→ HIGH if**: two consecutive quarters of >25% cc growth with gross margin sustained ≥64% AND disclosed evidence of repeat-purchase / DTC cohort-retention strength (or a credible third-party panel proxy) confirming durability beats the base rate; OR a LightSpray mass-market model ships at volume with a disclosed unit-cost / tariff benefit.
- **→ LOW if**: two consecutive quarters of sub-20% cc growth absent a macro shock, OR gross margin falls below ~60% on rising promotional activity (premium discipline breaking).
- **→ CLOSE if**: cc growth falls to high-single-digits AND the company begins systematic discounting (taste-cycle-peak thesis-breaker confirmed), OR a founder-operator governance breakdown (further abrupt executive exits / a strategy reversal).
