# VSH - Vishay Intertechnology

## Summary
Vishay has re-rated roughly 124% off its cyclical low as the market extrapolates a genuine components upturn (Q2 2026 book-to-bill 1.32, backlog 6.1 months, industrial revenue +30% YoY) and the 'Vishay 3.0' margin-recovery story into a structural re-rating. The variant read is that this is a low-moat, broad-line commodity components maker pouring $2.6B of capex into the top of a components upcycle, running negative free cash flow, carrying a 33-40% effective tax rate that skims a third off any margin gain, and underwriting a 30%+ gross-margin target that must beat the base rate that broad-line passive and discrete margins mean-revert. Vishay owns no decisive layer of any stack; it is the diversified generalist the Value Layer Monopoly lens flags as an anti-signal, so its pricing power is rented from the cycle rather than owned. The stock is optically cheap at roughly 1.3-1.6x EV/revenue precisely because the market has correctly priced these names as cyclicals for decades. The bet reduces to one question: does Joel Smejkal's self-help permanently reset the margin structure, or does the capex land as stranded fixed cost when the cycle rolls?

## Key Non-consensus Insights
- **The margin recovery is being priced as structural when the base rate says it is cyclical**
  - **Consensus:** Vishay 3.0 permanently resets gross margin from the ~19% trough to 30%+ by 2028; the 124% run prices a structural re-rate, and management pulling 24% GM forward a quarter validates it.
  - **Variant:** broad-line passives and discretes carry no qualification-gate moat (Industry-Semis #2), so peak margins mean-revert (#6). The 24%→30% path is utilization-led: the German fab lowers unit cost only while volumes hold, and if the cycle rolls, fixed-cost absorption reverses and gross margin falls back toward the low-20s. The 2028 target is a peak-cycle number dressed as a structural one.
  - **First confirming observable [2026-2027]:** gross margin holds ≥28% through a quarter with book-to-bill at or below 1.1, evidencing structural rather than utilization-driven margin.
  - **Falsifier:** GM stalls in the low-24s or declines as book-to-bill drops below 1.0 (ties to → LOW; mean-reversion confirmed).
- **VSH is deploying $2.6B into the top of a components upcycle, a textbook capital-cycle risk the AI narrative masks**
  - **Consensus:** the capacity build (German 12-inch fab, Newport ex-Nexperia, $2.6B through 2028) is a growth investment that captures AI-power, auto and defense content and structurally lowers cost.
  - **Variant:** every component maker is adding capacity at once (Murata +15% Fukui, Yageo +8B MLCC/month Malaysia, Infineon/onsemi/STM SiC). Industry-Semis #3/#17: the supply response is calibrated to inflated upcycle prices, then demand separates from inventory build and a pocket opens. Vishay is spending its largest-ever capex into peak book-to-bill, funded partly by an $830M equity raise and a now-in-the-money $750M convert (both dilutive) while free cash flow is negative.
  - **First confirming observable [2H 2026-2027]:** industry lead times shortening and distributor re-stocking completing.
  - **Falsifier:** demand outruns the industry-wide capacity adds into 2028 with lead times still extending (capital cycle is early, not late).
- **Customers ordering beyond 52 weeks is a double-ordering signal, not clean demand**
  - **Consensus:** a 6.1-month backlog and customers forecasting 6+ months out prove durable, visible demand.
  - **Variant:** in a supply-constrained upcycle, customers over-order to secure allocation (Industry-Semis #9). The same >52-week orders that read as backlog strength are the cancellation risk when supply normalises; the 2017-2019 auto-MLCC shortage and the 2022-2023 glut are the template, and they rhyme.
  - **First confirming observable:** point-of-sale sell-through tracked against backlog, with cancellation/reschedule rates staying low into 2027.
  - **Falsifier:** order cancellations or backlog erosion begin before revenue converts (ties to → CLOSE-adjacent).
- **Sell-side is bearish while momentum is bullish; the real edge is on durability, not direction**
  - **Consensus:** two camps, both loud. Wall Street rates VSH Hold / Moderate-Sell with targets ($18-28) mostly below the ~$36 price; the stock has ignored them.
  - **Variant:** both camps argue the wrong axis. The bulls (momentum) price a structural re-rate; the bears (targets) price the old cyclical. The mispriced variable is neither the cycle nor the recovery, both real, but whether the incremental $2.6B capex earns a return above cost of capital ([G-7]/[G-8]) on a commodity product set with a 33-40% tax rate skimming a third of any gain.
  - **First confirming observable [2027]:** incremental ROIC on the German fab / Newport, disclosed or inferable, running at least mid-teens as they ramp.
  - **Falsifier:** 2027-2028 ROIC stays mid-single-digits despite the revenue and margin ramp (capital destroyed; ties to → LOW).

## Outstanding Questions
- Is the 30% gross-margin target structural or utilization-dependent? What gross margin does Vishay hold at ~80% utilization versus the current tight-supply peak? This is the single most thesis-determinative unknown.
- What is the incremental ROIC on the $2.6B Vishay 3.0 capex, and does it clear cost of capital given a low-moat product set? The whole case is a capital-allocation bet ([G-7]).
- Why does VSH carry a 33-40% effective tax rate (US taxation of foreign earnings, repatriation), and is there any path to normalise it? It caps EPS leverage even if operating margin hits target.
- How much of the 1.32 book-to-bill is genuine end-demand versus double-ordering to secure allocation? What are current cancellation and reschedule rates?
- Post-2027, when capex normalises, what is through-cycle free cash flow, and can VSH resume its policy of returning ≥70% of FCF to shareholders, or is the balance sheet (convert dilution + raise) permanently more encumbered?
- In which lines does VSH have genuine pricing power (Bulk Metal foil resistors, mil/aero-qualified parts, auto-qualified diodes) versus pure commodity, and can it durably mix-shift toward the former?
- Does the AI-power / 800V content tailwind actually accrue to VSH (a Si-MOSFET, diode, film-cap generalist) or to the SiC/GaN and small-case-MLCC leaders (Infineon, STM, Murata) that the vault's own sector note favours?
- What breaks if industrial, its largest segment and the one up 30% YoY, rolls over? How concentrated is the recovery in one end-market?

## Business Model & Product Description
Vishay, founded 1962 by Felix Zandman (inventor of the Bulk Metal foil resistor), is one of the world's largest broad-line manufacturers of discrete semiconductors and passive components. If Murata is the specialist surgeon defending one operating theatre with proprietary ceramic chemistry, Vishay is the general hospital: broad, essential, present on virtually every board, but competing on breadth, supply assurance and service rather than a single defended layer. CEO Joel Smejkal (2023-) runs the 'Vishay 3.0' turnaround.

Revenue splits into two halves across roughly six reported segments:

| Half | Product families | ~Share | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Semiconductors** | MOSFETs, diodes/rectifiers, optoelectronics | ~45% | Si-generalist; competes with Infineon, onsemi, STM, Toshiba, Nexperia; commodity with pockets of auto/mil qualification |
| **Passives** | Resistors (incl. Bulk Metal foil), capacitors (tantalum, MLCC, film, power), inductors/magnetics | ~55% | #1 or top-3 in resistors and several capacitor niches; competes with Yageo/KEMET, TDK, Murata, Walsin |

End markets: industrial (largest), automotive, aerospace/defence, communications/data-center (AI power), medical, consumer. The economics are the tell: gross margin ~19% at trough, 23.3% in Q2 2026, targeted 31-33% by 2028; capital intensity ~10-11% of sales (high for a components maker), peaking H1 2026; a structural 33-40% effective tax rate. Net-cash balance sheet after an $830M equity raise, offset by a $750M 2.25% convertible (conversion price ~$30.16, now in-the-money and dilutive).

**Vishay 3.0** is the whole thesis in one initiative: $2.6B of capex through 2028 to (i) in-source wafer supply (the new 12-inch fab in Itzehoe, Germany; Newport 200mm fab acquired from Nexperia for ~$177M), (ii) win back high-margin design-in business Vishay previously under-served by outsourcing wafers, and (iii) capture AI-power, auto-electrification and defence content. Visible milestones: Newport reached gross-profit neutrality in Q1 2026; the German fab targets engineering wafers late 2026 and non-automotive production mid-2027; 24% gross margin was pulled forward one quarter to Q3 2026.

## Industry Context
Vishay sits in the component layer beneath the module makers (Vicor, MPS) and the systems vendors (Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider); its volume scales with board count and, increasingly, AI rack count. It is a price-taker on commodity SKUs and a price-setter only in a few qualified niches (foil resistors, mil/aero parts). Competitors on the passive side are Murata, TDK, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Yageo/KEMET, Taiyo Yuden, Walsin, and the Chinese cohort (Sunlord, Fenghua); on the discrete side, Infineon, onsemi, STMicroelectronics, Toshiba, Nexperia and ROHM. Vishay leads none of the highest-margin frontier segments: it has no 008004 MLCC and no leading SiC/GaN position, the two places the vault's sector note locates durable pricing power.

The 2026 setup is a genuine industry-wide upcycle: pricing is firming across the board (Murata +15-35%, Yageo +50% list, tantalum +15-30%, MOSFET allocation programmes at Vishay/Infineon/onsemi/STM), and Vishay is positioning its capacity build as a 'supply-assurance' share-gain lever. Structural demand forces are real: AI-power and 800V/HVDC architectures, auto electrification, defence and aerospace rearmament, factory automation and smart grid, and US-China supply-chain bifurcation that rewards Western-supply positioning. The open question is who captures the value. The vault's own [[Sectors/MLCC & Power Semiconductors]] assessment ranks broad-line passives/discretes mid-tier and explicitly does not recommend VSH (alongside onsemi, STM, TDK, Taiyo Yuden, Yageo), favouring Murata, Aixtron, Infineon, speculative Wolfspeed and POWI. That is a prior worth engaging: the content tailwind is real, but it accrues fastest to the moat-owning frontier, not the generalist.

## Management and culture
Hypothesis: Inert on [[Lens - Management and Culture]]. Gate 1 fails: broad-line passives/discretes is a mature, capacity-cyclical, low-differentiation industry, not the high-frequency-optionality environment (SpaceX, Stripe, Cloudflare) the lens calibrates against, and Vishay 3.0 is a capacity/margin reset within the existing business, not a new-optionality pipeline; grade on other models, chiefly [G-7]/[G-8] (ROIIC on the $2.6B capex, already this thesis's central question). [MC-2] 13 May 2025 Form 4s: CEO Smejkal and newly-named COO-Semiconductors Roy Shoshani each bought 10,000 shares on the open market at $14.70-14.95, dated evidence predating the ~124% run to ~$36 and clean of the halo guard. Founder-family control is durable and dated: Class B carries 10 votes/share (~45% of voting power), held via a Zandman family trust (Marc Zandman, Ruta Zandman, Ziv Shoshani co-trustees) since founder Felix Zandman's death, Marc Zandman Executive Chairman since 2011. Smejkal is a 33-year internal veteran (joined 1990; CEO since Jan 2023), so this is a textbook internal-promotion turnaround, not a prodigal-founder-return; two 2024-2025 C-suite hires (O'Sullivan, Shoshani) accompany the plan, but three years in is early to score against the calibration test (blocking stakeholders removed, high performers promoted, processes re-examined). Swing: whether German-fab and Newport ROIIC (2027-2028) shows capital allocated by a management team that has actually changed decision quality, not just balance-sheet capacity.

## Bull Case
The upturn is genuine and broad-based: Q2 2026 record adjusted revenue $919M (+20.5% YoY), book-to-bill 1.32, backlog $1.9B (6.1 months), industrial +30% and automotive +10% YoY, with pricing firming across the whole industry. Vishay 3.0 self-help is credible and running ahead of plan: 24% gross margin pulled forward a quarter, Newport turned gross-profit neutral, the German fab on schedule; the capacity build converts to lower unit cost and supply-assurance share gains from 2027. Structural content tailwinds (AI-power/800V, auto electrification, defence/aero, factory automation) give multi-year demand for Vishay's MOSFETs, diodes, film capacitors and resistors. The stock is optically cheap at ~1.3-1.6x EV/revenue on a net-cash balance sheet; if the 2028 targets land (~$4B revenue, 31-33% gross margin, 19-21% operating margin), normalised earnings imply roughly 10-12x 2028 EPS, a cyclical-inflection double if the margin reset proves durable. Sell-side sits below the price, so a sustained margin reset forces upgrades.

## Bear Case
Vishay has no moat: broad-line commodity passives and discretes with pricing power rented from the cycle rather than owned (Value Layer Monopoly diversification anti-signal; Industry-Semis #2 no qualification gate), so margins mean-revert (#6). It is spending $2.6B of capex, peaking in H1 2026, into an upcycle where every peer is adding capacity simultaneously; supply catches up, a demand pocket opens, gross margin falls back toward the low-20s and the new German fab becomes stranded fixed cost. The build is funded by an $830M equity raise and a now-dilutive $750M convert while free cash flow is negative. The 6.1-month backlog and >52-week orders are allocation-securing behaviour that cancels when supply normalises. A structural 33-40% tax rate skims a third off any operating-margin gain. And the stock already ran ~124%, so the cyclical recovery is largely priced; the mispriced-if-wrong variable is the 30% gross-margin target and the ROIIC on the capex. The losing world: industrial rolls over in 2027, book-to-bill breaks below 1.0, gross margin stalls at ~24%, capex stays cash-negative, and the stock de-rates toward the high-teens/low-20s where sell-side targets sit.

- **Industry pricing actions (Murata/Yageo hikes) sustaining or reversing into 2027:** the pricing-discipline read-through for Vishay's margin path.

## Risks
- **Thesis risk, margin is utilization-driven:** the 30% target reverts toward the low-20s when the cycle rolls because there is no moat to hold price.
- **Thesis risk, capital destruction:** the $2.6B capex earns sub-cost-of-capital returns on a commodity product set, leaving stranded capacity.
- **Thesis risk, wrong beneficiary:** AI-power/800V content accrues to SiC/GaN and small-case-MLCC leaders (Infineon, STM, Murata), not to Vishay's Si-generalist portfolio.
- **Position risk, cycle:** the components upcycle rolls over on a double-ordering unwind regardless of Vishay's execution.
- **Position risk, dilution:** the convert conversion + equity raise permanently expand the share count, so per-share economics lag revenue.
- **Position risk, tax:** the 33-40% effective rate caps EPS leverage even if operating margin hits target.

## Conviction Triggers
- **→ HIGH if**: gross margin holds ≥28% through a quarter with book-to-bill at or below 1.1 (structural, not utilization-driven), AND the German fab ramps to a disclosed or inferable incremental ROIC of at least mid-teens, AND free cash flow turns sustainably positive after the 2027 capex peak.
- **→ LOW if** (confirms the current rating): book-to-bill falls below 1.0, OR gross margin stalls in the low-24s or declines as volumes soften, OR 2026 free-cash-flow burn exceeds guidance and requires incremental debt.
- **→ CLOSE if**: order cancellations or backlog erosion begin before revenue converts, OR industrial revenue (the largest segment) contracts YoY, OR the 2028 30%+ gross-margin target is formally cut.
