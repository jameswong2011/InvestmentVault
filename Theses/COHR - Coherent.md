# COHR — Coherent Corp

## Summary

Coherent is the vertically integrated #2 of the AI-optics supercycle, the only company that owns every layer from 6-inch indium phosphide substrate through EML/CW/VCSEL lasers, silicon-photonic PICs, pluggable transceivers, optical circuit switches, and coherent transport, and the market is mispricing that breadth as conglomerate drag rather than as the widest single-vendor capture of the copper-to-optics conversion. FY26 revenue reached $7.12B (+22.5%), Datacenter & Communications hit 79% of the mix growing 58.6% YoY, FY27 is "basically completely booked out" with customers already placing POs into calendar 2028, and NVIDIA's $2B common-equity investment (March 2026) locks multi-year capacity and access. Yet the stock trades ~24% below its June ATH at ~44x forward, a discount to pure-play Lumentum's ~50x, because the core bull, a world-first 6-inch InP platform at 4× device density and ~60% lower cost per laser, has not yet reached gross margin: COHR printed 40.2% non-GAAP GM against Lumentum's 50.4%, and a credible engineering bear reads that ~10-point gap as evidence the 6-inch advantage is real on wafer cost but not on the parametric spec (RIN, PCE, wavelength grid) the highest-margin CPO lasers require. The investable question is not whether AI optics grows (it does) but whether COHR's 6-inch ramp closes the margin gap to Lumentum through FY27, converting a booked order book and a −$1B free-cash-flow capex bet into the >42% gross margin and >$3B/quarter the company guides to. Medium conviction: a real, deleveraged (0.7×), NVIDIA-backed multi-layer platform at a reasonable multiple, held back from high by an unresolved yield-quality question, deeply negative near-term FCF, and a structurally second-place position on the one layer (InP lasers) that actually prices the cycle.

Answered by COHR publishing those plots at 50°C, OR a hyperscaler qualifying COHR CPO lasers / 200G EML at volume.

- **What is the true ROIIC of the $1.1B capex, and does the 18-month payback survive ASP compression?** A forward CFO estimate is not a realized return. Answered by the FQ1'27 OCF inflection and by tracking realized payback as 2027 capacity fills; a slip past 18 months converts the negative FCF from investment into destruction.

- **How much of FY27's "completely booked out" order book carries take-or-pay teeth versus cancellable POs, and does the NVIDIA agreement include those minimums?** COHR cites pricing commitments and minimum-demand/take-or-pay LTAs to end of decade but discloses neither minimum-purchase levels nor cancellation terms, and not whether NVIDIA's deal carries them. - **If NVIDIA is the swing buyer across a LITE+COHR dual-source, what share of COHR datacenter revenue is NVIDIA-attributed, and what happens when Lumentum's Greensboro 6-inch lands mid-2028?** Customer concentration is undisclosed. - **Does VCSEL-based CPO/NPO (PhotonLink) ship at a workable datarate, or is management chasing 200G-PAM4-on-VCSEL that fails GR-468 reliability?** The optimal VCSEL CPO/NPO rate is 32G/64G NRZ; 200G PAM4 on a VCSEL is an engineering dead-end. - **Is COHR's yield ceiling upstream and outside its control: merchant/Sumitomo 6-inch InP substrate with poor uniformity, given AXT 6-inch is still R&D?** If the binding constraint is wafer uniformity rather than COHR's process, the margin gap does not close until AXT ships uniform 6-inch. - **When AI-optics growth normalizes from ~40–60% to 15–20%, what multiple does a vertically integrated #2 hold, and does the SiC/industrial legacy re-emerge as a drag or get fully divested?** Answered by the pace of remaining divestitures and the first deceleration print, the point at which the 44x forward multiple is tested against 20–30x semi-growth comps.

## Business Model & Product Description

If Lumentum is the "arms dealer" selling the single best laser die to everyone, Coherent is the **vertically integrated defense contractor**: it grows its own indium phosphide, forges its own lasers, assembles its own guided systems (transceivers, CPO engines), and now sells the whole battlefield network (OCS + coherent transport). The closer semiconductor analogy is a peak-era IDM: Coherent owns substrate-to-system where Lumentum runs a fabless-like focus on the highest-value die. Vertical integration is the entire strategic bet: it is COHR's structural edge over pure module makers (which get disintermediated as value migrates layers) and simultaneously the source of its margin gap (it carries lower-margin assembly, isolators, and legacy alongside the crown-jewel laser).

Coherent moved to a **two-segment** reporting structure in FY26:

| Segment | ~% of Q4 FY26 | Growth | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Datacenter & Communications** | ~79% ($1.62B) | +58.6% YoY | 800G/1.6T transceivers (EML + SiPh); InP EML/CW lasers (internal + merchant); CPO/NPO (PhotonLink: ELS + high-power CW + SiPh PIC); ZR/ZR+ coherent pluggables; pump lasers; high-end optical subsystems; Multi-Rail scale-across transport; OCS (>$4B opportunity) |
| **Industrial** | ~21% ($430.5M) | −15.8% YoY | SiC substrates (power/EV); industrial lasers; VCSELs for consumer 3D sensing (Apple Face ID multi-year agreement) |

The prior three-segment view (Networking ~62% / Lasers ~23% / Materials ~15%) frames the mix shift: Anderson is collapsing the company onto AI optics and pruning the rest.

**Key products and specifications:**

- **6-inch InP platform (the supply-side mechanism):** world-first 6-inch InP wafer fabs in Sherman, TX and Järfälla, Sweden. A 6-inch wafer yields ~4× the laser chips of a 3-inch at ~60% lower cost per laser. Qualifying 200G EML, 200G DFB-MZ, 100G EML, high-speed photodetectors, and high-power CW lasers on 6-inch; capacity doubling in CY26 and again after, nearly all incremental on 6-inch.
- **EML lasers (100G/200G):** distributed-feedback + electro-absorption modulator on InP via butt-joint regrowth. Internal for COHR modules plus external sales, but COHR still buys 200G EMLs from Lumentum at the parametric tails (the qualification-gate tell).
- **High-power CW / ELS:** 400 mW high-power CW laser for CPO and SiPh pluggables; COHR's own External Laser Source module powers its 6.4T socketed CPO, a rare vertically integrated CPO stack (own SiPh PIC + own ELS + own high-power source).
- **CPO / PhotonLink:** 6.4T (32×200G) socketed CPO on silicon photonics; a multimode socketed CPO built on COHR's high-speed VCSELs; an InP-modulator-on-silicon at 400G. PhotonLink bundles the full optical content across attach points.
- **VCSELs:** die quality ranked #2 to Broadcom; Apple Face ID franchise; PhotonLink multimode CPO/NPO path. Live debate on whether COHR targets the workable 32/64G NRZ rate or an off-spec 200G PAM4.
- **OCS:** liquid-crystal switch approach; >$4B stated opportunity widening from scale-out toward DCI. Trails Lumentum's MEMS on switching speed (seconds vs milliseconds), a disadvantage for bursty AI training traffic.
- **Coherent transport (scale-across):** ZR/ZR+ pluggables, Multi-Rail (sampled, revenue ramp 1H CY27), pump lasers, amplifiers: the transport counterpart to CPO/NPO as clusters spread across buildings and campuses.

**Revenue architecture: a conversion-capture heuristic** (more useful than reported segments):
1. **Bottleneck-owned (InP substrate + EML/CW lasers):** where the InP bind lives; COHR is vertically integrated into it but sits #2 on parametric yield.
2. **Integrated-optics content (PhotonLink CPO/NPO, transceivers):** content-parity capture across form factors; the contested-margin layer.
3. **Scale-across / systems (OCS, Multi-Rail, coherent transport, pump lasers):** the protocol-agnostic optionality leg, early and high-optionality.
4. **Legacy / consumer (SiC, industrial, Apple VCSEL):** shrinking, cash-generative, being pruned.

## Industry Context

Coherent spans **five of the six optical strata**: III-V epitaxy/substrate, EML/DFB/CW laser chips, transceiver modules, CPO integration, and OCS, a breadth no other listed name matches. What it does not own is the DSP/switch-ASIC layer (Marvell 80%+ 800G DSP; Broadcom Tomahawk), which is where the pluggable value that CPO removes actually sits.

**The EML oligopoly.** Five players control high-speed EML: Lumentum (50–60%, sole 200G volume), Coherent (#2, qualifying 200G on 6-inch), Broadcom (captive Breinigsville fab), Mitsubishi Electric, Sumitomo; top-five ≈ 76% of EML revenue. New entry is effectively impossible inside a decade (butt-joint regrowth, hyperscaler qualification). COHR is the strongest challenger to Lumentum by assets, but the challenger position is materially weaker than the monopoly it chases: COHR still buys the very component it competes on.

**The bifurcated pricing regime.** Chinese merchants dominate modules (Innolight #1 at ~23% share, seven of top ten) yet have ~0% InP laser localization at 200G, a Western component chokepoint. Module ASPs compress ~30–40% per generation; component/laser pricing rises on the InP bind. COHR's blended 40.2% margin reflects it straddling both sides: it sells the compressing module and the appreciating laser, where Lumentum concentrates on the appreciating die.

**The 6-inch race and where leverage sits.** COHR is 2+ years ahead on wafer diameter (Lumentum's Greensboro targets mid-2028) but behind on parametric yield and margin. Whoever converts diameter into parametric-spec yield first sets 200G+ pricing. Upstream, the InP substrate layer (AXT, 6-inch still R&D; Sumitomo; [[Theses/IQE - IQE]] epi) holds latent leverage: if 6-inch wafer uniformity is the true bind, the substrate suppliers, not COHR, gate the margin. NVIDIA holds architecture-setter and dual-source-buyer leverage, having put $2B into each of COHR and LITE.

**Value migration under CPO/NPO/OCS.** The architecture transition remaps value twice: from module to InP wafer, and from the EML monopoly to the more contestable CW/ELS layer. Vertical integration is COHR's hedge: it captures value wherever the migration lands (module, ELS, SiPh PIC, OCS, transport), whereas single-layer module makers ([[Theses/AAOI - Applied Optoelectronics]]) face disintermediation. The risk inside the hedge is that the CW/ELS layer COHR leans into is exactly the "relatively standardized/commoditized" layer flagged in the Lumentum bear case, content capture without pricing power.

## Bull Case

- **Widest copper→optics capture:** owns InP → lasers → SiPh → module → OCS → transport. As scale-out gives way to scale-up and scale-across, content per link rises across form factors and COHR captures the stack rather than a slice. PhotonLink content-parity means it wins whether CPO or NPO standardizes.
- **6-inch cost lead converts to margin:** 4× devices, ~60% lower cost/laser, 2+ years ahead of Lumentum's Greensboro. If parametric yield converges, GM climbs toward the 42%+ target and COHR out-earns on volume, which is the entire re-rate mechanism.
- **Booked, de-risked ramp:** FY27 "completely booked out," calendar-2028 POs, take-or-pay LTAs to end of decade; >$3B/quarter target by end-FY27 (~$12B+ annualized) with NVIDIA's $2B common stake + purchase commitment behind it.
- **Cleaner balance sheet + capital allocation:** deleveraged to 0.7× (from 2.0×); Anderson's portfolio surgery (exit non-#1/#2, divest A&D and materials tools) concentrates the company on its best market and improves the capital-allocation profile.
- **Cheaper than the #1 for a broader platform:** ~44x forward vs LITE's ~50x, with more scale-across optionality (OCS $4B, Multi-Rail, coherent DCI) not in the module-only comps.
- **Valuation framework:** if COHR reaches >$3B/quarter (~$12B+ annual) at 42%+ GM by end-FY27 and the market pays even ~35x normalized EPS power (~$12–14), the stock is meaningfully above spot; the trigger is GM convergence, not revenue.

## Bear Case

## Risks

**Thesis risks (the investment case is wrong):**
1. **Yield-convergence failure:** 6-inch cost never reaches parametric spec; the GM gap to Lumentum persists; COHR is a structural low-margin #2, the thesis-breaker.
2. **CPO-laser quality gate:** COHR's high-power DWDM CPO laser fails RIN/λ-grid/TEC spec at volume; it captures the 30–40% isolator/FAU content, never the 80%-GM CPO laser.
