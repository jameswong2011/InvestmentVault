# CRDO - Credo Technology

## Summary
Credo created the active electrical cable (AEC) category and owns 73–88% of it, and the market has responded by pricing the stock as a durable connectivity monopoly: ~$43B market cap, ~29x trailing EV/revenue, ~93x earnings, with sell-side declaring 'the bear case is dead' at a $283 average target. The two structural bear factors survive that verdict. First, the AEC lead is a field-reliability and design-in advantage ('ZeroFlap'), not a physics or qualification gate of the kind that protects EML lasers, EUV, or CoWoS, so it is more contestable than the multiple assumes while Astera, Marvell and Broadcom attack it and hyperscalers structurally want a second source. Second, revenue concentration is a survival variable, not a fading nuisance: the top three customers are 34% / 27% / 16% (77% combined) and the top four are ~87%, so a single anchor in-sourcing or losing a socket re-rates the business and the multiple simultaneously. The bullish second leg, optical, is a late entry into Marvell's 80%-share DSP stronghold at the exact moment LPO/CPO architectures remove the retimed DSP, and the $1.3B DustPhotonics full-stack bet adds integration and margin risk (gross margin already guided from ~68% toward 64–66%). This is a genuinely excellent, category-leading business at a price that discounts flawless multi-leg execution; the risk/reward does not clear the bar as a new position until AEC-share durability through the 224G transition and the optical leg's margins are demonstrated.

## Key Non-consensus Insights
- **AEC dominance is a reliability lead, not a qualification-gate monopoly**
  - **Consensus:** 73–88% AEC share equals a durable monopoly; the recent selloff was a buying opportunity and 'the bear case is dead'.
  - **Variant:** the moat is 'ZeroFlap' field-reliability reputation plus per-generation design-in, not a physics/yield gate. Copper AEC has structurally lower entry barriers than butt-joint EML regrowth or hybrid bonding; Astera (Taurus smart cable module), Marvell and Broadcom are all attacking, and hyperscaler procurement always seeks a second source on any part that scales into billions. Share this high in a contestable category is a melting asset unless switching costs are proven to rise each generation.
  - **First confirming observable [2026-2027]:** a hyperscaler dual-sourcing a 224G AEC socket to Astera/Marvell, or channel checks showing Credo AEC share slipping below ~70%.
  - **Falsifier:** Credo holds >80% AEC share through the 224G transition with stable-to-rising ASPs (would evidence genuine design lock-in; ties to → HIGH).
- **The multiple prices flawless multi-leg execution while the second leg is a laggard entry**
  - **Consensus:** optical is a clean >$600M FY27 leg that de-risks concentration and extends the runway; >80% FY27 growth is a safe extrapolation.
  - **Variant:** Credo is entering retimed optical DSP where Marvell already holds 80%+, precisely as the architecture shifts to LPO/CPO that strips the DSP out of short-reach modules (see [[Sectors/Optical Networking & Photonics]]). The DustPhotonics acquisition ($750M cash + up to ~$1.3B with earnout) buys a full SerDes→DSP→PIC→system stack, but full-stack integration is unproven and dilutive: blended gross margin is guided down from ~68% to 64–66% as optical and cable mix rises.
  - **First confirming observable [FY2027]:** optical revenue tracking to the >$600M guide with gross margin holding ≥66%.
  - **Falsifier:** optical revenue misses guide or gross margin compresses below 64% on mix (ties to → LOW).
- **Customer concentration is a binary survival test, not a diversification-over-time story**
  - **Consensus:** concentration is steadily declining (4th and 5th hyperscalers added, neoclouds targeted at ~20%, no customer above 35% by FY28), so the risk fades.
  - **Variant:** applying the anchor-customer test (Industry-Semis #10), the live question is not the trend but 'what happens if one anchor in-sources or loses a socket', and the answer is a revenue step-down and a multiple de-rate at once. The stock discounts the smoothing trend; the tail is what breaks it, and neocloud diversification substitutes hyperscaler credit risk for levered single-tenant credit risk.
  - **First confirming observable:** any single customer's AEC/optical order pause, in-sourcing disclosure, or a quarter where a >10% customer drops out of the count.
  - **Falsifier:** top customer <25% and top-four <70% of revenue with neoclouds ≥15%, sustained two quarters (concentration genuinely de-risked).
- **Copper short-reach is structurally advantaged today, but it is a bridge whose asymptote optics can reset**
  - **Consensus:** AEC rides the 224G copper sweet spot as passive DAC runs out of reach and optics stays too power-hungry and expensive for in-rack, a durable secular lane.
  - **Variant:** the same architecture transition that opens the AEC window (NVL72 → rack-scale, 224G/lane) also pulls scale-up toward co-packaged and near-packaged optics; copper reach roughly halves each speed generation, so the AEC TAM is a moving target that CPO/optical scale-up fabrics can compress at 448G. Owning the copper-DSP layer is valuable only for as long as copper is the answer to scale-up.
  - **First confirming observable [2027-2028]:** hyperscaler scale-up designs at the next generation choosing optical/CPO over copper AEC for in-rack links.
  - **Falsifier:** AEC content per rack still rising at the 448G generation (copper lane proves durable).

## Outstanding Questions
- What is the real switching cost of a Credo AEC design-in? Is a socket re-competed each platform generation, or does qualification carry forward, and what does that imply for the 73–88% share persisting past 224G?
- How does the FY27 >80% guide decompose across AEC unit growth, optical ramp, and price? What is organic AEC growth ex-optical, and is any of it forward-buy under allocation dynamics rather than deployment?
- What are the true unit economics of an AEC (a cable is BOM-heavy: connectors, copper, assembly) versus a bare DSP or IP licence? SemiAnalysis argues 'thin margins'; is blended ~68% gross margin sustainable as cable mix rises, or does it structurally trend toward the 64–66% guide floor?
- Can Credo win durable optical DSP share against Marvell's 80%+, or is it a perennial third source? Does the DustPhotonics full-stack change the competitive game, or does it add integration and goodwill risk at the exact moment retimed DSP faces LPO/CPO disintermediation?
- What is the credit and durability profile of the neocloud cohort management wants at ~20% of revenue? Neoclouds are levered, single-tenant, and capex-cyclical; is this genuine diversification or concentration into a weaker counterparty class?
- Governance: what is the size and dilution of the recently granted CEO performance PSU award, and what is Lip-Bu Tan's current relationship to the board now that he is CEO of Intel (a potential conflict and the loss of a founding sponsor)?
- Scale-up standard risk: Blue Heron supports UALink / ESUN / Ethernet, but if Nvidia's proprietary NVLink wins the intra-rack scale-up socket, how much of the retimer TAM shrinks?
- What is the drawdown if AI capex growth decelerates in 2027? The stock is high-beta to AI-infrastructure sentiment independent of Credo's own execution.

## Business Model & Product Description
Credo is a fabless connectivity semiconductor company. Its economic role is the 'nervous system' of the AI rack: it moves bits between compute, memory and switches at 224G per lane with acceptable power, cost and reliability, and it collects a toll on that data movement. Founded in 2008 with early backing and later chairmanship from Lip-Bu Tan's Walden International, the company's DNA is mixed-signal SerDes design, and every product is an expression of that one core competence applied to a different physical medium.

Revenue segments, by an economics-based heuristic rather than the sparse reported lines:

| Segment | What it is | Role in the model |
|---|---|---|
| **AEC (active electrical cables)** | Copper cables with Credo's DSP/SerDes silicon in the connector heads ('purple cables'); active equalisation extends copper reach where passive DAC fails at 224G | Historic majority of revenue; the 'ZeroFlap' field-reliability reputation is the differentiator. Ramped at three hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, xAI) |
| **Optical DSP** | Cardinal second-gen 1.6T DSP family, 3nm, 224G/lane; supports full-retimed and linear-receive optics (LRO) | The growth leg; targeted >$600M optical portfolio in FY27, on track to double optical revenue. Late entrant vs Marvell's 80% share |
| **Line-card PHY + retimers** | Toucan PCIe 6.0 retimer (PCI-SIG compliant at 32 GT/s, Feb 2026); Blue Heron 224G multiprotocol AI scale-up retimer (UALink / ESUN / Ethernet, production CQ3 2026) | Opens PCIe and scale-up fabric TAMs; design wins targeted FY26 convert to revenue FY27 |
| **SerDes IP / chiplets** | Licensable SerDes IP, the underlying analog franchise | Smaller, high-margin, seeds the product lines |
| **Silicon photonics** | DustPhotonics SiPho PICs (acquired 2026, ~$1.3B); gives Credo the full SerDes→DSP→PIC→system stack | Strategic bet that owning the PIC layer captures more optical value; unproven integration |

The mechanism that makes AEC work commercially: as port speeds rise to 224G/lane, passive copper (DAC) loses the reach needed even for in-rack links, while optics carries a power and cost penalty that is hard to justify below a few metres. An AEC inserts Credo's DSP into the cable to recover the copper signal, delivering optics-like reach at copper-like power and cost for the <3–7m in-rack domain. The FY26 print (revenue $1.3B, +206%; Q4 $437M, +157% YoY; non-GAAP gross margin 68.3%; non-GAAP operating margin 47.8%; non-GAAP net income $662M) shows the operating leverage of a fabless model where Q4 capex was $4.8M against $177.5M of free cash flow.

## Industry Context
Credo sits in the connectivity layer of the AI data centre, between the compute/XPU and the switch, owning the specific niche of DSP-in-cable plus retimers. The value chain runs SerDes IP → DSP → module/cable, and Credo is unusual in monetising the DSP directly inside the physical interconnect rather than selling it to a module maker.

Competitive structure (detail in [[Sectors/Custom Silicon & Networking Semiconductors]]):

| Player | Position vs Credo |
|---|---|
| **Astera Labs (ALAB)** | The direct threat; strongest in PCIe/CXL retimers and smart-fabric switching, pushing the Taurus smart cable module into AEC. Credo holds the AEC high ground on shipping volume and reliability; Astera holds the PCIe high ground. Cross-selling decides it |
| **Marvell (MRVL)** | 80%+ optical DSP share (the leg Credo is entering late); also has AEC and custom silicon; far larger and diversified |
| **Broadcom (AVGO)** | The SerDes benchmark (224G BER <1e-15); entering AEC adjacencies; can bundle across the stack |
| **MaxLinear, Semtech, Point2, Alphawave, Macom** | Sub-scale AEC/ACC and IP challengers named in the SemiAnalysis competitive map |

Structural forces reshaping the layer: the $660–690B 2026 hyperscaler capex wave is the demand driver; the 224G transition and NVL72 → rack-scale shift move scarcity toward interconnect bandwidth and power; the scale-up fabric wars (NVLink vs UALink vs Broadcom SUE vs OCP ESUN) determine whose retimers get designed in; and the copper-versus-optics boundary at short reach, plus LPO/CPO at the optical layer, sets how large and how durable the copper-DSP TAM is. Credo has real pricing power in AEC today; it has little in optical, where it is a share-taker against an entrenched leader.

## Bull Case
Credo is the category creator of AEC with 73–88% share, riding the largest infrastructure capex wave in computing history. FY27 is guided to >80% growth (>$2.4B revenue), with optical (>$600M target) becoming a genuine second leg, scale-up retimers (Blue Heron across UALink/ESUN/Ethernet) and PCIe (Toucan) opening new TAMs, and the DustPhotonics full stack positioning Credo to capture more optical value per port. The financial model is pristine for a hardware company: ~68% gross margin, ~48% operating margin, fabless with negligible capex, debt-free with ~$3.8B cash and free cash flow inflecting hard. Copper short-reach interconnect is structurally advantaged at 224G, and content per rack rises with every speed and radix increase. If AEC share holds, the optical and scale-up legs ramp, and concentration de-risks toward the 'no customer >35% by FY28' target, Credo re-rates from a single-product momentum name to a multi-product connectivity compounder, which is the case sell-side underwrites at $300+ targets.

## Bear Case
At ~29x trailing EV/revenue, ~30–40x sales and ~93x earnings, the stock prices multi-year flawless execution; sell-side's own bear scenarios sit near $196 (−16%), and the drawdown on any AI-capex wobble is far larger given the beta. The AEC moat is a reliability and design-in lead in a physically contestable copper category, and Astera, Marvell and Broadcom are attacking while hyperscalers actively seek a second source; SemiAnalysis argues demand is overstated and margins are thinner than the model implies. The optical leg is a late entry into Marvell's 80% stronghold precisely as LPO/CPO removes the retimed DSP, so the growth leg carries execution and margin risk (gross margin guided down to 64–66%), and DustPhotonics adds integration and goodwill risk. Copper is a bridge: the next architecture generation can route scale-up onto optics/CPO and reset the AEC asymptote. Above all, the top four customers are ~87% of revenue, so a single hyperscaler in-sourcing SerDes/connectivity IP, pausing orders, or shifting a socket delivers a revenue step-down and a multiple de-rate at the same time. In the world where this loses money, AI capex growth decelerates in 2027, one anchor diversifies away, and a ~30x revenue multiple compresses toward the sector.

## Risks
- **Thesis risk — AEC share erosion:** the moat is a reliability lead, not a qualification gate; Astera/Marvell/Broadcom take share at 224G and pricing power fades.
- **Thesis risk — optical leg fails to scale or dilutes margin:** late entry vs Marvell 80%, LPO/CPO disintermediates retimed DSP, DustPhotonics integration disappoints and goodwill is impaired.
- **Thesis risk — copper superseded:** optical/CPO wins the next-generation scale-up socket and the AEC TAM compresses at 448G.
- **Concentration risk (binary):** top four customers ~87%; a single anchor in-sourcing, pausing, or losing a socket is a step-change down in revenue.
- **Position risk — AI-capex cyclicality:** a 2027 capex deceleration de-rates the entire AI-interconnect complex regardless of Credo's execution.
- **Position risk — multiple compression:** at ~30x revenue the stock is priced for perfection and is high-beta to AI sentiment; any growth wobble compresses the multiple violently.
- **Governance risk:** large CEO performance PSU award (dilution/incentive alignment) and the changed role of founding sponsor Lip-Bu Tan.
