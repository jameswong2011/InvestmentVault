# SIVE — Sivers Semiconductors AB

## Summary

The only listed pure-play on the External Light Source (ELS) layer of the co-packaged optics ramp, trading at ~55x EV/Revenue after a 1,768% 3-month rally that priced in a transformation Sivers has not yet executed. The Photonics segment (Glasgow-based 100mm InP fab, "InP100" platform) supplies DFB lasers and laser arrays to Ayar Labs, Aeva, POET, O-Net + Enablence, Jabil, and Imec — virtually every Western CPO contender that lacks vertical InP capability and cannot secure Lumentum or Coherent allocation. The Wireless segment (Stockholm mmWave 5G transceivers) is dilutive but provides optionality on Tier-1 telecom OEM design wins and Tachyon Networks 28GHz FWA modules. Full-year 2025 revenue restated to SEK 306.6M (+25% YoY) under PCAOB upgrade ahead of Nasdaq NY dual-listing, against SEK 186.5M net loss and SEK 43.5M year-end cash — repaired temporarily by a SEK 125M directed share issue and a 10.85% coupon convertible bond whose conversion mechanics now sit deep in-the-money. The non-consensus thesis is structural overflow demand: as Lumentum sells out through 2027 and NVIDIA dual-allocates capacity, every second-tier SiPh/CPO contender (POET, Celestial AI via POET, Ayar Labs, hyperscaler internal programs) is forced to qualify a secondary InP supplier — and Sivers is the only listed Western option without 24-month qualification backlogs. The bear case is equally structural: 4-inch wafers cannot achieve the cost-per-chip of LITE's 6-inch Greensboro line (mid-2028) or Coherent's four 6-inch fabs (online ~2027), and the Swedish Economic Crime Authority's preliminary investigation into the Nasdaq listing announcement leaves the dual-listing — the primary catalyst the multiple expects — at material binary risk. Initial conviction **low**: this is a high-volatility small-cap option on the photonics overflow trade, not a quality compounder.

## Key Non-consensus Insights

- **Sivers is the only listed pure-play on the External Light Source tier of CPO — and the market has not yet repriced the implication that Lumentum's "arms dealer" position requires a Tier-2 backstop.** Coherent and Lumentum each have ELS product lines, but ELS is <5% of their revenue mix and their allocation goes to NVIDIA-priority customers. Sivers Photonics is ~100% exposed to the CPO/AI laser source layer; every percentage point of CPO ELS demand that LITE/COHR cannot fulfill maps directly to Sivers' P&L. With the CPO market projected to grow at 37% CAGR from 2026-2036 toward $20B+ by 2036, even 2-3% market share of ELS revenue against a Tier-1 squeezed allocation is multi-X for a SEK 306M revenue base. The asymmetry is enormous from a small base — but only if Sivers converts even one hyperscaler-named qualification, which has not yet happened in public disclosure.

- **The 4-inch InP fab disadvantage is mispriced as a structural disqualification when it is actually a temporary cost-per-chip handicap during a capacity-binding phase.** LITE's Greensboro 6-inch fab does not produce volume until mid-2028; Coherent's four 6-inch lines ramp through 2027. Through this 18-24-month capacity gap, every viable CW laser source the SiPh and CPO ecosystem can qualify is being absorbed at premium prices. Sivers can ship 6.4T CPO-class ELS modules from Glasgow today — 6-inch only matters for ASP compression at full-rate volume past 2028. The fab is small enough (~10K wafers/year capacity) to scale incrementally without the $1B+ greenfield capex risk LITE took at Greensboro. The market is conflating "structurally subscale" with "structurally locked out" — they are different problems with different probabilities.

- **Sivers has aggregated more public SiPh/CPO partnerships than any independent laser supplier outside the LITE/COHR duopoly, creating "default winner" optionality across multiple CPO architectures.** Named partnerships span POET (Optical Interposer + Sivers DFB for Light Engines), O-Net + Enablence (ELS modules for CPO datacenter), Jabil (1.6T LRO pluggable transceiver, April 2026), Imec + ASM AMICRA (hybrid InP-on-SiPh integration), Ayar Labs (customer for CW lasers), Aeva (most-likely customer for $53-138M cumulative LiDAR program), Celestial AI (indirect via POET), ALL.SPACE (SATCOM). The structural pattern: every SiPh contender that cannot secure LITE allocation defaults to Sivers because the alternative supplier set (Sumitomo, Mitsubishi, internal captive) does not sell merchant. This creates an asymmetric exposure where Sivers wins regardless of which downstream CPO/ASIC vendor takes share — but each individual partnership remains pre-revenue and binary, so the diversification benefit only materializes if at least 2-3 convert to volume by 2027.

- **The Wireless segment is a free option attached to the photonics story — the market is pricing it at zero (or negative).** mmWave 5G is currently dilutive (R&D-heavy, small product revenue base ~SEK 86M total product revenue across both segments in 2025) but holds a $5.4M Tier-1 telecom OEM beamforming design contract running through Q4 2026, a Tachyon Networks production partnership for 28GHz FWA modules with Q1 2026 shipments, and named customer ALL.SPACE in SATCOM. Bears see Wireless as a money-pit absorbing photonics cash; the non-consensus framing is that even sub-scale mmWave product revenue (SEK 50-100M/year by 2028 if FWA TAM materializes) provides downside protection against photonics execution risk. The segment also creates strategic optionality on telecom 5G mmWave re-acceleration (Verizon C-band saturation forcing higher-band spectrum activation, T-Mobile capacity expansion in dense urban markets) which is currently not in consensus 5G capex forecasts.

- **The Nasdaq NY dual-listing is simultaneously the primary catalyst and the largest binary risk — a configuration the multiple does not reflect.** A US listing would transform the available investor base (Tier-1 US institutional, Russell index inclusion, Nasdaq Biotech-style growth specialist coverage), trigger sell-side initiations (Morgan Stanley, Goldman, etc.), and re-rate the multiple regardless of operational delivery. But Sweden's Economic Crime Authority has opened a preliminary investigation into whether confidential details about the listing leaked ahead of the April announcement, PCAOB-restated financials revealed deeper-than-disclosed losses (SEK 186.5M FY25 net loss adjusted up from prior reporting), and the CEO Vickram Vathulya voluntarily locked his shares for 90 days post-capital raise — a defensive gesture that signals governance concern. The market currently prices listing completion at high probability (the rally is partly listing-anticipation); a delay past 2026 or formal probe charges would unwind the multiple expansion fast. This is a binary event, not a slow-bleed risk.

Without product-shipment evidence at hyperscaler-qualified volume, the photonics thesis remains forward-looking. 6. **Does POET-Sivers ELS hardware actually qualify with a hyperscaler in 2026, or does it remain a 2027+ revenue?** POET Technologies has run a "production-ready by year-end" narrative since 2017 across multiple partnership iterations (none of which produced disclosed hyperscaler revenue). The margin question dominates the long-term EBITDA trajectory more than the revenue ramp.

## Business Model & Product Description

Sivers Semiconductors operates two reported business segments with starkly different end-market dynamics. The operating reality is best understood through analogy: **Sivers Photonics (Glasgow) is to the AI photonics build-out what a Tier-2 specialty chip foundry would be to a leading-edge logic foundry shortage — it captures overflow demand at premium pricing during a capacity-binding window, then has to defend its position when primary capacity unlocks.** Sivers Wireless (Stockholm/Kista) is the legacy 5G mmWave business carrying forward design contracts and SATCOM optionality. The Photonics subsidiary very nearly went through a separate Nasdaq spinout in 2024 — management ultimately consolidated under the parent, signaling that the Photonics narrative now drives the parent equity story.

**Photonics Segment (Glasgow, ~50-60% of revenue est. — undisclosed split)**: 100mm (4-inch) indium phosphide wafer fabrication, "InP100" product platform. The Glasgow site is the only listed independent Western InP fab outside Lumentum (US/UK/Japan) and Coherent (US). Product taxonomy:

- **DFB (Distributed Feedback) CW Lasers** — Continuous-wave laser sources optimized for external integration with silicon photonic transceivers and CPO optical engines. Wavelength assignments target 1310nm O-band for AI cluster intra-datacenter (where chromatic dispersion is minimized in single-mode fiber). Power efficiency and spectral stability are the gating performance metrics; Sivers' InP100 platform is positioned as competitive with LITE/COHR CW products at 200-400mW output.

- **DFB Laser Arrays** — Multi-channel arrays (4ch, 8ch, 16ch) for high-density CPO architectures. Used in External Light Source modules where a single fiber input feeds multiple wavelength channels into a SiPh chip. The Sivers + POET Light Engine reference uses this product class.

- **Semiconductor Optical Amplifiers (SOAs)** — Optical amplifiers integrated with CW lasers to boost output power for CPO modules where ELS coupling losses are material. Higher technical difficulty than CW lasers; provides ASP uplift on the laser array side.

- **Detector Chips (Photodiodes)** — High-speed InP photodiodes for the receive side of optical links. Smaller revenue contributor but completes the chipset offering.

- **LiDAR Lasers** — Application-specific InP laser variants for automotive and industrial LiDAR. The Strategic LiDAR Customer (widely speculated to be Aeva based on the customer's NVIDIA DRIVE Hyperion adjacency) is announced to ramp Q4 2026 with $53-138M cumulative revenue over product life-cycle. This is the largest disclosed dollar commitment in the photonics pipeline.

Named photonics customers and partnerships: **Ayar Labs** (DFB CW lasers for SuperNova ELS feeding their TeraPHY chiplet — Ayar Labs raised $500M Series E in March 2026), **Aeva** (likely LiDAR customer), **ALL.SPACE** (SATCOM beam-steering terminals), **Celestial AI** (via POET Optical Interposer partnership), **Fortune 100 unicorn tech firms** ("hyperscaler" in disclosure language), **POET Technologies** (Light Engine reference designs combining POET's Optical Interposer + Sivers DFB lasers, prototypes H1 2026), **O-Net + Enablence Technologies** (ODM partner for ELS modules for CPO, end-2026 production readiness), **Jabil** (1.6T LRO pluggable transceiver module collaboration announced April 2026, 2.5x lower energy footprint than incumbent alternatives), **Imec** (wafer-scale integration of InP100 lasers onto SiPh via ASM AMICRA hybrid bonding), and **Tachyon Networks** (28GHz module partnership crossing both segments).

**Wireless Segment (Stockholm/Kista, ~30-40% of revenue est.)**: mmWave 5G transceiver design and IP. Product taxonomy:

- **TRX-BF Beamforming Transceivers** — 28GHz and 39GHz integrated radio circuits for 5G base stations and Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) customer premise equipment. RF SiGe BiCMOS technology; targets 5G mmWave deployments in dense urban (carrier base stations) and FWA (fixed broadband replacement).

- **Tier-1 Telecom OEM Custom ASIC Program** — $5.4M multi-year contract running Q1 2025 to Q4 2026 to develop a next-generation highly-integrated beamforming transceiver for various mmWave telecom applications. The customer's 1st/2nd generation equipment is targeted for late 2026 release; 3rd generation co-development is underway. This is the only disclosed multi-year wireless revenue commitment.

- **mmWave Modules with Tachyon Networks** — 28GHz antenna modules with shipments scheduled Q1 2026, additional orders expected late 2026 for 2027+ deployments.

- **Satellite/SATCOM** — ALL.SPACE is a named SATCOM customer; the beam-steering and high-frequency capability extends to LEO/MEO satellite ground terminal applications.

The wireless segment's strategic role is debated: management presents it as a complementary technology synergy with photonics (some shared engineering, common parent overhead amortization); investor critics argue it should be divested or wound down to redirect capital to Photonics. Sivers attempted a Wireless divestiture preliminary process in 2023-2024; no transaction closed. As of May 2026, both segments operate under the parent.

**Revenue Architecture — A Novel Segmentation:**
Given the absence of clean segment disclosure, the most useful framework groups Sivers revenue into four buckets by visibility and economics:

1. **Photonics product revenue** (~SEK 100-150M 2025 est., 50%+ gross margin): DFB lasers, arrays, SOAs sold per piece into qualified customer programs. This is the volume layer that the bull case requires to scale 80-100% per year through 2028. Currently anchored by Ayar Labs and the SATCOM customer, expanding with LiDAR customer ramp Q4 2026.

2. **Photonics NRE / development funding** (~SEK 50-80M 2025 est., low single-digit margin): Customer-funded engineering for CPO ELS prototypes (POET, O-Net, Jabil, Enablence). NRE is lumpy and lower-margin but de-risks the eventual production ramp at the customer's expense — if the partnerships convert, NRE flips into product revenue at much higher margins.

3. **Wireless development contracts** (~SEK 50-80M 2025 est., low margin): The $5.4M Tier-1 OEM ASIC program and similar contracts. Multi-year, milestone-driven; not gross-margin-accretive but covers absorption.

4. **Wireless product** (~SEK 20-40M 2025 est., variable margin): mmWave modules, Tachyon Networks 28GHz shipments. Volume too low to materially move the P&L until 5G mmWave capex re-accelerates.

**Manufacturing Footprint**: Glasgow 100mm (4-inch) InP wafer fab is the only owned production asset. Wireless transceivers are fabless (outsourced to merchant foundries — SiGe BiCMOS). Total photonics wafer-start capacity ~10K wafers/year, with stated intent to scale via brownfield capacity additions vs. greenfield 6-inch buildout. The 4-inch platform decision is explicitly defended by management as a cost-discipline choice during the capacity-binding window — but it caps the long-term cost-per-chip ceiling vs. LITE Greensboro 6-inch (mid-2028) and Coherent's four 6-inch lines (~2027).

## Industry Context

The InP laser source stack stratifies into four tiers, each operating at different combinations of scale, capability, and customer access:

**Tier 1 — Western incumbents** (Lumentum, Coherent): Sole or dominant suppliers of 200G EMLs at volume. Lumentum holds 50-60% EML market share, sold out through 2027, expanding 8x from FY23 levels, with $2B NVIDIA strategic investment and US Greensboro 6-inch fab building (production mid-2028). Coherent operates four 6-inch InP fabs (~2027 fully online) with claimed >3-inch yield parity, also $2B NVIDIA investment. These two effectively allocate Western InP laser capacity.

**Tier 2 — Specialty / merchant** (Sivers Photonics, Innolume, NeoPhotonics legacy, IPG specialty): Niche players with multi-customer engagements but no 200G EML at scale. Sivers' InP100 platform is the only listed independent option in this tier. Innolume (private, German, quantum-dot mode-locked combs) named by NVIDIA as a CPO laser partner; non-public.

**Tier 3 — Asian merchant + captive** (Sumitomo Electric, Mitsubishi Electric, NEL/NTT, Fujitsu Optical Components, Broadcom captive Breinigsville fab): Sumitomo and Mitsubishi qualify 200G EMLs alongside LITE; their merchant volume is constrained and largely allocated to Japanese OEMs (Furukawa, NEC) and select Chinese transceiver vendors. Broadcom's captive Breinigsville fab supplies Tomahawk-linked optics.

**Tier 4 — Chinese localization push** (Yuanjie Semiconductor, Everbright Photon, Henlitz, others): Currently ~3% localization rate at 25G+, 0% at 200G per BOC International estimates. China is targeting 2028-2030 commercial 200G EML validation under national strategic priority. Source Photonics China demo timelines have slipped 12-24 months relative to Western leaders.

**Where Sivers sits in this map**: Tier 2, with structural growth dependent on three forces: (a) **overflow demand from LITE/COHR sold-out allocation** (binding through ~2028), (b) **SiPh and CPO ecosystem growth** which requires external InP CW lasers regardless of which downstream ASIC/module vendor wins, and (c) **partnership-driven design wins** (POET, O-Net, Jabil, Imec) that route Sivers into customer programs the Tier-1 vendors are not engaged in.

**The "SiPh paradox" tailwind**: Silicon photonics cannot generate its own light (silicon's indirect bandgap precludes lasing) — every SiPh transceiver and every CPO module requires external III-V laser sources. As GlobalFoundries-AMF, Tower Semi, TSMC COUPE, and STMicroelectronics scale SiPh foundry capacity through 2026-2030, demand for InP CW lasers grows proportionally. The current InP supply gap is estimated at 70% (2M pieces demand vs 600K capacity), validating scarcity pricing across the tier. Sivers benefits directly.

**The "200G EML moat does not extend to CW lasers" reality**: Lumentum's 200G EML monopoly rests on butt-joint regrowth — a multi-decade epitaxial process moat that physically blocks new entrants. CW lasers do not require butt-joint regrowth (DFB-only architecture, no EA modulator); the manufacturing barrier is materially lower. Sivers, IQE-supplied epi wafers, and other Tier-2 vendors compete with LITE in CW lasers on different ground — output power, wavelength stability, packaging integration. This is structurally favorable for Sivers' positioning relative to a "compete with LITE on EML" framing.

**Competitive dynamics moving forward**: 
- 2026-2027: capacity-binding window favors Tier-2 overflow capture. Sivers' window.
- 2027-2028: LITE Greensboro 6-inch comes online; Coherent's 6-inch fully ramps; capacity expands ~3x. ASP pressure begins on CW lasers if Tier-1 willingness to allocate increases.
- 2028-2030: Chinese 200G EML / CW laser localization milestones expected to constrain Sivers' Western pricing as Chinese SiPh module vendors gain alternative supply.

**Value chain leverage**: Sivers sits at the "merchant component" layer — below the ASIC (Broadcom, NVIDIA, Marvell) and module (Innolight, Coherent, Sivers' own partners like Jabil) but above the foundry/equipment layer (TSMC COUPE, GlobalFoundries-AMF, Tower; Aixtron MOCVD, Veeco). At the merchant component layer, pricing power depends on (a) customer fragmentation (high — every downstream SiPh contender is a candidate buyer) and (b) supplier concentration in adjacent components (high — InP epitaxy concentrated at IQE, IntelliEPI, and Eindhoven's Smart Photonics). Sivers' pricing power is mid-tier: durable through 2027 capacity window, eroding 2028+ as Tier-1 capacity unlocks and Chinese localization advances.

## Bull Case

- **Photonics segment grows 80-120% per year 2026-2028** as CPO External Light Source demand exceeds LITE/COHR's allocable capacity. By 2028, Photonics reaches SEK 800M-1.2B revenue at 50%+ gross margin (vs. estimated SEK 100-150M 2025), compounding off the Aeva LiDAR ramp (Q4 2026 start, $138M upper-end cumulative through 2030-2033) + named hyperscaler ELS design wins via POET/O-Net/Jabil partnerships.
- **At least 2-3 named hyperscaler CPO ELS qualifications are announced by YE 2027** — POET-Sivers Light Engine, O-Net-Enablence-Sivers ELS module, and either Marvell or a Top-3 hyperscaler internal program reach commercial PO stage. This converts the partnership inventory from "potential" to "validated overflow capture."
- **Aeva (assumed LiDAR customer) achieves 2028 NVIDIA DRIVE Hyperion SOP** for at least one production OEM program (current candidates: a mainstream OEM nameplate alongside the existing partnerships). Sivers' $138M cumulative upper-bound becomes realistic; 2027-2030 LiDAR revenue trail visible.
- **Wireless segment crosses break-even by 2027** as Tier-1 telecom OEM 3rd-generation equipment ramps and Tachyon Networks 28GHz FWA volume scales. mmWave 5G capex re-accelerates as Verizon C-band saturation and T-Mobile dense-urban needs push higher-band spectrum activation.
- **Nasdaq NY dual-listing completes in H2 2026 without probe escalation**, expanding the investor base (Tier-1 US institutional, Russell index inclusion, US-based sell-side initiations from Morgan Stanley / Goldman / Cantor) and creating multiple re-rating room — even at 20-25x EV/Revenue on 2027 expected SEK 700-900M, the equity supports SEK 90-130/share.
- **Optionality on additional design wins**: SATCOM TAM continues to expand (ALL.SPACE, Starlink-adjacent peers, defense LEO), 1.6T LRO Jabil module qualifies with a hyperscaler, and a second LiDAR customer emerges as automotive ADAS demand broadens. Each adds incremental revenue without requiring fab capacity expansion.

**Valuation framework**: 2028E revenue SEK 1.2-1.5B at 22-28x EV/Revenue = EV SEK 26-42B, less share count growth from convertible conversion (+10-15% dilution) = SEK 75-120/share fair value range. Bull case mid-point ~SEK 100/share = ~70% upside from current ~59 SEK.

## Bear Case

The cost-per-chip gap widens as Tier-1 unlocks volume; Sivers becomes a perpetual second-source for niche programs without scaling into commodity CPO ELS revenue. Photonics revenue stalls at SEK 200-300M as overflow demand evaporates.
- **POET-Sivers, O-Net-Enablence-Sivers, and Jabil 1.6T LRO partnerships remain prototype/sample revenue through 2027**. POET Technologies has a multi-year pattern of revenue slippage (multiple "production-ready by year-end" announcements since 2017 across iterations); no Sivers partnership produces disclosed hyperscaler revenue by YE 2027. The narrative inventory of named partnerships fails to convert.
- **Aeva NVIDIA DRIVE Hyperion program delays past 2028** — automotive ADAS LiDAR programs have a history of OEM-side timeline slippage. Sivers' $53-138M cumulative LiDAR forecast pushes from 2026-2030 to 2029-2033, with the lower end (probable outcome) of $53M absorbed across 5-7 years — immaterial to revenue trajectory.
- **Cash burn forces 1-2 additional equity raises within 24 months at successively higher dilution.** The 10.85% coupon convertible bond converts at deep discount to current stock price; every additional raise dilutes existing shareholders 5-15%. By 2028, share count grows to 350M+ (vs 296.5M now). Combined with multiple compression, per-share value declines despite enterprise growth.
- **Swedish Economic Crime Authority probe escalates to formal charges**, Nasdaq NY listing delayed past 2027 or cancelled. F-1 review surfaces additional accounting concerns from the PCAOB restatement. US institutional investor base does not materialize; multiple compresses to 8-15x EV/Revenue.
- **AI infrastructure capex normalizes from 60%+ growth to 15-20%** by 2027, CPO TAM revisions push out 12-24 months, ELS demand grows linearly with SiPh foundry buildout instead of exponentially. Sivers' total addressable revenue caps at SEK 500-700M.
- **Wireless segment continues to absorb cash without volume conversion** — 5G mmWave capex does not re-accelerate; Tachyon Networks FWA fails to scale; Tier-1 telecom OEM 3rd-generation cycle pushes to 2028+. Wireless drags total gross margin and consumes capital that should fund Photonics expansion.

**Valuation framework**: 2028E revenue SEK 500-700M at 8-15x EV/Revenue = EV SEK 4-10B, share count grown to ~350M from dilution = SEK 11-29/share fair value. Bear case mid-point ~SEK 18-20/share = ~65-70% downside from current ~59 SEK.
